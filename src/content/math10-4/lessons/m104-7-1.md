How much fencing goes around a dog run? How much baseboard goes around a room? How much edging goes around a round garden? Each of these jobs needs the distance **around** a shape.

## Perimeter of a rectangle

:::definition Perimeter
The **perimeter** is the total distance around the outside of a shape. It is a length, so it is measured in units like metres (m) or centimetres (cm).
:::

To find a perimeter, add the lengths of **all** the sides. A rectangle has two long sides that match and two short sides that match.

:::example Fencing a dog run
A rectangular dog run is $12\ \text{m}$ long and $5\ \text{m}$ wide. How much fencing goes around it?
@step The four sides are $12$, $5$, $12$ and $5$.
@step $P = 12 + 5 + 12 + 5 = 34$
@answer The dog run needs $34\ \text{m}$ of fencing.
:::

:::tip Shortcuts
Rectangle: add the length and the width, then double. $P = 2(l + w) = 2(12 + 5) = 34$.
Square: all four sides are equal, so $P = 4 \times \text{side}$.
:::

:::mistake Adding only two sides
$12 + 5 = 17$ is only **half** of the way around. A rectangle has four sides. Trace all the way around with your finger.
:::

## Triangles and other shapes

For any shape with straight sides, add every side. Make sure all the sides use the **same unit**.

:::example Edging for a flower bed
A triangular flower bed has sides of $4.2\ \text{m}$, $3.5\ \text{m}$ and $5.8\ \text{m}$. How much edging goes around it?
@step $P = 4.2 + 3.5 + 5.8$
@answer $P = 13.5\ \text{m}$ of edging.

## Finding a missing side

Sometimes you know the perimeter and need one side. Take away the sides you know. What is left belongs to the missing side or sides.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":340,"yMin":0,"yMax":196,"step":340},"hideTickLabels":true,"polylines":[{"points":[[71,34.3],[271,34.3],[271,162.87],[71,162.87],[71,34.3]]}],"caption":"rectangular dog run","points":[{"x":148.8,"y":11,"label":"14 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":278,"y":86.29,"label":"?","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":148.8,"y":170.57,"label":"14 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":50,"y":86.29,"label":"?","color":"muted","open":true}]}
:::

:::example How wide is the dog run?
A rectangular dog run uses $46\ \text{m}$ of fencing. It is $14\ \text{m}$ long. How wide is it?
@step The two long sides use $14 + 14 = 28\ \text{m}$.
@step That leaves $46 - 28 = 18\ \text{m}$ for the two short sides.
@step Each short side is $18 \div 2 = 9\ \text{m}$.
@step Check: $14 + 9 + 14 + 9 = 46$ ✓
@answer The dog run is $9\ \text{m}$ wide.
:::

:::

## Circumference

:::definition Circumference, diameter and radius
The **circumference** is the perimeter of a circle. The **diameter** ($d$) is the distance across a circle through its centre. The **radius** ($r$) is the distance from the centre to the edge. The diameter is two times the radius.
:::

:::definition Pi
**Pi** ($\pi$) is the number of times the diameter fits around a circle. It is about $3.14$. Use the $\pi$ key on your calculator.
:::

$$C = \pi \times d \qquad \text{or} \qquad C = 2 \times \pi \times r$$

:::example Trim for a round table
A round table has a radius of $45\ \text{cm}$. How long is the trim around its edge? Round to the nearest tenth.
@step Double the radius: $d = 2 \times 45 = 90\ \text{cm}$.
@step $C = \pi \times 90 \approx 282.74$
@answer The trim is about $282.7\ \text{cm}$ long.
:::

:::mistake Radius or diameter?
$C = \pi \times d$ needs the **diameter**. If you use the radius by mistake, your answer is only half as long as it should be.
:::

:::summary
- Perimeter is the distance around a shape. Add all the sides, in the same unit.
- Rectangle: $P = 2(l + w)$. Square: $P = 4 \times \text{side}$.
- To find a missing side, subtract the sides you know from the perimeter.
- Circumference: $C = \pi \times d$, or $C = 2 \times \pi \times r$. Round only at the end.
:::
