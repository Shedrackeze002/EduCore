Fractions are part of many jobs. A mechanic picks a $\dfrac{9}{16}$ inch wrench. A cook measures $\dfrac{3}{4}$ cup of milk. A carpenter cuts a board $2\dfrac{1}{2}$ feet long. In this lesson you will learn what fractions mean, how to write the same amount in different ways, and how to tell which fraction is bigger.

## What a fraction means

A fraction names part of a whole. The whole is cut into equal parts.

:::definition Numerator and denominator
In the fraction $\dfrac{3}{4}$, the bottom number is the **denominator**. It tells how many equal parts the whole is cut into.
The top number is the **numerator**. It tells how many of those parts you have.
:::

:::example Slices of pizza
A pizza is cut into $8$ equal slices. Amara eats $3$ slices. What fraction of the pizza does she eat?
@step The pizza is cut into $8$ equal parts, so the denominator is $8$.
@step She eats $3$ parts, so the numerator is $3$.
@answer Amara eats $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the pizza.
:::

Measurements often have a whole number and a fraction together.

:::definition Mixed number
A **mixed number** has a whole number and a fraction, like $2\dfrac{1}{2}$. It means $2$ wholes plus $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of another whole.
:::

:::definition Equivalent fractions
**Equivalent fractions** name the same amount, like $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac{4}{8}$.
To make an equivalent fraction, multiply (or divide) the numerator **and** the denominator by the same number.
:::

:::example Find the missing numerator
Fill in the missing number: $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{?}{8}$
@step The bottom changed from $4$ to $8$. That is $\times 2$.
@step Do the same to the top: $3 \times 2 = 6$.
@answer $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{6}{8}$, so the missing number is $6$.
:::

:::example Find the missing denominator
Fill in the missing number: $\dfrac{12}{16} = \dfrac{3}{?}$
@step The top changed from $12$ to $3$. That is $\div 4$.
@step Do the same to the bottom: $16 \div 4 = 4$.
@answer $\dfrac{12}{16} = \dfrac{3}{4}$, so the missing number is $4$.
:::

:::mistake Adding instead of multiplying
$\dfrac{3}{4}$ is **not** equal to $\dfrac{5}{6}$. Adding $2$ to the top and bottom changes the amount. Only multiplying or dividing both parts by the same number keeps it the same.
:::

## Lowest terms

:::definition Lowest terms
A fraction is in **lowest terms** when no whole number bigger than $1$ divides both the numerator and the denominator. To get there, divide both by the biggest number that goes into both.
:::

:::example Raffle tickets
Rosa sold $18$ of the $24$ raffle tickets. What fraction did she sell, in lowest terms?
@step Part over whole: $\dfrac{18}{24}$.
@step The biggest number that divides $18$ and $24$ is $6$.
@step $18 \div 6 = 3$ and $24 \div 6 = 4$.
@answer She sold $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of the tickets.
:::

:::mistake Stopping too soon
Dividing $\dfrac{18}{24}$ by $2$ gives $\dfrac{9}{12}$. That is equivalent, but $9$ and $12$ can still both be divided by $3$. Keep going until nothing else divides both.
:::

Wrenches and sockets are labelled in lowest terms. If a tape measure shows that a bolt head is $\dfrac{10}{16}$ inch across, divide both by $2$ to get $\dfrac{5}{8}$. You need the $\dfrac{5}{8}$ inch wrench.

:::

## Comparing and ordering fractions

When the denominators are the same, the pieces are the same size. The fraction with the bigger numerator is bigger: $\dfrac{5}{8} > \dfrac{3}{8}$.

When the numerators are both $1$, think about pizza. A pizza cut into $3$ slices has bigger slices than one cut into $8$. So $\dfrac{1}{3} > \dfrac{1}{8}$.

When the denominators are different, write the fractions with the **same** denominator first.

:::example Which wrench is bigger?
Which is bigger: a $\dfrac{3}{8}$ inch wrench or a $\dfrac{5}{16}$ inch wrench?
@step Change eighths to sixteenths: $\dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{6}{16}$.
@step Compare the numerators: $6 > 5$, so $\dfrac{6}{16} > \dfrac{5}{16}$.
@answer The $\dfrac{3}{8}$ inch wrench is bigger.
:::

The number line shows both sizes between $0$ and $1$ inch. The tick marks are sixteenths.

:::mistake A bigger number is not always a bigger fraction
$\dfrac{5}{16}$ has bigger numbers than $\dfrac{3}{8}$, but it is the smaller fraction. Compare fractions only after they have the same denominator.
:::

:::example Ordering board lengths
Put these boards in order from shortest to longest: $2\dfrac{3}{4}$ ft, $2\dfrac{3}{8}$ ft, $2\dfrac{1}{2}$ ft.
@step The whole numbers are all $2$, so compare the fractions.
@step Write them in eighths: $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{6}{8}$, $\dfrac{3}{8}$, and $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{4}{8}$.
@step $\dfrac{3}{8} < \dfrac{4}{8} < \dfrac{6}{8}$
@answer $2\dfrac{3}{8}$ ft, $2\dfrac{1}{2}$ ft, $2\dfrac{3}{4}$ ft
:::

:::tip Whole numbers first
For mixed numbers, compare the whole numbers first. $3\dfrac{1}{8}$ is longer than $2\dfrac{7}{8}$ because $3 > 2$.
:::

:::summary
- The denominator is how many equal parts make the whole. The numerator is how many parts you have.
- Equivalent fractions name the same amount. Multiply or divide the top and bottom by the same number.
- Lowest terms: divide the top and bottom by the biggest number that goes into both.
- To compare fractions, give them the same denominator, then compare the numerators.
:::
