Boxes in a warehouse, tents at a campground and the roof of a gazebo are all 3-D objects. Knowing the parts of a 3-D object helps you build it, pack it and stack it.

## Faces, edges and vertices

:::definition Face, edge and vertex
- A **face** is a flat surface of a 3-D object.
- An **edge** is a line where two faces meet.
- A **vertex** is a corner where edges meet. We say one **vertex**, two **vertices**.
:::

A shipping box is a **rectangular prism**. In the drawing, dashed lines show the edges you cannot see from the front.

:::figure

:::example Counting the parts of a shipping box
How many faces, edges and vertices does a shipping box have?
@step Faces: top, bottom, front, back, left and right. That is $6$ faces.
@step Edges: $4$ around the top, $4$ around the bottom and $4$ going up the sides. $4 + 4 + 4 = 12$ edges.
@step Vertices: $4$ corners on the top and $4$ on the bottom. $4 + 4 = 8$ vertices.
@answer The box has $6$ faces, $12$ edges and $8$ vertices.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the hidden parts
Count the back, the bottom and the dashed edges too. Count in groups (top, bottom, sides) so you do not miss any or count any twice.
:::

:::

## Prisms and pyramids

:::definition Prism and pyramid
A **prism** has two matching ends joined by rectangles. A **pyramid** has one base, and triangles that meet at one point. Each one is named by the shape of its end or base.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"triPrism","labels":{}}
:::

:::example A camping tent
A tent is a **triangular prism**. Count its faces, edges and vertices.
@step Faces: $2$ triangle ends and $3$ rectangles. $2 + 3 = 5$ faces.
@step Edges: $3$ on each end and $3$ joining the ends. $3 + 3 + 3 = 9$ edges.
@step Vertices: $3$ on each end. $3 + 3 = 6$ vertices.
@answer The tent has $5$ faces, $9$ edges and $6$ vertices.
:::

A **net** is a flat pattern that folds up into a 3-D object. Box makers cut nets out of cardboard.

:::definition View
A **view** is the flat picture you see when you look straight at an object from one side. The **top view** is from above. The **front view** is from the front.
:::

:::summary
- Faces are flat surfaces, edges are where faces meet, and vertices are corners.
- A prism has two matching ends. A pyramid has one base and triangles that meet at a point.
- A net is a flat pattern that folds into a 3-D object.
- In a front view, each column shows its tallest stack.
:::
