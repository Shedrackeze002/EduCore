A calculator is a great tool, but it only gives the right answer if you press the right keys. A quick estimate in your head tells you whether an answer makes sense. This lesson shows how to estimate, and how to plan and check a word problem with more than one step.

## Estimating

You do not always need an exact answer. Sometimes "about how much" is enough, like when you check if you have enough money at the till.

:::definition Estimate
An **estimate** is an answer that is close to the exact answer. You find it by rounding the numbers so the maths is easy to do in your head.
:::

To estimate, round each number to its first digit (for example, $48 \approx 50$ and $412 \approx 400$). Then do the maths with the rounded numbers.

:::

:::example Estimating a total
Sam buys a jacket for \$48 and boots for \$31. About how much does he spend?
@step Round: $48 \approx 50$ and $31 \approx 30$.
@step Add: $50 + 30 = 80$.
@answer Sam spends about \$80. (The exact total is \$79.)
:::

:::example Estimating a product
A hotel has $42$ rooms on each floor and $19$ floors. About how many rooms does it have?
@step Round: $42 \approx 40$ and $19 \approx 20$.
@step Multiply: $40 \times 20 = 800$.
@answer The hotel has about $800$ rooms. (The exact number is $798$.)
:::

For division, change the first number to a nearby number that divides easily.

:::example Estimating a quotient
A truck driver drives $2{,}437$ km in $6$ days, the same distance each day. About how far does the driver go each day?
@step $2{,}437$ is close to $2{,}400$, and $24 \div 6 = 4$.
@step $2{,}400 \div 6 = 400$
@answer The driver goes about $400$ km each day.
:::

## Is the answer reasonable?

:::definition Reasonable
An answer is **reasonable** if it makes sense. It should be close to your estimate.
:::

:::example Checking a calculator answer
A warehouse has $52$ pallets with $38$ boxes on each. Jun's calculator says $19{,}760$ boxes. Is that reasonable?
@step Estimate: $52 \approx 50$ and $38 \approx 40$, so $50 \times 40 = 2{,}000$.
@step $19{,}760$ is about $10$ times bigger than $2{,}000$. Jun probably pressed an extra $0$.
@step The correct product is $52 \times 38 = 1{,}976$, which is close to $2{,}000$.
@answer No, $19{,}760$ is not reasonable. The answer should be about $2{,}000$.
:::

:::mistake Trusting the calculator without checking
A calculator does exactly what you type. One missed key or one extra $0$ gives a wrong answer. Always compare with an estimate.
:::

:::tip Count the zeros
When you multiply numbers like $50 \times 40$, multiply the first digits ($5 \times 4 = 20$). Then put back all the zeros. There are two zeros, so the answer is $2{,}000$.
:::

## Solving two-step problems

Many real problems need two steps. Use this plan:

1. **Read** the problem. What do you need to find?
2. **Choose** an operation for each step.
3. **Calculate.**
4. **Check** with an estimate.
5. **Answer** in a sentence with units.

:::example Boxes in a warehouse
A warehouse receives $22$ pallets with $48$ boxes on each pallet. Then $315$ boxes are shipped out. How many boxes are left?
@step Step 1: Equal groups, so **multiply**: $22 \times 48 = 1{,}056$ boxes.
@step Step 2: Boxes are taken away, so **subtract**: $1{,}056 - 315 = 741$.
@step Check: $20 \times 50 = 1{,}000$ and $1{,}000 - 300 = 700$. The answer $741$ is close to $700$.
@answer There are $741$ boxes left.
:::

:::mistake Stopping after one step
After Step 1, you know there are $1{,}056$ boxes. That is not the answer yet. Read the question again to see what it asks.
:::

## Problems with a remainder

Some problems end with a division that does not come out even. Think about what the remainder means.

:::example Buses for a field trip
Five classes go on a field trip. Each class has $28$ students. A bus holds $52$ students. How many buses are needed?
@step Step 1: Equal classes, so **multiply**: $5 \times 28 = 140$ students.
@step Step 2: Split into busloads, so **divide**: $140 \div 52 = 2$ remainder $36$.
@step The $36$ extra students still need a seat, so round **up** to $3$ buses.
@step Check: $5 \times 30 = 150$ and $150 \div 50 = 3$. That matches.
@answer They need $3$ buses.
:::

:::check m104-whole-problem 3
:::

:::summary
- To estimate, round each number so the maths is easy, then calculate in your head.
- An answer is reasonable if it is close to your estimate.
- For two-step problems: read, choose an operation for each step, calculate, check with an estimate, and answer in a sentence.
- With a remainder, decide whether to round up or round down.
:::
