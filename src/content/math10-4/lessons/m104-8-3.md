Logos, quilts, floor tiles and road signs often look balanced. Designers make patterns with symmetry, and by sliding, flipping and turning shapes. Game boards, floor plans and computer screens use the same moves.

## Lines of symmetry

:::definition Line of symmetry
A **line of symmetry** is a fold line. When you fold a shape along it, the two halves match exactly. A shape can have no lines of symmetry, one line, or many.
:::

A rectangle has 2 lines of symmetry: one up and down, and one across.

:::mistake The diagonal of a rectangle
A diagonal cuts a rectangle into two equal pieces, but it is **not** a line of symmetry. If you fold along it, the corners do not match.
:::

:::example The letter H on a sign
How many lines of symmetry does this letter H have?
@step Fold it left to right along a vertical line. The two halves match.
@step Fold it top to bottom along a horizontal line. The two halves match.
@step A slanted fold does not work.
@answer The letter H has $2$ lines of symmetry.
:::

## Symmetry in designs

For a design, a line of symmetry must work for **every** part: the outside shape and everything drawn inside it.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":7,"yMin":0,"yMax":7,"step":7},"hideTickLabels":true,"segments":[{"from":[1,1],"to":[6,1]},{"from":[6,1],"to":[6,6]},{"from":[6,6],"to":[1,6]},{"from":[1,6],"to":[1,1]},{"from":[3.5,1],"to":[6,3.5]},{"from":[6,3.5],"to":[3.5,6]},{"from":[3.5,6],"to":[1,3.5]},{"from":[1,3.5],"to":[3.5,1]}],"polylines":[],"points":[]}
:::

:::example A floor tile
A floor tile is a square with a diamond inside. How many lines of symmetry does the design have?
@step A square has $4$ lines of symmetry: vertical, horizontal and the two diagonals.
@step The diamond also matches along all $4$ of those lines.
@answer The tile has $4$ lines of symmetry.
:::

## Slides: translations

:::definition Translation and image
A **translation** is a slide. Every point moves the same distance in the same direction. The new position is called the **image**. We name the image of $P$ as $P'$ (say "P prime").
:::

On a grid, a point is written as $(x, y)$. The first number $x$ tells how far **across**. The second number $y$ tells how far **up**.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":10,"yMin":0,"yMax":10,"step":1},"segments":[],"polylines":[],"points":[{"x":2,"y":3,"color":"brand"},{"x":6,"y":5,"color":"accent"},{"x":1.507,"y":3.206,"label":"P","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":5.433,"y":5.206,"label":"P\u2032","color":"muted","open":true}]}
:::

:::example Moving a game piece
A game piece is at $P(2, 3)$. It slides $4$ units right and $2$ units up. Where is its image $P'$?
@step Right adds to $x$: $2 + 4 = 6$
@step Up adds to $y$: $3 + 2 = 5$
@answer The image is $P'(6, 5)$.
:::

:::mistake Across first, then up
Always write $x$ (across) first and $y$ (up) second. $(6, 5)$ and $(5, 6)$ are different points.
:::

## Flips and turns

:::definition Reflection and rotation
A **reflection** is a flip over a mirror line. Each image point is the same distance from the line as the original point, but on the other side. A **rotation** is a turn around a point.
:::

:::figure

:::

:::example Reflecting in a mirror line
Reflect $A(1, 2)$ in the dashed line at $x = 5$.
@step $A$ is $5 - 1 = 4$ units left of the line.
@step The image is $4$ units right of the line: $5 + 4 = 9$. The $y$ stays $2$.
@answer The image is $A'(9, 2)$.
:::

When the mirror line is an axis, use the signs. A reflection in the $x$-axis keeps $x$ and changes the sign of $y$: $(3, 2)$ becomes $(3, -2)$. A reflection in the $y$-axis changes the sign of $x$.

:::tip Flip or turn?
Trace $A \to B \to C$, then $A' \to B' \to C'$. If one goes clockwise and the other goes counterclockwise, the shape was **flipped**. If both go the same way, it was slid or turned.
:::

:::summary
- A line of symmetry is a fold line where both halves match.
- A translation slides a shape. Right/left changes $x$. Up/down changes $y$.
- A reflection flips a shape over a mirror line. The image is the same distance from the line, on the other side.
- A rotation turns a shape around a point.
:::
