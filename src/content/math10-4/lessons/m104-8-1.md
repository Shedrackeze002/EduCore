Carpenters, tile setters and sign makers work with angles and shapes every day. A cut that is a little off can ruin a cabinet or a floor. Knowing the names of angles and shapes helps you read a plan and follow instructions at work.

## Types of angles

:::definition Angle
An **angle** is made by two lines, called **arms**, that meet at a point. The point is the **vertex**. We measure angles in **degrees** (°). One full turn is $360^\circ$.
:::

We sort angles by size. The square corner of a sheet of paper is $90^\circ$. Compare every angle with it.

:::definition Acute, right, obtuse, straight and reflex
- An **acute** angle is less than $90^\circ$.
- A **right** angle is exactly $90^\circ$. It makes a square corner. A small square marks it.
- An **obtuse** angle is more than $90^\circ$ but less than $180^\circ$.
- A **straight** angle is exactly $180^\circ$. Its arms make a straight line.
- A **reflex** angle is more than $180^\circ$. It goes the long way around.
:::

:::example Opening a laptop
Hana opens her laptop so the screen makes an angle of $110^\circ$ with the keyboard. What type of angle is this?
@step $110^\circ$ is more than $90^\circ$, so it is wider than a square corner.
@step $110^\circ$ is less than $180^\circ$, so it is not a straight line.
@answer It is an **obtuse** angle.
:::

:::mistake Almost square is not square
An angle is right only when it is **exactly** $90^\circ$. An angle of $88^\circ$ is acute, and $92^\circ$ is obtuse.
:::

## Angles that add up

When a line meets a straight line, it makes two angles. Together they make a straight angle, so they **add to** $180^\circ$.

:::example A ladder on flat ground
A ladder makes an angle of $70^\circ$ with the ground. Find the angle on the other side of the ladder, marked **?**.
@step The two angles make a straight line, so they add to $180^\circ$.

@answer The angle is $110^\circ$. It is an obtuse angle.

## Naming triangles

:::definition Polygon
A **polygon** is a closed shape with straight sides. A triangle has 3 sides. A **quadrilateral** has 4 sides.
:::

We can name a triangle by its **sides**: **equilateral** (3 equal sides), **isosceles** (2 equal sides) or **scalene** (no equal sides).

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":8,"yMin":0,"yMax":8,"step":8},"hideTickLabels":true,"segments":[],"polylines":[{"points":[[1.879,1],[6.121,1],[4,7],[1.879,1]],"color":"brand"}],"points":[{"x":3.568,"y":0.381,"label":"4 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":5.428,"y":3.993,"label":"6 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":1.997,"y":3.891,"label":"6 m","color":"muted","open":true}]}
:::

:::example A triangular roof truss
A roof truss has sides of $6\ \text{m}$, $6\ \text{m}$ and $4\ \text{m}$. Name it by its sides.
@step Two sides are $6\ \text{m}$. They are equal. The third side is different.
@answer It is an **isosceles** triangle.
:::

We can also name a triangle by its **angles**: **acute** (all angles less than $90^\circ$), **right** (one $90^\circ$ angle) or **obtuse** (one angle more than $90^\circ$). The three angles of a triangle always add to $180^\circ$.

:::

:::example A corner shelf
Two angles of a corner shelf are $90^\circ$ and $35^\circ$. Find the third angle and name the triangle by its angles.
@step $90 + 35 = 125$
@step $180 - 125 = 55$, so the third angle is $55^\circ$.
@answer One angle is $90^\circ$, so it is a **right** triangle.
:::

:::mistake Sides or angles?
Read the question. "By its sides" uses equilateral, isosceles or scalene. "By its angles" uses acute, right or obtuse.
:::

## Naming quadrilaterals

:::definition Parallel
Two lines are **parallel** if they never meet, like the two rails of a train track. They stay the same distance apart.
:::

A piece of fabric has the shape above. It has no square corners. What is its most exact name?
@step Opposite sides are equal ($60\ \text{cm}$ and $36\ \text{cm}$) and parallel.
@step Not all 4 sides are equal, so it is not a rhombus. There are no right angles, so it is not a rectangle.

A square is also a rectangle and a rhombus. But "square" tells the most, so it is the **most exact** name.

:::summary
- Acute: less than $90^\circ$. Right: exactly $90^\circ$. Obtuse: between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$. Straight: $180^\circ$. Reflex: more than $180^\circ$.
- Angles that make a straight line add to $180^\circ$. Angles that make a square corner add to $90^\circ$.
- Triangles by sides: equilateral, isosceles, scalene. By angles: acute, right, obtuse. The angles add to $180^\circ$.
- Quadrilaterals: square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram, trapezoid. Use the most exact name.
:::
