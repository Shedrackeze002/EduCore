Money, tape measures and gas pumps all use decimals. At work you might run a cash register, cut pipe to length or split a lunch bill. A calculator helps, but you still need to know which numbers to put in and whether the answer makes sense.

## Decimals and place value

:::definition Decimal
A **decimal** is a number with a decimal point, like $4.75$. The digits after the point are parts of one whole.
- The first place after the point is **tenths**. $0.1$ is one tenth, or $\frac{1}{10}$.
- The second place is **hundredths**. $0.01$ is one hundredth, or $\frac{1}{100}$.
:::

Money uses hundredths. One cent is one hundredth of a dollar. So \$4.75 means 4 dollars and 75 cents.

:::figure

:::

On this number line, each small step is one tenth. The point $0.75$ sits halfway between $0.7$ and $0.8$.

:::example Reading a gas price
A sign says gas costs \$1.459 per litre. What does each digit mean?
@step The $1$ is one whole dollar.
@step The $4$ is 4 tenths of a dollar (40 cents). The $5$ is 5 hundredths (5 cents).
@step The $9$ is 9 thousandths of a dollar. That is less than one cent.
@answer Gas costs a little less than \$1.46 per litre.
:::

:::tip Money has two decimal places
Write money with two decimal places: \$6.50, not \$6.5. To round money to the nearest cent, look at the third decimal place. If it is 5 or more, round up. If it is 4 or less, round down.
:::

## Adding and subtracting decimals

Line up the decimal points. Fill any empty places with zeros. Then add or subtract like whole numbers, and bring the decimal point straight down.

:::example Total length of pipe
A plumber needs pieces of pipe that are $2.4\ \text{m}$, $1.35\ \text{m}$ and $0.8\ \text{m}$ long. What is the total length?
@step Line up the points. Give each number two decimal places: $2.40 + 1.35 + 0.80$
@step $2.40 + 1.35 + 0.80 = 4.55$
@answer The total length is $4.55\ \text{m}$.
:::

:::example Cutting a board
Jordan has a board that is $3.2\ \text{m}$ long. Jordan cuts off $1.45\ \text{m}$. How much is left?
@step Write $3.2$ as $3.20$ so both numbers have two decimal places.
@step $3.20 - 1.45 = 1.75$
@answer $1.75\ \text{m}$ of the board is left.
:::

:::mistake Lining up the last digits
If you line up the last digits of $2.4 + 1.35$, you get $1.59$. That is less than $2.4$ alone, so it cannot be right. Line up the **decimal points**: $2.40 + 1.35 = 3.75$.
:::

## Multiplying and dividing decimals

Multiply when you have several of the same amount, like litres of gas at a price per litre. Divide when you share an amount equally.

:::example Filling up the truck
Gas costs \$1.459 per litre. Priya pumps $42.6\ \text{L}$. What is the cost, to the nearest cent?
@step Cost = litres × price per litre
@step $42.6 \times 1.459 = 62.1534$
@step Round to two decimal places. The third decimal digit is $3$, so round down.
@answer The gas costs \$62.15.
:::

:::tip Estimate to check
$42.6\ \text{L}$ at about \$1.50 per litre is about $43 \times 1.5 \approx 64$ dollars. The answer \$62.15 is close, so it makes sense.
:::

:::example Splitting a bill
Three co-workers split a lunch bill of \$58.70 equally. How much does each person pay?
@step Sharing equally means divide: $58.70 \div 3 = 19.5666\ldots$
@step Round to the nearest cent. The third decimal digit is $6$, so round up.
@answer Each person pays about \$19.57.
:::

:::mistake Leaving extra decimal places
\$62.1534 is not an amount you can pay. Always round a money answer to two decimal places.
:::

## Totals and making change

:::definition Change
**Change** is the money you get back when you pay more than the total.
$$\text{change} = \text{money paid} - \text{total}$$
:::

:::figure

:::

:::example Change from a \$20 bill
Mei buys the items on the receipt above and pays with a \$20 bill. What is the total, and how much change should Mei get?
@step Total: $5.49 + 2.15 + 2.79 = 10.43$
@step Change: $20.00 - 10.43 = 9.57$
@answer The total is \$10.43, and the change is \$9.57.
:::

:::tip Count up to make change
Cashiers often count up from the total. From \$10.43, add 7 cents to get \$10.50. Add 50 cents to get \$11.00. Add \$9.00 to get \$20.00. The change is $0.07 + 0.50 + 9.00 = 9.57$ dollars.
:::

Canada no longer makes pennies. When you pay with **cash**, the total is rounded to the nearest 5 cents. Card payments are not rounded.

:::example Paying with cash
Mei pays the \$10.43 bill with cash. How much does she pay, and what is her change from \$20?
@step The total ends in $3$. A $3$ rounds up to $5$, so the cash total is \$10.45.
@step Change: $20.00 - 10.45 = 9.55$
@answer Mei pays \$10.45 and gets \$9.55 back.
:::

:::mistake Rounding each price
Add all the prices first. Then round the total. Do not round each price on its own.
:::

:::summary
- Tenths are the first place after the decimal point. Hundredths are the second. One cent is one hundredth of a dollar.
- To add or subtract decimals, line up the decimal points and fill empty places with zeros.
- Multiply for several of the same amount. Divide to share equally.
- Round money to the nearest cent: two decimal places.
- Change = money paid − total. A cash total is rounded to the nearest 5 cents.
:::
