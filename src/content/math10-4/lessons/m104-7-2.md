How much carpet covers a bedroom floor? How much sod covers a yard? How much paint covers a wall? To answer, you need the **area**: the amount of surface inside a shape.

## Area and square units

:::definition Area
**Area** is the amount of space inside a flat shape. It is measured in **square units**.
:::

:::definition Square unit
A **square unit** is a square that is $1$ unit long on each side. A square metre ($\text{m}^2$) is a square $1\ \text{m}$ by $1\ \text{m}$. A square centimetre ($\text{cm}^2$) is about the size of a fingernail.
:::

The rectangle above covers $3$ rows of $4$ squares. That is $3 \times 4 = 12$ squares, so its area is $12\ \text{m}^2$. Multiplying is a fast way to count the squares.

$$\text{Area of a rectangle} = \text{length} \times \text{width} \qquad A = l \times w$$

:::example Carpet for a bedroom
A bedroom floor is $4\ \text{m}$ long and $3.5\ \text{m}$ wide. How much carpet covers the floor?
@step $A = l \times w$
@step $A = 4 \times 3.5 = 14$
@answer The floor needs $14\ \text{m}^2$ of carpet.
:::

:::mistake Area or perimeter?
Perimeter goes **around** the edge (fencing, trim). Area covers the **inside** (carpet, sod, paint). For this floor, $4 + 3.5 + 4 + 3.5 = 15\ \text{m}$ is the perimeter, not the area.
:::

## Area of a triangle

A triangle is half of a rectangle. So its area is half of base times height.

:::definition Base and height
The **base** is the side the triangle sits on. The **height** is the distance straight up from the base to the top corner. The height makes a square corner (a right angle) with the base.
:::

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times b \times h$$

:::example Mulch for a garden bed
A triangular garden bed has a base of $6\ \text{m}$ and a height of $4\ \text{m}$. What is its area?
@step $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 4$
@step $6 \times 4 = 24$, and half of $24$ is $12$.
@answer The garden bed has an area of $12\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Using a slanted side
The height must go **straight up** from the base, like the dashed line. Do not use a slanted side as the height.
:::

## Area of a circle

$$A = \pi \times r \times r \qquad \text{(also written } A = \pi r^2\text{)}$$

Use the **radius**. If you are given the diameter, halve it first.

:::example A round rug
A round rug has a radius of $2\ \text{m}$. What is its area? Round to the nearest tenth.
@step $A = \pi \times 2 \times 2$
@step $A \approx 12.566$
@answer The rug covers about $12.6\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Using the diameter
For a rug $4\ \text{m}$ across, $\pi \times 4 \times 4 \approx 50.3$ is far too big. The radius is $4 \div 2 = 2\ \text{m}$.
:::

## How much material and what does it cost?

Stores sell flooring, sod and fabric **by the square metre**. Find the area first, then multiply by the price for one square metre.

:::figure

:::

:::example Sod for a backyard
Sod costs \$4.25 per square metre. A backyard is $8\ \text{m}$ by $5.5\ \text{m}$. What does the sod cost?
@step Area: $8 \times 5.5 = 44\ \text{m}^2$
@step Cost: $44 \times 4.25 = 187$
@answer The sod costs \$187.00.
:::

Paint is sold in cans. Each can covers a certain area. You cannot buy part of a can, so always round **up**.

:::example Paint for a wall
A wall is $4.5\ \text{m}$ long and $2.4\ \text{m}$ high. A door in the wall is $0.9\ \text{m}$ by $2\ \text{m}$ and will not be painted. One can of paint covers $4\ \text{m}^2$. How many cans are needed?
@step Wall: $4.5 \times 2.4 = 10.8\ \text{m}^2$. Door: $0.9 \times 2 = 1.8\ \text{m}^2$.
@step Area to paint: $10.8 - 1.8 = 9\ \text{m}^2$.
@step Cans: $9 \div 4 = 2.25$. Round up.
@answer Buy $3$ cans of paint.
:::

:::check m104-area 3
:::

:::summary
- Area is the space inside a shape, measured in square units like $\text{m}^2$ or $\text{cm}^2$.
- Rectangle: $A = l \times w$. Triangle: $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times b \times h$. Circle: $A = \pi \times r \times r$.
- The height of a triangle goes straight up from the base.
- To find a cost, multiply the area by the price per square metre. Round cans of paint **up**.
:::
