How many litres of water fill a fish tank? How much soil fills a garden bed? How many boxes fit in a shipping carton? These questions are about **volume**: the space inside a 3-D object.

## Volume of a box

:::definition Rectangular prism
A **rectangular prism** is a box shape. It has $6$ flat faces, and every face is a rectangle. A shoe box and a fish tank are rectangular prisms.
:::

:::definition Volume and cubic units
**Volume** is the amount of space inside a 3-D object. It is measured in **cubic units**. A cubic centimetre ($\text{cm}^3$) is a little cube $1\ \text{cm}$ long, $1\ \text{cm}$ wide and $1\ \text{cm}$ high, about the size of a sugar cube.
:::

To find the volume of a box, multiply its three measurements.

$$V = l \times w \times h \qquad \text{(length} \times \text{width} \times \text{height)}$$

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"rectPrism","labels":{"l":"40 cm","w":"30 cm","h":"20 cm"}}
:::

:::example A shipping box
A shipping box is $40\ \text{cm}$ long, $30\ \text{cm}$ wide and $20\ \text{cm}$ high. What is its volume?
@step $V = l \times w \times h$
@step $40 \times 30 = 1200$
@step $1200 \times 20 = 24\,000$
@answer The volume is $24\,000\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

:::mistake Adding instead of multiplying
$40 + 30 + 20 = 90$ is not the volume. Volume counts cubes in every row, every layer and every stack, so you **multiply**.
:::

## Volume in cubic metres

Big spaces, like a garden bed or the box of a truck, are measured in **cubic metres** ($\text{m}^3$). Make sure all three measurements are in metres before you multiply.

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"rectPrism","labels":{"l":"2.4 m","w":"1.2 m","h":"0.4 m"}}
:::

:::example Soil for a raised garden bed
A raised garden bed is $2.4\ \text{m}$ long, $1.2\ \text{m}$ wide and $0.4\ \text{m}$ deep. How much soil fills it?
@step $V = 2.4 \times 1.2 \times 0.4$
@step $V = 1.152$
@answer The bed holds $1.152\ \text{m}^3$ of soil.
:::

:::mistake Mixing metres and centimetres
If the depth is given as $40\ \text{cm}$, change it to $0.4\ \text{m}$ first. Using $2.4 \times 1.2 \times 40$ gives an answer $100$ times too big.
:::

## Volume and capacity

:::definition Capacity
**Capacity** is how much a container can hold. It is measured in litres (L) and millilitres (mL).
:::

Volume and capacity are linked:

$$1000\ \text{cm}^3 \text{ holds } 1\ \text{L} \qquad\qquad 1\ \text{cm}^3 \text{ holds } 1\ \text{mL}$$

So to change cubic centimetres to litres, **divide by $1000$**.

:::example A cooler
A cooler is $50\ \text{cm}$ long, $30\ \text{cm}$ wide and $30\ \text{cm}$ high inside. How many litres does it hold?
@step $V = 50 \times 30 \times 30 = 45\,000\ \text{cm}^3$
@step $45\,000 \div 1000 = 45$
@answer The cooler holds $45\ \text{L}$.
:::

## Filling and packing

Real jobs often take two steps. Draw a sketch and write down what you know.

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"rectPrism","labels":{"l":"60 cm","w":"30 cm","h":"40 cm"}}
:::

:::example Filling a fish tank
A fish tank at a pet store is $60\ \text{cm}$ long, $30\ \text{cm}$ wide and $40\ \text{cm}$ high. It is filled with water up to $5\ \text{cm}$ below the top. How many litres of water are in the tank?
@step The water is $40 - 5 = 35\ \text{cm}$ deep.
@step Volume of water: $60 \times 30 \times 35 = 63\,000\ \text{cm}^3$
@step Litres: $63\,000 \div 1000 = 63$
@answer The tank holds $63\ \text{L}$ of water.
:::

:::example Packing a carton
A carton is $60\ \text{cm}$ by $40\ \text{cm}$ by $30\ \text{cm}$. Small boxes are $20\ \text{cm}$ by $10\ \text{cm}$ by $15\ \text{cm}$, all facing the same way. How many fit?
@step Along the length: $60 \div 20 = 3$. Along the width: $40 \div 10 = 4$. Up the height: $30 \div 15 = 2$.
@step $3 \times 4 \times 2 = 24$
@answer $24$ small boxes fit in the carton.
:::

:::tip Check with volume
Carton: $60 \times 40 \times 30 = 72\,000\ \text{cm}^3$. Small box: $20 \times 10 \times 15 = 3000\ \text{cm}^3$. And $72\,000 \div 3000 = 24$ ✓
:::

:::check m104-volume 3
:::

:::summary
- Volume is the space inside a 3-D object, measured in cubic units like $\text{cm}^3$ or $\text{m}^3$.
- For a box (rectangular prism), $V = l \times w \times h$. Use the same unit for all three.
- $1000\ \text{cm}^3$ holds $1\ \text{L}$. Divide cubic centimetres by $1000$ to get litres.
- For packing, find how many fit along each edge, then multiply.
:::
