What power of $10$ gives $1000$? The answer, $3$, is called a logarithm. Logarithms are exponents, and they let you work backwards from a power to the exponent that made it.

## What is a logarithm?

:::definition Logarithm
For $b > 0$, $b \ne 1$ and $x > 0$, $\log_b x = y$ means $b^y = x$. The logarithm is the **exponent** you put on the base $b$ to get $x$. When no base is written, the base is $10$: $\log x = \log_{10} x$ is the **common logarithm**.
:::

Every exponential equation has a logarithmic form, and every logarithmic equation has an exponential form.

| Exponential form | Logarithmic form |
|---|---|
| $2^5 = 32$ | $\log_2 32 = 5$ |
| $10^{-2} = 0.01$ | $\log 0.01 = -2$ |
| $9^{1/2} = 3$ | $\log_9 3 = \frac{1}{2}$ |

:::example Evaluate logarithms
Evaluate $\log_2 32$, $\log 0.01$ and $\log_9 3$.
@step $2$ to what power is $32$? $2^5 = 32$, so $\log_2 32 = 5$.
@step $10$ to what power is $0.01$? $10^{-2} = \frac{1}{100} = 0.01$, so $\log 0.01 = -2$.
@step $9$ to what power is $3$? $\sqrt{9} = 9^{1/2} = 3$, so $\log_9 3 = \frac{1}{2}$.
@answer $5$, $-2$ and $\frac{1}{2}$
:::

:::mistake Mixing up the base and the argument
In $\log_5 125 = 3$, the base is $5$ and the answer is the exponent, so the exponential form is $5^3 = 125$, not $3^5 = 125$. The base of the logarithm is always the base of the power.
:::

## Estimating, change of base and solving

When the argument is not a power of the base, estimate first. Since $3^3 = 27$ and $3^4 = 81$, and $27 < 50 < 81$, the value of $\log_3 50$ is between $3$ and $4$. To find it, use the **change of base formula** with the LOG key:
$$\log_b x = \frac{\log x}{\log b}$$

:::example Change of base
Evaluate $\log_3 50$ to the nearest hundredth.
@step $\log_3 50 = \dfrac{\log 50}{\log 3} = \dfrac{1.69897\ldots}{0.47712\ldots} = 3.5609\ldots$
@step The answer is between $3$ and $4$, as estimated.
@answer $\log_3 50 \approx 3.56$
:::

To solve a simple logarithmic equation, rewrite it in exponential form.

:::example Solve for the unknown
Solve $\log_x 64 = 3$ and $\log_5 x = -2$.
@step $\log_x 64 = 3$ means $x^3 = 64$, so $x = \sqrt[3]{64} = 4$.
@step $\log_5 x = -2$ means $x = 5^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{25}$.
@answer $x = 4$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{25}$
:::

## The laws of logarithms

Because logarithms are exponents, the exponent laws become logarithm laws. For $M > 0$, $N > 0$, $b > 0$ and $b \ne 1$:

:::definition Laws of logarithms
**Product law:** $\log_b (MN) = \log_b M + \log_b N$

**Quotient law:** $\log_b \dfrac{M}{N} = \log_b M - \log_b N$

**Power law:** $\log_b M^n = n\log_b M$
:::

Multiplying powers adds their exponents, so the logarithm of a product is a sum. Dividing subtracts exponents, and a power of a power multiplies them.

:::example Combine, then evaluate
Evaluate $\log_6 4 + \log_6 9$, $\log_2 48 - \log_2 3$ and $3\log_5 10 - \log_5 8$.
@step Product law: $\log_6 4 + \log_6 9 = \log_6 36 = 2$, since $6^2 = 36$.
@step Quotient law: $\log_2 48 - \log_2 3 = \log_2 16 = 4$, since $2^4 = 16$.
@step Power law, then quotient law: $3\log_5 10 = \log_5 10^3 = \log_5 1000$, and $\log_5 1000 - \log_5 8 = \log_5 125 = 3$.
@answer $2$, $4$ and $3$
:::

:::mistake Laws that do not exist
$\log(M + N)$ is **not** $\log M + \log N$, and $\dfrac{\log M}{\log N}$ is **not** $\log \dfrac{M}{N}$. Test with numbers: $\log 2 + \log 8 = \log 16 \approx 1.204$, but $\log 10 = 1$.
:::

## Expanding, combining and solving

The laws work in both directions: to expand one logarithm into several, or to combine several into one.

:::example Write as a single logarithm
Write $2\log x + \log y - 3\log z$ as a single logarithm.
@step Power law: $\log x^2 + \log y - \log z^3$
@step Product law: $\log x^2 y - \log z^3$
@answer $\log \dfrac{x^2 y}{z^3}$
:::

:::example Use known values
Given $\log 2 \approx 0.3010$ and $\log 3 \approx 0.4771$, find $\log 18$ without the LOG key.
@step $18 = 2 \times 3^2$, so $\log 18 = \log 2 + 2\log 3$.
@step $\log 18 \approx 0.3010 + 2(0.4771) = 1.2552$
@answer $\log 18 \approx 1.2552$
:::

:::example Solve with a law
Solve $\log_3 x - \log_3 4 = 2$.
@step Quotient law: $\log_3 \dfrac{x}{4} = 2$
@step Exponential form: $\dfrac{x}{4} = 3^2 = 9$, so $x = 36$.
@step Check: $x = 36$ is positive, so $\log_3 x$ is defined.
@answer $x = 36$
:::

:::summary
- $\log_b x = y$ means $b^y = x$: a logarithm is an exponent. $\log x$ means $\log_{10} x$.
- Estimate between powers of the base, then use $\log_b x = \dfrac{\log x}{\log b}$ for a decimal value.
- Product law $\log_b MN = \log_b M + \log_b N$; quotient law $\log_b \frac{M}{N} = \log_b M - \log_b N$; power law $\log_b M^n = n\log_b M$.
- There is no law for $\log(M + N)$ or for $\dfrac{\log M}{\log N}$.
- To solve, combine into one logarithm, rewrite in exponential form, and check the argument is positive.
:::
