Savings accounts, town populations, used cars and medications in the bloodstream all change by a percent at a time, and earthquakes, sound and acidity are measured on scales that jump by factors of 10. Exponential and logarithmic functions model all of them. In every problem, the formula and the rate are given: your job is to use them and interpret the answer.

## Compound interest

:::definition Compound interest
$A = P(1 + i)^n$, where $P$ is the principal (the amount invested), $i$ is the interest rate per compounding period as a decimal, $n$ is the number of compounding periods and $A$ is the final amount. The interest earned is $A - P$.
:::

If the annual rate is compounded $k$ times a year, divide the rate by $k$ and multiply the number of years by $k$.

:::example Monthly compounding
Kofi invests \$5000 at 4.2% per year, compounded monthly, for 6 years. Find the final amount and the interest earned.
@step $i = \dfrac{0.042}{12} = 0.0035$ per month and $n = 12 \times 6 = 72$ months.
@step $A = 5000(1.0035)^{72} = 5000(1.28603\ldots) = 6430.150\ldots$
@step Interest: $6430.15 - 5000 = 1430.15$
@answer The investment is worth \$6430.15, so it earned \$1430.15 in interest.
:::

To find a **time**, the unknown is in the exponent, so use logarithms.

:::example How long to double?
How many years does it take money to double at 5% per year, compounded annually?
@step Doubling means $A = 2P$: $2P = P(1.05)^n$, so $1.05^n = 2$.
@step $n\log 1.05 = \log 2$, so $n = \dfrac{\log 2}{\log 1.05} = 14.2067\ldots$
@answer About 14.2 years. With interest paid once a year, the money has doubled after the 15th payment.
:::

## Growth, depreciation and half-life

The same idea gives three more models. Each multiplies by the same factor in each time step.

| Situation | Model |
|---|---|
| Growth at rate $r$ | $P = P_0(1 + r)^t$ |
| Depreciation at rate $r$ | $V = V_0(1 - r)^t$ |
| Half-life $h$ | $A = A_0\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/h}$ |

In the half-life model, $\dfrac{t}{h}$ counts how many half-lives have passed.

:::example A medical tracer
A tracer has a half-life of 6 hours, and a patient is given 80 mg. How much remains after 15 hours, and when will 5 mg remain?
@step After 15 hours: $\dfrac{t}{h} = \dfrac{15}{6} = 2.5$ half-lives, so $A = 80\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2.5} = 14.142\ldots$ mg.
@step For 5 mg: $5 = 80\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/6}$, so $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/6} = \dfrac{5}{80} = \dfrac{1}{16} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^4$.
@step $\dfrac{t}{6} = 4$, so $t = 24$.
@answer About 14.1 mg remains after 15 hours, and 5 mg remains after 24 hours.
:::

:::mistake Using t instead of t/h
After 15 hours with a half-life of 6 hours, the exponent is $\frac{15}{6}$, not $15$. Using $15$ would mean 15 half-lives have passed, and almost nothing would be left.
:::

## Using a given regression equation

With technology, you can fit an exponential regression $y = a(b)^x$ to data. In this course the equation is given to you; your job is to use it and judge the answer.

:::example Club members
An online gaming club tracked its members from 2018 to 2024. Exponential regression gives $y = 320(1.18)^x$, where $x$ is the number of years since 2018. Estimate the members in 2023 and in 2026.
@step 2023 is $x = 5$: $y = 320(1.18)^5 = 732.08\ldots$, about 732 members. This is **interpolation**, inside the data.
@step 2026 is $x = 8$: $y = 320(1.18)^8 = 1202.8\ldots$, about 1203 members. This is **extrapolation**, outside the data.
@answer About 732 members in 2023 and 1203 in 2026. The 2026 value is less reliable, because 18% growth a year may not continue.
:::

## Logarithmic scales

When a quantity covers a huge range, scientists use a **logarithmic scale**: each step of 1 on the scale means multiplying by 10.

:::definition Three logarithmic scales
**pH:** $\text{pH} = -\log[\text{H}^+]$, where $[\text{H}^+]$ is the hydrogen ion concentration in mol/L. A lower pH means more acidic.

**Decibels:** $\beta = 10\log \dfrac{I}{I_0}$, where $I_0$ is the quietest sound a person can hear.

**Richter scale:** an increase of $1$ in magnitude means $10$ times the intensity, so $\dfrac{I_1}{I_2} = 10^{M_1 - M_2}$.
:::

:::example Three scales
(a) Find the pH when $[\text{H}^+] = 3.2 \times 10^{-4}$ mol/L. (b) How many times as intense is a magnitude 6.3 earthquake as a magnitude 4.8 earthquake? (c) One speaker produces 70 dB. What level do two identical speakers produce?
@step (a) $\text{pH} = -\log(3.2 \times 10^{-4}) = 3.4948\ldots \approx 3.49$
@step (b) $\dfrac{I_1}{I_2} = 10^{6.3 - 4.8} = 10^{1.5} = 31.62\ldots$, about 32 times.
@step (c) Twice the intensity: $10\log \dfrac{2I}{I_0} = 10\log 2 + 10\log \dfrac{I}{I_0} = 3.0103\ldots + 70 \approx 73.0$ dB.
@answer pH about 3.49; about 32 times as intense; about 73.0 dB.
:::

:::mistake A difference is not a ratio
A magnitude 6 earthquake is not twice as intense as a magnitude 3: it is $10^{6 - 3} = 1000$ times as intense. On a logarithmic scale, **differences** in the scale become **ratios** in the quantity.
:::

:::check m302-log-model 1
:::

Data that rises quickly and then levels off can be modelled by a **logarithmic regression** $y = a + b\ln x$, where $\ln$ is the natural logarithm key on your calculator. For example, if the number of pastries per hour a new baker can decorate after $x$ days is $y = 12.5 + 6.2\ln x$, then after 10 days $y = 12.5 + 6.2(2.3025\ldots) \approx 26.8$ pastries per hour.

:::summary
- Compound interest: $A = P(1 + i)^n$, with $i$ the rate per period and $n$ the number of periods; interest is $A - P$.
- Growth $P_0(1 + r)^t$, depreciation $V_0(1 - r)^t$ and half-life $A_0\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/h}$.
- To find a time, isolate the power, take $\log$ of both sides and divide.
- A given regression equation can interpolate (inside the data, more reliable) or extrapolate (outside, less reliable).
- On a logarithmic scale (pH, decibels, Richter), a difference on the scale is a ratio in the quantity.
:::
