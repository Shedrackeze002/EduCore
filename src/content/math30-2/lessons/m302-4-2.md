Rational expressions follow the same rules as fractions: multiply straight across, divide by flipping, and add or subtract over a common denominator. The algebra is easier if you factor first and keep track of the non-permissible values as you go.

## Multiplying rational expressions

To multiply, factor every numerator and denominator, state the non-permissible values, divide out factors that appear in any numerator and any denominator, then multiply what is left.

:::example A product
Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 9}{4x} \times \dfrac{8x^2}{x + 3}$.
@step Factor: $\dfrac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{4x} \times \dfrac{8x^2}{x + 3}$.
@step Non-permissible values from both denominators: $4x = 0$ and $x + 3 = 0$, so $x \ne -3,\ 0$.
@step Divide out $(x + 3)$, and divide $8x^2$ by $4x$ to get $2x$.
@answer $2x(x - 3)$, $x \ne -3,\ 0$
:::

:::tip Factored or expanded?
$2x(x - 3)$ and $2x^2 - 6x$ are the same answer. The factored form makes it easy to see whether anything else divides out.
:::

## Dividing rational expressions

Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal: $\dfrac{a}{b} \div \dfrac{c}{d} = \dfrac{a}{b} \times \dfrac{d}{c}$.

:::example A quotient
Simplify $\dfrac{2x - 6}{x^2} \div \dfrac{x^2 - 9}{3x}$.
@step Factor and flip the divisor: $\dfrac{2(x - 3)}{x^2} \times \dfrac{3x}{(x - 3)(x + 3)}$.
@step Non-permissible values: $x^2 = 0$ and $3x = 0$ give $x \ne 0$; the divisor's numerator $x^2 - 9$ cannot be $0$ either, so $x \ne -3,\ 3$.
@step Divide out $(x - 3)$ and one factor of $x$: $\dfrac{2 \cdot 3}{x(x + 3)}$.
@answer $\dfrac{6}{x(x + 3)}$, $x \ne -3,\ 0,\ 3$
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the divisor's numerator
After you flip the divisor, its numerator becomes a denominator. In the example, $x = 3$ makes the divisor $0$, and you cannot divide by $0$. Include the zeros of the divisor's numerator in the non-permissible values.
:::

## Adding and subtracting with monomial denominators

To add or subtract, rewrite each expression over the **lowest common denominator** (LCD): the least common multiple of the coefficients times each variable to its highest power.

:::example Different powers of x
Simplify $\dfrac{3}{4x} + \dfrac{5}{6x^2}$.
@step The LCD of $4x$ and $6x^2$ is $12x^2$.
@step $\dfrac{3}{4x} \cdot \dfrac{3x}{3x} + \dfrac{5}{6x^2} \cdot \dfrac{2}{2} = \dfrac{9x}{12x^2} + \dfrac{10}{12x^2}$
@answer $\dfrac{9x + 10}{12x^2}$, $x \ne 0$
:::

:::example Subtracting a binomial numerator
Simplify $\dfrac{x + 2}{3x} - \dfrac{x - 1}{6x}$.
@step The LCD is $6x$: $\dfrac{2(x + 2) - (x - 1)}{6x}$.
@step Expand carefully: $2x + 4 - x + 1 = x + 5$.
@answer $\dfrac{x + 5}{6x}$, $x \ne 0$
:::

:::mistake Subtracting only the first term
$2(x + 2) - (x - 1)$ is $2x + 4 - x + 1$, not $2x + 4 - x - 1$. Put brackets around a numerator you subtract so the minus sign reaches every term.
:::

## Binomial denominators

When the denominators are different binomials with no common factor, the LCD is their product. Factor any difference of squares first: the LCD uses each different factor once.

:::example Two binomials
Simplify $\dfrac{2}{x + 3} - \dfrac{1}{x - 2}$.
@step LCD: $(x + 3)(x - 2)$, so $x \ne -3,\ 2$.
@step $\dfrac{2(x - 2) - (x + 3)}{(x + 3)(x - 2)}$
@step Numerator: $2x - 4 - x - 3 = x - 7$.
@answer $\dfrac{x - 7}{(x + 3)(x - 2)}$, $x \ne -3,\ 2$
:::

:::example A shared factor
Simplify $\dfrac{4}{x^2 - 9} + \dfrac{1}{x + 3}$.
@step Factor: $x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3)$. The LCD is $(x - 3)(x + 3)$, so $x \ne -3,\ 3$.
@step The second fraction is missing $(x - 3)$: $\dfrac{4 + (x - 3)}{(x - 3)(x + 3)}$.
@answer $\dfrac{x + 1}{(x - 3)(x + 3)}$, $x \ne -3,\ 3$
:::

:::check m302-rational-operations 3
:::

:::summary
- Multiply: factor, state restrictions, divide out common factors, multiply what is left.
- Divide: multiply by the reciprocal, and add the zeros of the divisor's numerator to the restrictions.
- Add or subtract: build the LCD from every different factor, multiply each numerator by what its denominator is missing, and bracket any numerator you subtract.
- Check the final numerator for a factor it shares with the denominator.
:::
