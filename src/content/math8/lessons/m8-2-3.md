Prices go up and down, teams score more goals one season than the last, and stores take a discount and then add sales tax. Percents help you describe these changes and work out what you really pay.

## Percent increase and decrease

:::definition Percent increase and percent decrease
A **percent increase** tells how much an amount grew, compared with the original amount. A **percent decrease** tells how much it shrank, compared with the original amount.
$$\text{percent change} = \dfrac{\text{amount of change}}{\text{original amount}} \times 100\%$$
:::

:::example A whole-number percent
The chess club had $40$ members last year and has $50$ members this year. What is the percent increase?
@step Amount of change: $50 - 40 = 10$
@step Divide by the original amount: $\frac{10}{40} = 0.25$
@answer $0.25 \times 100\% = 25\%$, so the percent increase is $25\%$.
:::

:::example Rounding to a tenth of a percent
A school website had $365$ visits in September and $412$ visits in October. Find the percent increase to the nearest tenth of a percent.
@step Amount of change: $412 - 365 = 47$
@step $\frac{47}{365} \approx 0.128767$
@step $0.128767 \times 100\% \approx 12.88\%$
@answer The percent increase is about $12.9\%$.
:::

:::mistake Divide by the original amount
Always divide by the amount you started with, not the new amount. From $40$ to $50$ is a $25\%$ increase, because $\frac{10}{40} = 25\%$. It is not $\frac{10}{50} = 20\%$.
:::

## A discount, then sales tax

When an item is on sale, take the discount off first. Then the sales tax is a percent of the **sale price**.

:::example A jacket on sale
A jacket has a regular price of \$80. It is on sale for $25\%$ off, and a $5\%$ sales tax is added to the sale price. What is the total cost?
@step Discount: $0.25 \times 80 = 20$, so the sale price is $80 - 20 = 60$.
@step Tax: $0.05 \times 60 = 3$
@step Total: $60 + 3 = 63$
@answer The total cost is \$63.00.
:::

:::tip Round money to the nearest cent
When a money amount has more than two decimal places, round it to the nearest cent at each step, the way a cash register does.
:::

## Two discounts in a row

A store might take one discount and then another discount off the sale price. The second percent is taken from a **smaller** amount, so the two percents do not simply add.

:::example 20% off, then 10% off
A game costs \$50. The store takes $20\%$ off, then another $10\%$ off the sale price. What is the final price? What single discount is that?
@step After $20\%$ off: $50 - 0.20 \times 50 = 50 - 10 = 40$
@step After another $10\%$ off: $40 - 0.10 \times 40 = 40 - 4 = 36$
@step Total discount: $50 - 36 = 14$, and $\frac{14}{50} = 0.28 = 28\%$
@answer The final price is \$36.00. That is $28\%$ off, not $30\%$ off.
:::

:::mistake Adding the percents
$20\%$ off and then $10\%$ off is **not** $30\%$ off. The $10\%$ is taken from \$40, not from \$50, so it saves only \$4 instead of \$5.
:::

## A percent of a percent

:::example Students on the bus
At a school with $500$ students, $60\%$ take the bus. Of those, $25\%$ ride for more than $30$ minutes. What percent of all the students ride the bus for more than $30$ minutes? How many students is that?
@step $25\%$ of $60\%$: $0.25 \times 0.60 = 0.15$, which is $15\%$.
@step $15\%$ of $500$: $0.15 \times 500 = 75$
@answer $15\%$ of all the students, or $75$ students, ride for more than $30$ minutes.
:::

:::summary
- Percent change $= \frac{\text{amount of change}}{\text{original amount}} \times 100\%$. Always divide by the original amount.
- For a discount and then tax, take the discount off first, then find the tax on the sale price.
- Two discounts in a row give less than the sum of the percents: $20\%$ then $10\%$ is $28\%$ off.
- To find a percent of a percent, multiply the decimals: $25\%$ of $60\%$ is $0.25 \times 0.60 = 15\%$.
:::
