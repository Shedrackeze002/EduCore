Hana has $6$ cups of trail mix. How many $\dfrac{3}{4}$-cup servings can she make? Questions like "how many fit into" are division questions, even with fractions.

## What does dividing by a fraction mean?

$3 \div \dfrac{1}{4}$ asks: how many quarters fit into $3$?

## Dividing with a common denominator

When two fractions have the same denominator, the pieces are the same size. Then you only need to divide the numerators.

:::

The top strip shows $\dfrac{3}{4}$. The bottom strip shows the same amount as $\dfrac{6}{8}$.

:::example Eighths in three-quarters
Find $\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{1}{8}$.
@step Write both with denominator $8$: $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{6}{8}$.
@step $\dfrac{6}{8} \div \dfrac{1}{8}$ asks how many eighths are in $6$ eighths: $6 \div 1 = 6$.
@answer $\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{1}{8} = 6$
:::

:::example An answer less than 1
Find $\dfrac{2}{3} \div \dfrac{3}{4}$.
@step Common denominator $12$: $\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{8}{12}$ and $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{9}{12}$.
@step Divide the numerators: $8 \div 9 = \dfrac{8}{9}$.
@answer $\dfrac{2}{3} \div \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{8}{9}$
:::

## Dividing by multiplying by the reciprocal

:::definition Reciprocal
The **reciprocal** of a fraction is the fraction flipped upside down. The reciprocal of $\dfrac{2}{5}$ is $\dfrac{5}{2}$. A number times its reciprocal is $1$.
:::

Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal.

$$\dfrac{a}{b} \div \dfrac{c}{d} = \dfrac{a}{b} \times \dfrac{d}{c}$$

:::example Using the reciprocal
Find $\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{2}{5}$. Write the answer as a mixed number.
@step Multiply by the reciprocal of $\dfrac{2}{5}$: $\dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{5}{2} = \dfrac{15}{8}$.
@step $\dfrac{15}{8} = 1\dfrac{7}{8}$
@step Check with a common denominator: $\dfrac{15}{20} \div \dfrac{8}{20} = \dfrac{15}{8}$. It matches.
@answer $\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{2}{5} = 1\dfrac{7}{8}$
:::

:::mistake Flipping the wrong fraction
Only the **second** fraction (the divisor) is flipped. $\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{2}{5}$ is not $\dfrac{4}{3} \times \dfrac{2}{5}$.
:::

:::

## Dividing mixed numbers

Write each mixed number as an improper fraction. Then multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.

:::example Two mixed numbers
Find $3\dfrac{3}{4} \div 1\dfrac{1}{2}$.
@step $3\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{15}{4}$ and $1\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.
@step $\dfrac{15}{4} \div \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{15}{4} \times \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{30}{12}$
@step Simplify: $\dfrac{30}{12} = \dfrac{5}{2} = 2\dfrac{1}{2}$.
@answer $3\dfrac{3}{4} \div 1\dfrac{1}{2} = 2\dfrac{1}{2}$
:::

:::tip Estimate first
$3\dfrac{3}{4}$ is about $4$, and $1\dfrac{1}{2}$ fits into $4$ between $2$ and $3$ times. So $2\dfrac{1}{2}$ makes sense.
:::

:::check m8-fraction-divide 3
:::

:::summary
- $a \div \dfrac{c}{d}$ asks how many $\dfrac{c}{d}$s fit into $a$.
- With a common denominator, divide the numerators.
- Or multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor: $\dfrac{a}{b} \div \dfrac{c}{d} = \dfrac{a}{b} \times \dfrac{d}{c}$.
- Write mixed numbers as improper fractions first, and give answers in lowest terms.
:::
