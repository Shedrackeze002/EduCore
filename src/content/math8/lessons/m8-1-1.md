A checkerboard has 8 rows of 8 squares, so it has 64 squares in all. Numbers like 64 can be arranged into a perfect square shape. They show up when you tile a floor, plant a square garden or set up chairs in rows. In this lesson you will find these numbers and learn how to undo squaring with a square root.

## Perfect squares

Imagine you have 16 square tiles. You can arrange them into a square with 4 tiles along each side. That is because $4 \times 4 = 16$.

:::definition Perfect square
A **perfect square** is a whole number that is another whole number times itself. Since $16 = 4 \times 4$, the number $16$ is a perfect square. We write $4 \times 4$ as $4^2$ and say "four squared".
:::

The first twelve perfect squares are worth knowing by heart:

$$1,\ 4,\ 9,\ 16,\ 25,\ 36,\ 49,\ 64,\ 81,\ 100,\ 121,\ 144$$

:::example Is 20 a perfect square?
Can 20 tiles make a solid square?
@step $4 \times 4 = 16$, which is too small. $5 \times 5 = 25$, which is too big.
@step $20$ is between $16$ and $25$, so no whole number times itself makes $20$.
@step You can make a $4$ by $5$ rectangle, but not a square.
@answer $20$ is **not** a perfect square.
:::

:::mistake Squaring is not doubling
$4^2$ means $4 \times 4 = 16$. It does **not** mean $4 \times 2 = 8$. The small 2 tells you how many times to use 4 as a factor.
:::

## Square roots

Squaring a number and taking its square root are opposite operations. One undoes the other.

:::definition Square root
The **square root** of a number is the number that multiplies by itself to give it. The symbol is $\sqrt{\ }$. For example, $\sqrt{81} = 9$, because $9 \times 9 = 81$.
:::

:::example Finding a square root
Find $\sqrt{196}$.
@step $10^2 = 100$ and $20^2 = 400$, so $\sqrt{196}$ is between $10$ and $20$.
@step $196$ ends in $6$. Only numbers ending in $4$ or $6$ have squares that end in $6$. So try $14$ and $16$.
@step $14 \times 14 = 196$ (and $16 \times 16 = 256$ is too big).
@answer $\sqrt{196} = 14$
:::

:::mistake A square root is not half
$\sqrt{36}$ is $6$, not $18$. Dividing by 2 gives a number that **adds** to itself to make 36. A square root **multiplies** by itself.
:::

## Area and side length of a square

The area of a square is side times side: $A = s^2$. So if you know the area, the side length is the square root: $s = \sqrt{A}$.

:::example From side to area
A square floor tile has sides of $15\ \text{cm}$. What is its area?
@step $A = s^2 = 15^2 = 15 \times 15 = 225$
@answer The area is $225\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::example From area to side
Mei plants a square vegetable garden with an area of $144\ \text{m}^2$. How long is each side? How much fencing goes around it?
@step $s = \sqrt{144} = 12$, because $12 \times 12 = 144$.
@step The perimeter is $4 \times 12 = 48$.
@answer Each side is $12\ \text{m}$ long, and Mei needs $48\ \text{m}$ of fencing.
:::

:::tip Check your units
Area is in square units, like $\text{cm}^2$ or $\text{m}^2$. A side length is in plain units, like $\text{cm}$ or $\text{m}$.
:::

## Perfect squares and prime factorization

For bigger numbers, prime factors help you decide whether a number is a perfect square.

:::definition Prime factorization
The **prime factorization** of a number writes it as a product of prime numbers only. For example, $72 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 = 2^3 \times 3^2$.
:::

A perfect square splits into two identical groups of factors. So in a perfect square, **every prime factor appears an even number of times**.

:::example A perfect square
Is $324$ a perfect square? If it is, find $\sqrt{324}$.
@step $324 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 2^2 \times 3^4$
@step Every exponent is even, so split the factors into two identical groups: $324 = (2 \times 3^2) \times (2 \times 3^2)$.
@step Each group is $2 \times 9 = 18$.
@answer $324$ is a perfect square, and $\sqrt{324} = 18$.
:::

:::example Not a perfect square
Is $72$ a perfect square?
@step $72 = 2^3 \times 3^2$
@step The prime factor $2$ appears $3$ times, which is odd. The factors cannot split into two identical groups.
@step Multiplying by one more $2$ fixes it: $72 \times 2 = 144 = 2^4 \times 3^2 = 12^2$.
@answer $72$ is **not** a perfect square, but $72 \times 2 = 144$ is.
:::

:::check m8-perfect-squares 3
:::

:::summary
- A perfect square is a whole number times itself, like $49 = 7 \times 7 = 7^2$.
- The square root undoes squaring: $\sqrt{49} = 7$.
- For a square, $A = s^2$ and $s = \sqrt{A}$.
- In the prime factorization of a perfect square, every prime factor appears an even number of times.
:::
