Before you wrap a gift, paint a toy box or sew a tent, you need to know how much paper, paint or fabric to buy. That amount depends on the area of every outside face of the object.

## What is surface area?

:::definition Surface area
The **surface area** of a 3-D object is the total area of all its faces. It is measured in square units, such as $\text{cm}^2$ or $\text{m}^2$.
:::

A net shows every face of an object laid flat, so the surface area is the area of the net. A rectangular prism has $6$ rectangle faces in $3$ matching pairs: top and bottom, front and back, and the two ends.

:::example A shipping box
A closed shipping box is $30\ \text{cm}$ long, $20\ \text{cm}$ wide and $10\ \text{cm}$ high. What is its surface area?
@step Top and bottom: $2 \times 30 \times 20 = 1200\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Front and back: $2 \times 30 \times 10 = 600\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Two ends: $2 \times 20 \times 10 = 400\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Add: $1200 + 600 + 400 = 2200$
@answer The surface area is $2200\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

## A formula for rectangular prisms

Each pair of faces gives one term, so for a prism with length $l$, width $w$ and height $h$:

$$SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh$$

:::example A box of crackers
A box of crackers is $12.5\ \text{cm}$ long, $8\ \text{cm}$ wide and $4\ \text{cm}$ high. Find its surface area.
@step $2lw = 2 \times 12.5 \times 8 = 200$
@step $2lh = 2 \times 12.5 \times 4 = 100$
@step $2wh = 2 \times 8 \times 4 = 64$
@step $SA = 200 + 100 + 64 = 364$
@answer The surface area is $364\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Counting only three faces
$l \times w + l \times h + w \times h$ covers only three faces: one of each pair. Remember the $2$ in front of each term, or the answer is half as big as it should be.
:::

:::

## Triangular prisms

A right triangular prism has $2$ triangle ends and $3$ rectangle faces. Each rectangle has one side of the triangle as its width and the length of the prism as its length.

:::example A ramp for a toy car track
A ramp is a right triangular prism. Each end is a right triangle with legs of $6\ \text{cm}$ and $8\ \text{cm}$ and a sloped side of $10\ \text{cm}$. The ramp is $15\ \text{cm}$ long. Find its surface area.
@step Two triangles: $2 \times \tfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 8 = 48\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Three rectangles: $6 \times 15 + 8 \times 15 + 10 \times 15 = 90 + 120 + 150 = 360\ \text{cm}^2$
@step $SA = 48 + 360 = 408$
@answer The surface area is $408\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::tip Rectangles in one step
The three rectangles all have the same length, so their total area is the perimeter of the triangle times the length: $(6 + 8 + 10) \times 15 = 360$.
:::

For a tent, the ends are isosceles triangles. Use the **height** of the triangle for its area, and the **sloped side** for the sloped rectangles.

:::example A pup tent with a floor
Each end of a tent is a triangle with a base of $1.2\ \text{m}$, a height of $0.8\ \text{m}$ and sloped sides of $1\ \text{m}$. The tent is $2.5\ \text{m}$ long and has a floor. How much fabric is used?
@step Two triangle ends: $2 \times \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1.2 \times 0.8 = 0.96\ \text{m}^2$
@step Floor: $1.2 \times 2.5 = 3\ \text{m}^2$
@step Two sloped sides: $2 \times 1 \times 2.5 = 5\ \text{m}^2$
@answer The tent uses $0.96 + 3 + 5 = 8.96\ \text{m}^2$ of fabric.
:::

:::mistake Height or sloped side?
The height of the triangle is used only for the area of the triangles. The sloped rectangles are as wide as the **sloped side**, not the height.
:::

## Solving problems

Read the problem to decide which faces count. An open box has no top. A tent may have no floor. A painted box may not be painted on the bottom.

:::example A planter box
Owen builds a wooden planter box with no top. It is $80\ \text{cm}$ long, $30\ \text{cm}$ wide and $25\ \text{cm}$ high. How much wood covers the outside?
@step Bottom only: $80 \times 30 = 2400\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Front and back: $2 \times 80 \times 25 = 4000\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Two ends: $2 \times 30 \times 25 = 1500\ \text{cm}^2$
@answer The box needs $2400 + 4000 + 1500 = 7900\ \text{cm}^2$ of wood.
:::

When paint or fabric comes in cans or rolls, divide the area by the amount one can or roll covers, then **round up**: part of a can is not enough. For example, $2.13\ \text{m}^2$ of paint with cans that cover $0.8\ \text{m}^2$ each needs $2.13 \div 0.8 \approx 2.66$, so $3$ cans.

:::check m8-surface-area-prism 3
:::

:::summary
- Surface area is the total area of all the faces of an object: the area of its net.
- Rectangular prism: $SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh$.
- Triangular prism: $2$ triangles plus $3$ rectangles. Use the triangle's height for the triangles and each side length for the rectangles.
- Count only the faces the problem needs, such as no top on an open box.
- Round up when buying cans or rolls.
:::
