Ladders, ramps, TV screens and ball diamonds all hide right triangles. Once you spot the right triangle, the Pythagorean theorem finds the missing length. You can also use it backwards to check whether a corner is really square.

## A plan for problems

1. Sketch the situation and mark the right angle.
2. Label the sides you know and the side you want.
3. Decide: is the unknown side the hypotenuse or a leg?
4. Use $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, then check that your answer makes sense.

:::example A ladder against a wall
A $5\ \text{m}$ ladder leans against a wall. The foot of the ladder is $1.4\ \text{m}$ from the wall. How high up the wall does the ladder reach?
@step The wall and the ground make a right angle. The ladder is across from it, so the ladder is the hypotenuse. The height $h$ is a leg.
@step $h^2 = 5^2 - 1.4^2 = 25 - 1.96 = 23.04$
@step $h = \sqrt{23.04} = 4.8$
@answer The ladder reaches $4.8\ \text{m}$ up the wall.
:::

:::example A TV screen
TV sizes are measured along the diagonal. A TV screen has a $50\ \text{in}$ diagonal and is $43.6\ \text{in}$ wide. How tall is it, to the nearest tenth of an inch?
@step The width, the height and the diagonal form a right triangle. The diagonal is the hypotenuse.
@step $h^2 = 50^2 - 43.6^2 = 2500 - 1900.96 = 599.04$
@step $h = \sqrt{599.04} \approx 24.48$
@answer The screen is about $24.5\ \text{in}$ tall.
:::

## Distances on a grid

To find the distance between two points on a grid, draw a right triangle. The segment between the points is the hypotenuse.

:::example Counting squares
Each square on the grid is $1$ unit long. How long is $AB$?
@step Count the squares: the horizontal leg is $6$ units and the vertical leg is $8$ units.
@step $AB^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100$
@step $AB = \sqrt{100} = 10$
@answer $AB$ is $10$ units long.
:::

## Pythagorean triples

:::definition Pythagorean triple
A **Pythagorean triple** is a set of three whole numbers $a$, $b$ and $c$ with $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. Some common ones are $3, 4, 5$ and $5, 12, 13$ and $8, 15, 17$ and $7, 24, 25$.
:::

If you multiply every number in a triple by the same whole number, you get another triple. For example, $3, 4, 5$ times $2$ gives $6, 8, 10$, and $36 + 64 = 100$.

:::example Making a new triple
Multiply the triple $5, 12, 13$ by $3$. Check that the result is a triple.
@step $5 \times 3 = 15$, $12 \times 3 = 36$ and $13 \times 3 = 39$.
@step $15^2 + 36^2 = 225 + 1296 = 1521$ and $39^2 = 1521$.
@answer $15, 36, 39$ is a Pythagorean triple.
:::

Knowing triples saves time: a right triangle with legs $15\ \text{m}$ and $36\ \text{m}$ has a hypotenuse of exactly $39\ \text{m}$.

## Is it a right triangle?

The theorem also works in reverse. Square the two shorter sides and add. If the sum equals the square of the longest side, the triangle is a right triangle. If not, it is not.

:::example A right triangle
A triangle has sides of $9\ \text{cm}$, $12\ \text{cm}$ and $15\ \text{cm}$. Is it a right triangle?
@step The longest side is $15$, so test $9^2 + 12^2 = 81 + 144 = 225$.
@step $15^2 = 225$
@answer $225 = 225$, so it **is** a right triangle.
:::

:::example Not a right triangle
A triangle has sides of $7\ \text{m}$, $9\ \text{m}$ and $11\ \text{m}$. Is it a right triangle?
@step $7^2 + 9^2 = 49 + 81 = 130$
@step $11^2 = 121$
@answer $130 \ne 121$, so it is **not** a right triangle.
:::

:::mistake Use the longest side as c
Only the longest side can be the hypotenuse. Always square the **two shorter** sides and compare with the square of the **longest** side.
:::

:::tip Checking a corner
Builders mark $3$ units along one side of a corner and $4$ units along the other. If the diagonal between the marks is exactly $5$ units, the corner is square.
:::

:::summary
- Sketch, mark the right angle, and decide whether you want the hypotenuse or a leg.
- On a grid, count the horizontal and vertical legs, then use $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$.
- A Pythagorean triple is three whole numbers with $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, like $3, 4, 5$.
- If $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ for the two shorter sides and the longest side, the triangle is a right triangle.
:::
