Two pieces of a quilt, two wings of a model plane, two panels of a roof truss: each pair has to match exactly, or the job does not fit together. Shapes that match exactly are called congruent.

## Congruent polygons

:::definition Congruent
Two polygons are **congruent** when they have the same size and the same shape. One fits exactly on top of the other, even if you have to turn it or flip it over first. The symbol $\cong$ means "is congruent to".
:::

:::definition Corresponding sides and angles
When one polygon is placed on top of a congruent polygon, the parts that match are **corresponding** parts. Corresponding sides are equal in length, and corresponding angles are equal in measure.
:::

## Congruence statements

:::definition Congruence statement
A **congruence statement** such as $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$ lists the vertices in **matching order**: the first matches the first, the second matches the second, and so on. Here $A \leftrightarrow D$, $B \leftrightarrow E$ and $C \leftrightarrow F$.
:::

From the matching vertices you can read every pair of corresponding parts. Side $AB$ joins the 1st and 2nd vertices, so it matches $DE$, which joins the 1st and 2nd vertices of $DEF$.

:::example Reading a congruence statement
$\triangle PQR \cong \triangle XZY$. Which side corresponds to $QR$, and which angle corresponds to $\angle R$?
@step Match the letters in order: $P \leftrightarrow X$, $Q \leftrightarrow Z$, $R \leftrightarrow Y$.
@step $QR$ joins $Q$ and $R$, which match $Z$ and $Y$. So $QR$ corresponds to $ZY$.
@step $R$ is the 3rd letter, and the 3rd letter of $XZY$ is $Y$.
@answer $QR$ corresponds to $ZY$, and $\angle R$ corresponds to $\angle Y$.
:::

:::mistake Going by the picture
The second polygon may be turned or flipped, so do not match vertices by where they sit on the page. Always use the order of the letters in the statement.
:::

## Finding missing measures

Corresponding parts of congruent polygons are equal. So a measure on one polygon tells you the measure of its partner on the other.

:::example A quadrilateral
Quadrilateral $ABCD \cong$ quadrilateral $PQRS$. In $ABCD$, $AB = 5\ \text{cm}$, $BC = 8\ \text{cm}$ and $\angle D = 75^\circ$. Find $QR$ and $\angle S$.
@step $A \leftrightarrow P$, $B \leftrightarrow Q$, $C \leftrightarrow R$, $D \leftrightarrow S$.
@step $QR$ matches $BC$, so $QR = 8\ \text{cm}$.
@step $\angle S$ matches $\angle D$, so $\angle S = 75^\circ$.
@answer $QR = 8\ \text{cm}$ and $\angle S = 75^\circ$.
:::

Sometimes you need one more step, such as the angle sum of a triangle ($180^\circ$) or of a quadrilateral ($360^\circ$).

:::example Two steps
$\triangle JKL \cong \triangle TUV$. $\angle J = 50^\circ$ and $\angle U = 64^\circ$. Find $\angle V$.
@step $\angle U$ matches $\angle K$, so $\angle K = 64^\circ$.
@step In $\triangle JKL$: $\angle L = 180^\circ - 50^\circ - 64^\circ = 66^\circ$.
@answer $\angle V$ matches $\angle L$, so $\angle V = 66^\circ$.
:::

## Are two polygons congruent?

To decide, compare **every** pair of corresponding sides **and every** pair of corresponding angles. All of them must be equal.

- Two polygons can have equal angles but different side lengths. One is an enlargement of the other, so they are not congruent.
- Two quadrilaterals can have equal sides but different angles. A square and a rhombus with $4\ \text{cm}$ sides are not congruent.

:::summary
- Congruent polygons have the same size and shape: all corresponding sides and all corresponding angles are equal.
- In a congruence statement like $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$, the vertices are listed in matching order.
- Use the statement, not the picture, to find corresponding parts.
- Copy a measure to its corresponding part, then use angle sums ($180^\circ$ or $360^\circ$) if needed.
- Check every side and every angle before you say two polygons are congruent.
:::
