What is the chance it rains on both days of your camping weekend? What is the chance a basketball player makes two free throws in a row? When two events do not affect each other, there is a quick way to find the probability that both happen.

## Independent events

:::definition Independent events
Two events are **independent** when the result of one does not change the probability of the other.
:::

- Rolling a die and flipping a coin are independent. The die cannot change the coin.
- Picking a card, **putting it back**, and picking again are independent. The deck is the same both times.
- Picking a card and then picking a second card **without** putting the first one back are **not** independent. The deck has changed.

:::example Independent or not?
Which pair is independent? (a) Drawing a name from a hat, keeping it out, then drawing a second name. (b) Spinning a spinner twice.
@step (a) After the first name is kept out, there are fewer names, so the second draw changes. Not independent.
@step (b) The spinner is the same for both spins. Independent.
@answer (b) Spinning a spinner twice
:::

:::example Heads and red
Harpreet flips a coin and spins a spinner with $4$ equal sections: $2$ red, $1$ blue and $1$ green. What is the probability of heads and red?
@step Table check: the coin has $2$ outcomes and the spinner has $4$ equal sections, so there are $2 \times 4 = 8$ equally likely outcomes. Heads with a red section: $2$ of them.
@step $P(\text{heads and red}) = \dfrac{2}{8} = \dfrac{1}{4}$
@step Tree check: multiply along the path H then R: $\dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{4}$
@answer $P(\text{heads and red}) = \dfrac{1}{4}$
:::

## The multiplication rule

The table and the tree give the same answer as multiplying the two probabilities.

:::definition Probability of two independent events
If $A$ and $B$ are independent, then
$$P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B)$$
:::

:::example A die and a spinner
Amara rolls a die and spins a spinner with $8$ equal sections, $3$ of them red. What is the probability of an even number and red?
@step $P(\text{even}) = \dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$
@step $P(\text{red}) = \dfrac{3}{8}$
@step $P(\text{even and red}) = \dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{3}{16}$
@answer $\dfrac{3}{16}$
:::

:::example Cards put back
From a standard deck of $52$ cards, Owen picks a card, puts it back, shuffles and picks again. What is the probability of a heart and then a king?
@step $P(\text{heart}) = \dfrac{13}{52} = \dfrac{1}{4}$ and $P(\text{king}) = \dfrac{4}{52} = \dfrac{1}{13}$
@step The card is put back, so the picks are independent: $\dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{1}{13} = \dfrac{1}{52}$
@answer $\dfrac{1}{52}$
:::

## Solving problems

Many real problems give probabilities as percents. Change them to decimals, multiply, then change back to a percent. For an event that does **not** happen, subtract from $100\%$ first.

:::example A camping weekend
The forecast gives a $30\%$ chance of rain on Saturday and a $40\%$ chance on Sunday. Assume the days are independent. Find the probability of rain on both days, on neither day, and on at least one day.
@step Both: $0.3 \times 0.4 = 0.12$, so $12\%$
@step Neither: no rain is $70\%$ and $60\%$, so $0.7 \times 0.6 = 0.42$, or $42\%$
@step At least one day is the opposite of neither: $100\% - 42\% = 58\%$
@answer Both: $12\%$. Neither: $42\%$. At least one: $58\%$.
:::

:::example Free throws
Kiona makes $80\%$ of her free throws. Assume her shots are independent. What is the probability she makes two in a row?
@step $0.8 \times 0.8 = 0.64$
@answer $64\%$
:::

:::mistake Adding instead of multiplying
The chance of rain on **both** days is not $30\% + 40\% = 70\%$. Both days must be rainy, which is less likely than either day alone. Multiply: $12\%$.
:::

:::tip Expected number
To predict how often something happens, multiply its probability by the number of times. Over $50$ weekends like this one, expect rain on both days about $0.12 \times 50 = 6$ times.
:::

:::summary
- Events are independent when one does not change the probability of the other. Putting a card back keeps the picks independent.
- A table or tree diagram shows all the outcomes. Multiply along a branch of a tree.
- $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B)$ for independent events.
- $P(\text{not } A) = 1 - P(A)$, or $100\%$ minus the percent.
- $P(\text{at least one}) = 1 - P(\text{neither})$
:::
