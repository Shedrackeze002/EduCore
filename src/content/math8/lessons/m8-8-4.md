A soup can is made from a flat sheet of metal rolled into a tube, with a circle at each end. Its paper label covers only the curved part. To find how much metal or paper a cylinder needs, unroll it.

## Unrolling a cylinder

A right cylinder has two circle ends and a curved surface. When you cut the curved surface straight down and flatten it, you get a **rectangle**.

- The width of the rectangle is the **height** of the cylinder, $h$.
- The length of the rectangle wraps once around the circle, so it equals the **circumference**, $2\pi r$.

So the curved surface has area $2\pi r \times h = 2\pi rh$.

:::example The label on a can
A can of tomatoes has a radius of $4\ \text{cm}$ and a height of $11\ \text{cm}$. The label covers the curved surface. What is the area of the label, to the nearest tenth of a square centimetre?
@step Unrolled, the label is a rectangle $2\pi r$ long and $h$ wide.
@step $A = 2 \times \pi \times 4 \times 11 \approx 276.46$
@answer The label has an area of about $276.5\ \text{cm}^2$.

## The surface area formula

:::definition Surface area of a cylinder
For a closed cylinder with radius $r$ and height $h$, add the two circles and the curved surface:
$$SA = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh$$
:::

:::

:::example A round gift box
A gift box is a cylinder with a radius of $3\ \text{cm}$ and a height of $10\ \text{cm}$. Find its surface area to the nearest tenth of a square centimetre.
@step Two circles: $2 \times \pi \times 3^2 \approx 56.549\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Curved surface: $2 \times \pi \times 3 \times 10 \approx 188.496\ \text{cm}^2$
@step $SA \approx 56.549 + 188.496 = 245.045$
@answer The surface area is about $245.0\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::tip Round once, at the end
Use the $\pi$ key and keep all the digits on your calculator until the last step. Rounding each part first can change the last digit of the answer.
:::

:::mistake Radius or diameter?
The formula uses the **radius**. If you are given the diameter, halve it first. A hockey puck with a diameter of $7.6\ \text{cm}$ has a radius of $3.8\ \text{cm}$, not $7.6\ \text{cm}$.
:::

:::example A hockey puck
A hockey puck has a diameter of $7.6\ \text{cm}$ and a height of $2.5\ \text{cm}$. Find its surface area to the nearest tenth of a square centimetre.
@step Radius: $r = 7.6 \div 2 = 3.8\ \text{cm}$
@step Two circles: $2 \times \pi \times 3.8^2 \approx 90.73\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Curved surface: $2 \times \pi \times 3.8 \times 2.5 \approx 59.69\ \text{cm}^2$
@answer $SA \approx 90.73 + 59.69 = 150.42$, so about $150.4\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

## Solving problems with cylinders

Decide which parts of the cylinder count before you calculate.

- A glass, a flower pot or a pool has a bottom but **no top**: one circle plus the curved surface.
- A pipe or a cardboard tube has **no ends**: only the curved surface.
- Paint or materials may have a price per square metre.

:::example A clay flower pot
A flower pot is a cylinder with a diameter of $20\ \text{cm}$ and a height of $18\ \text{cm}$. It has no top. The outside, including the bottom, is painted. What area is painted, to the nearest tenth of a square centimetre?
@step Radius: $20 \div 2 = 10\ \text{cm}$
@step One circle (the bottom): $\pi \times 10^2 \approx 314.16\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Curved surface: $2 \times \pi \times 10 \times 18 \approx 1130.97\ \text{cm}^2$
@answer Painted area $\approx 314.16 + 1130.97 = 1445.13$, about $1445.1\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::example Painting a rain barrel
A closed rain barrel has a radius of $0.6\ \text{m}$ and a height of $1.5\ \text{m}$. Paint costs \$4.50 per square metre. Round the area to the nearest tenth, then find the cost.
@step $SA = 2\pi(0.6)^2 + 2\pi(0.6)(1.5) \approx 7.92$, so about $7.9\ \text{m}^2$.
@step Cost: $7.9 \times 4.50 = 35.55$
@answer The paint costs \$35.55.
:::

:::summary
- The curved surface of a cylinder unrolls into a rectangle $2\pi r$ long and $h$ wide.
- Closed cylinder: $SA = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh$.
- Halve the diameter to get the radius.
- Open top: one circle plus the curved surface. No ends: the curved surface only.
- Use the $\pi$ key and round only at the end.
:::
