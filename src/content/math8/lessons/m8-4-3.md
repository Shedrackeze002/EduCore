Recipes, building projects and camping trips are full of fractions: $\dfrac{3}{4}$ cup of sugar, a board $7\dfrac{1}{2}$ feet long, $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a bag of marshmallows. In this lesson, you decide when to multiply and when to divide.

## Multiply or divide?

:::definition Key words
**Of** usually means multiply: $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $\dfrac{3}{4}$ cup is $\dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{4}$. **How many … fit in** (or **how many … in**) means divide: how many $\dfrac{3}{4}$-cup servings are in $6$ cups is $6 \div \dfrac{3}{4}$.
:::

:::example Half a recipe
A recipe uses $\dfrac{3}{4}$ cup of sugar. Diego makes $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of the recipe. How much sugar does he need?
@step "$\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $\dfrac{3}{4}$" means multiply: $\dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{4}$.
@step $\dfrac{1 \times 3}{2 \times 4} = \dfrac{3}{8}$
@answer Diego needs $\dfrac{3}{8}$ cup of sugar.
:::

:::example Servings of trail mix
Hana has $6$ cups of trail mix. How many $\dfrac{3}{4}$-cup servings can she make?
@step "How many $\dfrac{3}{4}$s in $6$" means divide: $6 \div \dfrac{3}{4}$.
@step $6 \times \dfrac{4}{3} = \dfrac{24}{3} = 8$
@answer Hana can make $8$ servings.
:::

:::tip Does the answer make sense?
Each serving is less than $1$ cup, so there should be **more** than $6$ servings. $8$ makes sense.
:::

## Problems with mixed numbers

:::example Cutting a board
Malik has a board that is $7\dfrac{1}{2}$ ft long. He cuts it into pieces that are each $1\dfrac{1}{4}$ ft long. How many pieces does he get?
@step Divide: $7\dfrac{1}{2} \div 1\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{15}{2} \div \dfrac{5}{4}$.
@step $\dfrac{15}{2} \times \dfrac{4}{5} = \dfrac{60}{10} = 6$
@answer Malik gets $6$ pieces.
:::

:::example More batches
One batch of bannock uses $1\dfrac{3}{4}$ cups of flour. Aiyana makes $2\dfrac{1}{2}$ batches. How much flour does she need?
@step Multiply: $2\dfrac{1}{2} \times 1\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{5}{2} \times \dfrac{7}{4}$.
@step $\dfrac{5 \times 7}{2 \times 4} = \dfrac{35}{8} = 4\dfrac{3}{8}$
@answer Aiyana needs $4\dfrac{3}{8}$ cups of flour.
:::

:::example A garden plot
A school garden is $3\dfrac{1}{2}$ m long and $2\dfrac{1}{4}$ m wide. What is its area?
@step Area $=$ length $\times$ width $= \dfrac{7}{2} \times \dfrac{9}{4} = \dfrac{63}{8}$.
@step $\dfrac{63}{8} = 7\dfrac{7}{8}$
@answer The area is $7\dfrac{7}{8}$ m².
:::

## Multi-step problems

Some problems need more than one step. Make a plan before you calculate.

:::example Pieces and the leftover
Lena has a $10$ ft board. She cuts as many $1\dfrac{3}{4}$ ft pieces as she can. How many full pieces does she get, and how long is the leftover piece?
@step Divide: $10 \div 1\dfrac{3}{4} = 10 \times \dfrac{4}{7} = \dfrac{40}{7} = 5\dfrac{5}{7}$.
@step Only whole pieces count, so she gets $5$ full pieces.
@step The $5$ pieces use $5 \times \dfrac{7}{4} = \dfrac{35}{4} = 8\dfrac{3}{4}$ ft.
@step Leftover: $10 - 8\dfrac{3}{4} = 1\dfrac{1}{4}$ ft.
@answer Lena gets $5$ full pieces and a $1\dfrac{1}{4}$ ft leftover piece.
:::

:::mistake A fraction of a piece
The answer to "how many full pieces" is not $5\dfrac{5}{7}$. The $\dfrac{5}{7}$ is a piece that is too short. Round down, then find the leftover length.
:::

:::example Scaling a cookie recipe
A recipe for $24$ cookies uses $2\dfrac{1}{4}$ cups of flour. How much flour is needed for $40$ cookies?
@step $40$ cookies is $\dfrac{40}{24} = \dfrac{5}{3}$ of the recipe.
@step $2\dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{5}{3} = \dfrac{9}{4} \times \dfrac{5}{3} = \dfrac{45}{12} = \dfrac{15}{4}$
@step $\dfrac{15}{4} = 3\dfrac{3}{4}$
@answer $3\dfrac{3}{4}$ cups of flour are needed.
:::

:::summary
- "Of" usually means multiply. "How many … in" means divide.
- Write mixed numbers as improper fractions, then multiply or divide.
- Check that the answer makes sense: dividing by a number less than $1$ gives a bigger answer.
- For "how many full pieces", use the whole-number part and find the leftover separately.
:::
