Architects, game designers and furniture makers draw objects from different directions. Three flat drawings — one from the front, one from the top and one from the side — can show exactly how an object is built.

## Views of an object

:::definition View
A **view** is a flat drawing of what you see when you look straight at an object from one direction. The **front view** is seen from the front, the **top view** from directly above, and the **side view** from one side.
:::

Amira built this object from 8 linking cubes. The arrow shows the front. No cubes are hidden.

:::figure

The object has two rows of stacks. The **back row** has a stack of 1 on the left and a stack of 3 on the right. The **front row** has stacks of 2, 1 and 1.

To draw a view, picture each cube face you would see as one square on grid paper.

:::example The front view
Draw the front view of Amira's object.
@step Look in the direction of the arrow. Go across the columns from left to right.
@step Left column: stacks of $1$ (back) and $2$ (front). You see the taller one, so the column is $2$ squares high.
@step Middle column: only a stack of $1$. The column is $1$ square high.
@step Right column: stacks of $3$ (back) and $1$ (front). The back stack is taller, so you see $3$ squares.
@answer The front view has columns $2$, $1$ and $3$ squares high.
:::

## The top view

From directly above, every stack shows as **one** square, however tall it is. The top view shows where the stacks stand.

:::example The top view
Draw the top view of Amira's object.
@step Put the back row at the top of the drawing and the front row at the bottom.
@step Back row: stacks in the left and right columns, so draw $2$ squares with a gap in the middle.
@step Front row: stacks in all three columns, so draw $3$ squares.
@answer The top view is $2$ squares on top (left and right) and $3$ squares along the bottom.
:::

:::mistake Flipping the top view
The **front** row belongs at the **bottom** of the top view, nearest to you, just like a map with south at the bottom. Putting the front row at the top turns the drawing upside down.
:::

## The right side view

:::definition Right side view
The **right side view** is what you see when you stand on the right side of the object and look left. The front of the object is now on your **left**.
:::

Each column of the side view is one **row** of the object, going from the front row (left) to the back row (right). Its height is the tallest stack in that row.

:::example The right side view
Draw the right side view of Amira's object.
@step Stand on the right. The front row is on your left, and the back row is on your right.
@step Front row: stacks of $2$, $1$ and $1$. The tallest is $2$.
@step Back row: stacks of $1$ and $3$. The tallest is $3$.
@answer The right side view has columns $2$ and $3$ squares high, with the shorter column on the left.
:::

:::tip Turn yourself, not the object
Before drawing a side view, imagine walking around to that side. Ask: what is on my left now, and what is on my right?

## Counting cubes and matching views

To count the cubes, go one stack at a time so you do not count a cube twice. Every cube sits on the table or on another cube, so a tall stack goes all the way down.

:::example How many cubes?
How many cubes are in Amira's object? How many more would make a rectangular prism $3$ cubes wide, $2$ deep and $3$ high?
@step Back row: $1 + 3 = 4$ cubes. Front row: $2 + 1 + 1 = 4$ cubes.
@step Total: $4 + 4 = 8$ cubes.
@step The prism would have $3 \times 2 \times 3 = 18$ cubes.
@answer The object has $8$ cubes. It needs $18 - 8 = 10$ more cubes to become the prism.
:::

To find an object from its three views, use the top view first to see where the stacks are. Then use the front and side views to check the height of each column and row. Check every view: two objects can share one view but differ in another.

:::

:::summary
- A view is a flat drawing of an object seen straight on from one direction.
- Front view: the columns from left to right, each as tall as the tallest stack in that column.
- Top view: one square for each stack, with the back row at the top and the front row at the bottom.
- Right side view: stand on the right, so the front of the object is on the left; each column is as tall as the tallest stack in that row.
- Count cubes one stack at a time, and check an object against every view.
:::
