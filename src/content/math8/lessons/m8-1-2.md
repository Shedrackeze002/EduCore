Amara wants a square garden with an area of $40\ \text{m}^2$. How long is each side? No whole number times itself makes 40, so the answer is not a whole number. In this lesson you will estimate square roots like $\sqrt{40}$ without a calculator.

## Numbers that are not perfect squares

Most whole numbers are not perfect squares. Their square roots are decimals that go on and on without a repeating pattern. You can still estimate them by comparing with perfect squares you know.

:::definition Non-perfect square
A **non-perfect square** is a whole number that is not a perfect square, like $2$, $40$ or $90$. Its square root is not a whole number.
:::

:::definition Benchmark
A **benchmark** is a number you know well that helps you estimate. To estimate a square root, use the perfect squares just below and just above the number.
:::

## Between two whole numbers

:::example Between which two whole numbers?
Between which two consecutive whole numbers is $\sqrt{40}$?
@step The perfect square just below $40$ is $36 = 6^2$. The perfect square just above $40$ is $49 = 7^2$.
@step $36 < 40 < 49$, so $\sqrt{36} < \sqrt{40} < \sqrt{49}$.
@answer $\sqrt{40}$ is between $6$ and $7$.
:::

:::mistake A square root is not half the number
$\sqrt{40}$ is not $20$. Check: $20 \times 20 = 400$, which is far from $40$. Always look for the perfect squares near the number.
:::

:::check m8-estimate-sqrt 1
:::

## Which whole number is it closer to?

Look at how far the number is from each benchmark.

:::example Closer to 6 or to 7?
Is $\sqrt{40}$ closer to $6$ or to $7$?
@step $40 - 36 = 4$ and $49 - 40 = 9$.
@step $40$ is closer to $36$ than to $49$.
@answer $\sqrt{40}$ is closer to $6$.
:::

## Estimating to one decimal place

To get one decimal place, decide how far the number is between the two perfect squares. Then check by squaring.

:::example Estimate the square root of 40
Estimate $\sqrt{40}$ to one decimal place.
@step $\sqrt{40}$ is between $6$ and $7$.
@step From $36$ to $49$ is $13$ steps, and $40$ is $4$ of those steps. $\frac{4}{13} \approx 0.31$, so $\sqrt{40}$ is about $6.31$.
@step Check by squaring: $6.3^2 = 39.69$ and $6.4^2 = 40.96$.
@step $40$ is closer to $39.69$ than to $40.96$.
@answer $\sqrt{40} \approx 6.3$
:::

:::example Squaring changes the estimate
Estimate $\sqrt{90}$ to one decimal place.
@step $81 < 90 < 100$, so $\sqrt{90}$ is between $9$ and $10$.
@step $90$ is $9$ of the $19$ steps from $81$ to $100$. $\frac{9}{19} \approx 0.47$, so $\sqrt{90}$ is about $9.47$.
@step Check: $9.4^2 = 88.36$ and $9.5^2 = 90.25$. $90$ is much closer to $90.25$.
@answer $\sqrt{90} \approx 9.5$
:::

:::tip Always check by squaring
The "steps" estimate gets you close. Squaring the tenths on either side tells you which one is better.
:::

## Square roots in problems

:::example A square rug
A square rug has an area of $8\ \text{m}^2$. Estimate the length of each side to one decimal place.
@step The side length is $\sqrt{8}$. Since $4 < 8 < 9$, it is between $2$ and $3$.
@step $8$ is $4$ of the $5$ steps from $4$ to $9$, so try $2.8$.
@step $2.8^2 = 7.84$ and $2.9^2 = 8.41$. $8$ is closer to $7.84$.
@answer Each side is about $2.8\ \text{m}$ long.
:::

:::example Counting square roots
How many whole numbers have a square root between $5$ and $6$?
@step $5^2 = 25$ and $6^2 = 36$.
@step The whole numbers between them are $26, 27, \ldots, 35$.
@answer There are $10$ whole numbers.
:::

:::check m8-estimate-sqrt 3
:::

:::summary
- The square root of a non-perfect square is not a whole number.
- Use the perfect squares just below and just above the number as benchmarks.
- Compare the distances to decide which whole number the square root is closer to.
- For one decimal place, estimate how far along it is, then check by squaring the tenths on either side.
:::
