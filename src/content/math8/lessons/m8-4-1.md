A pan of brownies is $\dfrac{3}{4}$ full. Owen eats $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of what is left. What fraction of the whole pan did he eat? To answer, you multiply fractions.

## A fraction of a whole number

The word **of** often means multiply. $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of $12$ means $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 12$.

:::example Two-thirds of the team
A soccer team has $12$ players. $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of them ride the bus to games. How many players is that?
@step Find $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of $12$: $12 \div 3 = 4$.
@step Two-thirds is $2$ of those parts: $2 \times 4 = 8$.
@step Or multiply the numerator by the whole number: $\dfrac{2 \times 12}{3} = \dfrac{24}{3} = 8$.
@answer $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 12 = 8$, so $8$ players ride the bus.
:::

:::example Reading the area model
Use the area model to find $\dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{3}{4}$.
@step The square has $3 \times 4 = 12$ small rectangles.
@step The overlap is $2$ rows by $3$ columns: $2 \times 3 = 6$ small rectangles.
@step So $\dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{6}{12}$. In lowest terms, $\dfrac{6}{12} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
@answer $\dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

## Multiplying numerators and denominators

The area model shows a shortcut. The overlap has (numerator × numerator) rectangles out of (denominator × denominator).

$$\dfrac{a}{b} \times \dfrac{c}{d} = \dfrac{a \times c}{b \times d}$$

Now the brownie question from the start: Owen eats $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of the pan, and $\dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{1 \times 3}{2 \times 4} = \dfrac{3}{8}$ of the pan.

You can simplify **before** you multiply: divide any numerator and any denominator by a common factor. The numbers stay small.

:::example Simplify first
Find $\dfrac{4}{9} \times \dfrac{3}{8}$. Write the answer in lowest terms.
@step $4$ and $8$ share the factor $4$: $4 \div 4 = 1$ and $8 \div 4 = 2$.
@step $3$ and $9$ share the factor $3$: $3 \div 3 = 1$ and $9 \div 3 = 3$.
@step Now multiply: $\dfrac{1}{3} \times \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{6}$.
@answer $\dfrac{4}{9} \times \dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{1}{6}$
:::

:::tip Check without simplifying first
$\dfrac{4 \times 3}{9 \times 8} = \dfrac{12}{72}$, and $\dfrac{12}{72} = \dfrac{1}{6}$ after dividing by $12$. Same answer, bigger numbers.
:::

:::mistake Using a common denominator
You do **not** need a common denominator to multiply. That is for adding and subtracting. Multiply straight across.
:::

## Multiplying mixed numbers

To multiply mixed numbers, write each one as an improper fraction first.

:::example Two mixed numbers
Find $2\dfrac{1}{2} \times 1\dfrac{1}{3}$. Write the answer as a mixed number.
@step $2\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{5}{2}$ and $1\dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{4}{3}$.
@step $\dfrac{5}{2} \times \dfrac{4}{3} = \dfrac{20}{6} = \dfrac{10}{3}$
@step $\dfrac{10}{3} = 3\dfrac{1}{3}$, because $10 \div 3 = 3$ R $1$.
@answer $2\dfrac{1}{2} \times 1\dfrac{1}{3} = 3\dfrac{1}{3}$
:::

An area model checks the answer. Split each mixed number into its whole part and its fraction part, and multiply each part by each part.

:::figure
{"type":"areaModel","top":["2","\\dfrac{1}{2}"],"side":["1","\\dfrac{1}{3}"],"cells":[["2","\\dfrac{1}{2}"],["\\dfrac{2}{3}","\\dfrac{1}{6}"]]}
:::

Add the four parts: $2 + \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{6} = 2 + \dfrac{3}{6} + \dfrac{4}{6} + \dfrac{1}{6} = 2 + \dfrac{8}{6} = 3\dfrac{1}{3}$.

:::mistake Multiplying the parts separately
$2\dfrac{1}{2} \times 1\dfrac{1}{3}$ is **not** $2 \times 1$ plus $\dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{1}{3}$, which gives $2\dfrac{1}{6}$. The area model shows two more parts, $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3}$, that this misses.
:::

:::check m8-fraction-multiply 3
:::

:::summary
- "Of" means multiply: $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of $12$ is $\dfrac{2}{3} \times 12 = 8$.
- An area model shows a product as the overlap of two shaded parts of a square.
- Multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators. Simplify first to keep the numbers small.
- Write mixed numbers as improper fractions before you multiply. Give answers in lowest terms.
:::
