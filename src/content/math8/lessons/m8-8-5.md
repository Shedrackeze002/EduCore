How much water fits in a fish tank? How much juice is in a can? Both questions are about **volume**: the amount of space inside a 3-D object.

## Volume of a rectangular prism

:::definition Volume
The **volume** of an object is the amount of space it takes up. It is measured in cubic units, such as $\text{cm}^3$ or $\text{m}^3$.
:::

Picture filling a box with centimetre cubes. The bottom layer covers the base, so it has as many cubes as the **area of the base**. Then you stack layers up to the height. That gives a rule for **every** right prism:

$$V = (\text{area of base}) \times \text{height}$$

For a rectangular prism, the base is a rectangle, so $V = l \times w \times h$.

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"rectPrism","labels":{"l":"30 cm","w":"20 cm","h":"10 cm"}}
:::

:::example A shipping box
A box is $30\ \text{cm}$ long, $20\ \text{cm}$ wide and $10\ \text{cm}$ high. What is its volume?
@step Area of base: $30 \times 20 = 600\ \text{cm}^2$
@step $V = 600 \times 10 = 6000$
@answer The volume is $6000\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

## Volume of a triangular prism

For a triangular prism, the **base** is the triangle at the end, and the **height** of the prism is its length. The prism can lie on its side; the triangle is still the base.

:::

:::example A ramp
A ramp is a right triangular prism. Each end has legs of $6\ \text{cm}$ and $8\ \text{cm}$ and a sloped side of $10\ \text{cm}$. The ramp is $15\ \text{cm}$ long. What is its volume?
@step Area of the triangle: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 8 = 24\ \text{cm}^2$
@step $V = 24 \times 15 = 360$
@answer The volume is $360\ \text{cm}^3$. The sloped side is not needed.
:::

:::mistake The wrong base
The base of a prism is one of the two matching ends, not the face the prism sits on. For a triangular prism, use the area of the triangle, and multiply by the length of the prism.
:::

## Capacity and missing dimensions

:::definition Capacity
The **capacity** of a container is how much it can hold, often measured in millilitres or litres. $1\ \text{mL} = 1\ \text{cm}^3$ and $1\ \text{L} = 1000\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

:::example A fish tank
The inside of a fish tank is $60\ \text{cm}$ long, $30\ \text{cm}$ wide and $40\ \text{cm}$ deep. How many litres does it hold?
@step $V = 60 \times 30 \times 40 = 72\,000\ \text{cm}^3$
@step $72\,000 \div 1000 = 72$
@answer The tank holds $72\ \text{L}$.
:::

If you know the volume, you can work backwards: height $=$ volume $\div$ (area of base).

:::example A missing height
A gift box has a volume of $3600\ \text{cm}^3$. Its base is $15\ \text{cm}$ by $12\ \text{cm}$. How high is it?
@step Area of base: $15 \times 12 = 180\ \text{cm}^2$
@step $h = 3600 \div 180 = 20$
@answer The box is $20\ \text{cm}$ high.
:::

## Volume of a cylinder

A cylinder is like a prism with a circle for its base. The area of the circle is $\pi r^2$, so:

$$V = \pi r^2 h$$

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"cylinder","labels":{"r":"4 cm","h":"10 cm"}}
:::

:::example A can of soup
A can has a radius of $4\ \text{cm}$ and a height of $10\ \text{cm}$. Find its volume to the nearest tenth of a cubic centimetre.
@step Area of base: $\pi \times 4^2 \approx 50.27\ \text{cm}^2$
@step $V = \pi \times 4^2 \times 10 \approx 502.65$
@answer The volume is about $502.7\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

:::example A drinking glass
The inside of a glass has a diameter of $7\ \text{cm}$ and a height of $12\ \text{cm}$. How many millilitres does it hold, to the nearest millilitre?
@step Radius: $7 \div 2 = 3.5\ \text{cm}$
@step $V = \pi \times 3.5^2 \times 12 \approx 461.81\ \text{cm}^3$
@answer Since $1\ \text{cm}^3 = 1\ \text{mL}$, the glass holds about $462\ \text{mL}$.
:::

:::mistake Squaring the diameter
Square the **radius**, not the diameter. Using $7^2$ instead of $3.5^2$ makes the answer four times too big.
:::

## Problems with cylinders

To find a missing height, divide the volume by the area of the circle: $h = V \div (\pi r^2)$.

:::example A water tank
A cylindrical tank holds $500\ \text{L}$ when full. Its radius is $0.4\ \text{m}$. How tall is it, to the nearest hundredth of a metre? Use $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1000\ \text{L}$.
@step Volume: $500 \div 1000 = 0.5\ \text{m}^3$
@step Area of base: $\pi \times 0.4^2 \approx 0.5027\ \text{m}^2$
@step $h = 0.5 \div 0.5027 \approx 0.9947$
@answer The tank is about $0.99\ \text{m}$ tall.
:::

:::check m8-volume-cylinder 3
:::

:::summary
- Volume is the space inside an object, in cubic units.
- For any right prism, $V = (\text{area of base}) \times \text{height}$. For a cylinder, $V = \pi r^2 h$.
- The base of a triangular prism is the triangle at the end.
- $1\ \text{mL} = 1\ \text{cm}^3$ and $1\ \text{L} = 1000\ \text{cm}^3$.
- To find a missing height, divide the volume by the area of the base.
:::
