A bus travels $180$ km in $2$ hours. A $3$ kg bag of apples costs \$7.20. Your heart beats about $70$ times a minute. Each of these compares two quantities with **different** units.

## Rates

:::definition Rate
A **rate** compares two quantities measured in different units, such as kilometres and hours, or dollars and kilograms. Examples: $180$ km in $2$ h, \$7.20 for $3$ kg, $18$ heartbeats in $15$ s.
:::

A ratio compares quantities with the same unit, like cups of concentrate to cups of water. A rate always keeps its units. "$180$ in $2$" means nothing until you say kilometres and hours.

:::mistake Dropping the units
"$90$" is not a speed. "$90$ km/h" is. Always write the units of a rate.
:::

## Unit rates

:::definition Unit rate
A **unit rate** is a rate for **one** unit of the second quantity: per hour, per kilogram, per minute. A speed in km/h is a unit rate.
:::

To find a unit rate, divide by the number of units.

:::example Bus speed
A bus travels $180$ km in $2$ hours. What is its average speed?
@step Speed is the distance in $1$ hour. Divide the distance by the time: $180 \div 2 = 90$.
@answer The average speed is $90$ km/h.
:::

Sometimes it is easier to scale up to one unit.

:::example Heart rate
After warming up for gym class, Maya counts $18$ heartbeats in $15$ seconds. What is her heart rate in beats per minute?
@step One minute is $60$ seconds, and $60 \div 15 = 4$. So a minute is $4$ groups of $15$ seconds.
@step In a minute, there are $4$ times as many beats: $18 \times 4 = 72$.
@answer Maya’s heart rate is $72$ beats per minute.
:::

## Unit prices

:::definition Unit price
A **unit price** is the cost of one unit of an item, such as the price per kilogram, per litre or per granola bar.
:::

:::example Price per kilogram
A $2.5$ kg bag of carrots costs \$4.79. What is the price per kilogram, to the nearest cent?
@step Divide the price by the mass: $4.79 \div 2.5 = 1.916$.
@step Round to the nearest cent (two decimal places). The third decimal place is $6$, so round up: $1.916 \approx 1.92$.
@answer The carrots cost about \$1.92 per kilogram.
:::

:::tip Rounding money
To round to the nearest cent, look at the third decimal place. If it is $5$ or more, round the cents up. Otherwise, keep them.
:::

## Finding the better buy

To compare two sizes of the same thing, find the unit price of each. The lower unit price is the **better buy**.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Box of cereal","Price"],"rows":[["750 g","$4.99"],["1.2 kg","$7.49"]]}
:::

:::example Which box of cereal?
Use the prices in the table. Find each unit price per kilogram, to the nearest cent. Which box is the better buy?
@step Use the same unit for both sizes: $750$ g $= 0.75$ kg.
@step Small box: $4.99 \div 0.75 \approx 6.653$, which is about \$6.65 per kilogram.
@step Large box: $7.49 \div 1.2 \approx 6.242$, which is about \$6.24 per kilogram.
@answer The $1.2$ kg box has the lower unit price, so it is the better buy.
:::

:::mistake Comparing different units
Do not compare a price per gram with a price per kilogram. Change both sizes to the same unit before you divide.
:::

:::tip Is it really a better buy?
The bigger size is not always cheaper per unit, so always check. And a bigger size is only a better buy if you will use it all. Food that spoils is not a bargain.
:::

:::check m8-unit-rate 3
:::

:::summary
- A rate compares quantities with different units, like km and h.
- A unit rate is the amount for one unit: divide by the number of units (or scale up to one unit).
- A unit price is the cost of one unit. Round money to the nearest cent.
- To find the better buy, compare unit prices in the same unit. The lower unit price is the better buy.
:::
