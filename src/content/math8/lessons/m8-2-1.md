A hockey team sells $150\%$ as many tickets as last season. A savings account pays $0.5\%$ interest. Percents are not always between 1% and 100%. In this lesson you will make sense of percents that are bigger than the whole or smaller than one hundredth of it.

## Percents greater than 100%

Percent means "out of 100". A full hundred grid is $100\%$, one whole. A percent greater than $100\%$ is **more than one whole**.

The picture shows $150\%$: one full grid ($100\%$) plus half of another grid ($50\%$).

:::example What does 150% mean?
Write $150\%$ as a fraction and as a decimal.
@step $150\% = \frac{150}{100}$
@step $\frac{150}{100} = 1.5$, which is $1\frac{1}{2}$.
@answer $150\% = 1.5 = 1\frac{1}{2}$. It is one and a half times the whole.
:::

## Percents less than 1%

$1\%$ is one small square of a hundred grid. A percent less than $1\%$ is **only part of one square**. For example, $0.5\%$ is half of one square.

:::figure

:::

A percent less than $1\%$ can also be written with a fraction, like $\frac{1}{2}\%$ or $\frac{1}{4}\%$. Since $\frac{1}{2} = 0.5$ and $\frac{1}{4} = 0.25$, we have $\frac{1}{2}\% = 0.5\%$ and $\frac{1}{4}\% = 0.25\%$.

:::mistake 0.5% is not one half
$0.5$ is one half, which is $50\%$. But $0.5\%$ is only half of $1\%$. It is $5$ out of every $1000$.
:::

## Percents as decimals

To change a percent to a decimal, **divide by 100**: move the decimal point 2 places to the left. To change a decimal to a percent, **multiply by 100**.

:::example Percent to decimal
Write $250\%$, $0.4\%$ and $\frac{3}{4}\%$ as decimals.
@step $250 \div 100 = 2.5$, so $250\% = 2.5$.
@step $0.4 \div 100 = 0.004$, so $0.4\% = 0.004$.
@step $\frac{3}{4}\% = 0.75\%$, and $0.75 \div 100 = 0.0075$.
@answer $250\% = 2.5$, $0.4\% = 0.004$ and $\frac{3}{4}\% = 0.0075$.
:::

:::example Decimal to percent
Write $1.08$ and $0.006$ as percents.
@step $1.08 \times 100 = 108$, so $1.08 = 108\%$.
@step $0.006 \times 100 = 0.6$, so $0.006 = 0.6\%$.
@answer $1.08 = 108\%$ and $0.006 = 0.6\%$.
:::

:::tip Check the size
A decimal greater than $1$ is more than $100\%$. A decimal less than $0.01$ is less than $1\%$.
:::

## Percents as fractions

Write the percent over $100$. Clear any decimal, then simplify.

:::example More than 100%
Write $125\%$ as a fraction in lowest terms.
@step $125\% = \frac{125}{100}$
@step Divide the top and the bottom by $25$: $\frac{125}{100} = \frac{5}{4}$
@answer $125\% = \frac{5}{4} = 1\frac{1}{4}$
:::

:::example Less than 1%
Write $0.5\%$ and $\frac{1}{4}\%$ as fractions in lowest terms.
@step $0.5\% = \frac{0.5}{100}$. Multiply the top and the bottom by $10$: $\frac{5}{1000}$. Divide both by $5$: $\frac{1}{200}$.
@step $\frac{1}{4}\%$ means $\frac{1}{4}$ out of $100$: $\frac{1}{4} \div 100 = \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{1}{100} = \frac{1}{400}$.
@answer $0.5\% = \frac{1}{200}$ and $\frac{1}{4}\% = \frac{1}{400}$.
:::

:::summary
- A percent greater than $100\%$ is more than one whole: $150\% = 1.5$.
- A percent less than $1\%$ is part of one hundredth: $0.5\% = 0.005$.
- Percent to decimal: divide by $100$. Decimal to percent: multiply by $100$.
- Percent to fraction: write it over $100$, clear any decimal, then simplify. $\frac{1}{4}\% = \frac{1}{400}$.
:::
