Slide a puzzle piece across the table, turn it around, or flip it over: it is still the same piece. Moving a shape does not change its size or its shape. This lesson uses that idea to find congruent polygons on a grid.

## Transformations make congruent images

:::definition Transformation and image
A **transformation** moves a shape to a new position. The new shape is the **image**. Image vertices are named with a prime mark: the image of $A$ is $A'$ (say "A prime").
:::

- A **translation** slides every point the same distance in the same direction.
- A **rotation** turns the shape about a fixed point.
- A **reflection** flips the shape over a line, like a mirror.

Translations, rotations and reflections do not stretch or shrink a shape. So the image is always **congruent** to the original: every side and every angle keeps its size.

## Which transformation was used?

To tell the three transformations apart, trace the vertices in order ($A \to B \to C \to D$) on the original and on the image.

- **Translation:** the image faces the same way, and every vertex moves the same distance in the same direction.
- **Rotation:** the image is turned, but the vertices still go around in the **same** direction (both clockwise or both counterclockwise).
- **Reflection:** the image is a mirror image, so the vertices go around in the **opposite** direction.

## Finding congruent shapes on a grid

To check whether a shape is congruent to another, imagine turning or flipping it so it lines up. Then compare:

- the length of each side (count grid squares, and look at how steeply each slanted side climbs), and
- the shape of each corner.

If even one vertex is a square out of place, a side or an angle changes, and the shapes are not congruent.

:::tip Trace and test
Trace one shape on tracing paper. Slide, turn or flip the paper to see whether it fits exactly over the other shape.
:::

## Writing a congruence statement

When a transformation maps one polygon onto another, each vertex lands on its partner. Follow every vertex to its image, then write the image vertices in the same order.

:::example Following the vertices
Triangle $ABC$ is reflected onto triangle $PQR$, as shown. Write a congruence statement.
@step $A$ is reflected onto $Q$, $B$ onto $R$, and $C$ onto $P$.
@step Write the image letters in the order $A$, $B$, $C$: $Q$, $R$, $P$.
@answer $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle QRP$.
:::

:::mistake Alphabetical order
$\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR$ looks neat, but it is wrong here: it says $A$ matches $P$. The order of the letters must follow the matching vertices, not the alphabet.
:::

:::summary
- Translations (slides), rotations (turns) and reflections (flips) give images that are congruent to the original.
- Every side and angle of the image equals the matching side and angle of the original.
- Trace the vertices in order: a reflection reverses their direction; a translation or rotation does not.
- Shapes are congruent only if they match exactly after a slide, turn or flip.
- Write a congruence statement by following each vertex to its image, in order.
:::
