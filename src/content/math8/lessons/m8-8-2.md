A cereal box, a can of soup and a chocolate-bar box all start as flat pieces of cardboard. Each flat piece is cut, folded and glued into a 3-D object. In this lesson you will match flat patterns with the objects they make.

## Faces, edges and vertices

:::definition Face, edge and vertex
- A **face** is a flat surface of a 3-D object.
- An **edge** is a line segment where two faces meet.
- A **vertex** is a corner where edges meet. The plural is **vertices**.
:::

:::figure

:::

A **prism** has two matching ends joined by rectangles. A **pyramid** has one base and triangles that meet at a point. Each is named by the shape of its end or base.

:::example A box for a chocolate bar
A chocolate-bar box is a triangular prism. How many faces, edges and vertices does it have?
@step Faces: $2$ triangle ends and $3$ rectangles. $2 + 3 = 5$ faces.
@step Edges: $3$ on each end, and $3$ joining the ends. $3 + 3 + 3 = 9$ edges.
@step Vertices: $3$ on each end. $3 + 3 = 6$ vertices.
@answer The box has $5$ faces, $9$ edges and $6$ vertices.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the hidden parts
Dashed lines in a drawing show edges at the back. Count those too. Count in groups (one end, the other end, the edges joining them) so you do not miss any or count any twice.

## Nets

:::definition Net
A **net** is a flat pattern that folds up into a 3-D object. Each face of the object appears once in its net.
:::

This net has $6$ rectangles in $3$ matching pairs. The strip of four wraps around the sides, and the two flaps fold over to make the top and the bottom. It folds into a **rectangular prism**.

## Nets of a cube

A cube has $6$ equal square faces, so every net of a cube has $6$ squares. But not every pattern of $6$ squares folds into a cube. There are exactly $11$ different nets of a cube.

:::mistake A block of four
In the second pattern, four squares make a $2$ by $2$ block. When you fold it, two squares land on the same face, and one face of the cube is left open. A $2$ by $2$ block never appears in a net of a cube.
:::

In a straight row of three squares, the first and third squares end up **opposite** each other. That helps you find opposite faces.

## Nets of pyramids and cylinders

A square pyramid has $1$ square base and $4$ triangles. In its net, the triangles sit on the four sides of the square and fold up to meet at the top.

:::example The label on a can
A can has a radius of $3\ \text{cm}$ and a height of $10\ \text{cm}$. What are the length and width of the rectangle in its net?
@step The width is the height of the can: $10\ \text{cm}$.
@step The length is the circumference: $2 \times \pi \times 3 \approx 18.8\ \text{cm}$.
@answer The rectangle is about $18.8\ \text{cm}$ long and $10\ \text{cm}$ wide.
:::

:::mistake Both circles on one side
In a cylinder net, one circle goes on each long side of the rectangle. If both circles are on the same side, they fold onto the same end and the other end is left open.
:::

:::summary
- Faces are flat surfaces, edges are where two faces meet, and vertices are corners.
- A prism with an end of $n$ sides has $n + 2$ faces, $3n$ edges and $2n$ vertices. A pyramid with a base of $n$ sides has $n + 1$ faces, $2n$ edges and $n + 1$ vertices.
- A net is a flat pattern that folds into a 3-D object, with each face shown once.
- A cube has $11$ nets. A $2$ by $2$ block of squares never folds into a cube.
- A cylinder's net is two circles and a rectangle whose length is the circumference.
:::
