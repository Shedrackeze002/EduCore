Mei and her brother share a pizza. Mei eats $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of it and her brother eats $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of it. How much of the pizza is gone? To answer questions like this, you add and subtract fractions.

## Fractions with the same denominator

:::definition Numerator and denominator
In $\dfrac{3}{8}$, the **denominator** ($8$) tells how many equal parts the whole is cut into. The **numerator** ($3$) tells how many of those parts you have.
:::

When two fractions have the same denominator, the parts are the same size. Add or subtract the numerators. Keep the denominator.

:::example Painting a fence
Jordan paints $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of a fence on Saturday and $\dfrac{2}{8}$ of it on Sunday. How much of the fence is painted?
@step The denominators are the same, so add the numerators: $3 + 2 = 5$.
@step Keep the denominator: $\dfrac{3}{8} + \dfrac{2}{8} = \dfrac{5}{8}$.
@answer Jordan has painted $\dfrac{5}{8}$ of the fence.
:::

:::mistake Adding the denominators
$\dfrac{3}{8} + \dfrac{2}{8}$ is **not** $\dfrac{5}{16}$. The parts are still eighths. Only the numerators are added.
:::

## Writing fractions in lowest terms

:::definition Lowest terms
A fraction is in **lowest terms** when the numerator and the denominator have no common factor except $1$. For example, $\dfrac{3}{4}$ is in lowest terms, but $\dfrac{6}{8}$ is not, because $2$ divides both $6$ and $8$.
:::

:::example Subtracting tenths
Find $\dfrac{7}{10} - \dfrac{3}{10}$. Write the answer in lowest terms.
@step The denominators are the same. Subtract the numerators: $7 - 3 = 4$, so the difference is $\dfrac{4}{10}$.
@step $2$ divides both $4$ and $10$: $\dfrac{4 \div 2}{10 \div 2} = \dfrac{2}{5}$.
@answer $\dfrac{7}{10} - \dfrac{3}{10} = \dfrac{2}{5}$
:::

:::tip Answers bigger than 1
A sum can be more than $1$. For example, $\dfrac{5}{6} + \dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{9}{6} = \dfrac{3}{2}$. You can leave the answer as $\dfrac{3}{2}$ or write it as the mixed number $1\dfrac{1}{2}$.
:::

## Different denominators

Halves and thirds are different sizes, so you cannot add them straight away. First rewrite both fractions with a **common denominator**.

:::definition Common denominator
A **common denominator** is a number that both denominators divide into. For halves and thirds, $6$ works, because $2 \times 3 = 6$ and $3 \times 2 = 6$.
:::

:::example Pizza for two
Mei eats $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of a pizza and her brother eats $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of it. What fraction of the pizza did they eat?
@step $8$ is a multiple of $4$, so use $8$ as the common denominator.
@step Multiply the top and the bottom by $2$: $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1 \times 2}{4 \times 2} = \dfrac{2}{8}$.
@step Add: $\dfrac{3}{8} + \dfrac{2}{8} = \dfrac{5}{8}$.
@answer They ate $\dfrac{5}{8}$ of the pizza.
:::

:::example Thirds and quarters
Find $\dfrac{2}{3} - \dfrac{1}{4}$.
@step Multiples of $3$: $3, 6, 9, 12$. Multiples of $4$: $4, 8, 12$. Use $12$.
@step $\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{8}{12}$ and $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{3}{12}$.
@step Subtract: $\dfrac{8}{12} - \dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac{5}{12}$.
@answer $\dfrac{2}{3} - \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{5}{12}$
:::

:::mistake Changing only the denominator
$\dfrac{1}{4}$ is **not** equal to $\dfrac{1}{8}$. When you multiply the denominator by $2$, multiply the numerator by $2$ too: $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{2}{8}$.
:::

## The lowest common denominator

Multiplying the two denominators always gives a common denominator, but a smaller one keeps the numbers friendly. The **lowest common denominator** is the smallest number that both denominators divide into. To find it, list multiples of each denominator until they match.

:::example Sixths and eighths
Find $\dfrac{5}{6} + \dfrac{3}{8}$. Write the answer in lowest terms.
@step Multiples of $6$: $6, 12, 18, 24$. Multiples of $8$: $8, 16, 24$. The lowest common denominator is $24$, not $48$.
@step $\dfrac{5}{6} = \dfrac{20}{24}$ and $\dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{9}{24}$.
@step Add: $\dfrac{20}{24} + \dfrac{9}{24} = \dfrac{29}{24}$. The numbers $29$ and $24$ have no common factor except $1$, so this is in lowest terms.
@answer $\dfrac{5}{6} + \dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{29}{24}$, which is $1\dfrac{5}{24}$.
:::

:::example Three fractions
Find $\dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{1}{6} - \dfrac{1}{3}$.
@step $12$ is a multiple of $4$, $6$ and $3$. Use $12$.
@step $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{9}{12}$, $\dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{2}{12}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{4}{12}$.
@step Work left to right: $\dfrac{9}{12} + \dfrac{2}{12} - \dfrac{4}{12} = \dfrac{9 + 2 - 4}{12} = \dfrac{7}{12}$.
@answer $\dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{1}{6} - \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{7}{12}$
:::

:::

:::summary
- Same denominators: add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator.
- Different denominators: rewrite the fractions with a common denominator first. Multiply the numerator and the denominator by the same number.
- The lowest common denominator is the smallest number both denominators divide into. List multiples to find it.
- Write answers in lowest terms: divide the numerator and the denominator by their greatest common factor.
:::
