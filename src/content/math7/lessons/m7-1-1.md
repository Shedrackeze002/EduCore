Can $234$ students be split into teams of $9$ with nobody left out? You could do the long division. Or you could add three digits and know the answer in a few seconds. In this lesson you will learn quick tests that tell you when one number divides evenly into another.

## What divisible means

:::definition Divisible
A number is **divisible** by another number when the division leaves no remainder. $24 \div 6 = 4$ with nothing left over, so $24$ is divisible by $6$.
:::

:::definition Factor
When $24$ is divisible by $6$, we say $6$ is a **factor** of $24$. The factors of $24$ are $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $6$, $8$, $12$ and $24$.
:::

:::definition Divisibility rule
A **divisibility rule** is a quick test that tells you whether a number is divisible by another number, without doing the division.
:::

Here are the rules you will use. Each one looks at only part of the number.

## Rules that use the last digits

Every whole number is made of tens plus a ones digit. Any number of tens is divisible by $2$, $5$ and $10$, so only the **last digit** matters for those three rules.

$100$ is divisible by $4$, so all the hundreds are too. Only the **last two digits** matter for $4$. In the same way, $1000$ is divisible by $8$, so only the **last three digits** matter for $8$.

:::example Is 5316 divisible by 4? By 8?
Use the divisibility rules for $4$ and $8$.
@step For $4$, look at the last two digits: $16$. Since $16 = 4 \times 4$, the number $5316$ is divisible by $4$.
@step For $8$, look at the last three digits: $316$. Since $8 \times 39 = 312$ and $8 \times 40 = 320$, $316$ is not a multiple of $8$.
@answer $5316$ is divisible by $4$ but not by $8$.
:::

:::mistake Looking at only the last digit for 4
$34$ ends in $4$, but $34$ is **not** divisible by $4$ ($4 \times 8 = 32$ and $4 \times 9 = 36$). For $4$, you must look at the last **two** digits.
:::

## Rules that use the digit sum

To test for $3$ or $9$, add the digits. If the **digit sum** is divisible by $3$, so is the number. The same works for $9$.

A number is divisible by $6$ when it passes **both** tests: the test for $2$ and the test for $3$.

:::example Testing 4536
Which of $3$, $6$ and $9$ divide $4536$?
@step Add the digits: $4 + 5 + 3 + 6 = 18$.
@step $18$ is divisible by $3$ and by $9$, so $4536$ is divisible by $3$ and by $9$.
@step The last digit, $6$, is even, so $4536$ is divisible by $2$. It is divisible by both $2$ and $3$, so it is divisible by $6$.
@answer $4536$ is divisible by $3$, $6$ and $9$.
:::

:::example Testing 7341
Is $7341$ divisible by $3$? By $9$? By $6$?
@step Add the digits: $7 + 3 + 4 + 1 = 15$.
@step $15$ is divisible by $3$ but not by $9$.
@step The last digit, $1$, is odd, so $7341$ is not divisible by $2$, and so not by $6$.
@answer $7341$ is divisible by $3$ only.
:::

:::mistake Even is not enough for 6
$28$ is even, but its digit sum is $10$, which is not divisible by $3$. So $28$ is **not** divisible by $6$. Check both rules every time.
:::

:::check m7-divisibility 2
:::

:::example A missing digit
The number $52\square4$ is divisible by $9$. What digit goes in the box?
@step Add the digits you know: $5 + 2 + 4 = 11$.
@step The digit sum must be a multiple of $9$. The next multiple of $9$ after $11$ is $18$.
@step $18 - 11 = 7$. Check: $5274$ has digit sum $18$.
@answer The missing digit is $7$.
:::

## Sorting numbers and sharing equally

A **Carroll diagram** sorts numbers using two rules. Each number goes in exactly one section. This one sorts by $3$ and by $4$.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["","Divisible by 3","Not divisible by 3"],"rows":[["Divisible by 4","312, 1200","128, 2020"],["Not divisible by 4","135, 2001","70, 1001"]]}
:::

For example, $312$ has digit sum $6$ (divisible by $3$), and its last two digits make $12 = 4 \times 3$ (divisible by $4$), so it goes in the top-left section. A **Venn diagram** sorts in a similar way: the numbers in the overlap of two circles are divisible by both numbers.

:::example Teams for a track meet
Can $234$ students be split into teams of $9$ with nobody left out? If so, how many teams are there?
@step Nobody is left out when $234$ is divisible by $9$.
@step Add the digits: $2 + 3 + 4 = 9$, which is divisible by $9$.
@step Divide: $234 \div 9 = 26$.
@answer Yes. There are $26$ teams of $9$.
:::

## Why we cannot divide by 0

Division undoes multiplication. $12 \div 3 = 4$ because $4 \times 3 = 12$.

So $12 \div 0 = \ ?$ would mean $? \times 0 = 12$. But any number times $0$ is $0$. No number works, so $12 \div 0$ has no answer. We say we **cannot divide by $0$**.

Dividing **into** $0$ is fine: $0 \div 5 = 0$, because $0 \times 5 = 0$.

:::mistake Saying 12 ÷ 0 = 0
Check it with multiplication: $0 \times 0 = 0$, not $12$. So $0$ is not the answer. There is no answer.
:::

:::check m7-divisibility-apply 3
:::

:::summary
- A number is divisible by another when there is no remainder.
- Last digit: $2$ (even), $5$ ($0$ or $5$), $10$ ($0$).
- Last two digits for $4$; last three digits for $8$.
- Digit sum for $3$ and $9$. For $6$, check $2$ and $3$.
- A Carroll or Venn diagram sorts numbers by two rules.
- We cannot divide by $0$, because no number times $0$ gives a non-zero number.
:::
