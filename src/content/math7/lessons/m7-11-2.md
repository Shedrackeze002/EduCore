In many games you do two things at once: flip a coin and spin a spinner, or roll two dice. Before you can find a probability, you need to know every outcome that can happen. That list is called the sample space.

## What is a sample space?

:::definition Sample space
The **sample space** is the list of all possible outcomes of an experiment. For one coin, the sample space is H, T (heads, tails). For one die, it is $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$.
:::

:::definition Independent events
Two events are **independent** when the result of one does not change the probabilities of the other. Flipping a coin and rolling a die are independent: the coin does not care what the die shows.
:::

When you do two independent things, each outcome of the sample space is a pair: one result for the first object and one result for the second. We write H3 for heads and a $3$, or $(2, 5)$ for a $2$ on the red die and a $5$ on the blue die.

:::example One spinner
A spinner has $4$ equal sections coloured red, blue, green and yellow. What is its sample space?
@step Each colour is one possible outcome.
@answer The sample space is red, blue, green, yellow: $4$ outcomes.
:::

## Using a table

A table is a quick way to show the sample space of two events. Put the outcomes of the first event down the side and the outcomes of the second event across the top. Each cell is one outcome.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["","1","2","3","4","5","6"],"rows":[["H","H1","H2","H3","H4","H5","H6"],["T","T1","T2","T3","T4","T5","T6"]]}
:::

:::example A coin and a die
Ella flips a coin and rolls a six-sided die. How many outcomes are in the sample space? List the outcomes that have tails.
@step The table has $2$ rows (H, T) and $6$ columns ($1$ to $6$).
@step Number of outcomes: $2 \times 6 = 12$
@step The tails row: T1, T2, T3, T4, T5, T6
@answer There are $12$ outcomes. Six of them have tails.
:::

:::tip Multiply to count
Number of outcomes $=$ (outcomes of the first event) $\times$ (outcomes of the second event). A coin and a die: $2 \times 6 = 12$.
:::

## Using a tree diagram

:::definition Tree diagram
A **tree diagram** shows the outcomes of the first event as branches. From each branch, new branches show the outcomes of the second event. Follow a path from left to right to read one outcome.
:::

:::example A coin and a colour spinner
Malik flips a coin and spins a spinner with $3$ equal sections: red (R), blue (B) and green (G). Use the tree diagram above to list the sample space.
@step The coin gives two first branches: H and T.
@step Each coin branch splits into three spinner branches: R, B and G.
@step Read each path: HR, HB, HG, TR, TB, TG.
@answer The sample space has $2 \times 3 = 6$ outcomes: HR, HB, HG, TR, TB, TG.
:::

:::mistake HT and TH are different
When you flip a loonie and a quarter, “heads on the loonie, tails on the quarter” (HT) and “tails on the loonie, heads on the quarter” (TH) are two different outcomes. The sample space is HH, HT, TH, TT: $4$ outcomes, not $3$.
:::

:::

## Counting outcomes that fit

Once you have the sample space, you can count the outcomes that fit a condition. For two dice, write the **sum** in each cell of the table.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Red ↓ Blue →","1","2","3","4","5","6"],"rows":[["1","2","3","4","5","6","7"],["2","3","4","5","6","7","8"],["3","4","5","6","7","8","9"],["4","5","6","7","8","9","10"],["5","6","7","8","9","10","11"],["6","7","8","9","10","11","12"]]}
:::

:::example Sums of two dice
Hamid rolls a red die and a blue die and adds the numbers. How many outcomes are in the sample space, and how many have a sum of $8$?
@step Sample space: $6 \times 6 = 36$ outcomes.
@step Find every $8$ in the table: $(2, 6)$, $(3, 5)$, $(4, 4)$, $(5, 3)$, $(6, 2)$.
@answer There are $36$ outcomes, and $5$ of them have a sum of $8$.
:::

:::mistake Add or multiply?
A coin has $2$ outcomes and a spinner has $4$. The sample space has $2 \times 4 = 8$ outcomes, not $2 + 4 = 6$. Each coin result goes with **every** spinner result.
:::

:::check m7-sample-space 3
:::

:::summary
- The sample space is the list of all possible outcomes.
- Two events are independent when one does not change the probabilities of the other.
- Show the sample space of two events with a table or a tree diagram. Each cell or path is one outcome.
- Number of outcomes $=$ outcomes of the first event $\times$ outcomes of the second event.
- HT and TH are different outcomes.
:::
