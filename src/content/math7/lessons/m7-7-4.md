How much ground does a lawn sprinkler water? How much dough is in a pizza? Both questions ask for the area of a circle, and the formula comes from turning a circle into a shape you already know.

## From a circle to a parallelogram

Cut a circle into equal sectors, like pizza slices. Line them up so they point up and down, one after another.

The new shape is almost a parallelogram. The more sectors you cut, the closer it gets.

- Its height is the radius, $r$.
- Its base is half of the circumference: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2\pi r = \pi r$.

So the area is base $\times$ height $= \pi r \times r$:

$$A = \pi r^2$$

:::definition r squared
$r^2$ (read "r squared") means $r \times r$. A radius of $6\ \text{cm}$ gives $r^2 = 6 \times 6 = 36$.
:::

## Using A = πr²

:::example A circle from its radius
A circle has a radius of $6\ \text{cm}$. Find its area to the nearest tenth of a square centimetre.
@step $A = \pi r^2 = \pi \times 6^2 = \pi \times 36$
@step $A \approx 113.10$
@answer The area is about $113.1\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::tip Square first
Square the radius first, then multiply by $\pi$. Use the $\pi$ key on your calculator and round only at the end.
:::

:::example A trampoline
A round trampoline has a diameter of $10\ \text{m}$. Find the area of the mat to the nearest tenth of a square metre.
@step The formula needs the radius: $r = 10 \div 2 = 5\ \text{m}$.
@step $A = \pi \times 5^2 = \pi \times 25 \approx 78.54$
@answer The mat has an area of about $78.5\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Putting the diameter into the formula
$\pi \times 10^2 \approx 314.2\ \text{m}^2$ is **four times** too big. Always halve the diameter before you square it.
:::

## Shapes made with circles

Split the shape into parts you know. Add the parts, or subtract a part that is cut out.

:::example A window
A window is a rectangle $80\ \text{cm}$ wide and $100\ \text{cm}$ tall, with a half circle on top. Find the area of the glass to the nearest square centimetre.
@step Rectangle: $80 \times 100 = 8000\ \text{cm}^2$
@step The half circle has a diameter of $80\ \text{cm}$, so $r = 40\ \text{cm}$. Half circle: $\pi \times 40^2 \div 2 \approx 2513.27\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Total: $8000 + 2513.27 \approx 10\,513.27$
@answer The glass has an area of about $10\,513\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::example A deck around a pool
A round pool has a radius of $3\ \text{m}$. A deck $1\ \text{m}$ wide goes all the way around it. Find the area of the deck to the nearest tenth of a square metre.
@step The pool and deck together form a circle with radius $3 + 1 = 4\ \text{m}$: $\pi \times 4^2 \approx 50.265\ \text{m}^2$
@step The pool: $\pi \times 3^2 \approx 28.274\ \text{m}^2$
@step Deck: $50.265 - 28.274 \approx 21.99$
@answer The deck has an area of about $22.0\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::check m7-area-circle 3
:::

:::summary
- A circle can be cut into sectors and rearranged into a near-parallelogram with base $\pi r$ and height $r$.
- The area of a circle is $A = \pi r^2$. Square the radius, then multiply by $\pi$.
- If you are given the diameter, halve it first.
- For shapes made with circles, add the parts or subtract the part that is cut out.
:::
