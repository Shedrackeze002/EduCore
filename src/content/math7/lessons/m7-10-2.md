Ayesha usually walks to school in about 15 minutes. One snowy morning it took her 42 minutes. That one unusual day can change the "typical" walking time a lot, or hardly at all, depending on which measure you use.

## What is an outlier?

:::definition Outlier
An **outlier** is a value that is much greater or much less than the rest of the data.
:::

:::example Spotting the outlier
Ayesha's walking times, in minutes, were $14$, $16$, $15$, $13$, $17$ and $42$. Which value is an outlier?
@step In order: $13,\ 14,\ 15,\ 16,\ 17,\ 42$
@step Most values are between $13$ and $17$. There is a big gap before $42$.
@answer $42$ minutes is an outlier.
:::

:::tip Look for a gap
Put the data in order. An outlier sits far from the others, with a big gap between it and the next value.
:::

## Outliers and the mean

:::example Mean with and without the outlier
Find the mean of Ayesha's walking times with and without the outlier.
@step Without $42$: $14 + 16 + 15 + 13 + 17 = 75$, and $75 \div 5 = 15$
@step With $42$: $75 + 42 = 117$, and $117 \div 6 = 19.5$
@answer The mean goes from $15$ min to $19.5$ min. The outlier pulls the mean up by $4.5$ min.
:::

A very large outlier pulls the mean **up**. A very small outlier pulls the mean **down**. The mean uses every value, so it is the measure an outlier changes the most.

:::mistake Forgetting to change the count
When you remove an outlier, there is one value fewer. Divide by the new number of values.
:::

## Outliers, the median and the mode

Owen recorded his reading time, in minutes, for six days: $25$, $28$, $30$, $30$, $32$, $35$. On the seventh day he read for only $2$ minutes.

:::example Comparing all three
Find the mean, median and mode with and without the $2$.
@step Without: mean $= 180 \div 6 = 30$, median $= (30 + 30) \div 2 = 30$, mode $= 30$.
@step With: in order $2,\ 25,\ 28,\ 30,\ 30,\ 32,\ 35$. Mean $= 182 \div 7 = 26$, median $= 30$ (the 4th value), mode $= 30$.
@answer The mean drops by $4$ minutes. The median and the mode do not change.
:::

| Measure | Effect of an outlier |
|---|---|
| mean | changes the most, pulled toward the outlier |
| median | changes a little, or not at all |
| mode | usually does not change (unless the outlier repeats a value) |

:::check m7-outliers 2
:::

## Should you keep the outlier?

If an outlier is a mistake, like a typing error, fix it or leave it out. If it really happened, keep it in the data, but report the median as the typical value.

:::example One bad game
Olivia's mean score for $5$ bowling games is $120$. In game $6$ she bowls only $66$. What is her new mean?
@step The first $5$ games add to $120 \times 5 = 600$.
@step Add game $6$: $600 + 66 = 666$
@step New mean: $666 \div 6 = 111$
@answer The new mean is $111$. One low game pulled the mean down by $9$.
:::

:::check m7-outliers 3
:::

:::summary
- An outlier is much greater or much less than the rest of the data.
- An outlier pulls the mean toward it. The mean changes the most.
- The median changes a little or not at all. The mode usually does not change.
- When a data set has an outlier, the median usually describes a typical value best.
:::
