Hold a paper cut-out shape up to a mirror and you see it flipped. Spin a pinwheel and its blades turn around the centre. Flips and turns are two more transformations, and on a Cartesian plane they follow simple patterns.

## Reflections in the $y$-axis

:::definition Reflection
A **reflection** flips a shape over a mirror line. Each image point is the same distance from the mirror line as the original point, but on the other side.
:::

:::example Reflecting in the $y$-axis
Reflect triangle $ABC$, with $A(1, 1)$, $B(5, 2)$ and $C(2, 4)$, in the $y$-axis.
@step $A$ is $1$ unit right of the $y$-axis, so $A'$ is $1$ unit left: $A'(-1, 1)$.
@step In the same way, $B'(-5, 2)$ and $C'(-2, 4)$.
@step The $y$-coordinates stay the same. The $x$-coordinates change sign.
@answer $A'(-1, 1)$, $B'(-5, 2)$ and $C'(-2, 4)$.
:::

:::mistake Measure from the mirror line
The image is the same distance from the **mirror line**, not from the origin or from the original point.
:::

## Rotations of $180^\circ$

:::definition Rotation
A **rotation** turns a shape around a fixed point, the **centre of rotation**. In this lesson the centre is the origin. A turn can be **clockwise** (the way clock hands move) or **counterclockwise**.
:::

A $180^\circ$ rotation is a half turn. Clockwise or counterclockwise does not matter. Right becomes left and up becomes down, so **both** coordinates change sign.

:::example A half turn
Rotate triangle $ABC$, with $A(1, 2)$, $B(4, 1)$ and $C(3, 4)$, $180^\circ$ about the origin.
@step Change both signs: $A'(-1, -2)$, $B'(-4, -1)$ and $C'(-3, -4)$.
@answer The image is in Quadrant III, upside down.
:::

Trace the shape and the axes. Hold the origin with your pencil point and turn the paper. When the axes line up again, the shape is in its new position.

## Rotations of $90^\circ$

For a quarter turn, picture the path from the origin to the point. Turn the whole path a quarter turn.

- **Counterclockwise:** right becomes up, up becomes left, left becomes down, down becomes right.
- **Clockwise:** right becomes down, down becomes left, left becomes up, up becomes right.

:::example A quarter turn counterclockwise
Rotate $P(4, 2)$ $90^\circ$ counterclockwise about the origin.
@step $P$ is $4$ right and $2$ up from the origin.
@step Turn each move: $4$ right becomes $4$ up, and $2$ up becomes $2$ left.
@answer $P'$ is $2$ left and $4$ up: $P'(-2, 4)$.

:::example A quarter turn clockwise
Rotate triangle $ABC$, with $A(2, 1)$, $B(5, 1)$ and $C(2, 3)$, $90^\circ$ clockwise about the origin.
@step $A$: $2$ right and $1$ up become $2$ down and $1$ right, so $A'(1, -2)$.
@step $B$: $5$ right and $1$ up become $5$ down and $1$ right, so $B'(1, -5)$.
@step $C$: $2$ right and $3$ up become $2$ down and $3$ right, so $C'(3, -2)$.
@answer $A'(1, -2)$, $B'(1, -5)$ and $C'(3, -2)$.
:::

A turn of $270^\circ$ one way ends in the same place as a turn of $90^\circ$ the other way. So $270^\circ$ counterclockwise is the same as $90^\circ$ clockwise.

:::mistake Clockwise or counterclockwise?
Check the direction before you turn. A quarter turn the wrong way puts the image in the opposite quadrant from where it belongs.
:::

## Which transformation?

Compare the shape with its image. A **reflection** flips the shape: if you go $A \to B \to C$ around one way, the image goes $A' \to B' \to C'$ the other way. A **translation** or **rotation** keeps the same order. A translation keeps the shape facing the same way; a rotation turns it.

:::

:::check m7-rotate 3
:::

:::summary
- A reflection flips a shape over a mirror line. In the $x$-axis, $y$ changes sign. In the $y$-axis, $x$ changes sign.
- A rotation turns a shape around a centre. A $180^\circ$ turn changes both signs.
- For a $90^\circ$ turn, turn each move from the origin a quarter turn in the right direction.
- $270^\circ$ one way is the same as $90^\circ$ the other way.
- Reflections reverse the order of the vertices. Translations and rotations do not.
:::
