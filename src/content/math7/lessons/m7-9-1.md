How do you tell a friend exactly where to meet on a map, or where a ship is hiding in a game of Battleship? You give two numbers. The Cartesian plane uses two numbers to name the location of any point.

## The Cartesian plane

:::definition Cartesian plane
A **Cartesian plane** is a grid made by two number lines that cross at right angles. The horizontal number line is the **$x$-axis**. The vertical number line is the **$y$-axis**. They cross at the **origin**, $(0, 0)$.
:::

The two axes split the plane into four **quadrants**. They are numbered I, II, III and IV, going counterclockwise from the top right.

:::definition Ordered pair
An **ordered pair** $(x, y)$ gives the location of a point. The first number, the **$x$-coordinate**, tells how far right (+) or left (−) of the origin. The second number, the **$y$-coordinate**, tells how far up (+) or down (−).
:::

:::example Plotting a point
Plot $B(-3, 4)$.
@step Start at the origin.
@step The $x$-coordinate is $-3$: move $3$ units **left** along the $x$-axis.
@step The $y$-coordinate is $4$: then move $4$ units **up**.
@answer Draw a dot there and label it $B$.
:::

:::mistake Across first, then up or down
Always move along the $x$-axis first, then up or down. The points $(-3, 4)$ and $(4, -3)$ are in different places.
:::

## Reading coordinates

To read a point, go back to the origin along the grid lines. Count how far across, then how far up or down.

:::figure

:::

:::example Reading points
Find the coordinates of points $C$ and $E$.
@step $C$ is $2$ units left of the origin and $3$ units down. So $C$ is $(-2, -3)$.
@step $E$ is on the $y$-axis. It does not move left or right, so $x = 0$. It is $2$ units down.
@answer $C(-2, -3)$ and $E(0, -2)$.
:::

:::tip Points on an axis
A point on the $x$-axis has $y = 0$, like $(4, 0)$. A point on the $y$-axis has $x = 0$, like $(0, -2)$. Points on an axis are not in any quadrant.
:::

## Quadrants and signs

The signs of the coordinates tell you the quadrant without drawing anything.

| Quadrant | $x$ | $y$ | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | $+$ | $+$ | $(3, 5)$ |
| II | $-$ | $+$ | $(-7, 2)$ |
| III | $-$ | $-$ | $(-1, -6)$ |
| IV | $+$ | $-$ | $(5, -8)$ |

:::example Naming the location
Where is each point? (a) $(-7, 2)$ (b) $(5, -8)$ (c) $(0, 6)$
@step (a) $x$ is negative (left) and $y$ is positive (up): Quadrant II.
@step (b) $x$ is positive (right) and $y$ is negative (down): Quadrant IV.
@step (c) $x = 0$, so the point is on the $y$-axis, $6$ units up.
@answer (a) Quadrant II (b) Quadrant IV (c) on the $y$-axis
:::

## Distances and shapes on the plane

When two points have the same $y$-coordinate, they are on a horizontal line. Count the units between them, crossing the $y$-axis if you need to.

:::figure

:::

:::example Length across an axis
How long is side $AB$, from $A(-3, 2)$ to $B(4, 2)$?
@step From $x = -3$ to $x = 0$ is $3$ units.
@step From $x = 0$ to $x = 4$ is $4$ units.
@answer $AB = 3 + 4 = 7$ units.
:::

:::example The missing corner
Rectangle $ABCD$ has $A(-3, 2)$, $B(4, 2)$ and $C(4, -1)$. Find $D$, then the perimeter.
@step $D$ is below $A$, so it has the same $x$-coordinate as $A$: $x = -3$.
@step $D$ is across from $C$, so it has the same $y$-coordinate as $C$: $y = -1$.
@step The length is $7$ units and the width is $2 - (-1) = 3$ units. Perimeter $= 2 \times (7 + 3) = 20$.
@answer $D(-3, -1)$, and the perimeter is $20$ units.
:::

:::mistake Subtracting across the axis
From $-3$ to $4$ is not $4 - 3 = 1$ unit. Count the units on both sides of the origin: $3 + 4 = 7$.
:::

:::check m7-plot-points 3
:::

:::summary
- The $x$-axis and $y$-axis cross at the origin, $(0, 0)$, and make four quadrants.
- In $(x, y)$, move across first ($x$: right +, left −), then up or down ($y$: up +, down −).
- The signs tell the quadrant: I $(+, +)$, II $(-, +)$, III $(-, -)$, IV $(+, -)$.
- A point with a $0$ coordinate is on an axis, not in a quadrant.
- To find a distance across an axis, add the units on each side of $0$.
:::
