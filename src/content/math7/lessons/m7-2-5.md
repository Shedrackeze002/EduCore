"$25\%$ off!" "Plus tax." "Add a $15\%$ tip." Percents are everywhere when you shop or eat out. In this lesson you will solve problems with discounts, sales tax, tips and test scores. Money answers are rounded to the nearest cent.

## Discounts

:::definition Discount
A **discount** is the amount taken off a price. The **sale price** is the price after the discount: sale price = price − discount.
:::

:::example A hoodie on sale
A hoodie costs \$59.99. It is on sale for $25\%$ off. What is the sale price, to the nearest cent?
@step Estimate: $25\%$ is one quarter. A quarter of \$60 is \$15, so the sale price is about \$45.
@step Discount: $0.25 \times 59.99 = 14.9975$, which rounds to \$15.00.
@step Sale price: $59.99 - 15.00 = 44.99$
@answer The sale price is \$44.99.
:::

:::tip Another way
With $25\%$ off, you pay $100\% - 25\% = 75\%$ of the price: $0.75 \times 59.99 = 44.9925$, which also rounds to \$44.99.
:::

:::mistake The discount is not the price you pay
The discount (\$15.00) is the amount you **save**. Subtract it from the price to get what you pay.
:::

## Sales tax and tips

:::definition Sales tax
A **sales tax** is a percent of the price that is **added** at the checkout. Each question tells you the tax rate to use.
:::

:::example Sales tax
A basketball costs \$36.80. There is a $5\%$ sales tax. What is the total cost?
@step Estimate: $10\%$ of \$37 is about \$3.70, so $5\%$ is about \$1.85.
@step Tax: $0.05 \times 36.80 = 1.84$
@step Total: $36.80 + 1.84 = 38.64$
@answer The total cost is \$38.64.
:::

A **tip** works the same way: it is a percent of the bill that you add on.

:::example A tip with mental math
The bill at a restaurant is \$46.80. Hamid's family leaves a $15\%$ tip. How much is the tip, and what is the total?
@step $10\%$ of \$46.80 is \$4.68. $5\%$ is half of that, \$2.34.
@step Tip: $4.68 + 2.34 = 7.02$
@step Total: $46.80 + 7.02 = 53.82$
@answer The tip is \$7.02 and the total is \$53.82.
:::

:::mistake Subtracting the tax
Tax and tips are **added** to the price. Discounts are **subtracted**. Ask yourself: should the total be more or less than the price?
:::

## Test scores

A test score as a percent is the marks you earned divided by the total marks, times $100$.

:::example A science test
Aiyana got $34$ out of $40$ on a science test. What is her score as a percent?
@step Estimate: $34$ out of $40$ is more than $\frac{3}{4}$, so the score is more than $75\%$.
@step Divide: $34 \div 40 = 0.85$
@step Multiply by $100$: $0.85 \times 100 = 85$
@answer Aiyana scored $85\%$.
:::

## Problems with two percents

Some problems need two steps. Round money to the nearest cent after each step, just like a store does.

:::example Discount, then tax
A winter jacket costs \$89.99. It is $30\%$ off, and then a $5\%$ sales tax is added to the sale price. What is the total, to the nearest cent?
@step Estimate: $30\%$ off \$90 is \$63. Add a little tax: a bit more than \$63.
@step Discount: $0.30 \times 89.99 = 26.997$, which rounds to \$27.00.
@step Sale price: $89.99 - 27.00 = 62.99$
@step Tax: $0.05 \times 62.99 = 3.1495$, which rounds to \$3.15.
@step Total: $62.99 + 3.15 = 66.14$
@answer The jacket costs \$66.14 in all.
:::

:::summary
- Discount: find the percent of the price, then subtract. Sale price = price − discount.
- Sales tax and tips: find the percent, then add.
- Test score: marks earned ÷ total marks, then multiply by $100$.
- Estimate first with $10\%$, $25\%$ or $50\%$, and round money to the nearest cent.
:::
