At a track meet, Kiona jumps $2\frac{3}{8}$ m and Jun jumps $2.4$ m. Who jumped farther? The numbers are written in different ways, so it is hard to tell at a glance. In this lesson you will compare and order fractions, decimals and whole numbers using benchmarks, place value and equivalent forms.

## Benchmarks

:::definition Benchmark
A **benchmark** is a friendly number you compare other numbers with. For numbers between $0$ and $1$, the benchmarks $0$, $\frac{1}{2}$ and $1$ are the most useful.
:::

A fraction is less than $\frac{1}{2}$ when its numerator is less than half of its denominator. It is more than $\frac{1}{2}$ when its numerator is more than half.

:::figure
{"type":"numberLine","min":0,"max":1,"step":0.25,"points":[{"value":0.375,"label":"3/8"},{"value":0.5,"label":"1/2"},{"value":0.5714,"label":"4/7"}]}
:::

:::example Compare with one half
Which is greater, $\frac{3}{8}$ or $\frac{4}{7}$?
@step Half of $8$ is $4$, and $3 < 4$, so $\frac{3}{8} < \frac{1}{2}$.
@step Half of $7$ is $3.5$, and $4 > 3.5$, so $\frac{4}{7} > \frac{1}{2}$.
@answer $\frac{3}{8} < \frac{4}{7}$
:::

:::example Compare with one
Which is greater, $\frac{7}{8}$ or $\frac{9}{10}$?
@step $\frac{7}{8}$ is $\frac{1}{8}$ less than $1$. $\frac{9}{10}$ is $\frac{1}{10}$ less than $1$.
@step $\frac{1}{10}$ is a smaller piece than $\frac{1}{8}$, so $\frac{9}{10}$ is closer to $1$.
@answer $\frac{7}{8} < \frac{9}{10}$
:::

## Place value

To compare decimals, give them the same number of decimal places by adding zeros at the end. Then compare the digits from left to right. The first place where they differ decides.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Number","Ones","Tenths","Hundredths","Thousandths"],"rows":[["0.470","0","4","7","0"],["0.407","0","4","0","7"]]}
:::

:::example Two close decimals
Compare $0.47$ and $0.407$.
@step Write $0.47$ as $0.470$.
@step The ones and the tenths are the same. In the hundredths place, $7 > 0$.
@answer $0.47 > 0.407$
:::

:::mistake More digits does not mean bigger
$0.407$ has more digits than $0.47$, but it is smaller. Line up the place values before you compare.
:::

## Fractions and decimals together

When one number is a fraction and the other is a decimal, write them in the same form. Changing the fraction to a decimal usually works best.

:::example A fraction and a decimal
Compare $\frac{5}{8}$ and $0.62$.
@step $\frac{5}{8} = 5 \div 8 = 0.625$
@step Compare $0.625$ and $0.620$. In the thousandths place, $5 > 0$.
@answer $\frac{5}{8} > 0.62$
:::

:::example A repeating decimal
Compare $\frac{2}{3}$ and $0.67$.
@step $\frac{2}{3} = 0.\overline{6} = 0.6666\ldots$
@step Compare $0.666\ldots$ and $0.670$. In the hundredths place, $6 < 7$.
@answer $\frac{2}{3} < 0.67$
:::

:::example Who jumped farther?
Kiona jumps $2\frac{3}{8}$ m and Jun jumps $2.4$ m. Who jumped farther?
@step $2\frac{3}{8} = 2 + 0.375 = 2.375$
@step Compare $2.375$ and $2.400$. In the tenths place, $3 < 4$.
@answer $2.375 < 2.4$, so Jun jumped farther.
:::

## Ordering a set of numbers

To put several numbers in order:

1. Write every number as a decimal.
2. Compare the whole-number parts first.
3. Then compare the decimal parts place by place.
4. Write the numbers in order, in their **original** form.

:::example Five numbers
Order from least to greatest: $1\frac{7}{8}$, $2$, $\frac{9}{4}$, $2.15$, $2.205$
@step $1\frac{7}{8} = 1.875$ and $\frac{9}{4} = 2\frac{1}{4} = 2.25$
@step $1.875$ is the only number less than $2$, so it comes first. Then comes $2 = 2.000$.
@step Compare the rest: $2.150 < 2.205 < 2.250$
@answer $1\frac{7}{8}$, $2$, $2.15$, $2.205$, $\frac{9}{4}$
:::

:::summary
- Benchmarks: compare fractions with $0$, $\frac{1}{2}$ and $1$.
- Decimals: add zeros so they have the same number of places, then compare from left to right.
- A fraction and a decimal: change the fraction to a decimal first.
- To order a set, write every number as a decimal, then order them and give the answer in the original forms.
:::
