A new bench is going into a park between two tall spruce trees. Where should it go so that it is exactly the same distance from each tree? A perpendicular bisector answers that question. In this lesson you will also split angles exactly in half.

## Two kinds of bisectors

:::definition Bisect and midpoint
To **bisect** something is to cut it into two equal parts. The **midpoint** of a segment is the point exactly halfway between its endpoints.
:::

:::definition Perpendicular bisector
The **perpendicular bisector** of segment $AB$ is a line that crosses $AB$ at its midpoint **and** makes a right angle with it.
:::

:::definition Angle bisector
An **angle bisector** is a ray that splits an angle into two equal angles.

:::example Spotting a bisector
In the second drawing, ray $BD$ makes a $42^\circ$ angle with each arm. Is $BD$ the bisector of $\angle ABC$? How big is $\angle ABC$?
@step The two angles, $\angle ABD$ and $\angle DBC$, are both $42^\circ$, so they are equal.
@step The whole angle is $42^\circ + 42^\circ = 84^\circ$.
@answer Yes, $BD$ bisects $\angle ABC$, and $\angle ABC = 84^\circ$.
:::

:::mistake Both conditions
A line through the midpoint at a slant is not a perpendicular bisector. A perpendicular line that misses the midpoint is not one either. It needs **both**: the midpoint and a $90^\circ$ angle.
:::

## Midpoints on a grid

On a grid, find the midpoint by going halfway across and halfway up or down.

:::figure

:::

:::example Finding a midpoint
Find the midpoint $M$ of segment $AB$, with $A(2, 3)$ and $B(8, 7)$.
@step From $A$ to $B$: $6$ right and $4$ up.
@step Half of that move: $3$ right and $2$ up.
@step From $A$: $x = 2 + 3 = 5$ and $y = 3 + 2 = 5$.
@answer The midpoint is $M(5, 5)$.
:::

:::tip Working backward
If you know $A$ and the midpoint $M$, make the move from $A$ to $M$ once more, starting at $M$. You land on $B$.
:::

## Constructing bisectors

**A perpendicular bisector**, with a compass and a ruler:

1. Open the compass to more than half the length of $AB$.
2. Place the compass point on $A$. Draw an arc above $AB$ and an arc below $AB$.
3. Keep the same opening. Place the compass point on $B$ and draw arcs that cross the first two arcs. Label the crossings $C$ and $D$.
4. Use the ruler to join $C$ and $D$. Line segment $CD$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$.

**An angle bisector**, with a compass and a ruler:

1. Place the compass point on the vertex $B$. Draw an arc that crosses both arms. Label the crossings $D$ and $E$.
2. Place the compass point on $D$. Draw an arc inside the angle.
3. Keep the same opening. Place the compass point on $E$ and draw an arc that crosses the last arc. Label the crossing $F$.
4. Use the ruler to draw ray $BF$. It bisects $\angle ABC$.

There are other methods too. **Fold** tracing paper so that $A$ lands on $B$ (or so that one arm of the angle lies on the other), and the crease is the bisector. Or **measure**: find half the length with a ruler, or half the angle with a protractor.

:::tip Verify your construction
Measure $AM$ and $MB$ with a ruler, and the angle at $M$ with a protractor: you should get two equal lengths and $90^\circ$. For an angle bisector, measure both new angles: they should be equal.
:::

## Using equal lengths and equal angles

Every point on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$ is the **same distance** from $A$ as from $B$. That is why the bench goes on the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining the two trees.

:::example Equal distances
Line $\ell$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$. Point $P$ is on $\ell$, and $PA = 5$ cm. Find $PB$.
@step $P$ is on the perpendicular bisector, so it is the same distance from $A$ and from $B$.
@answer $PB = 5$ cm.
:::

:::example Perimeter of a triangle
Line $\ell$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$ at $M$. Point $P$ is on $\ell$. $PA = 6.5$ cm and $AM = 4$ cm. Find the perimeter of triangle $PAB$.
@step $P$ is on the perpendicular bisector, so $PB = PA = 6.5$ cm.
@step $M$ is the midpoint, so $AB = 2 \times 4 = 8$ cm.
@step Perimeter $= 6.5 + 6.5 + 8 = 21$
@answer The perimeter is $21$ cm.
:::

:::mistake Half, not the whole
An angle bisector makes two angles that are each **half** of the whole angle. A perpendicular bisector cuts the segment into two halves. Check whether the question asks for the whole or for one half.
:::

:::example Bisecting twice
$\angle ABC = 120^\circ$. Ray $BD$ bisects $\angle ABC$. Then ray $BE$ bisects $\angle DBC$. Find $\angle ABE$.
@step $\angle ABD = \angle DBC = 120^\circ \div 2 = 60^\circ$
@step $\angle DBE = 60^\circ \div 2 = 30^\circ$
@step $\angle ABE = 60^\circ + 30^\circ = 90^\circ$
@answer $\angle ABE = 90^\circ$.
:::

:::example Using an equation
Point $P$ is on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$. $PA = (3x + 2)$ cm and $PB = 17$ cm. Find $x$.
@step $PA = PB$, so $3x + 2 = 17$.
@step Subtract $2$ from both sides: $3x = 15$.
@step Divide both sides by $3$: $x = 5$.
@answer $x = 5$. Check: $3(5) + 2 = 17$.
:::

:::summary
- To bisect is to cut into two equal parts. The midpoint is halfway along a segment.
- A perpendicular bisector passes through the midpoint at $90^\circ$. Every point on it is the same distance from both endpoints.
- An angle bisector splits an angle into two equal angles, each half of the whole.
- Construct bisectors with a compass and ruler, by folding, or by measuring, and verify by measuring.
:::
