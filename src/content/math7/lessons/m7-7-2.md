Patio stones, floor tiles and parking stalls are often shaped like slanted rectangles. You can find their area with one multiplication, as long as you choose the right measurement for the height.

## From a rectangle to a parallelogram

:::definition Parallelogram
A **parallelogram** is a four-sided shape with two pairs of parallel sides. Opposite sides are equal in length. A rectangle is a special parallelogram with square corners.
:::

Imagine a parallelogram cut out of paper. Cut a right triangle off one end, along the dashed height, and slide it to the other end. The pieces make a rectangle.

- The rectangle’s length is the parallelogram’s **base**.
- The rectangle’s width is the parallelogram’s **height**.
- Nothing was added or removed, so the area did not change.

$$A = bh$$

## Base and height

:::definition Base and height
The **base** ($b$) is any side you choose to measure along. The **height** ($h$) is the distance between the base and the opposite side, measured at a right angle ($90^\circ$) to the base. It is often drawn as a dashed line with a small square at the right angle.
:::

:::example Ignore the slanted side
Find the area of the parallelogram above.
@step The base is $12\ \text{m}$. The dashed height, $8\ \text{m}$, meets the base at a right angle.
@step The slanted side, $10\ \text{m}$, is not the height, so it is not used.
@step $A = bh = 12 \times 8 = 96$
@answer The area is $96\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Using the slanted side
$12 \times 10 = 120\ \text{m}^2$ is too big. The slanted side is always longer than the height. Look for the measurement that makes a right angle with the base.
:::

:::

## When the height is outside

In a very slanted parallelogram, the height may not fit inside the shape. Then the base is extended with a dashed line, and the height is drawn outside. The formula is the same.

:::

:::example A height outside the shape
Find the area of the parallelogram above.
@step The base is $4\ \text{cm}$. The height, measured at a right angle to the extended base, is $6\ \text{cm}$.
@step $A = bh = 4 \times 6 = 24$
@answer The area is $24\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::example Decimal measurements
A patio stone is a parallelogram with a base of $32.5\ \text{cm}$ and a height of $20\ \text{cm}$. Find its area.
@step $A = bh = 32.5 \times 20$
@step $A = 650$
@answer The patio stone has an area of $650\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

## Working backwards and combining shapes

If you know the area, you can find a missing base or height. Substitute what you know into $A = bh$, then divide.

:::example Find the height
A parallelogram has an area of $72\ \text{cm}^2$ and a base of $9\ \text{cm}$. Find its height.
@step $A = bh$, so $72 = 9 \times h$.
@step Divide both sides by $9$: $h = 72 \div 9 = 8$
@step Check: $9 \times 8 = 72$ ✓
@answer The height is $8\ \text{cm}$.
:::

:::tip Take away a hole
A lawn shaped like a parallelogram has a base of $20\ \text{m}$ and a height of $12\ \text{m}$. A rectangular flower bed, $4\ \text{m}$ by $3\ \text{m}$, sits in the middle. The grass covers $20 \times 12 - 4 \times 3 = 240 - 12 = 228\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::check m7-area-parallelogram 3
:::

:::summary
- A parallelogram can be cut and rearranged into a rectangle, so $A = bh$.
- The **height** is measured at a right angle to the base. It may be drawn inside or outside the shape.
- The slanted side is **not** the height.
- To find a missing base or height, substitute into $A = bh$ and divide.
:::
