## Undo in reverse order

In $3x + 5 = 26$, two things happen to $x$: first it is multiplied by 3, then 5 is added. To get $x$ alone, undo them in the **reverse** order: undo the adding first, then the multiplying.

:::example Solve a two-step equation
Solve $3x + 5 = 26$.
@step Subtract $5$ from both sides: $3x + 5 - 5 = 26 - 5$, so $3x = 21$.
@step Divide both sides by $3$: $\dfrac{3x}{3} = \dfrac{21}{3}$, so $x = 7$.
@step Check: $3(7) + 5 = 21 + 5 = 26$ ✓
@answer $x = 7$
:::

:::example The variable on the right
Solve $26 = 4n + 6$.
@step The sides can be swapped: $4n + 6 = 26$.
@step Subtract $6$ from both sides: $4n = 20$.
@step Divide both sides by $4$: $n = 5$.
@step Check: $4(5) + 6 = 26$ ✓
@answer $n = 5$
:::

:::mistake Dividing first
For $3x + 5 = 26$, dividing only $3x$ by 3 gives $x + 5 = 26$, which is not balanced. The $5$ is not multiplied by $x$, so subtract it **before** you divide.
:::

## Number puzzles

:::example Think of a number
Simran thinks of a number, multiplies it by 5, then adds 8. The result is 53. What was her number?
@step Let $n$ be the number. Multiply by 5: $5n$. Add 8: $5n + 8$. So $5n + 8 = 53$.
@step Subtract $8$ from both sides: $5n = 45$.
@step Divide both sides by $5$: $n = 9$.
@step Check: $5 \times 9 + 8 = 45 + 8 = 53$ ✓
@answer Simran’s number was $9$.
:::

## Writing equations for problems

Many problems have an amount that is charged or added **for each** one, and an amount that is added **once**.

- The amount for each one is the **coefficient**.
- The one-time amount is the **constant term**.
- The total goes on the other side of the equals sign.

:::example A phone bill
A phone plan costs \$25 a month plus \$10 for each GB of data. The bill was \$65. How many GB were used?
@step Let $g$ be the number of GB. The equation is $10g + 25 = 65$.
@step Subtract $25$ from both sides: $10g = 40$.
@step Divide both sides by $10$: $g = 4$.
@step Check in the story: 4 GB at \$10 is \$40, and \$40 + \$25 = \$65 ✓
@answer 4 GB of data were used.
:::

:::tip Watch for swapped numbers
$25g + 10 = 65$ uses the same numbers, but it means \$25 for each GB. Read the story and ask: which amount is "for each"?
:::

## Problems that need a plan

Sometimes you must think about the situation before you can write the equation.

:::example Fencing a garden
Leilani used 34 m of fencing to go all the way around a rectangular garden. The garden is 5 m wide. How long is it?
@step The perimeter of a rectangle is $2l + 2w$. Here $2w = 2 \times 5 = 10$, so $2l + 10 = 34$.
@step Subtract $10$ from both sides: $2l = 24$.
@step Divide both sides by $2$: $l = 12$.
@step Check: $2(12) + 2(5) = 24 + 10 = 34$ ✓
@answer The garden is $12\ \text{m}$ long.
:::

:::example Three numbers in a row
The sum of three consecutive whole numbers is 51. What are the numbers?
@step Let $n$ be the smallest. The others are $n + 1$ and $n + 2$. Their sum is $3n + 3$, so $3n + 3 = 51$.
@step Subtract $3$: $3n = 48$. Divide by $3$: $n = 16$.
@step Check: $16 + 17 + 18 = 51$ ✓
@answer The numbers are 16, 17 and 18.
:::

:::

:::summary
- To solve $ax + b = c$, undo the adding first (subtract $b$), then undo the multiplying (divide by $a$).
- Do each step to both sides, then check by substituting.
- In problems, the amount "for each" is the coefficient, and the one-time amount is the constant term.
- Check your answer in the story, not just in the equation.
:::
