Race times, rainfall, masses and prices are often decimals. When you add or subtract them, one small slip with the decimal point can make an answer ten times too big. In this lesson you will estimate first, then add and subtract decimals carefully, and use your estimate to check.

## Estimate first

:::definition Estimate
An **estimate** is a close answer you find with friendly numbers. You can usually do it in your head. It tells you about how big the real answer should be.
:::

To estimate a sum or difference, round each decimal to the nearest whole number. For numbers less than $1$, round to the nearest tenth.

:::example Estimating a sum
Estimate $12.6 + 4.75$.
@step $12.6$ rounds to $13$, and $4.75$ rounds to $5$.
@answer $12.6 + 4.75 \approx 13 + 5 = 18$
:::

## Adding decimals

Line up the **decimal points**, so tenths are under tenths and hundredths are under hundredths. Add zeros at the end so every number has the same number of decimal places. Then add as you would with whole numbers.

$$\begin{array}{r} 12.60 \\ +\ \ 4.75 \\ \hline 17.35 \end{array}$$

:::example Two numbers
Calculate $12.6 + 4.75$.
@step Estimate: $13 + 5 = 18$.
@step Write $12.6$ as $12.60$ and line up the decimal points: $12.60 + 4.75 = 17.35$
@step $17.35$ is close to $18$, so the answer is reasonable.
@answer $12.6 + 4.75 = 17.35$
:::

:::example Packing for a camping trip
A tent has a mass of $2.35$ kg, a sleeping bag $1.8$ kg and a stove $0.475$ kg. What is the total mass?
@step Estimate: $2 + 2 + 0.5 = 4.5$.
@step Give every number three decimal places: $2.350 + 1.800 + 0.475 = 4.625$
@answer The total mass is $4.625$ kg, close to the estimate of $4.5$ kg.
:::

:::mistake Lining up the last digits
If you line up the last digits of $12.6$ and $4.75$, you add $1.26 + 4.75$ by mistake and get $6.01$. The estimate of $18$ shows that is far too small. Always line up the decimal points.
:::

## Subtracting decimals

Subtract the same way: line up the decimal points, fill in zeros, and regroup when a digit on top is smaller than the digit below it.

:::example Subtracting from a whole number
A water jug holds $20$ L. Mateo pours out $7.35$ L. How much is left?
@step Estimate: $20 - 7 = 13$.
@step Write $20$ as $20.00$: $20.00 - 7.35 = 12.65$
@step $12.65$ is close to $13$, so the answer is reasonable.
@answer There are $12.65$ L left.
:::

:::example Race times
Kiona ran the $100$ m in $12.42$ s. Simran ran it in $13.52$ s. How much faster was Kiona?
@step Estimate: $13.5 - 12.4 = 1.1$. The times are close, so round to the nearest tenth.
@step $13.52 - 12.42 = 1.10$
@answer Kiona was $1.1$ s faster.
:::

## Solving problems

Some problems need more than one step. Decide what to find first, and write each step.

:::example Birthday money
Rosa has \$50. She buys a T-shirt for \$17.29, socks for \$5.99 and a book for \$10.19. How much money does she have left?
@step Estimate: $50 - (17 + 6 + 10) = 17$.
@step Total cost: $17.29 + 5.99 + 10.19 = 33.47$
@step Money left: $50.00 - 33.47 = 16.53$
@answer Rosa has \$16.53 left. That is close to the estimate of \$17.
:::

:::tip Use the estimate as a check
If your answer is far from your estimate, look again. The most common slip is a decimal point in the wrong place.
:::

:::

:::summary
- Estimate first by rounding to friendly numbers.
- Line up the decimal points. Add zeros so every number has the same number of decimal places.
- Add or subtract as with whole numbers, regrouping when needed.
- Compare the answer with the estimate to check that it is reasonable.
:::
