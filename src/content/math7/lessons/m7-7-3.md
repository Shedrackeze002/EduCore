Sails, pennants and yield signs are triangles. Two copies of any triangle fit together to make a parallelogram, and that tells us how to find the area of a triangle.

## Half of a parallelogram

Take two copies of the same triangle. Turn one copy around and fit it against the other. Together they make a parallelogram with the **same base and height** as the triangle. So the triangle is exactly half of that parallelogram.

$$A = bh \div 2 \qquad \left(\text{or } A = \tfrac{1}{2}bh\right)$$

:::example A first triangle
Find the area of the triangle above.
@step $A = bh \div 2 = 10 \times 6 \div 2$
@step $A = 60 \div 2 = 30$
@answer The area is $30\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::tip Right triangles
In a right triangle, the two sides that meet at the right angle are a base and a height. A right triangle with legs of $9\ \text{cm}$ and $12\ \text{cm}$ has an area of $9 \times 12 \div 2 = 54\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

## Height outside the triangle

The **height** of a triangle goes from the top vertex straight down to the line of the base, at a right angle. In an obtuse triangle (one angle bigger than $90^\circ$), the height can land outside the triangle. Then the base is extended with a dashed line.

:::example An obtuse triangle
Find the area of the triangle above.
@step The base is $4\ \text{cm}$. The height is $8\ \text{cm}$: it meets the extended base at a right angle.
@step The side of $10\ \text{cm}$ is slanted, so it is not used.
@step $A = bh \div 2 = 4 \times 8 \div 2 = 16$
@answer The area is $16\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Using a slanted side as the height
$4 \times 10 \div 2 = 20\ \text{cm}^2$ is wrong. A slanted side is longer than the height. Use the length that makes a right angle with the base, even when it is outside the triangle.
:::

:::

## Composite shapes and missing heights

A **composite shape** is made of simpler shapes. Find the area of each part, then add.

:::example The front of a shed
The front wall of a shed is a rectangle with a triangle on top, as shown. Find its area.
@step Rectangle: $6 \times 3 = 18\ \text{m}^2$
@step Triangle: its base is the top of the rectangle, $6\ \text{m}$, and its height is $2\ \text{m}$. $A = 6 \times 2 \div 2 = 6\ \text{m}^2$
@step Total: $18 + 6 = 24$
@answer The wall has an area of $24\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::example Find a missing height
A triangular sail has an area of $15\ \text{m}^2$ and a base of $5\ \text{m}$. How tall is it?
@step $A = bh \div 2$, so $bh = 2A = 2 \times 15 = 30$.
@step $5 \times h = 30$, so $h = 30 \div 5 = 6$.
@step Check: $5 \times 6 \div 2 = 15$ ✓
@answer The sail is $6\ \text{m}$ tall.
:::

:::check m7-area-triangle 3
:::

:::summary
- A triangle is half of a parallelogram with the same base and height: $A = bh \div 2$.
- The height meets the base (or the base extended) at a right angle. It can be inside, on a side, or outside the triangle.
- A slanted side is not the height.
- For composite shapes, split the shape, find each area, and add (or subtract a hole).
- To find a missing height, double the area, then divide by the base.
:::
