How much do $6$ packs of hockey tape cost at \$3.45 each? How much does each person pay when $4$ friends share a \$61.80 pizza bill? In this lesson you will multiply and divide decimals, by hand for one-digit numbers and with a calculator for bigger ones. An estimate first always tells you where the decimal point belongs.

## Multiplying by a one-digit number

An area model shows what $4 \times 2.3$ means: $4$ groups of $2$ plus $4$ groups of $0.3$.

:::figure
{"type":"areaModel","top":["2","0.3"],"side":["4"],"cells":[["8","1.2"]]}
:::

So $4 \times 2.3 = 8 + 1.2 = 9.2$. A faster way:

1. Estimate.
2. Multiply as if there were no decimal point.
3. Give the product the same number of decimal places as the decimal you started with.

:::example Hockey tape
$6$ packs of hockey tape cost \$3.45 each. What is the total cost?
@step Estimate: $3.5 \times 6 = 21$.
@step Multiply without the decimal point: $345 \times 6 = 2070$.
@step $3.45$ has $2$ decimal places, so the product does too: $20.70$.
@answer The total is \$20.70, close to the estimate of \$21.
:::

## Multiplying with a calculator

For multipliers with two or more digits, use a calculator. Still estimate first, so you can catch a slip on the keys.

:::example Area of a deck
A deck is $4.25$ m long and $3.6$ m wide. What is its area?
@step Estimate: $4 \times 4 = 16$.
@step Calculator: $4.25 \times 3.6 = 15.3$
@answer The area is $15.3\ \text{m}^2$, close to the estimate of $16\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::example Filling up with gas
Gas costs \$1.419 per litre. How much do $34.6$ L cost, to the nearest cent?
@step Estimate: $35 \times 1.5 = 52.5$.
@step Calculator: $34.6 \times 1.419 = 49.0974$
@step Round to the nearest cent ($2$ decimal places): $49.10$.
@answer The gas costs \$49.10.
:::

:::mistake A decimal point in the wrong place
If you get $1.53$ or $153$ for $4.25 \times 3.6$, the estimate of $16$ shows the decimal point is in the wrong place. The digits are right, but the size is not.
:::

## Dividing by a one-digit number

Divide as you would with whole numbers. The decimal point in the answer goes directly above the decimal point in the number you are dividing. Another way to think about it: $14.4$ is $144$ tenths.

:::example Cutting a rope
A rope $14.4$ m long is cut into $6$ equal pieces. How long is each piece?
@step Estimate with numbers that divide easily: $12 \div 6 = 2$.
@step $14.4$ is $144$ tenths, and $144 \div 6 = 24$. So the answer is $24$ tenths, or $2.4$.
@step Check by multiplying: $2.4 \times 6 = 14.4$.
@answer Each piece is $2.4$ m long.
:::

:::example Add a zero
Calculate $7.5 \div 2$.
@step Estimate: $8 \div 2 = 4$.
@step $2$ does not divide evenly into $75$ tenths, so write $7.5$ as $7.50$, which is $750$ hundredths.
@step $750 \div 2 = 375$, so the answer is $375$ hundredths.
@answer $7.5 \div 2 = 3.75$
:::

## Dividing with a calculator

For divisors with more than one digit, or with decimals, use a calculator. Estimate first.

:::example Price per kilogram
A $2.4$ kg bag of apples costs \$6.48. What is the price per kilogram?
@step Price per kilogram = price $\div$ mass.
@step Estimate: $6 \div 2 = 3$.
@step Calculator: $6.48 \div 2.4 = 2.7$
@answer The apples cost \$2.70 per kilogram.
:::

:::example Full bottles
How many $1.5$ L bottles can be filled completely from $11.4$ L of lemonade?
@step Estimate: $12 \div 1.5 = 8$.
@step Calculator: $11.4 \div 1.5 = 7.6$
@step The $0.6$ is not a full bottle, so round **down**.
@answer They can fill $7$ bottles.
:::

:::tip Dividing by a number less than 1
$5 \div 0.5 = 10$. Dividing by a number less than $1$ gives an answer **bigger** than the number you started with. Use this to check your calculator answers.
:::

:::summary
- Estimate first. The estimate tells you where the decimal point goes.
- Multiplying by hand: multiply without the decimal point, then count decimal places.
- Dividing by hand: keep the decimal point above the decimal point, and add zeros if you need more places.
- Use a calculator for multipliers with two or more digits and for divisors with more than one digit.
- Round money to the nearest cent. Think about whether a remainder means rounding up or down.
:::
