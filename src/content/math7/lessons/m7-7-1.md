Bicycle wheels, hula hoops, pizzas and hockey pucks are all circles. In this lesson you will learn the names of the parts of a circle, and a special number, $\pi$, that links the distance around a circle to the distance across it.

## Radius and diameter

:::definition Centre, radius and diameter
The **centre** is the point in the middle of a circle. Every point on the circle is the same distance from the centre. The **radius** ($r$) is the distance from the centre to the circle. The **diameter** ($d$) is the distance across the circle, through the centre.
:::

A diameter is made of two radii, so

$$d = 2r \qquad \text{and} \qquad r = d \div 2$$

:::example From radius to diameter and back
A circle has a radius of $4\ \text{cm}$. A saucer has a diameter of $15\ \text{cm}$. Find the diameter of the circle and the radius of the saucer.
@step Circle: $d = 2r = 2 \times 4 = 8\ \text{cm}$
@step Saucer: $r = d \div 2 = 15 \div 2 = 7.5\ \text{cm}$
@answer The circle’s diameter is $8\ \text{cm}$. The saucer’s radius is $7.5\ \text{cm}$.
:::

## Central angles

:::definition Central angle
A **central angle** has its vertex at the centre of a circle, and its arms are two radii. The central angles around the centre of a circle add to $360^\circ$, one full turn.

## Circumference and pi

:::definition Circumference
The **circumference** ($C$) is the distance around a circle. It is the perimeter of the circle.
:::

Some students measured round objects and divided the circumference by the diameter:

| Object | Diameter | Circumference | $C \div d$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Can lid | $8.0\ \text{cm}$ | $25.1\ \text{cm}$ | $3.14$ |
| Bicycle wheel | $66.0\ \text{cm}$ | $207.3\ \text{cm}$ | $3.14$ |
| Dinner plate | $26.0\ \text{cm}$ | $81.7\ \text{cm}$ | $3.14$ |

For **every** circle, $C \div d$ is the same number, a little more than 3.

:::definition Pi
**Pi** ($\pi$) is the number you get when you divide the circumference of any circle by its diameter. $\pi = 3.14159\ldots$ Its decimal digits go on forever without repeating, so we use the $\pi$ key on a calculator.
:::

$$C = \pi d \qquad \text{or} \qquad C = 2\pi r$$

:::example Using the diameter
A circle has a diameter of $9\ \text{cm}$. Find its circumference to the nearest tenth of a centimetre.
@step $C = \pi d = \pi \times 9$
@step $C \approx 28.27$
@answer The circumference is about $28.3\ \text{cm}$.
:::

:::example Using the radius
A hula hoop has a radius of $40\ \text{cm}$. Find its circumference to the nearest tenth of a centimetre.
@step $C = 2\pi r = 2 \times \pi \times 40$
@step $C \approx 251.33$
@answer The circumference is about $251.3\ \text{cm}$.
:::

:::mistake Using the radius in C = πd
$\pi \times 40 \approx 125.7\ \text{cm}$ is only half of the distance around the hoop. When you are given the radius, double it first, or use $C = 2\pi r$.
:::

## Working backwards

If you know the circumference, divide by $\pi$ to find the diameter: $d = C \div \pi$.

:::example A tree trunk
Kiona wraps a measuring tape around a tree trunk. The circumference is $150\ \text{cm}$. Find the diameter to the nearest tenth of a centimetre.
@step $d = C \div \pi = 150 \div \pi$
@step $d \approx 47.746$
@answer The trunk’s diameter is about $47.7\ \text{cm}$.
:::

:::tip Radius from circumference
Find the diameter first, then halve it. A circular garden with $C = 20\ \text{m}$ has $d = 20 \div \pi \approx 6.37\ \text{m}$, so $r \approx 3.18$, or about $3.2\ \text{m}$.
:::

:::summary
- The **radius** goes from the centre to the circle. The **diameter** goes across through the centre. $d = 2r$ and $r = d \div 2$.
- The **central angles** of a circle add to $360^\circ$.
- The **circumference** is the distance around a circle. $C \div d = \pi \approx 3.14$ for every circle.
- $C = \pi d = 2\pi r$, and $d = C \div \pi$. Use the $\pi$ key and round at the end.
:::
