**Factoring** is multiplication in reverse. When you multiply, you turn a product into a sum. When you factor, you turn a sum back into a product. The first and most important factoring skill is removing the **greatest common factor**.

## Factoring is the reverse of multiplying

$$3x(2x - 5) \xrightarrow{\text{expand}} 6x^2 - 15x \xrightarrow{\text{factor}} 3x(2x - 5)$$

:::definition Factored form
A polynomial is in **factored form** when it is written as a product, such as $3x(2x - 5)$.
:::

## Finding the GCF of the terms

The GCF of the terms of a polynomial is

- the GCF of the **coefficients**, times
- each variable that appears in **every** term, raised to its **lowest** power.

:::example GCF of terms
Find the GCF of $12x^3y^2$, $18x^2y^3$ and $-6x^2y$.
@step Coefficients: $\text{GCF}(12, 18, 6) = 6$
@step $x$: lowest power is $x^2$. $y$: lowest power is $y^1$.
@answer GCF $= 6x^2y$
:::

## Removing the GCF

1. Find the GCF of all the terms.
2. Divide **each term** by the GCF to find what goes in the brackets.
3. Write GCF $\times$ (quotients).
4. **Check** by multiplying back out.

:::example Factoring a trinomial
Factor $8x^3 - 12x^2 + 4x$.
@step GCF $= 4x$
@step $\dfrac{8x^3}{4x} = 2x^2$, $\dfrac{-12x^2}{4x} = -3x$, $\dfrac{4x}{4x} = 1$
@answer $4x(2x^2 - 3x + 1)$
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the 1
When a term equals the GCF, it leaves **1** in the brackets: $4x = 4x \cdot 1$. Without it, $4x(2x^2 - 3x)$ only has two terms when expanded.
:::

:::example Two variables
Factor $15a^2b - 10ab^2$.
@step GCF $= 5ab$
@answer $5ab(3a - 2b)$
:::

:::example A negative common factor
Factor $-6m^2 + 9m$.
@step When the first term is negative, factor out a negative GCF: $-3m$.
@answer $-3m(2m - 3)$ (equivalently $3m(-2m + 3)$)
:::

:::tip Is it completely factored?
After you remove the GCF, the terms inside the brackets should have **no common factor** left (other than 1). If they do, you did not use the greatest common factor.
:::

## Factoring by grouping (a preview)

Sometimes the common factor is a **binomial**:

$$x(x + 4) + 3(x + 4) = (x + 4)(x + 3)$$

and four terms can be grouped in pairs to reveal one:

$$2x^2 + 6x + 5x + 15 = 2x(x + 3) + 5(x + 3) = (x + 3)(2x + 5)$$

You will use this idea to factor trinomials by decomposition in Lesson 4.5.

:::summary
- Factoring reverses multiplication.
- GCF of terms = GCF of coefficients × common variables to their lowest powers.
- Divide each term by the GCF; a term equal to the GCF leaves 1.
- Check by expanding; nothing common should remain inside the brackets.
:::
