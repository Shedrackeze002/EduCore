You can now write the equation of a line from a graph, a point and a slope, or two points. In this lesson you will add one more case — a point and a parallel or perpendicular line — and use linear equations to solve real problems.

## Parallel and perpendicular lines

The plan is always the same:

1. Find the slope of the given line.
2. Use the same slope (parallel) or the negative reciprocal (perpendicular).
3. Substitute the given point into slope-point form.

:::example A parallel line
Write the equation of the line through $(4, -1)$ that is parallel to $3x - 2y + 8 = 0$.
@step Slope of the given line: $m = -\dfrac{3}{-2} = \dfrac32$
@step Parallel slope: $\dfrac32$
@step $y + 1 = \dfrac32(x - 4)$, which simplifies to $y = \dfrac32 x - 7$
@answer General form: $3x - 2y - 14 = 0$
:::

:::example A perpendicular line
Write the equation of the line through $(-2, 5)$ that is perpendicular to $y = \tfrac14 x + 3$.
@step Perpendicular slope: $-4$
@step $y - 5 = -4(x + 2)$
@answer $y = -4x - 3$
:::

## Linear models

Many real situations have a **fixed amount** plus a **constant rate**:

$$\text{total} = (\text{rate}) \times (\text{quantity}) + (\text{fixed amount})$$

The rate is the slope and the fixed amount is the vertical intercept.

:::example Writing and using a model
A plumber charges a call-out fee plus an hourly rate. A 2-hour job costs \$190 and a 5-hour job costs \$385.
@step The points are $(2, 190)$ and $(5, 385)$. Rate: $\dfrac{385 - 190}{5 - 2} = 65$ dollars per hour.
@step $C - 190 = 65(h - 2)$, so $C = 65h + 60$. The call-out fee is \$60.
@step A 7.5-hour job costs $65(7.5) + 60 = \$547.50$.
@step For a \$450 bill: $450 = 65h + 60$, so $h = 6$ hours.
:::

:::tip Interpret every number
In $C = 65h + 60$: 65 is dollars per hour (slope), 60 is the fixed fee (intercept), $h \ge 0$ (domain).
:::

:::summary
- Parallel: same slope. Perpendicular: negative reciprocal. Then use slope-point form.
- Linear model: total = rate × quantity + fixed amount.
- Use two data points to find the rate, then the intercept; substitute to predict or solve.
:::
