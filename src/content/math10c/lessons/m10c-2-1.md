How do surveyors measure the height of a mountain without climbing it? They use **trigonometry** — the mathematics of triangles. It all starts with one remarkable fact about similar right triangles.

## Naming the sides

In a right triangle, the side across from the right angle is the **hypotenuse**. It is always the longest side. The other two sides are named **relative to an acute angle**, often called $\theta$ (theta):

- the **opposite** side is across from $\theta$ — it does not touch $\theta$;
- the **adjacent** side touches $\theta$ and is not the hypotenuse.

## Similar triangles have equal ratios

Draw several right triangles that all have a $35^\circ$ angle. They are **similar** (same shape, different sizes), so their corresponding sides are proportional. That means the ratio $\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$ is the **same in every one of them**:

| Triangle | Opposite | Adjacent | $\dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| small | 2.1 | 3.0 | 0.70 |
| medium | 4.2 | 6.0 | 0.70 |
| large | 7.0 | 10.0 | 0.70 |

The ratio depends only on the angle — not on the size of the triangle. This ratio gets a name.

:::definition Tangent ratio
For an acute angle $\theta$ in a right triangle,
$$\tan\theta = \frac{\text{length of side opposite }\theta}{\text{length of side adjacent to }\theta}$$
:::

Your calculator knows the tangent of every angle. Make sure it is in **degree mode**: $\tan 45^\circ$ should give exactly 1.

:::example Writing a tangent ratio
In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle C = 90^\circ$, $AC = 8$ and $BC = 6$. Find $\tan A$ and $\tan B$.
@step For $\angle A$: opposite $= BC = 6$, adjacent $= AC = 8$, so $\tan A = \dfrac68 = \dfrac34$.
@step For $\angle B$: opposite $= AC = 8$, adjacent $= BC = 6$, so $\tan B = \dfrac86 = \dfrac43$.
@answer $\tan A = \tfrac34 = 0.75$ and $\tan B = \tfrac43 \approx 1.33$ — reciprocals of each other.
:::

## Finding a side length

If you know an angle and one leg, the tangent ratio gives the other leg.

:::example The unknown is opposite
In a right triangle, $\theta = 32^\circ$ and the adjacent side is $15\ \text{cm}$. Find the opposite side $x$.
@step $\tan 32^\circ = \dfrac{x}{15}$
@step Multiply both sides by 15: $x = 15\tan 32^\circ$
@answer $x \approx 9.4\ \text{cm}$
:::

:::example The unknown is adjacent
$\theta = 58^\circ$ and the opposite side is $20\ \text{m}$. Find the adjacent side $x$.
@step $\tan 58^\circ = \dfrac{20}{x}$
@step Multiply by $x$, then divide by $\tan 58^\circ$: $x = \dfrac{20}{\tan 58^\circ}$
@answer $x \approx 12.5\ \text{m}$
:::

:::mistake The unknown in the denominator
When $x$ is in the denominator, the answer is $\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\tan\theta}$, **not** opposite $\times \tan\theta$. Check: is your answer sensible compared with the other side?
:::

## Finding an angle

If you know both legs, use the **inverse tangent**, $\tan^{-1}$, to find the angle.

:::example Finding an angle
A ramp rises $1.2\ \text{m}$ over a horizontal distance of $14\ \text{m}$. What angle does it make with the ground?
@step $\tan\theta = \dfrac{1.2}{14}$
@step $\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1.2}{14}\right)$
@answer $\theta \approx 4.9^\circ$
:::

:::tip Angle of elevation
The angle measured **up** from the horizontal to a line of sight is the **angle of elevation**. Many tangent problems use it: the horizontal distance is adjacent and the height is opposite.
:::

:::summary
- Hypotenuse: across from the right angle. Opposite: across from $\theta$. Adjacent: next to $\theta$.
- Similar right triangles have equal side ratios, so the ratios depend only on the angle.
- $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$.
- Unknown on top: multiply. Unknown on the bottom: divide.
- Use $\tan^{-1}$ to find an angle, in degree mode.
:::
