When you expand $(x + 2)(x + 5)$ you get $x^2 + 7x + 10$. Notice that $2 + 5 = 7$ and $2 \times 5 = 10$. That pattern is the key to factoring every trinomial of the form $x^2 + bx + c$.

## Seeing the pattern

$$(x + p)(x + q) = x^2 + qx + px + pq = x^2 + (p + q)x + pq$$

So to factor $x^2 + bx + c$, find two integers $p$ and $q$ such that

$$p + q = b \qquad\text{and}\qquad pq = c.$$

With algebra tiles, factoring means arranging one $x^2$ tile, $b$ $x$-tiles and $c$ unit tiles into a rectangle. The dimensions of the rectangle are the factors.

:::example Positive terms
Factor $x^2 + 9x + 20$.
@step Need product $20$ and sum $9$. Factor pairs of 20: $1 \times 20$, $2 \times 10$, $4 \times 5$.
@step $4 + 5 = 9$ ✓
@answer $(x + 4)(x + 5)$
:::

## Using the signs

| $c$ | $b$ | The two integers |
|---|---|---|
| positive | positive | both positive |
| positive | negative | both negative |
| negative | either | opposite signs (the larger one has the sign of $b$) |

:::example Negative middle term
Factor $x^2 - 11x + 24$.
@step Product $+24$, sum $-11$: both integers are negative.
@step $-3 \times -8 = 24$ and $-3 + (-8) = -11$ ✓
@answer $(x - 3)(x - 8)$
:::

:::example Negative constant term
Factor $x^2 + 2x - 35$.
@step Product $-35$: opposite signs. Sum $+2$: the larger one is positive.
@step $7 \times (-5) = -35$ and $7 + (-5) = 2$ ✓
@answer $(x + 7)(x - 5)$
:::

## Remove a common factor first

Always look for a GCF before anything else.

:::example GCF, then trinomial
Factor $3x^2 - 15x - 42$ completely.
@step GCF $= 3$: $3(x^2 - 5x - 14)$
@step Product $-14$, sum $-5$: $-7$ and $2$
@answer $3(x - 7)(x + 2)$
:::

:::example Two variables
Factor $a^2 + 7ab + 12b^2$.
@step Treat it like $a^2 + 7a + 12$ with $b$ attached to the second terms: $3 \times 4 = 12$, $3 + 4 = 7$
@answer $(a + 3b)(a + 4b)$
:::

:::mistake Not checking
Always expand your answer to check. $(x + 7)(x - 5) = x^2 - 5x + 7x - 35 = x^2 + 2x - 35$ ✓
:::

:::summary
- $x^2 + bx + c = (x + p)(x + q)$ where $p + q = b$ and $pq = c$.
- Use the signs of $b$ and $c$ to narrow the search.
- Remove any common factor first.
- Check by multiplying.
:::
