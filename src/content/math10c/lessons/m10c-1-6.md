Pyramids, cones and spheres have slanted or curved surfaces. To find their surface areas you need one new idea — **slant height** — and two formulas that come from clever geometry.

## Height versus slant height

:::definition Right pyramid and right cone
In a **right pyramid** or **right cone**, the **apex** (the top point) is directly above the centre of the base.
:::

:::definition Slant height
The **slant height** $s$ is the distance from the apex down the middle of a slanted face to the edge of the base. The **height** $h$ is the perpendicular distance from the apex straight down to the base.
:::

The height, the slant height and a line in the base form a **right triangle** in which the slant height is the **hypotenuse**.

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"cone","labels":{"r":"r","h":"h","s":"s"},"show":["h","s","r"]}
:::

- Right cone: $s^2 = h^2 + r^2$
- Right square pyramid with base side $b$: $s^2 = h^2 + \left(\dfrac{b}{2}\right)^2$

:::example Finding a slant height
A right cone has radius $5\ \text{cm}$ and height $12\ \text{cm}$. Find its slant height.
@step $s^2 = 12^2 + 5^2 = 144 + 25 = 169$
@answer $s = \sqrt{169} = 13\ \text{cm}$
:::

:::mistake Height is not slant height
The height is inside the solid; the slant height lies on the surface. The slant height is always longer than the height.
:::

## Surface area of a right pyramid

A right pyramid’s surface is its base plus congruent triangular faces. Each triangle has base equal to a side of the base and height equal to the slant height.

For a square pyramid with base side $b$ and slant height $s$:

$$SA = b^2 + 4\left(\tfrac12 b s\right) = b^2 + 2bs$$

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"squarePyramid","labels":{"b":"10 m","s":"13 m"},"show":["s"]}
:::

:::example A square pyramid
Find the surface area of a square pyramid with base side $10\ \text{m}$ and slant height $13\ \text{m}$.
@step Base: $10^2 = 100\ \text{m}^2$
@step Four triangles: $4 \times \tfrac12(10)(13) = 260\ \text{m}^2$
@answer $SA = 360\ \text{m}^2$
:::

:::example When you are given the height
A square pyramid has base side $16\ \text{ft}$ and height $15\ \text{ft}$. Find its surface area.
@step Slant height: $s = \sqrt{15^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{289} = 17\ \text{ft}$
@step $SA = 16^2 + 2(16)(17) = 256 + 544$
@answer $SA = 800\ \text{ft}^2$
:::

## Surface area of a right cone

If you cut the curved surface of a cone and flatten it, you get a sector of a circle with radius $s$. Its area is $\pi r s$. So:

$$SA = \underbrace{\pi r^2}_{\text{base}} + \underbrace{\pi r s}_{\text{lateral area}}$$

:::example A cone given its height
A cone has diameter $12\ \text{cm}$ and height $8\ \text{cm}$. Find its surface area.
@step $r = 6$; $s = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = 10\ \text{cm}$
@step $SA = \pi(6)^2 + \pi(6)(10) = 36\pi + 60\pi = 96\pi$
@answer $SA \approx 301.6\ \text{cm}^2$
:::

## Surface area of a sphere

Archimedes discovered that a sphere’s surface area equals the curved area of the cylinder that just contains it: $2\pi r \times 2r$.

$$SA_{\text{sphere}} = 4\pi r^2$$

A **hemisphere** is half a sphere. Its curved surface is $2\pi r^2$. A solid hemisphere also has a flat circular face, so its total surface area is $2\pi r^2 + \pi r^2 = 3\pi r^2$.

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"sphere","labels":{"r":"7 in"}}
:::

:::example A sphere
Find the surface area of a ball with radius $7\ \text{in}$.
@step $SA = 4\pi(7)^2 = 196\pi$
@answer $SA \approx 615.8\ \text{in}^2$
:::

:::example A dome
A dome is a hemisphere with diameter $20\ \text{m}$. What is the area of its curved roof?
@step $r = 10$; curved area $= 2\pi(10)^2 = 200\pi$
@answer $\approx 628.3\ \text{m}^2$
:::

:::check m3-sa-sphere 3
:::

:::summary
- Slant height $s$ is the hypotenuse: cone $s^2 = h^2 + r^2$; square pyramid $s^2 = h^2 + (b/2)^2$.
- Square pyramid: $SA = b^2 + 2bs$.
- Cone: $SA = \pi r^2 + \pi r s$.
- Sphere: $SA = 4\pi r^2$; hemisphere curved $2\pi r^2$, closed $3\pi r^2$.
:::
