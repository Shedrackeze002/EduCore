What could $9^{\frac12}$ possibly mean? You cannot multiply 9 by itself “half a time”. But the exponent laws tell us exactly what it must be — and the answer connects exponents to radicals.

## Unit fraction exponents

If the exponent laws still work, then

$$9^{\frac12} \times 9^{\frac12} = 9^{\frac12 + \frac12} = 9^1 = 9.$$

So $9^{\frac12}$ is the number that multiplies by itself to give 9: it is $\sqrt{9} = 3$. In the same way, $8^{\frac13} \times 8^{\frac13} \times 8^{\frac13} = 8$, so $8^{\frac13} = \sqrt[3]{8} = 2$.

:::definition Unit fraction exponent
$a^{\frac1n} = \sqrt[n]{a}$, where $n$ is a natural number (and $a \ge 0$ when $n$ is even).
:::

## Rational exponents

Using the power-of-a-power law:

$$a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \left(a^{\frac1n}\right)^m = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m \quad\text{or}\quad a^{\frac{m}{n}} = (a^m)^{\frac1n} = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$$

The **denominator is the index** (the root) and the **numerator is the power**.

:::example Converting forms
(a) Write $x^{\frac34}$ in radical form. (b) Write $\sqrt[5]{y^2}$ with a rational exponent.
@step (a) $x^{\frac34} = \sqrt[4]{x^3}$ or $\left(\sqrt[4]{x}\right)^3$
@step (b) $\sqrt[5]{y^2} = y^{\frac25}$
:::

## Evaluating

Take the **root first**, then the power — the numbers stay small.

:::example Evaluating rational exponents
Evaluate (a) $27^{\frac23}$ (b) $16^{-\frac34}$ (c) $\left(\tfrac{8}{125}\right)^{\frac23}$.
@step (a) $27^{\frac23} = \left(\sqrt[3]{27}\right)^2 = 3^2 = 9$
@step (b) $16^{-\frac34} = \dfrac{1}{16^{\frac34}} = \dfrac{1}{(\sqrt[4]{16})^3} = \dfrac{1}{2^3} = \dfrac18$
@step (c) $\left(\tfrac{8}{125}\right)^{\frac23} = \left(\tfrac25\right)^2 = \dfrac{4}{25}$
:::

## Exponent laws with rational exponents

All the exponent laws still apply. You just need to be careful with fraction arithmetic.

:::example Simplifying
Simplify (a) $x^{\frac12} \cdot x^{\frac13}$ (b) $\left(x^{\frac34}\right)^{-2}$ (c) $(8x^6)^{\frac23}$.
@step (a) $x^{\frac12 + \frac13} = x^{\frac36 + \frac26} = x^{\frac56}$
@step (b) $x^{\frac34 \times (-2)} = x^{-\frac32} = \dfrac{1}{x^{\frac32}}$
@step (c) $8^{\frac23}x^{6 \times \frac23} = 4x^4$
:::

:::tip Typing rational exponents
In Gradileo, put fractional exponents in brackets: type `x^(5/6)`, not `x^5/6`.

## Applications

:::example Radius from volume
A spherical balloon holds $4000\ \text{cm}^3$. Using $r = \left(\dfrac{3V}{4\pi}\right)^{\frac13}$, find its radius.
@step $\dfrac{3(4000)}{4\pi} \approx 954.93$
@answer $r \approx 954.93^{\frac13} \approx 9.8\ \text{cm}$

:::mistake Reading the fraction exponent upside down
In $a^{\frac{m}{n}}$ the **denominator** is the root and the numerator is the power: $8^{\frac23} = \left(\sqrt[3]{8}\right)^2 = 4$, not $\left(\sqrt{8}\right)^3$. Remember "the root is at the bottom, like the root of a tree".
:::

:::mistake Making the answer negative when the exponent is negative
A negative exponent means **reciprocal**, not negative: $16^{-\frac12} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{16}} = \dfrac14$, which is positive.
:::

:::summary
- $a^{\frac1n} = \sqrt[n]{a}$ and $a^{\frac mn} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$.
- Denominator = index (root), numerator = power. Negative exponent = reciprocal.
- Evaluate by taking the root first.
- All exponent laws apply to rational exponents.
:::
