Where does a line cross the axes, and which values can $x$ and $y$ take? These **characteristics** of a linear relation often carry the most important meaning in a real situation — a starting fee, a time when something runs out.

## Intercepts

:::definition Intercepts
The **x-intercept** is where a graph crosses the $x$-axis (where $y = 0$). The **y-intercept** is where it crosses the $y$-axis (where $x = 0$). Intercepts can be given as values ($x$-intercept $= 4$) or as points ($(4, 0)$).
:::

:::example Intercepts from an equation
Find the intercepts of $3x - 4y = 12$.
@step $x$-intercept: let $y = 0$. $3x = 12$, so $x = 4$. Point $(4, 0)$.
@step $y$-intercept: let $x = 0$. $-4y = 12$, so $y = -3$. Point $(0, -3)$.
:::

:::tip Graphing with intercepts
Two intercepts are two points — enough to draw the line.

### How many intercepts?

- Most lines have **two** intercepts.
- A horizontal line $y = k$ ($k \ne 0$) has only a $y$-intercept; a vertical line $x = h$ ($h \ne 0$) has only an $x$-intercept.
- A line through the origin has **one** point $(0, 0)$ that is both intercepts.
- The line $y = 0$ (the $x$-axis) has infinitely many $x$-intercepts.

## Domain and range of linear relations

| Line | Domain | Range |
|---|---|---|
| slanted, e.g. $y = 2x + 1$ | $\{x \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$ | $\{y \mid y \in \mathbb{R}\}$ |
| horizontal, $y = 3$ | $\{x \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$ | $\{3\}$ |
| vertical, $x = -2$ | $\{-2\}$ | $\{y \mid y \in \mathbb{R}\}$ |

In a real situation the domain and range are often **restricted** — time cannot be negative, a tank cannot hold less than 0 L.

## Meaning in context

:::example Draining a pool
A pool holds $800\ \text{L}$ and drains at $40\ \text{L/min}$: $V = 800 - 40t$.
@step $V$-intercept: when $t = 0$, $V = 800$ — the starting volume.
@step $t$-intercept: $0 = 800 - 40t$, so $t = 20$ — the pool is empty after 20 min.
@step Slope: $-40$ — the volume decreases by 40 L each minute.
@answer Domain: $\{t \mid 0 \le t \le 20,\ t \in \mathbb{R}\}$; range: $\{V \mid 0 \le V \le 800,\ V \in \mathbb{R}\}$
:::

:::example A taxi fare
A taxi charges $C = 3.75 + 2.10d$ dollars for $d$ km. What does each number mean?
@step $3.75$ is the $C$-intercept: the fee before the taxi moves.
@step $2.10$ is the rate of change: the cost per kilometre.
:::

:::summary
- $x$-intercept: set $y = 0$. $y$-intercept: set $x = 0$.
- Slanted lines have all real numbers as domain and range; horizontal and vertical lines are special.
- In context, intercepts are often starting values or “run-out” times, and the domain and range are restricted.
