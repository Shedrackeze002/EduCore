To **solve a triangle** means to find all of its unknown side lengths and angle measures. With the Pythagorean theorem and the three trigonometric ratios, you can solve any right triangle from just two pieces of information (as long as one is a side).

## Tools for solving

1. **Pythagorean theorem:** $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, where $c$ is the hypotenuse. Use it when you know two sides.
2. **Angle sum:** the two acute angles add to $90^\circ$.
3. **Trigonometric ratios:** SOH CAH TOA, to link an angle with two sides.

:::example Finding a missing leg
A right triangle has hypotenuse $17$ and one leg $8$. Find the other leg.
@step $b^2 = 17^2 - 8^2 = 289 - 64 = 225$
@answer $b = 15$
:::

:::example Solve the triangle
In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle C = 90^\circ$, $\angle A = 35^\circ$ and $AB = 24$. Solve the triangle.
@step $\angle B = 90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ$
@step $BC$ is opposite $\angle A$: $BC = 24\sin 35^\circ \approx 13.8$
@step $AC$ is adjacent to $\angle A$: $AC = 24\cos 35^\circ \approx 19.7$
@answer $\angle B = 55^\circ$, $BC \approx 13.8$, $AC \approx 19.7$
:::

:::tip Check with Pythagoras
$13.8^2 + 19.7^2 \approx 578$ and $24^2 = 576$. Close enough given rounding — the solution is consistent.
:::

## Solving from two sides

:::example Two legs given
In $\triangle DEF$, $\angle F = 90^\circ$, $DF = 9$ and $EF = 12$. Solve the triangle.
@step $DE = \sqrt{9^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{225} = 15$
@step For $\angle D$: opposite $= EF = 12$, adjacent $= DF = 9$. $\angle D = \tan^{-1}\left(\tfrac{12}{9}\right) \approx 53^\circ$
@step $\angle E \approx 90^\circ - 53^\circ = 37^\circ$
@answer $DE = 15$, $\angle D \approx 53^\circ$, $\angle E \approx 37^\circ$
:::

:::mistake Rounding too early
Keep full calculator values for intermediate steps and round only your final answers. Early rounding can change the last digit.
:::

## In context

:::example A ladder
A $7\ \text{m}$ ladder reaches $6.5\ \text{m}$ up a wall. How far is its foot from the wall, and what angle does it make with the ground?
@step Distance: $\sqrt{7^2 - 6.5^2} = \sqrt{6.75} \approx 2.6\ \text{m}$
@step Angle: $\sin\theta = \dfrac{6.5}{7}$, so $\theta = \sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{6.5}{7}\right) \approx 68.2^\circ$
:::

:::check m4-pythagoras 3
:::

:::summary
- Solving a triangle means finding every unknown side and angle.
- Use the Pythagorean theorem for two known sides, the angle sum for the second acute angle, and SOH CAH TOA to connect angles and sides.
- Round only at the end, and check with a second method when you can.
:::
