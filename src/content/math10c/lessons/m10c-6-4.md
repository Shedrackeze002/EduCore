Railway tracks never meet. The corner of a sheet of paper is a perfect right angle. Slope gives a quick test for both relationships.

## Parallel lines

:::definition Parallel lines
Non-vertical lines are **parallel** if and only if they have **equal slopes**. (All vertical lines are parallel to each other.)
:::

Lines with the same slope rise at the same rate, so the distance between them never changes.

## Perpendicular lines

:::definition Perpendicular lines
Two lines (neither vertical) are **perpendicular** if their slopes are **negative reciprocals**: $m_2 = -\dfrac{1}{m_1}$, which means $m_1 \times m_2 = -1$.
:::

:::figure

:::

Rotating a line $90^\circ$ swaps its rise and run and changes one sign: slope $\dfrac{2}{3}$ becomes $-\dfrac{3}{2}$.

A horizontal line (slope 0) is perpendicular to a vertical line (undefined slope).

:::example Parallel and perpendicular slopes
A line has slope $-\dfrac{4}{5}$. Find the slope of a parallel line and a perpendicular line.
@step Parallel: same slope, $-\dfrac45$
@step Perpendicular: flip and change the sign, $\dfrac54$
@answer Check: $-\dfrac45 \times \dfrac54 = -1$ ✓
:::

:::mistake Flip AND change the sign
The perpendicular slope to $3$ is $-\dfrac13$, not $\dfrac13$ and not $-3$.
:::

## Classifying pairs of lines

:::example Parallel, perpendicular or neither?
Line 1 passes through $(1, 2)$ and $(4, 8)$. Line 2 passes through $(0, 5)$ and $(6, 2)$.
@step $m_1 = \dfrac{8 - 2}{4 - 1} = 2$
@step $m_2 = \dfrac{2 - 5}{6 - 0} = -\dfrac12$
@answer $m_1 m_2 = -1$, so the lines are perpendicular.
:::

## Finding an unknown

:::example Making lines perpendicular
Line 1 has slope $\dfrac23$. Line 2 passes through $(1, 4)$ and $(5, k)$. Find $k$ so the lines are perpendicular.
@step Line 2 needs slope $-\dfrac32$.
@step $\dfrac{k - 4}{5 - 1} = -\dfrac32$, so $k - 4 = -6$
@answer $k = -2$
:::

:::summary
- Parallel: equal slopes.
- Perpendicular: negative reciprocal slopes, $m_1 m_2 = -1$.
- Horizontal and vertical lines are perpendicular.
- Set up the slope formula equal to the required slope to find an unknown coordinate.
:::
