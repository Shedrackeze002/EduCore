Some polynomials have special patterns that let you factor them in one step. Recognizing them — and knowing a reliable strategy for any polynomial — is the final piece of factoring.

## Difference of squares

Multiply $(a - b)(a + b)$: the middle terms cancel.

$$(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 + ab - ab - b^2 = a^2 - b^2$$

:::definition Difference of squares
$$a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$$
:::

A difference of squares is really a trinomial $ax^2 + bx + c$ with $b = 0$. For $x^2 - 49$, we need two integers with product $-49$ and sum $0$: $7$ and $-7$.

:::example Differences of squares
Factor (a) $x^2 - 81$ (b) $16m^2 - 25n^2$ (c) $2x^2 - 50$.
@step (a) $x^2 - 9^2 = (x - 9)(x + 9)$
@step (b) $(4m)^2 - (5n)^2 = (4m - 5n)(4m + 5n)$
@step (c) GCF first: $2(x^2 - 25) = 2(x - 5)(x + 5)$
:::

:::example Factoring twice
Factor $x^4 - 16$ completely.
@step $x^4 - 16 = (x^2)^2 - 4^2 = (x^2 - 4)(x^2 + 4)$
@step $x^2 - 4$ is another difference of squares: $(x - 2)(x + 2)$
@answer $(x - 2)(x + 2)(x^2 + 4)$
:::

:::mistake A sum of squares does not factor
$x^2 + 4$ cannot be factored over the integers. No two integers have product $4$ and sum $0$.
:::

## Perfect square trinomials

$$(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2 \qquad (a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$$

Recognize one when:

1. the first and last terms are perfect squares, and
2. the middle term is $2 \times \sqrt{\text{first}} \times \sqrt{\text{last}}$ (with either sign).

:::example Perfect square trinomials
Factor (a) $x^2 + 14x + 49$ (b) $9x^2 - 12xy + 4y^2$.
@step (a) $\sqrt{x^2} = x$, $\sqrt{49} = 7$, and $2(x)(7) = 14x$ ✓ So $(x + 7)^2$.
@step (b) $\sqrt{9x^2} = 3x$, $\sqrt{4y^2} = 2y$, and $2(3x)(2y) = 12xy$ with a minus sign ✓ So $(3x - 2y)^2$.
:::

## A strategy for factoring completely

1. **Common factor?** Always check first.
2. **Count the terms:**
   - Two terms → difference of squares?
   - Three terms → perfect square trinomial? Otherwise $x^2 + bx + c$ (product-sum) or $ax^2 + bx + c$ (decomposition).
   - Four terms → grouping.
3. **Check each factor** — can any be factored again?
4. **Check your answer** by multiplying.

:::example Choosing a strategy
Factor $3x^3 - 12x^2 + 12x$ completely.
@step GCF: $3x(x^2 - 4x + 4)$
@step $x^2 - 4x + 4$ is a perfect square: $(x - 2)^2$
@answer $3x(x - 2)^2$
:::

:::summary
- $a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$ — a trinomial with $b = 0$. A sum of squares does not factor.
- $a^2 \pm 2ab + b^2 = (a \pm b)^2$.
