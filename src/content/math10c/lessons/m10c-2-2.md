The tangent ratio uses the two legs. But what if a problem involves the **hypotenuse** — like the length of a ladder or a kite string? Two more ratios, **sine** and **cosine**, complete the set of primary trigonometric ratios.

## Sine and cosine

:::definition The primary trigonometric ratios
For an acute angle $\theta$ in a right triangle:
$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$$
:::

A popular memory aid is **SOH CAH TOA**:

- **S**ine = **O**pposite / **H**ypotenuse
- **C**osine = **A**djacent / **H**ypotenuse
- **T**angent = **O**pposite / **A**djacent

Because the hypotenuse is the longest side, $\sin\theta$ and $\cos\theta$ are always between 0 and 1.

:::example Writing all three ratios
In right $\triangle PQR$, $\angle R = 90^\circ$, $PR = 5$, $QR = 12$ and $PQ = 13$. Write the three ratios for $\angle P$.
@step Opposite $\angle P$: $QR = 12$. Adjacent: $PR = 5$. Hypotenuse: $PQ = 13$.
@step $\sin P = \dfrac{12}{13}$, $\cos P = \dfrac{5}{13}$, $\tan P = \dfrac{12}{5}$
:::

## Choosing the right ratio

Ask two questions:

1. Which side do I **know**?
2. Which side do I **want**?

Then pick the ratio that uses exactly those two sides.

| Known and unknown sides | Ratio |
|---|---|
| opposite and hypotenuse | sine |
| adjacent and hypotenuse | cosine |
| opposite and adjacent | tangent |

## Finding side lengths

:::example Unknown leg with sine
A $6\ \text{m}$ ladder leans against a wall, making a $72^\circ$ angle with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach?
@step The ladder is the hypotenuse; the height is opposite the $72^\circ$ angle: use sine.
@step $\sin 72^\circ = \dfrac{h}{6}$, so $h = 6\sin 72^\circ$
@answer $h \approx 5.7\ \text{m}$
:::

:::example Unknown hypotenuse with cosine
The adjacent side to a $40^\circ$ angle is $18\ \text{cm}$. Find the hypotenuse $x$.
@step $\cos 40^\circ = \dfrac{18}{x}$
@step $x = \dfrac{18}{\cos 40^\circ}$
@answer $x \approx 23.5\ \text{cm}$
:::

:::tip Check your answer
The hypotenuse must be longer than either leg. If your “hypotenuse” is shorter than a leg, you multiplied when you should have divided.
:::

## Finding angles

Use $\sin^{-1}$ or $\cos^{-1}$ when the known sides include the hypotenuse.

:::example Inverse cosine
A kite string is $50\ \text{m}$ long. The kite is directly above a point $30\ \text{m}$ from the person flying it. What angle does the string make with the ground?
@step Adjacent $= 30$, hypotenuse $= 50$: use cosine.
@step $\cos\theta = \dfrac{30}{50} = 0.6$
@answer $\theta = \cos^{-1}(0.6) \approx 53.1^\circ$
:::

:::summary
- $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}$, $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}$, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$ — SOH CAH TOA.
- Choose the ratio that links the side you know and the side you want.
- When the unknown is the hypotenuse, divide by the ratio.
- Use $\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$, $\tan^{-1}$ to find angles.
:::
