How much cardboard does it take to make a cereal box? How much metal to make a soup can? These are **surface area** questions. In this lesson you will find the surface area of right prisms and right cylinders by thinking about their **nets**.

## Surface area and nets

:::definition Surface area
The **surface area** of a 3-D object is the total area of all of its faces (or surfaces). It is measured in square units such as $\text{cm}^2$ or $\text{ft}^2$.
:::

:::definition Net
A **net** is a 2-D pattern that folds up to make a 3-D object. The surface area of an object equals the area of its net.
:::

A **right prism** has two congruent, parallel bases and rectangular sides that are perpendicular to the bases. It is named after the shape of its base: rectangular prism, triangular prism, and so on.

## Rectangular prisms

A rectangular prism has 3 pairs of congruent faces: top and bottom, front and back, two ends.

$$SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh = 2(lw + lh + wh)$$

:::

:::example Surface area of a box
Find the surface area of the box above.
@step Top and bottom: $2(12 \times 5) = 120\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Front and back: $2(12 \times 8) = 192\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Two ends: $2(5 \times 8) = 80\ \text{cm}^2$
@answer $SA = 120 + 192 + 80 = 392\ \text{cm}^2$
:::

## Triangular prisms

A triangular prism has 2 triangular bases and 3 rectangular faces. The three rectangles have the same length (the length of the prism), and their widths are the three sides of the triangle. So together they form one long rectangle whose width is the **perimeter of the triangle**.

$$SA = 2(\text{area of triangle}) + (\text{perimeter of triangle}) \times (\text{length of prism})$$

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"triPrism","labels":{"a":"6 in","b":"8 in","c":"10 in","l":"15 in"}}
:::

:::example A triangular prism
The bases are right triangles with legs 6 in and 8 in and hypotenuse 10 in. The prism is 15 in long.
@step Two triangles: $2 \times \tfrac12(6)(8) = 48\ \text{in}^2$
@step Three rectangles: $(6 + 8 + 10)(15) = 360\ \text{in}^2$
@answer $SA = 48 + 360 = 408\ \text{in}^2$
:::

:::tip A general rule for every right prism
$SA = 2B + Ph$, where $B$ is the area of one base, $P$ is the perimeter of the base, and $h$ is the height (length) of the prism.
:::

:::

## Right cylinders

Unroll a can and you get two circles and a rectangle. The rectangle’s length is the **circumference** of the circle, $2\pi r$, and its width is the height $h$.

$$SA = \underbrace{2\pi r^2}_{\text{two circles}} + \underbrace{2\pi r h}_{\text{curved surface}}$$

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"cylinder","labels":{"d":"10 cm","h":"14 cm"}}
:::

:::example A closed cylinder
Find the surface area of a cylinder with diameter $10\ \text{cm}$ and height $14\ \text{cm}$, to the nearest tenth.
@step Radius: $r = 10 \div 2 = 5\ \text{cm}$
@step Circles: $2\pi(5)^2 = 50\pi$
@step Curved surface: $2\pi(5)(14) = 140\pi$
@answer $SA = 190\pi \approx 596.9\ \text{cm}^2$
:::

:::mistake Using the diameter as the radius
The formulas use the **radius**. If you are given the diameter, divide by 2 first.
:::

:::

## Thinking about the real situation

Real objects are not always closed. Ask: which surfaces actually exist, or need to be covered?

:::example A label on a can
A label covers only the curved surface of a can with diameter $8\ \text{cm}$ and height $11\ \text{cm}$. What is the area of the label?
@step Only the lateral area: $2\pi r h = 2\pi(4)(11) = 88\pi$
@answer $\approx 276.5\ \text{cm}^2$
:::

:::example An open box
How much cardboard is needed for an open-top box $30\ \text{cm}$ long, $20\ \text{cm}$ wide and $10\ \text{cm}$ high?
@step Bottom only (no top): $30 \times 20 = 600$
@step Front and back: $2(30 \times 10) = 600$; ends: $2(20 \times 10) = 400$
@answer $600 + 600 + 400 = 1600\ \text{cm}^2$
:::

:::summary
- Surface area = total area of all faces = area of the net (square units).
- Rectangular prism: $SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)$.
- Any right prism: $SA = 2B + Ph$.
- Cylinder: $SA = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h$; the curved part alone is $2\pi r h$.
- Read the context: open tops, labels and hidden faces change which surfaces you include.
:::
