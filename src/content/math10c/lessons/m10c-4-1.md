Multiplying polynomials is the algebra version of something you already know well: multiplying multi-digit numbers. In this lesson you will multiply binomials with pictures (algebra tiles and area models), connect it to arithmetic, and then do it symbolically.

## Vocabulary

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| term | a number, a variable, or their product | $-5x^2$ |
| monomial | one term | $3xy$ |
| binomial | two terms | $2x + 3$ |
| trinomial | three terms | $x^2 - 4x + 7$ |
| polynomial | one or more terms added together | $x^3 + 2x - 1$ |
| like terms | same variables with the same exponents | $4x$ and $-9x$ |

## Multiplying with an area model

The area of a rectangle is length × width. If the sides are $(x + 3)$ and $(x + 2)$, split the rectangle into four parts:

:::figure
{"type":"areaModel","top":["x","2"],"side":["x","3"],"cells":[["x^2","2x"],["3x","6"]]}
:::

The total area is the sum of the parts:

$$(x + 3)(x + 2) = x^2 + 2x + 3x + 6 = x^2 + 5x + 6$$

With **algebra tiles**, a big square tile is $x^2$, a long rectangle is $x$, and a small square is $1$. Building the rectangle with tiles shows exactly the same four groups.

## Connection to two-digit multiplication

$23 \times 14$ can be calculated with the same model by splitting the numbers into tens and ones:

$$(20 + 3)(10 + 4) = 200 + 80 + 30 + 12 = 322$$

Compare with $(2x + 3)(x + 4) = 2x^2 + 8x + 3x + 12 = 2x^2 + 11x + 12$. If you substitute $x = 10$, you get $200 + 110 + 12 = 322$ — the same calculation!

## Multiplying symbolically

The area model is really the **distributive property**: multiply **each term** of the first binomial by **each term** of the second.

A helpful memory aid for two binomials is **FOIL**: **F**irst, **O**uter, **I**nner, **L**ast.

:::example Using the distributive property
Expand and simplify $(2x - 5)(3x + 4)$.
@step First: $2x \cdot 3x = 6x^2$
@step Outer: $2x \cdot 4 = 8x$
@step Inner: $-5 \cdot 3x = -15x$
@step Last: $-5 \cdot 4 = -20$
@answer $6x^2 + 8x - 15x - 20 = 6x^2 - 7x - 20$
:::

:::example Two variables
Expand $(a + 2b)(3a - b)$.
@step $3a^2 - ab + 6ab - 2b^2$
@answer $3a^2 + 5ab - 2b^2$
:::

:::example Squaring a binomial
Expand $(x - 6)^2$.
@step $(x - 6)^2 = (x - 6)(x - 6)$
@step $x^2 - 6x - 6x + 36$
@answer $x^2 - 12x + 36$
:::

:::mistake The missing middle term
$(x - 6)^2$ is **not** $x^2 + 36$ or $x^2 - 36$. Always write the square as a product of two binomials and multiply every pair of terms.
:::

:::summary
- The area model and algebra tiles show that each term multiplies each term.
- Binomial multiplication mirrors two-digit multiplication.
- Use the distributive property (FOIL for two binomials), then combine like terms.
- $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$ — never forget the middle term.
:::
