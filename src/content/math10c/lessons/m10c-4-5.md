When the leading coefficient is not 1, as in $6x^2 + 7x - 3$, the simple product-sum trick needs one more step. The **decomposition** method (sometimes called the $ac$ method) works for every factorable trinomial.

## Decomposition, step by step

To factor $ax^2 + bx + c$:

1. Multiply $a \times c$.
2. Find two integers $m$ and $n$ with $mn = ac$ and $m + n = b$.
3. **Decompose** the middle term: rewrite $bx$ as $mx + nx$.
4. **Group** the four terms in pairs and factor out the GCF of each pair.
5. Factor out the common binomial.

:::example Positive terms
Factor $2x^2 + 11x + 12$.
@step $ac = 2 \times 12 = 24$. Need product $24$ and sum $11$: $3$ and $8$.
@step $2x^2 + 3x + 8x + 12$
@step $x(2x + 3) + 4(2x + 3)$
@answer $(2x + 3)(x + 4)$
:::

:::example A negative constant
Factor $6x^2 + 7x - 3$.
@step $ac = 6 \times (-3) = -18$. Need product $-18$ and sum $7$: $9$ and $-2$.
@step $6x^2 + 9x - 2x - 3$
@step $3x(2x + 3) - 1(2x + 3)$
@answer $(2x + 3)(3x - 1)$
:::

:::mistake Signs when grouping
In $6x^2 + 9x - 2x - 3$, the second pair is $-2x - 3 = -1(2x + 3)$. Factor out the **negative** so that the binomials match.
:::

:::tip Order does not matter
Writing $6x^2 - 2x + 9x - 3$ instead gives $2x(3x - 1) + 3(3x - 1) = (3x - 1)(2x + 3)$ — the same answer.
:::

## More cases

:::example A common factor first
Factor $12x^2 - 26x + 10$ completely.
@step GCF $= 2$: $2(6x^2 - 13x + 5)$
@step $ac = 30$, sum $-13$: $-3$ and $-10$
@step $6x^2 - 3x - 10x + 5 = 3x(2x - 1) - 5(2x - 1)$
@answer $2(2x - 1)(3x - 5)$
:::

:::example Two variables
Factor $4x^2 - 4xy - 15y^2$.
@step $ac = -60$, sum $-4$: $6$ and $-10$
@step $4x^2 + 6xy - 10xy - 15y^2 = 2x(2x + 3y) - 5y(2x + 3y)$
@answer $(2x + 3y)(2x - 5y)$
:::

## Guess and check

Some people prefer to test pairs of binomials directly. For $6x^2 + 7x - 3$, the first terms could be $(6x)(x)$ or $(3x)(2x)$ and the last terms $(3)(-1)$ or $(-3)(1)$. Try combinations until the middle term is $7x$. Decomposition is more systematic, but guess and check can be faster once you have practised.

:::summary
- Decomposition: find $m, n$ with $mn = ac$ and $m + n = b$; split the middle term; group; factor out the common binomial.
- Watch signs when factoring the second pair.
- Remove a GCF first, and check by multiplying.
:::
