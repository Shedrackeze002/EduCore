Graphing shows the solution of a system, but it is not always exact. **Substitution** is an algebraic method that always gives the exact solution.

## The substitution method

1. **Isolate** one variable in one equation (choose a variable with coefficient 1 or $-1$ if you can).
2. **Substitute** that expression into the other equation.
3. **Solve** the resulting one-variable equation.
4. **Substitute back** to find the other variable.
5. **Verify** in both original equations.

:::example A variable already isolated
Solve $\begin{cases} y = 2x - 3 \\ 3x + 2y = 15 \end{cases}$
@step Substitute $y = 2x - 3$ into the second equation: $3x + 2(2x - 3) = 15$
@step $3x + 4x - 6 = 15$, so $7x = 21$ and $x = 3$
@step $y = 2(3) - 3 = 3$
@step Check: $3(3) + 2(3) = 15$ ✓
@answer $(3, 3)$
:::

:::example Isolating first
Solve $\begin{cases} x + 3y = 7 \\ 2x - 5y = -8 \end{cases}$
@step From the first equation: $x = 7 - 3y$
@step Substitute: $2(7 - 3y) - 5y = -8$
@step $14 - 6y - 5y = -8$, so $-11y = -22$ and $y = 2$
@step $x = 7 - 3(2) = 1$
@answer $(1, 2)$
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the brackets
When you substitute an expression, put it in brackets: $2(7 - 3y)$, not $2 \cdot 7 - 3y$.
:::

## Fractional solutions

Substitution handles answers that are not whole numbers — something graphing struggles with.

:::example A fractional solution
Solve $\begin{cases} x - 2y = 1 \\ 4x + 2y = 9 \end{cases}$
@step $x = 1 + 2y$
@step $4(1 + 2y) + 2y = 9$, so $4 + 10y = 9$ and $y = \tfrac12$
@step $x = 1 + 2\left(\tfrac12\right) = 2$
@answer $\left(2, \tfrac12\right)$
:::

:::check r9-substitution 3
:::

:::summary
- Isolate, substitute (with brackets), solve, substitute back, verify.
- Choose the variable with coefficient 1 or $-1$ to isolate.
- Substitution gives exact answers, including fractions.
:::
