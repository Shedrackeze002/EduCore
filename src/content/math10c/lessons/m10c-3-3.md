Some numbers, like $\tfrac34$ or $0.\overline{6}$, can be written as fractions. Others, like $\sqrt{2}$ and $\pi$, cannot — their decimals go on forever without repeating. In this lesson you will meet these **irrational numbers** and see how all the numbers you know fit together into the **real number system**.

## Rational and irrational numbers

:::definition Rational number
A number that can be written as $\dfrac{a}{b}$ where $a$ and $b$ are integers and $b \neq 0$. As decimals, rational numbers **terminate** ($0.375$) or **repeat** ($0.\overline{27}$).
:::

:::definition Irrational number
A real number that **cannot** be written as a fraction of integers. Its decimal never terminates and never repeats.
:::

Examples of irrational numbers:

- square roots of whole numbers that are not perfect squares: $\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{5}, \sqrt{20}$
- cube roots of numbers that are not perfect cubes: $\sqrt[3]{4}$
- $\pi = 3.14159265\ldots$
- decimals with a non-repeating pattern: $0.1010010001\ldots$

:::mistake Simplify before you classify
$\sqrt{49} = 7$ and $\sqrt{\dfrac{9}{16}} = \dfrac34$ are **rational**. Always check whether the radicand is a perfect square (or cube) first.
:::

## The real number system

The sets of numbers are nested inside one another:

| Set | Symbol | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Natural numbers | $\mathbb{N}$ | $1, 2, 3, \ldots$ |
| Whole numbers | $\mathbb{W}$ | $0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ |
| Integers | $\mathbb{I}$ | $\ldots, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ |
| Rational numbers | $\mathbb{Q}$ | all fractions of integers |
| Irrational numbers | $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ | real numbers that are not rational |
| Real numbers | $\mathbb{R}$ | all rational and irrational numbers |

Every natural number is whole, every whole number is an integer, and every integer is rational. The rational and irrational numbers **do not overlap**, and together they make up the real numbers.

:::example Classifying a number
To which sets does $-\sqrt{25}$ belong?
@step $-\sqrt{25} = -5$
@answer Integers, rational numbers and real numbers. (Not natural or whole, because it is negative.)
:::

## Radicals and their index

:::definition Radical
An expression such as $\sqrt[n]{x}$. The number $x$ is the **radicand** and $n$ is the **index**. When no index is written, it is 2 (a square root).
:::

$\sqrt[n]{x}$ asks: “What number, used as a factor $n$ times, gives $x$?”

- $\sqrt[4]{81} = 3$ because $3^4 = 81$.
- $\sqrt[5]{-32} = -2$ because $(-2)^5 = -32$.
- $\sqrt[4]{-16}$ is **not** a real number: no real number to an even power is negative.

## Approximating and ordering irrational numbers

Use **benchmarks** — nearby perfect squares or cubes.

:::example Locating a square root
Estimate $\sqrt{30}$ to one decimal place.
@step $5^2 = 25$ and $6^2 = 36$, so $5 < \sqrt{30} < 6$.
@step 30 is 5 of the 11 steps from 25 to 36, so try about $5.5$: $5.5^2 = 30.25$, slightly too big.
@answer $\sqrt{30} \approx 5.5$ (calculator: $5.477\ldots$)
:::

:::example Ordering numbers
Order from least to greatest: $\sqrt{20}$, $4.3$, $\sqrt[3]{70}$, $\tfrac{9}{2}$.
@step $\sqrt{20} \approx 4.47$, $\sqrt[3]{70} \approx 4.12$, $\tfrac92 = 4.5$
@answer $\sqrt[3]{70},\ 4.3,\ \sqrt{20},\ \tfrac92$
:::

:::summary
- Rational: can be written as $\tfrac{a}{b}$; decimals terminate or repeat. Irrational: cannot; decimals never terminate or repeat.
- $\mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{W} \subset \mathbb{I} \subset \mathbb{Q}$; $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ together form $\mathbb{R}$.
- In $\sqrt[n]{x}$, $n$ is the index. Even roots of negatives are not real; odd roots of negatives are negative.
- Use benchmarks to approximate, then convert to decimals to order.
:::
