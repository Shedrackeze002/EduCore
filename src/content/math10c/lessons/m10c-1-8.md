This lesson completes the measurement toolkit. You will find the volume of spheres, work backwards from a volume or surface area to a missing dimension, and solve problems about **composite objects** made of several shapes.

## Volume of a sphere

Archimedes showed that a sphere fills exactly two thirds of the cylinder that just contains it. That cylinder has volume $\pi r^2(2r) = 2\pi r^3$, so

$$V_{\text{sphere}} = \tfrac23(2\pi r^3) = \tfrac43\pi r^3 \qquad V_{\text{hemisphere}} = \tfrac23\pi r^3$$

:::example Volume of a ball
A basketball has a diameter of $24\ \text{cm}$. Find its volume.
@step $r = 12\ \text{cm}$
@step $V = \tfrac43\pi(12)^3 = 2304\pi$
@answer $V \approx 7238.2\ \text{cm}^3$
:::

## Finding an unknown dimension

If you know the volume or surface area, substitute what you know and **solve for the unknown**. Undo operations in reverse order.

:::example Height of a cylinder
A cylindrical tank has radius $3\ \text{m}$ and volume $200\ \text{m}^3$. Find its height.
@step $200 = \pi(3)^2 h = 9\pi h$
@step $h = \dfrac{200}{9\pi}$
@answer $h \approx 7.1\ \text{m}$
:::

:::example Radius of a sphere from its volume
A spherical tank holds $5000\ \text{L} = 5\ \text{m}^3$. Find its radius.
@step $5 = \tfrac43\pi r^3$
@step $r^3 = \dfrac{3(5)}{4\pi} \approx 1.194$
@answer $r = \sqrt[3]{1.194} \approx 1.1\ \text{m}$
:::

:::example Radius of a sphere from its surface area
A sphere has a surface area of $500\ \text{cm}^2$. Find its radius.
@step $500 = 4\pi r^2$, so $r^2 = \dfrac{500}{4\pi} \approx 39.79$
@answer $r \approx 6.3\ \text{cm}$
:::

:::tip Square roots and cube roots
If the unknown is squared ($r^2$), finish with a square root. If it is cubed ($r^3$), finish with a cube root.
:::

## Composite objects

A **composite object** is made of two or more simple solids.

- **Volume:** add the volumes of the parts (or subtract a part that is removed, like a hole).
- **Surface area:** add only the surfaces that are **exposed**. Faces that are joined together are hidden and must not be counted.

:::example Volume of a silo
A silo is a cylinder with radius $4\ \text{m}$ and height $15\ \text{m}$, topped by a hemisphere. Find its volume.
@step Cylinder: $\pi(4)^2(15) = 240\pi$
@step Hemisphere: $\tfrac23\pi(4)^3 = \tfrac{128}{3}\pi$
@answer Total $= 240\pi + 42.\overline{6}\pi \approx 888.0\ \text{m}^3$
:::

:::example Surface area of the same silo
How much surface must be painted, including the floor?
@step The top of the cylinder is covered by the hemisphere — do not count it.
@step Floor: $\pi(4)^2 = 16\pi$; cylinder side: $2\pi(4)(15) = 120\pi$; dome: $2\pi(4)^2 = 32\pi$
@answer Total $= 168\pi \approx 527.8\ \text{m}^2$
:::

:::example A block with a hole
A concrete block is $40\ \text{cm} \times 20\ \text{cm} \times 20\ \text{cm}$ with a cylindrical hole of radius $5\ \text{cm}$ through its length. How much concrete is in it?
@step Prism: $40 \times 20 \times 20 = 16\,000\ \text{cm}^3$
@step Hole: $\pi(5)^2(40) = 1000\pi \approx 3141.6\ \text{cm}^3$
@answer $16\,000 - 3141.6 \approx 12\,858.4\ \text{cm}^3$
:::

:::mistake Counting hidden faces
When two solids are joined, the faces that touch are inside the object. Leave them out of the surface area.

## A problem-solving routine

1. **Sketch** the object and label every dimension you know.
2. **Decide** what is being asked: surface area (covering) or volume (filling)?
3. **Break** composite objects into simple solids.
4. **Calculate** each part, keeping $\pi$ exact until the end.
5. **Check** the units (square vs cubic) and whether the answer is reasonable.

:::summary
- Sphere: $V = \tfrac43\pi r^3$; hemisphere $V = \tfrac23\pi r^3$.
- To find a missing dimension, substitute and solve, finishing with a square or cube root when needed.
- Composite volume: add the parts (subtract holes).
- Composite surface area: add exposed surfaces only.
:::
