A recipe calls for 750 mL, a trail is 3.4 km long, a board is 89 inches. Converting between units of the same system is a skill you will use for the rest of your life. The key idea is **proportional reasoning**: multiplying by a fraction that equals 1.

## Conversion factors are equal to 1

Because $100\ \text{cm} = 1\ \text{m}$, the fractions

$$\frac{100\ \text{cm}}{1\ \text{m}} \quad\text{and}\quad \frac{1\ \text{m}}{100\ \text{cm}}$$

both equal 1. Multiplying a measurement by 1 does not change its size — only its units. Choose the fraction that **cancels the unit you start with**.

:::definition Unit analysis
**Unit analysis** means writing units at every step and cancelling them like factors. If the units you are left with are the ones you want, your set-up is correct.
:::

:::example Converting kilometres to metres
Convert $3.75\ \text{km}$ to metres.
@step $1\ \text{km} = 1000\ \text{m}$, so multiply by $\dfrac{1000\ \text{m}}{1\ \text{km}}$.
@step $3.75\ \text{km} \times \dfrac{1000\ \text{m}}{1\ \text{km}} = 3750\ \text{m}$
@answer $3.75\ \text{km} = 3750\ \text{m}$
:::

:::tip Bigger or smaller?
Converting to a **smaller** unit gives a **bigger** number (multiply). Converting to a **larger** unit gives a **smaller** number (divide). Ask yourself this before you calculate.
:::

## Converting within SI

Every SI step is a power of 10, so conversions move the decimal point.

| From → to | Multiply by |
|---|---|
| km → m | 1000 |
| m → cm | 100 |
| cm → mm | 10 |
| m → mm | 1000 |
| km → cm | 100 000 |

:::example Millimetres to metres
Convert $4560\ \text{mm}$ to metres.
@step $4560\ \text{mm} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{m}}{1000\ \text{mm}}$
@answer $= 4.56\ \text{m}$
:::

## Converting within imperial

Imperial relationships are not powers of 10, so you must know them: $12\ \text{in} = 1\ \text{ft}$, $3\ \text{ft} = 1\ \text{yd}$, $36\ \text{in} = 1\ \text{yd}$, $5280\ \text{ft} = 1\ \text{mi}$.

:::example Mixed units to a single unit
A student is $5\ \text{ft}\ 9\ \text{in}$ tall. What is the height in inches?
@step $5\ \text{ft} = 5 \times 12 = 60\ \text{in}$
@step $60 + 9 = 69$
@answer $69\ \text{in}$
:::

:::example A single unit to mixed units
A board is $89\ \text{in}$ long. Express its length in feet and inches.
@step $89 \div 12 = 7$ remainder $5$
@step The quotient is the number of feet; the remainder is the extra inches.
@answer $89\ \text{in} = 7\ \text{ft}\ 5\ \text{in}$
:::

:::mistake Writing a decimal as feet and inches
$89 \div 12 = 7.41\overline{6}$, but that does **not** mean 7 ft 4 in. The decimal part is a fraction of a foot: $0.41\overline{6} \times 12 = 5$ inches.
:::

## Converting square and cubic units

A square that is $1\ \text{m}$ on each side is $100\ \text{cm}$ on each side, so its area is

$$1\ \text{m}^2 = 100\ \text{cm} \times 100\ \text{cm} = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2.$$

For area, **square** the linear conversion factor. For volume, **cube** it:

$$1\ \text{m}^3 = 100^3\ \text{cm}^3 = 1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3, \qquad 1\ \text{ft}^2 = 12^2\ \text{in}^2 = 144\ \text{in}^2, \qquad 1\ \text{yd}^3 = 3^3\ \text{ft}^3 = 27\ \text{ft}^3.$$

:::example Area conversion
A rug has an area of $2.5\ \text{m}^2$. What is its area in square centimetres?
@step $1\ \text{m}^2 = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$
@step $2.5 \times 10\,000 = 25\,000$
@answer $25\,000\ \text{cm}^2$
:::

:::example Volume conversion
A garden bed needs $4\ \text{yd}^3$ of soil. How many cubic feet is that?
@step $1\ \text{yd}^3 = 27\ \text{ft}^3$
@answer $4 \times 27 = 108\ \text{ft}^3$
:::

:::mistake Using the linear factor for area
$1\ \text{ft}^2$ is **not** $12\ \text{in}^2$. Picture the square: it is 12 in by 12 in, so it contains $144$ one-inch squares.
:::

:::summary
- Conversion factors are fractions equal to 1; choose the one that cancels the starting unit.
- Smaller unit → bigger number (multiply); larger unit → smaller number (divide).
- Know the imperial facts: 12 in = 1 ft, 3 ft = 1 yd, 5280 ft = 1 mi.
- For mixed units, divide and use the remainder.
- Square the factor for area units and cube it for volume units.
:::
