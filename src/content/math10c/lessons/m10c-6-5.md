How far apart are two points on a grid? Where is the point exactly halfway between them? The **distance formula** and the **midpoint formula** answer these questions — and both come from ideas you already know.

## The distance formula

Join two points $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$ and draw a right triangle with a horizontal leg and a vertical leg.

:::figure

:::

The horizontal leg is $x_2 - x_1$ and the vertical leg is $y_2 - y_1$. By the Pythagorean theorem:

:::definition Distance formula
$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$
:::

:::example Finding a distance
Find the distance between $A(1, 2)$ and $B(7, 6)$.
@step $d = \sqrt{(7 - 1)^2 + (6 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 16} = \sqrt{52}$
@answer $d = 2\sqrt{13} \approx 7.2$
:::

:::tip Order does not matter here
Because the differences are squared, $(1 - 7)^2 = (7 - 1)^2$. Just be consistent.
:::

## The midpoint formula

The midpoint is halfway in both directions, so its coordinates are the **averages** of the endpoints’ coordinates.

:::definition Midpoint formula
$$M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\ \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$$
:::

:::example Finding a midpoint
Find the midpoint of the segment from $(-3, 8)$ to $(6, -2)$.
@step $x$: $\dfrac{-3 + 6}{2} = \dfrac32$
@step $y$: $\dfrac{8 + (-2)}{2} = 3$
@answer $M\left(\tfrac32, 3\right) = (1.5, 3)$
:::

## Finding an endpoint

If you know the midpoint and one endpoint, the trip from the endpoint to the midpoint is the same as the trip from the midpoint to the other endpoint.

:::example Finding the other endpoint
$M(2, -1)$ is the midpoint of $\overline{AB}$ and $A$ is $(-4, 3)$. Find $B$.
@step From $A$ to $M$: $x$ increases by 6, $y$ decreases by 4.
@step Do the same from $M$: $(2 + 6, -1 - 4)$
@answer $B(8, -5)$
:::

## Solving geometry problems

:::example Classifying a triangle
Is the triangle with vertices $P(0, 0)$, $Q(4, 2)$ and $R(-2, 4)$ a right triangle?
@step $PQ^2 = 16 + 4 = 20$, $PR^2 = 4 + 16 = 20$, $QR^2 = 36 + 4 = 40$
@answer $20 + 20 = 40$, so it is a right triangle (and isosceles, since $PQ = PR$).
:::

:::example The centre of a circle
A diameter of a circle has endpoints $(-1, 5)$ and $(7, -1)$. Find the centre and the radius.
@step Centre = midpoint: $\left(\dfrac{-1 + 7}{2}, \dfrac{5 + (-1)}{2}\right) = (3, 2)$
@step Radius = distance from centre to an endpoint: $\sqrt{(7 - 3)^2 + (-1 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$
:::

:::mistake Adding coordinates in the distance formula
The distance formula uses **differences**: $d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$. The midpoint formula uses **sums** divided by 2: $M = \left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$. Mixing the two is the most common error.
:::

:::mistake Losing the sign when subtracting a negative
For $(-3, 2)$ and $(5, -4)$: $x_2 - x_1 = 5 - (-3) = 8$, not $2$. Use brackets around every coordinate you substitute.
:::

:::summary
- Distance: $d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$ — the Pythagorean theorem on a grid.
- Midpoint: average the $x$-coordinates and average the $y$-coordinates.
- To find an endpoint, repeat the move from the known endpoint to the midpoint.
- Use distances to classify triangles and midpoints to find centres.
:::
