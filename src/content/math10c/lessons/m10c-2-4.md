Trigonometry lets you measure things you cannot reach: the height of a cliff, the width of a river, the distance to a ship. This is called **indirect measurement**.

## Angles of elevation and depression

:::definition Angle of elevation
The angle measured **up** from the horizontal to an object above you.
:::

:::definition Angle of depression
The angle measured **down** from the horizontal to an object below you.
:::

Because the two horizontal lines are parallel, the angle of depression from the top equals the angle of elevation from the bottom (they are **alternate angles**). So you can always place the angle at the bottom of your triangle.

:::example Angle of depression
From the top of an $80\ \text{m}$ cliff, the angle of depression to a boat is $25^\circ$. How far is the boat from the base of the cliff?
@step The angle of elevation from the boat is also $25^\circ$.
@step $\tan 25^\circ = \dfrac{80}{x}$, so $x = \dfrac{80}{\tan 25^\circ}$
@answer $x \approx 171.6\ \text{m}$
:::

## Using a clinometer

A **clinometer** measures angles of elevation. To find a tree’s height:

1. Stand a measured distance $d$ from the tree.
2. Sight the top and read the angle $\theta$.
3. Height above your eye $= d\tan\theta$.
4. **Add your eye height**, since the angle was measured from your eye, not the ground.

:::example Indirect measurement
Ella stands $20\ \text{m}$ from a flagpole and measures an angle of elevation of $38^\circ$ to the top. Her eyes are $1.6\ \text{m}$ above the ground. How tall is the flagpole?
@step Height above eye level: $20\tan 38^\circ \approx 15.6\ \text{m}$
@answer Flagpole $\approx 15.6 + 1.6 = 17.2\ \text{m}$
:::

## Problems with two right triangles

Many real problems involve two right triangles that share a side. Solve them **one triangle at a time**: use the triangle where you know enough, find the shared side, then move to the other triangle.

:::example Observers on opposite sides
Two people stand on opposite sides of a $50\ \text{m}$ tower. Their angles of elevation to the top are $40^\circ$ and $55^\circ$. How far apart are they?
@step Left triangle: $d_1 = \dfrac{50}{\tan 40^\circ} \approx 59.59\ \text{m}$
@step Right triangle: $d_2 = \dfrac{50}{\tan 55^\circ} \approx 35.01\ \text{m}$
@answer Distance apart $\approx 59.59 + 35.01 \approx 94.6\ \text{m}$
:::

:::example Two boats on the same side
From the top of a $60\ \text{m}$ lighthouse, the angles of depression to two boats in line are $45^\circ$ and $30^\circ$. How far apart are the boats?
@step Near boat: $\dfrac{60}{\tan 45^\circ} = 60\ \text{m}$
@step Far boat: $\dfrac{60}{\tan 30^\circ} \approx 103.92\ \text{m}$
@answer The boats are about $103.92 - 60 = 43.9\ \text{m}$ apart.
:::

:::example A flagpole on a building
From $30\ \text{m}$ away, the angle of elevation to the base of a flagpole on a roof is $35^\circ$, and to its top is $42^\circ$. How tall is the flagpole?
@step To the top: $30\tan 42^\circ \approx 27.01\ \text{m}$
@step To the roof: $30\tan 35^\circ \approx 21.01\ \text{m}$
@answer Flagpole $\approx 27.01 - 21.01 = 6.0\ \text{m}$
:::

:::tip Draw and label first
Sketch both triangles, mark the right angles, label what you know and what you want, and decide whether to **add** or **subtract** the pieces.

:::summary
- Elevation is measured up from the horizontal; depression is measured down. They are equal by alternate angles.
- With a clinometer, add eye height to the calculated height.
- For two triangles, solve one at a time through the shared side, then add or subtract.
:::
