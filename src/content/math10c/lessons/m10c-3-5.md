Exponents are shorthand for repeated multiplication. In this lesson you will review the exponent laws and discover, through patterns, what zero and negative exponents must mean.

## The exponent laws

For non-zero bases $a$ and $b$ and integers $m$ and $n$:

| Law | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Product | $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$ | $x^3 \cdot x^4 = x^7$ |
| Quotient | $\dfrac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$ | $\dfrac{x^8}{x^3} = x^5$ |
| Power of a power | $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$ | $(x^2)^5 = x^{10}$ |
| Power of a product | $(ab)^m = a^m b^m$ | $(2x)^3 = 8x^3$ |
| Power of a quotient | $\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^n = \dfrac{a^n}{b^n}$ | $\left(\dfrac{x}{3}\right)^2 = \dfrac{x^2}{9}$ |

:::mistake Apply the exponent to everything in the brackets
$(3x^2)^2 = 9x^4$, not $3x^4$. The coefficient is part of the product inside the brackets.
:::

## The zero exponent

Look at the pattern as the exponent goes down by 1 — each step **divides by 2**:

$$2^3 = 8,\quad 2^2 = 4,\quad 2^1 = 2,\quad 2^0 = \ ?$$

Continuing the pattern, $2^0 = 2 \div 2 = 1$. The quotient law agrees: $\dfrac{a^n}{a^n} = a^{n-n} = a^0$, and anything divided by itself is 1.

:::definition Zero exponent
$a^0 = 1$ for any $a \neq 0$.
:::

:::mistake Where is the base?
$-5^0 = -(5^0) = -1$, but $(-5)^0 = 1$. The exponent only applies to what is directly in front of it.
:::

## Negative exponents

Keep going with the pattern:

$$2^0 = 1,\quad 2^{-1} = \frac12,\quad 2^{-2} = \frac14,\quad 2^{-3} = \frac18$$

:::definition Negative exponent
$a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}$ and $\dfrac{1}{a^{-n}} = a^n$ for $a \neq 0$. A negative exponent means **reciprocal**, not negative.
:::

:::example Evaluating powers
Evaluate (a) $4^{-2}$ (b) $\left(\tfrac23\right)^{-3}$ (c) $(-2)^{-3}$.
@step (a) $4^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{4^2} = \dfrac{1}{16}$
@step (b) $\left(\tfrac23\right)^{-3} = \left(\tfrac32\right)^3 = \dfrac{27}{8}$
@step (c) $(-2)^{-3} = \dfrac{1}{(-2)^3} = -\dfrac18$
:::

## Simplifying expressions

Apply the laws, then write the final answer with **positive exponents only**.

:::example Products and quotients
Simplify $\dfrac{(2x^{-2}y^3)^3}{4x^{-5}y}$.
@step Power of a product: $(2x^{-2}y^3)^3 = 8x^{-6}y^9$
@step Divide: coefficients $\tfrac84 = 2$; $x^{-6-(-5)} = x^{-1}$; $y^{9-1} = y^8$
@answer $2x^{-1}y^8 = \dfrac{2y^8}{x}$
:::

:::example A product
Simplify $(3a^{-4}b^2)(-2a^5b^{-6})$.
@step Coefficients: $3 \times (-2) = -6$
@step $a^{-4+5} = a^1$, $b^{2-6} = b^{-4}$
@answer $-6ab^{-4} = -\dfrac{6a}{b^4}$
:::

:::tip Order of work
Simplify inside brackets → apply outside exponents → multiply/divide like bases → move negative exponents across the fraction bar.

:::summary
- Product: add exponents. Quotient: subtract. Power of a power: multiply.
- $a^0 = 1$ and $a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}$ — patterns show why.
- An exponent applies to everything in the brackets it is attached to.
- Final answers use positive exponents.
:::
