A warehouse stacks boxes, a mason stacks concrete blocks and a rail yard stacks containers. To plan or describe a stack, workers use **views**: flat pictures of what you see from the top, the front and the side. In this lesson you will read and draw views of structures made of cubes.

## Top views and counting cubes

:::definition Views
A **top view** shows what you see looking straight down. A **front view** shows what you see from the front, and a **side view** shows what you see from the right side. Each view is flat: it shows width and height, or width and depth, but never all three.
:::

A top view of a stack of cubes can carry numbers. Each number tells how many cubes are stacked in that spot. In this lesson the front of the stack is at the **bottom** of the table.

:::figure

:::

:::example Counting boxes in a warehouse
Cube-shaped boxes are stacked as in the top view above. How many boxes are there?
@step Back row: $3 + 1 + 2 = 6$
@step Front row: $2 + 1 + 1 = 4$
@step Total: $6 + 4 = 10$
@answer There are $10$ boxes.
:::

:::tip Hidden cubes
From the front or the side you cannot see every cube, because some hide behind others. A top view with numbers shows every stack, so it is the best view for counting.
:::

## Front and side views

Picture standing in front of the stack in the top view above. Each **column** of the table (Left, Centre, Right) becomes one column of squares in the front view. You see only the **tallest** stack in each column, because shorter stacks hide behind it.

For the right side view, walk around to the right. Each **row** of the table becomes one column of squares, and now the **front row is on your left**.

:::example Front and right side views
Use the top view above. Find the heights in the front view and in the right side view.
@step Left column: stacks $3$ and $2$, tallest $3$. Centre: $1$ and $1$, tallest $1$. Right: $2$ and $1$, tallest $2$.
@step Front view, left to right: $3$, $1$, $2$.
@step Front row: $2$, $1$, $1$, tallest $2$. Back row: $3$, $1$, $2$, tallest $3$.
@step Right side view, left to right (front row first): $2$, $3$.
@answer The front view has columns of $3$, $1$ and $2$ squares. The right side view has columns of $2$ and $3$ squares.
:::

## Isometric drawings

:::definition Isometric drawing
An **isometric drawing** shows the top, the front and one side of an object in one picture. Vertical edges stay vertical, and the other edges slant at $30^\circ$. Edges that are equal in real life are drawn equal, so you can measure from the drawing.
:::

An isometric drawing shows what an object looks like. The separate views give the exact shape of each face, which is what a builder needs.

:::example Views of a crate
The crate in the drawing is $5\ \text{ft}$ long across the front, $3\ \text{ft}$ deep and $2.5\ \text{ft}$ tall. Describe its top, front and side views.
@step Top view: looking down, you see the length and the depth: a rectangle $5\ \text{ft}$ by $3\ \text{ft}$.
@step Front view: you see the length and the height: a rectangle $5\ \text{ft}$ by $2.5\ \text{ft}$.
@step Side view: you see the depth and the height: a rectangle $3\ \text{ft}$ by $2.5\ \text{ft}$.
@answer Top: $5$ by $3$. Front: $5$ by $2.5$. Side: $3$ by $2.5$ (all in feet).
:::

## Working backward from views

Sometimes you are given the front and side views and must find a top view that fits. Test each column and each row.

:::example Fewest and most cubes
A structure on a $2$ by $2$ base has the front and right side views shown. What are the fewest and the most cubes it could have?
@step The side view says the front row is $3$ tall and the back row is $1$ tall.
@step The front view says the Left column is $3$ tall. The back row is only $1$ tall, so the $3$ must be front-left.
@step The Right column is $2$ tall, so the $2$ must be front-right.
@step The back row needs at least one cube and at most $1$ in each spot: $1$ or $2$ cubes in all.
@answer Fewest: $3 + 2 + 1 = 6$ cubes. Most: $3 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 7$ cubes.
:::

:::tip Two views are not always enough
In the example, two different structures have the same front and side views. That is why plans for a build show a top view with heights, or three views together.
:::

:::check m203-views 3
:::

:::summary
- A top view is seen from above, a front view from the front and a side view from the right side.
- In a top view with numbers, add all the numbers to count the cubes.
- Front view: the tallest stack in each column, left to right. Right side view: the tallest stack in each row, with the front row on the left.
- An isometric drawing shows three faces at once; vertical edges stay vertical and the others slant at $30^\circ$.
- Two views can fit more than one structure, so check every column and row when you work backward.
:::
