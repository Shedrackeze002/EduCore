A roof truss, a staircase with a landing, a survey with several sightings, a pipe that must fit inside a cube van: each of these is a chain of right triangles. Solve them one triangle at a time, and use what you find in one triangle to unlock the next.

## A plan for chains of triangles

The plan from two-triangle problems still works. You just use it more than once.

1. **Sketch** the situation and number the right triangles $1$, $2$, $3$.
2. **Mark the shared sides.** A side found in one triangle is a known side in the next.
3. **Start** with a triangle where you know enough: an angle and a side, or two sides.
4. **Find the shared side first**, then move along the chain.
5. **Keep all the digits** until the final answer, and check that each hypotenuse is the longest side in its triangle.

:::tip Name what you find
Give every side you calculate a letter ($h$, $d$, $k$ ...) and write its value beside the sketch. It keeps the chain organized and makes your work easy to check.
:::

:::example Three survey stakes
Stakes P, Q and R are in a line going away from the base of a $60\ \text{m}$ communications tower. The angles of elevation to the top are $55^\circ$ from P, $38^\circ$ from Q and $24^\circ$ from R. Find the distances $x$ (P to Q) and $y$ (Q to R), to the nearest tenth of a metre.
@step All three triangles share the $60$ m height, which is opposite each angle. Each distance from the base is adjacent: TOA.
@step P: $\dfrac{60}{\tan 55^\circ} \approx 42.01\ \text{m}$; Q: $\dfrac{60}{\tan 38^\circ} \approx 76.80\ \text{m}$; R: $\dfrac{60}{\tan 24^\circ} \approx 134.76\ \text{m}$
@step $x \approx 76.80 - 42.01 = 34.79$ and $y \approx 134.76 - 76.80 = 57.96$
@answer $x \approx 34.8\ \text{m}$ and $y \approx 58.0\ \text{m}$.

## Roof trusses

:::definition Parts of a truss
A **roof truss** is a frame of lumber. Its **bottom chord** is horizontal and its two **top chords** (rafters) meet at the peak. The **king post** stands straight up from the centre of the bottom chord to the peak. Shorter **webs** brace the frame.
:::

In the truss below, two vertical webs stand one-third of the span in from each end, and two diagonal webs run from the top of each vertical web down to the foot of the king post.

:::example King post and diagonal web
A truss spans $24\ \text{ft}$ and each rafter makes a $26^\circ$ angle with the bottom chord. Find the king post $k$ and a diagonal web $w$, to the nearest tenth of a foot.
@step Triangle 1 (half the truss): half the span is $12$ ft, adjacent to the $26^\circ$ angle, and $k$ is opposite. $k = 12\tan 26^\circ \approx 5.85\ \text{ft}$
@step Triangle 2 (out to the vertical web): the web is $24 \div 3 = 8$ ft from the end. $v = 8\tan 26^\circ \approx 3.90\ \text{ft}$
@step Triangle 3 (the diagonal web): its legs are $v$ and the gap to the king post, $12 - 8 = 4$ ft. $w = \sqrt{4^2 + 3.90^2} \approx 5.59\ \text{ft}$
@answer $k \approx 5.9\ \text{ft}$ and $w \approx 5.6\ \text{ft}$.

:::example Two flights and a landing
Every step has a riser of $18\ \text{cm}$ and a tread of $27\ \text{cm}$. The first flight rises $108\ \text{cm}$, the landing is $100\ \text{cm}$ long, and the second flight rises $90\ \text{cm}$. Find the angle of the steps, the total length of the stringers on one side, and the angle $\theta$ of a straight line from the bottom of the stairs to the top.
@step Triangle 1 (one step): $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{18}{27}$, so $\alpha \approx 33.7^\circ$.
@step Triangles 2 and 3 (the flights): each stringer is a hypotenuse, so $L = \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\sin\alpha}$. $L_1 = \dfrac{108}{\sin\alpha} \approx 194.70$ and $L_2 = \dfrac{90}{\sin\alpha} \approx 162.25$, for a total of about $357\ \text{cm}$.
@step The runs are $\dfrac{108}{\tan\alpha} = 162\ \text{cm}$ and $\dfrac{90}{\tan\alpha} = 135\ \text{cm}$. The large triangle has rise $108 + 90 = 198\ \text{cm}$ and run $162 + 100 + 135 = 397\ \text{cm}$.
@step $\tan\theta = \dfrac{198}{397}$, so $\theta \approx 26.5^\circ$.
@answer The steps make about $33.7^\circ$ with the floor, the stringers total about $357\ \text{cm}$, and the straight line makes about $26.5^\circ$. It is less steep because the landing adds run but no rise.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the landing
The landing is flat. It has no stringer, but it **does** add to the total run of the stairs. Leave it out of the stringers and include it in the large triangle.
:::

## Three dimensions: the diagonal of a box

:::example Will the pipe fit?
The cargo area of a cube van is $4.2\ \text{m}$ long, $2.1\ \text{m}$ wide and $1.9\ \text{m}$ high. Find the longest pipe $p$ that fits, to the nearest hundredth of a metre, and the angle $\theta$ it makes with the floor, to the nearest tenth of a degree.
@step Triangle 1 (the floor): $d^2 = 4.2^2 + 2.1^2 = 22.05$, so $d \approx 4.70\ \text{m}$
@step Triangle 2 (standing up): $p^2 = d^2 + 1.9^2 = 22.05 + 3.61 = 25.66$, so $p \approx 5.07\ \text{m}$
@step In triangle 2, the height is opposite $\theta$ and $d$ is adjacent: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{1.9}{4.70}$, so $\theta \approx 22.0^\circ$
@answer A pipe up to about $5.07\ \text{m}$ long fits, sloping at about $22.0^\circ$ to the floor.
:::

:::summary
- Number the triangles and mark the sides they share. A side found in one triangle becomes a known side in the next.
- Start with a triangle where you know an angle and a side, or two sides. Find the shared side first.
- Survey sightings share a height; truss triangles share chords and webs; stair flights share the step angle.
- For a box, find the floor diagonal first, then use it as a leg of the standing triangle.
- Keep all the digits until the end, and check each hypotenuse is the longest side.
:::
