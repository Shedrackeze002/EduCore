A gazebo roof is a pyramid, a grain-bin roof is a cone, and a water tower may be a sphere. These shapes come to a point or curve all the way around, so you need one new measurement to find their surface area: the **slant height**.

## Pyramids and slant height

:::definition Pyramid and slant height
A **pyramid** has a polygon base and triangular faces that meet at a point called the **apex**. The **slant height** $s$ is the height of one triangular face, measured from the middle of a base edge straight up the face to the apex.
:::

A square pyramid unfolds into a square and four matching triangles. Each triangle has an area of $\tfrac12 b s$, so

$$SA = b^2 + 4 \times \tfrac12 b s = b^2 + 2bs$$

:::example A glass paperweight
A solid glass paperweight is a square pyramid with a base side of $10\ \text{cm}$ and a slant height of $13\ \text{cm}$. Find its surface area.
@step Base: $10 \times 10 = 100\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Four triangles: $4 \times \tfrac12 \times 10 \times 13 = 260\ \text{cm}^2$
@answer $SA = 100 + 260 = 360\ \text{cm}^2$
:::

For a **roof**, leave out the base: only the triangles are covered. A rectangular pyramid has two pairs of matching triangles, each pair with its own slant height.

## Cones

A cone unfolds into a circle (the base) and a sector of a bigger circle (the curved side). The curved side has an area of $\pi r s$, where $r$ is the radius of the base and $s$ is the slant height.

$$SA = \pi r^2 + \pi r s$$

:::example A cone-shaped roof
A round gazebo has a cone-shaped roof with a diameter of $12\ \text{ft}$ and a slant height of $8\ \text{ft}$. How much roofing covers it, to the nearest square foot?
@step A roof has no base, so use the curved area only.
@step $r = 12 \div 2 = 6\ \text{ft}$
@step $A = \pi r s = \pi(6)(8) = 48\pi \approx 150.80$
@answer About $151\ \text{ft}^2$ of roofing
:::

## When you know the height, not the slant height

Plans often give the **height** $h$: the straight-up distance from the centre of the base to the apex. The slant height is longer. The height, the slant height and a distance across the base form a right triangle, so use the Pythagorean theorem:

- Cone: $s = \sqrt{h^2 + r^2}$
- Square pyramid: $s = \sqrt{h^2 + \left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2}$

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"cone","labels":{"d":"10 in","h":"12 in","s":"s"}}
:::

:::example A sheet-metal funnel
A funnel is a cone with no base. It has a diameter of $10\ \text{in}$ and a height of $12\ \text{in}$. How much sheet metal makes it, to the nearest square inch?
@step $r = 10 \div 2 = 5\ \text{in}$
@step $s = \sqrt{12^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{169} = 13\ \text{in}$
@step Curved side: $\pi r s = \pi(5)(13) = 65\pi \approx 204.20$
@answer About $204\ \text{in}^2$
:::

:::example A pyramid roof
The roof of a square pump house is a square pyramid with a base side of $6\ \text{m}$. The peak is $2\ \text{m}$ above the base. How much roofing covers the four faces, to the nearest tenth of a square metre?
@step Half the base: $6 \div 2 = 3\ \text{m}$
@step $s = \sqrt{2^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{13} \approx 3.606\ \text{m}$
@step Four triangles: $4 \times \tfrac12 \times 6 \times \sqrt{13} \approx 43.27$
@answer About $43.3\ \text{m}^2$ of roofing
:::

:::mistake Height is not slant height
The height goes straight down **inside** the shape; the slant height runs **along a face**. Using the height $2$ in place of $s \approx 3.606$ for the roof above gives only $24\ \text{m}^2$, and the crew runs out of roofing. Check which one the plan gives, and use the Pythagorean theorem when you need $s$.
:::

## Spheres and hemispheres

:::definition Sphere and hemisphere
A **sphere** is a ball: every point on it is the same distance $r$ from the centre. A **hemisphere** is half of a sphere.
:::

The surface area of a sphere is exactly four times the area of a circle with the same radius. A hemisphere has half of that as its curved surface:

$$SA_{\text{sphere}} = 4\pi r^2 \qquad \text{curved part of a hemisphere} = 2\pi r^2$$

A **solid** hemisphere also has a flat circle, so its total surface area is $2\pi r^2 + \pi r^2 = 3\pi r^2$.

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"sphere","labels":{"d":"24 cm"}}
:::

:::example A basketball
A basketball has a diameter of $24\ \text{cm}$. Find its surface area to the nearest square centimetre.
@step $r = 24 \div 2 = 12\ \text{cm}$
@step $SA = 4\pi(12)^2 = 576\pi \approx 1809.56$
@answer About $1810\ \text{cm}^2$
:::

:::example A dome roof
A planetarium dome is a hemisphere with a diameter of $10\ \text{m}$. How much roofing covers the dome, to the nearest tenth of a square metre?
@step $r = 10 \div 2 = 5\ \text{m}$. Only the curved part is roofed.
@step $2\pi r^2 = 2\pi(5)^2 = 50\pi \approx 157.08$
@answer About $157.1\ \text{m}^2$
:::

:::summary
- The slant height $s$ runs along a face; the height $h$ goes straight down inside. Surface area uses $s$.
- Square pyramid: $SA = b^2 + 4 \times \tfrac12 b s$. Leave out the base for a roof.
- Cone: $SA = \pi r^2 + \pi r s$. The curved part alone is $\pi r s$.
- If you know the height, find the slant height first: $s = \sqrt{h^2 + r^2}$ or $s = \sqrt{h^2 + (b/2)^2}$.
- Sphere: $SA = 4\pi r^2$. Hemisphere: curved part $2\pi r^2$, or $3\pi r^2$ with the flat base.
:::
