A chequing account is where your pay lands and your bills leave. Banks and credit unions charge for that service, and choosing the right plan can save you a hundred dollars or more a year. Keeping your own record and checking it against the bank’s statement catches mistakes before they cost you.

## Account plans and service charges

:::definition Chequing account, transaction and service charge
A **chequing account** is for everyday money: deposits, debit purchases, bill payments, e-transfers and cheques. Each of these is a **transaction**. A **service charge** is a fee the bank or credit union charges for the account, usually a monthly fee plus a fee for each transaction over the number included in the plan.
:::

Plans and fees differ from one institution to another and change often, so every question gives you the fees to use.

:::example A limited plan
At Prairie Credit Union, the Everyday plan costs \$4.95 a month, includes 12 transactions, and charges \$1.25 for each extra transaction. Bao made 19 transactions in May. What was the service charge?
@step Extra transactions: $19 - 12 = 7$
@step Cost of the extras: $7 \times 1.25 = 8.75$
@step Service charge: $4.95 + 8.75 = 13.70$
@answer Bao’s service charge for May was \$13.70.
:::

:::mistake Charging for the included transactions
Only the transactions **past** the included ones cost extra. Multiplying all 19 transactions by \$1.25 would give a much bigger, wrong charge.
:::

Watch for other fees too. Using another bank’s ATM can cost twice: your own bank charges a fee, and the owner of the ATM adds its own.

## Comparing plans and the break-even point

An unlimited plan has a bigger monthly fee, but no charge per transaction. Which plan is cheaper depends on how many transactions you make. Northern Bank offers a **Basic** plan at \$3.95 a month with 10 transactions included and \$1.00 for each extra, and an **Unlimited** plan at \$11.95 a month.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Transactions in a month","Basic plan","Unlimited plan"],"rows":[["10","$3.95","$11.95"],["14","$7.95","$11.95"],["18","$11.95","$11.95"],["22","$15.95","$11.95"]]}
:::

:::definition Break-even point
The **break-even point** is the number of transactions where two plans cost the same. Below it, the plan with the smaller monthly fee is cheaper. Above it, the other plan is cheaper.
:::

:::example Finding the break-even point
Find the break-even point for the two Northern Bank plans.
@step Difference in monthly fees: $11.95 - 3.95 = 8.00$
@step Extra transactions to make up the difference: $8.00 \div 1.00 = 8$
@step Add the included transactions: $10 + 8 = 18$
@answer The plans cost the same at 18 transactions. Someone who makes about 22 transactions a month should choose the Unlimited plan; someone who makes 14 should choose Basic.
:::

## Overdrafts

:::definition Overdraft
An account is **overdrawn** when more money goes out than is in it, so the balance drops below zero. **Overdraft protection** lets the payment go through, but the bank usually charges a fee plus interest on the overdrawn amount for each day until it is paid back.
:::

:::example The cost of an overdraft
Liam has \$210.40 in his account when a \$485.00 rent payment goes through. His bank charges a \$5 overdraft fee plus interest of 21% per year on the overdrawn amount, charged daily (amount × 0.21 ÷ 365 × days). The account stays overdrawn for 9 days. What does the overdraft cost?
@step Overdrawn by: $485.00 - 210.40 = 274.60$
@step Interest: $274.60 \times 0.21 \div 365 \times 9 \approx 1.42$
@step Total cost: $5 + 1.42 = 6.42$
@answer The overdraft costs Liam \$6.42.
:::

## Reading a statement

A bank **statement** lists every transaction in a month. It starts with the **opening balance**, adds deposits, subtracts withdrawals, and ends with the **closing balance**.

$$\text{closing balance} = \text{opening balance} + \text{deposits} - \text{withdrawals}$$

:::example Checking the closing balance
Use totals to check the closing balance in the statement above.
@step Deposits: $1{,}125.60$
@step Withdrawals: $87.42 + 65.00 + 4.95 = 157.37$
@step Closing balance: $642.15 + 1{,}125.60 - 157.37 = 1{,}610.38$
@answer The closing balance is \$1,610.38, which matches the statement.

:::tip Keep a running balance
Work down the table one line at a time: add each deposit and subtract each withdrawal. A running balance shows you right away if a payment would overdraw the account.

## Reconciling an account

Your own record of the account is called a **register**. At the end of the month, it rarely matches the statement exactly, because of timing.

:::definition Reconciling
To **reconcile** an account, adjust the statement for things the bank has not seen yet, and adjust the register for things you have not recorded yet. When both adjusted balances match, the account is reconciled.
- **Outstanding cheques** have been written but not yet cashed, so subtract them from the statement balance.
- **Deposits not yet on the statement** are added to the statement balance.
- **Fees and interest** on the statement but not in the register are subtracted from (fees) or added to (interest) the register.
:::

:::example Reconciling for June
Ngozi’s statement shows \$1,482.35. Cheques #112 for \$250.00 and #114 for \$86.40 are outstanding, and a \$400.00 deposit made on June 30 is not on the statement. Her register shows \$1,550.40, but she has not recorded the \$4.95 monthly fee or \$0.50 interest. Reconcile the account.
@step Adjusted statement balance: $1{,}482.35 + 400.00 - 250.00 - 86.40 = 1{,}545.95$
@step Corrected register balance: $1{,}550.40 - 4.95 + 0.50 = 1{,}545.95$
@answer Both balances are \$1,545.95, so the account is reconciled.
:::

:::mistake Adjusting the wrong record
Outstanding cheques and late deposits change the **statement** balance, because the bank has not seen them yet. Fees and interest change the **register**, because you have not recorded them yet. Mixing these up means the two balances will not match.
:::

:::check m203-bank-reconcile 3
:::

:::summary
- Service charge = monthly fee + (extra transactions × fee for each). Only transactions past the included ones cost extra.
- The break-even point is where two plans cost the same: (difference in monthly fees) ÷ (fee per extra transaction), plus the included transactions.
- An overdraft costs a fee plus daily interest: amount × rate ÷ 365 × days.
- Closing balance = opening balance + deposits − withdrawals.
- Reconcile: statement + deposits not shown − outstanding cheques = register − fees + interest.
:::
