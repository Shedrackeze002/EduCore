A painter quotes a job, a roofer orders shingles, and an HVAC crew prices sheet metal. Each one starts with a surface area, changes it to the right unit, then works out how much to buy and what it costs.

## Converting square units

Area units are squares, so the length conversion is **squared**. One square foot is $12\ \text{in}$ by $12\ \text{in}$:

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":12,"yMin":0,"yMax":12,"step":1},"hideTickLabels":true,"caption":"One square foot is 12 in by 12 in: it holds 144 squares of one square inch"}
:::

| Length | Area |
|---|---|
| $1\ \text{ft} = 12\ \text{in}$ | $1\ \text{ft}^2 = 12 \times 12 = 144\ \text{in}^2$ |
| $1\ \text{yd} = 3\ \text{ft}$ | $1\ \text{yd}^2 = 3 \times 3 = 9\ \text{ft}^2$ |
| $1\ \text{m} = 100\ \text{cm}$ | $1\ \text{m}^2 = 100 \times 100 = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$ |

To change to a **smaller** unit, multiply. To change to a **bigger** unit, divide.

:::example Sheet metal in square feet
A duct needs $3456\ \text{in}^2$ of sheet metal. How many square feet is that?
@step Square feet are bigger, so divide by $144$.
@step $3456 \div 144 = 24$
@answer $24\ \text{ft}^2$
:::

:::example Glass in square centimetres
A window pane has an area of $2.5\ \text{m}^2$. How many square centimetres is that?
@step Square centimetres are smaller, so multiply by $10\,000$.
@answer $2.5 \times 10\,000 = 25\,000\ \text{cm}^2$
:::

:::mistake Using the length factor for area
$3456\ \text{in}^2$ is **not** $3456 \div 12 = 288\ \text{ft}^2$. Dividing by 12 converts only one side of each square. A square foot holds $12 \times 12 = 144$ square inches, so divide by 144. In the same way, $1\ \text{m}^2$ is $10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$, not $100\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::check m203-sa-apply 1
:::

## How much to buy

Paint, shingles and sheet metal are sold in whole cans, bundles and sheets. Divide the area by what one unit covers, then **round up**, even when the decimal is small. A part-can is still a can you have to buy.

:::example Painting a shed
Rosalie is painting the four outside walls of a shed that is $10\ \text{ft}$ long, $8\ \text{ft}$ wide and $8\ \text{ft}$ high. The $3\ \text{ft}$ by $7\ \text{ft}$ door is not painted. The walls need two coats, and one can covers $350\ \text{ft}^2$. How many cans should she buy?
@step Four walls: $2(10 + 8) \times 8 = 288\ \text{ft}^2$
@step Subtract the door: $288 - 21 = 267\ \text{ft}^2$
@step Two coats: $267 \times 2 = 534\ \text{ft}^2$
@step Cans: $534 \div 350 \approx 1.53$
@answer Round up: $2$ cans
:::

:::tip Coats and waste
Two coats means covering the area twice. Many trades also add 10% for waste, seams and overlaps: multiply the area by $1.1$ before you divide.
:::

:::check m203-sa-apply 2
:::

## Material cost

When a price is given per square unit, **cost = area × price per square unit**. Round the area the way the question asks, then multiply. If tax applies, the question gives the rate; multiply by $1.05$ for 5% GST, for example.

:::example Insulating a tank
Kwame wraps the curved side of a cylindrical tank with insulation. The tank has a diameter of $1.8\ \text{m}$ and a height of $3.2\ \text{m}$. Insulation costs \$14.50 per square metre. Find the area to the nearest tenth of a square metre, then the cost.
@step Curved side: $A = \pi d h = \pi(1.8)(3.2) \approx 18.10$, so about $18.1\ \text{m}^2$
@step Cost: $18.1 \times 14.50 = 262.45$
@answer The insulation costs \$262.45.
:::

:::check m203-sa-apply 2
:::

## Composite objects

Many real objects are made of two shapes. Find each surface that gets covered and add them. Do not count the surface where two shapes join, or a floor that is not covered.

:::

:::example Painting a grain bin
A steel grain bin is a cylinder $18\ \text{ft}$ across with walls $20\ \text{ft}$ high. Its cone-shaped roof rises $6\ \text{ft}$ above the top of the wall. The wall and roof are painted, but not the floor. One can covers $400\ \text{ft}^2$. How many cans are needed for one coat?
@step Wall (curved side of the cylinder): $\pi d h = \pi(18)(20) = 360\pi \approx 1130.97\ \text{ft}^2$
@step Roof slant height: $r = 9\ \text{ft}$, so $s = \sqrt{9^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{117} \approx 10.817\ \text{ft}$
@step Roof (curved side of the cone): $\pi r s = \pi(9)(10.817) \approx 305.83\ \text{ft}^2$
@step Total: $1130.97 + 305.83 \approx 1436.8\ \text{ft}^2$, and $1436.8 \div 400 \approx 3.59$
@answer Round up: $4$ cans
:::

:::check m203-sa-apply 3
:::

:::summary
- Square the length factor for area: $1\ \text{ft}^2 = 144\ \text{in}^2$, $1\ \text{yd}^2 = 9\ \text{ft}^2$, $1\ \text{m}^2 = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.
- Cans, bundles or sheets to buy = area ÷ coverage of one, always rounded up.
- Cost = area × price per square unit; add tax only at the rate the question gives.
- For composite objects, add only the surfaces that are covered.
:::
