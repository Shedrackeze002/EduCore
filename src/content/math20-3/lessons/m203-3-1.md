A ready-mix driver needs to know how many cubic metres of concrete to bring, and a rancher needs to know how many litres a water tank holds. Both questions are about **volume** and **capacity**, and for prisms and cylinders they come from one simple idea.

## Volume of a prism

:::definition Volume
**Volume** is the amount of space a 3-D object takes up. It is measured in cubic units, such as $\text{cm}^3$, $\text{m}^3$ or $\text{ft}^3$: the number of unit cubes that would fill the object.
:::

Picture filling a box with one layer of unit cubes on the bottom, then stacking layers up to the top. The first layer has as many cubes as the **area of the base**, and the number of layers is the **height**. That idea works for every prism:

$$V = (\text{area of the base}) \times h \qquad \text{Rectangular prism: } V = lwh$$

:::figure

:::

:::example A storage crate
A storage crate is $4\ \text{ft}$ long, $3\ \text{ft}$ wide and $2.5\ \text{ft}$ high. What is its volume?
@step Area of the base: $4 \times 3 = 12\ \text{ft}^2$
@step $V = 12 \times 2.5 = 30$
@answer $V = 30\ \text{ft}^3$
:::

## Triangular and other prisms

The **base** of a prism is the shape that runs all the way through it, even when the prism lies on its side. For a tent or a ramp, the base is the **triangular end**, and you multiply its area by the length of the prism.

:::

:::example An A-frame tent
An A-frame tent has triangular ends with a base of $8\ \text{ft}$ and a height of $6\ \text{ft}$. The tent is $10\ \text{ft}$ long. What volume of air is inside?
@step Area of the triangular end: $\tfrac12 \times 8 \times 6 = 24\ \text{ft}^2$
@step $V = 24 \times 10 = 240$
@answer $V = 240\ \text{ft}^3$
:::

A swimming pool with a sloping floor is a prism too. Its side is a trapezoid, and the pool is that trapezoid pushed across the width.

:::example A pool with a sloping floor
A pool is $12\ \text{m}$ long and $5\ \text{m}$ wide. The floor slopes evenly from $1\ \text{m}$ deep to $2.2\ \text{m}$ deep. What is its volume?
@step Side (a trapezoid): $\tfrac12(1 + 2.2) \times 12 = 1.6 \times 12 = 19.2\ \text{m}^2$
@step $V = 19.2 \times 5 = 96$
@answer $V = 96\ \text{m}^3$
:::

## Volume and capacity

:::definition Capacity
**Capacity** is the amount a container can hold, usually measured in millilitres (mL), litres (L) or gallons.
:::

A cube $1\ \text{cm}$ on each side holds exactly $1\ \text{mL}$. A cube $10\ \text{cm}$ on each side holds $1\ \text{L}$, and a cube $1\ \text{m}$ on each side holds $1000\ \text{L}$:

$$1\ \text{cm}^3 = 1\ \text{mL} \qquad 1000\ \text{cm}^3 = 1\ \text{L} \qquad 1\ \text{m}^3 = 1000\ \text{L}$$

For imperial units, this course uses $1\ \text{ft}^3 \approx 6.229$ imperial gallons. Always use the conversion that a question gives you.

:::example An aquarium
The inside of an aquarium is $80\ \text{cm}$ long, $35\ \text{cm}$ wide and $45\ \text{cm}$ deep. How many litres does it hold?
@step $V = 80 \times 35 \times 45 = 126\,000\ \text{cm}^3$
@step $126\,000 \div 1000 = 126$
@answer It holds $126\ \text{L}$.
:::

:::example Soil for a raised bed
A raised garden bed is $8\ \text{ft}$ by $4\ \text{ft}$ and is filled with soil $9\ \text{in}$ deep. Bagged soil holds $1.5\ \text{ft}^3$ per bag. How many bags are needed?
@step Change the depth to feet: $9 \div 12 = 0.75\ \text{ft}$.
@step $V = 8 \times 4 \times 0.75 = 24\ \text{ft}^3$
@step Bags: $24 \div 1.5 = 16$
@answer $16$ bags
:::

:::mistake Mixing units
Multiplying $8\ \text{ft} \times 4\ \text{ft} \times 9\ \text{in}$ as $8 \times 4 \times 9 = 288$ gives a number that is neither cubic feet nor cubic inches. Change every length to the **same unit** before you multiply.
:::

## Cylinders

A cylinder is like a prism with a circular base. The base has an area of $\pi r^2$, so

$$V = \pi r^2 h$$

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"cylinder","labels":{"d":"1.5 m","h":"2 m"}}
:::

:::example A water tank
A cylindrical water tank has an inside diameter of $1.5\ \text{m}$ and a height of $2\ \text{m}$. How many litres does it hold, to the nearest litre?
@step $r = 1.5 \div 2 = 0.75\ \text{m}$
@step $V = \pi(0.75)^2(2) = 1.125\pi \approx 3.5343\ \text{m}^3$
@step $3.5343 \times 1000 \approx 3534.3$
@answer About $3534\ \text{L}$
:::

:::example A stock tank in gallons
A round stock tank is $8\ \text{ft}$ across and holds water $2\ \text{ft}$ deep. Using $1\ \text{ft}^3 \approx 6.229$ imperial gallons, about how many gallons of water are in it?
@step $r = 8 \div 2 = 4\ \text{ft}$
@step $V = \pi(4)^2(2) = 32\pi \approx 100.53\ \text{ft}^3$
@step $100.53 \times 6.229 \approx 626.2$
@answer About $626$ imperial gallons
:::

:::mistake Squaring the diameter
$V = \pi r^2 h$ squares the **radius**. Squaring the diameter makes the answer four times too big: $\pi(1.5)^2(2) \approx 14.1\ \text{m}^3$ instead of about $3.5\ \text{m}^3$ for the tank above.
:::

:::summary
- Volume = area of the base × height, in cubic units. Rectangular prism: $V = lwh$.
- For a triangular or trapezoidal prism, the base is the end shape: multiply its area by the length.
- $1\ \text{cm}^3 = 1\ \text{mL}$, $1000\ \text{cm}^3 = 1\ \text{L}$ and $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1000\ \text{L}$. For imperial capacity, use the conversion given (for example $1\ \text{ft}^3 \approx 6.229$ gallons).
- Cylinder: $V = \pi r^2 h$, with $r$ = diameter ÷ 2.
- Put every length in the same unit before you multiply.
:::
