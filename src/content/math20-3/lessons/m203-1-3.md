Mixing herbicide, spreading fertilizer, buying paint and comparing prices in pounds and kilograms all use the same two tools: **proportions** and **unit analysis**. Set them up carefully and the units will tell you whether your answer makes sense.

## Setting up a proportion

:::definition Proportion
A **proportion** is an equation that says two ratios are equal, such as $\dfrac{150\ \text{mL}}{10\ \text{L}} = \dfrac{x}{65\ \text{L}}$.
:::

Write the two ratios with the **same units in the same places**: top with top, bottom with bottom. Then multiply both sides by the number under $x$.

:::example Mixing herbicide
A herbicide label says to mix $150\ \text{mL}$ of herbicide with every $10\ \text{L}$ of water. How much herbicide goes into a sprayer tank holding $65\ \text{L}$ of water?
@step $\dfrac{150\ \text{mL}}{10\ \text{L}} = \dfrac{x}{65\ \text{L}}$
@step Multiply both sides by $65$: $x = \dfrac{150 \times 65}{10} = 975$
@answer Mix $975\ \text{mL}$ of herbicide into the tank.
:::

:::example Spreading fertilizer
Fertilizer is spread at $2.5\ \text{kg}$ for every $100\ \text{m}^2$ of lawn. How much is needed for a lawn of $640\ \text{m}^2$?
@step $\dfrac{2.5\ \text{kg}}{100\ \text{m}^2} = \dfrac{x}{640\ \text{m}^2}$
@step $x = \dfrac{2.5 \times 640}{100} = 16$
@answer The lawn needs $16\ \text{kg}$ of fertilizer.
:::

:::mistake Flipping one ratio
$\dfrac{150\ \text{mL}}{10\ \text{L}} = \dfrac{65\ \text{L}}{x}$ mixes up the units: millilitres on top on one side, litres on top on the other. It gives about $4.3\ \text{mL}$, which is far too little. Keep the same units in the same places.
:::

## Rounding up for real purchases

Stores sell whole cans, bags and packages. When the proportion gives a decimal, think about the job: you usually need to **round up** so you have enough.

:::example Paint for a community hall
The walls of a room have a total area of $85\ \text{m}^2$ and need two coats. One can of paint covers $38\ \text{m}^2$. How many cans are needed?
@step Two coats: $2 \times 85 = 170\ \text{m}^2$
@step $\dfrac{1\ \text{can}}{38\ \text{m}^2} = \dfrac{x}{170\ \text{m}^2}$, so $x = \dfrac{170}{38} \approx 4.47$
@answer Four cans are not enough, so buy $5$ cans.
:::

## Unit analysis

:::definition Conversion factor
A **conversion factor** is a fraction equal to $1$, such as $\dfrac{1000\ \text{m}}{1\ \text{km}}$ or $\dfrac{0.4536\ \text{kg}}{1\ \text{lb}}$. Multiplying by it changes the units without changing the amount.
:::

In **unit analysis**, you write a rate as a fraction and multiply by conversion factors. Put each unit you want to get rid of on the **opposite** side (top or bottom), so it cancels.

:::example Price per pound
Ground beef costs \$13.20 per kilogram. What is the price per pound? Use $1\ \text{lb} \approx 0.4536\ \text{kg}$.
@step Kilograms are on the bottom of the rate, so put kilograms on the top of the factor: $\dfrac{13.20\ \text{dollars}}{1\ \text{kg}} \times \dfrac{0.4536\ \text{kg}}{1\ \text{lb}}$
@step The kilograms cancel: $13.20 \times 0.4536 \approx 5.99$ dollars per pound.
@answer The beef costs about \$5.99 per pound. That makes sense: a pound is less than a kilogram, so it costs less.
:::

:::example Kilometres per hour to metres per second
A grain truck travels at $90\ \text{km/h}$. What is its speed in metres per second?
@step $\dfrac{90\ \text{km}}{1\ \text{h}} \times \dfrac{1000\ \text{m}}{1\ \text{km}} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{h}}{3600\ \text{s}}$
@step The kilometres and the hours cancel: $\dfrac{90 \times 1000}{3600} = 25$
@answer The truck travels $25\ \text{m/s}$.
:::

## Chains of conversion factors

Some conversions change both the top and the bottom unit. Use one factor for each unit, and keep all the digits until the end.

:::example A sprayer rate
An American manual says to spray $10$ US gallons per acre, but the sprayer controller uses litres per hectare. Use $1\ \text{US gal} \approx 3.785\ \text{L}$ and $1\ \text{acre} \approx 0.4047\ \text{ha}$. Round to the nearest tenth.
@step $\dfrac{10\ \text{gal}}{1\ \text{acre}} \times \dfrac{3.785\ \text{L}}{1\ \text{gal}} \times \dfrac{1\ \text{acre}}{0.4047\ \text{ha}}$
@step The gallons and the acres cancel: $\dfrac{10 \times 3.785}{0.4047} \approx 93.53$
@answer Enter about $93.5\ \text{L/ha}$.
:::

:::tip Check the size
Before you trust an answer, ask whether it should be bigger or smaller. A hectare is about $2.5$ acres and a gallon is almost $4$ litres, so the number of litres per hectare should be much bigger than the number of gallons per acre. It is.
:::

:::check m203-unit-analysis 3
:::

:::summary
- In a proportion, keep the same units in the same places, then multiply both sides by the number under $x$.
- Round up when you must buy whole cans, bags or packages.
- A conversion factor is a fraction equal to $1$. Place each unwanted unit on the opposite side so it cancels.
- For rates such as km/h to m/s or gal/acre to L/ha, use one factor for each unit.
- Check that the size of the answer makes sense.
:::
