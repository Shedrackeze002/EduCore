A road-salt pile is a cone, a hip-roof attic is a pyramid, and a propane tank has rounded ends. Each of these shapes holds less than the box or cylinder that fits around it. In this lesson you will find out exactly how much less.

## Pyramids hold one third

Fill a hollow pyramid with sand and pour it into a prism with the same base and height. It takes exactly **three** pyramids to fill the prism. So

$$V_{\text{pyramid}} = \tfrac13 \times (\text{area of the base}) \times h$$

where $h$ is the **height**: the straight-up distance from the base to the apex.

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"squarePyramid","labels":{"b":"9 cm","h":"12 cm"}}
:::

:::example A glass paperweight
A glass paperweight is a square pyramid with a base side of $9\ \text{cm}$ and a height of $12\ \text{cm}$. What is its volume?
@step Base area: $9 \times 9 = 81\ \text{cm}^2$
@step $V = \tfrac13 \times 81 \times 12 = 324$
@answer $V = 324\ \text{cm}^3$
:::

:::example A hip-roof attic
The attic under a hip roof is a rectangular pyramid. Its base is $36\ \text{ft}$ by $24\ \text{ft}$ and its height is $8\ \text{ft}$. What is its volume?
@step Base area: $36 \times 24 = 864\ \text{ft}^2$
@step $V = \tfrac13 \times 864 \times 8 = 2304$
@answer $V = 2304\ \text{ft}^3$
:::

## Cones

In the same way, a cone holds one third of the cylinder with the same radius and height:

$$V_{\text{cone}} = \tfrac13 \pi r^2 h$$

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"cone","labels":{"d":"8 m","h":"2.5 m"}}
:::

:::example A road-salt pile
A conical pile of road salt is $8\ \text{m}$ across the bottom and $2.5\ \text{m}$ high. What is its volume, to the nearest tenth of a cubic metre?
@step $r = 8 \div 2 = 4\ \text{m}$
@step $V = \tfrac13 \pi(4)^2(2.5) = \tfrac13 \times 40\pi \approx 41.89$
@answer About $41.9\ \text{m}^3$ of salt
:::

:::

## Height, not slant height

The volume formulas use the **height** $h$, which goes straight down inside the shape. On a pile of gravel it is easier to measure the **slant height** $s$ down the side. Find $h$ with the Pythagorean theorem first:

$$h = \sqrt{s^2 - r^2} \ \text{(cone)} \qquad h = \sqrt{s^2 - \left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2} \ \text{(square pyramid)}$$

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"cone","labels":{"d":"10 m","s":"6.5 m","h":"h"}}
:::

:::example A gravel pile
A conical gravel pile is $10\ \text{m}$ across the bottom. The slant height, from the top down the side to the edge, is $6.5\ \text{m}$. What is its volume, to the nearest cubic metre?
@step $r = 10 \div 2 = 5\ \text{m}$
@step $h = \sqrt{6.5^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{17.25} \approx 4.153\ \text{m}$
@step $V = \tfrac13 \pi(5)^2(4.153) \approx 108.7$
@answer About $109\ \text{m}^3$
:::

:::mistake Slant height in a volume formula
Using the slant height $6.5$ as the height gives $\tfrac13 \pi(5)^2(6.5) \approx 170\ \text{m}^3$, far too much gravel. The slant height belongs in surface area; volume needs the true height.
:::

:::

## Spheres and hemispheres

A sphere holds two thirds of the cylinder that just fits around it. That gives

$$V_{\text{sphere}} = \tfrac43 \pi r^3 \qquad V_{\text{hemisphere}} = \tfrac23 \pi r^3$$

Notice that the radius is **cubed**: $r^3 = r \times r \times r$.

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"hemisphere","labels":{"d":"30 cm"}}
:::

:::example A basketball
A basketball has a diameter of $24\ \text{cm}$. What is its volume, to the nearest cubic centimetre?
@step $r = 24 \div 2 = 12\ \text{cm}$, so $r^3 = 1728$.
@step $V = \tfrac43 \pi(1728) = 2304\pi \approx 7238.23$
@answer About $7238\ \text{cm}^3$
:::

:::example A mixing bowl
A mixing bowl is a hemisphere with an inside diameter of $30\ \text{cm}$. How many litres does it hold, to the nearest tenth of a litre?
@step $r = 15\ \text{cm}$, so $V = \tfrac23 \pi(15)^3 = 2250\pi \approx 7068.6\ \text{cm}^3$
@step $7068.6 \div 1000 \approx 7.07$
@answer About $7.1\ \text{L}$
:::

Many objects are made of several shapes: add the volumes of the parts. A propane tank, for example, is a cylinder with a hemisphere on each end, and the two ends together make one whole sphere.

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"capsule","labels":{"r":"0.5 m","h":"2 m"}}
:::

:::example A propane tank
A propane tank has an inside radius of $0.5\ \text{m}$, and its cylinder part is $2\ \text{m}$ long. How many litres does it hold, to the nearest litre?
@step Cylinder: $\pi(0.5)^2(2) = 0.5\pi \approx 1.5708\ \text{m}^3$
@step Two hemispheres make one sphere: $\tfrac43 \pi(0.5)^3 \approx 0.5236\ \text{m}^3$
@step Total: $1.5708 + 0.5236 = 2.0944\ \text{m}^3$, and $2.0944 \times 1000 = 2094.4$
@answer About $2094\ \text{L}$
:::

:::check m203-vol-sphere 3
:::

:::summary
- Pyramid: $V = \tfrac13 \times$ base area $\times h$. Cone: $V = \tfrac13 \pi r^2 h$.
- Use the height, not the slant height. If you know $s$, find $h = \sqrt{s^2 - r^2}$ first.
- Sphere: $V = \tfrac43 \pi r^3$. Hemisphere: $V = \tfrac23 \pi r^3$. Cube the radius, not the diameter.
- For a composite object, add the volumes of its parts.
:::
