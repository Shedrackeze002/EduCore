Concrete is ordered by the cubic metre or the cubic yard, water and fuel by the litre or gallon, and gravel by the truckload. In this lesson you will convert cubic units, solve multi-step jobs, and work backward from a volume to a missing measurement.

## Converting cubic units

Volume units are cubes, so the length conversion is **cubed**. A cubic yard is a cube $3\ \text{ft}$ on each side:

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"cube","labels":{"l":"3 ft","w":"3 ft","h":"3 ft"}}
:::

| Length | Volume |
|---|---|
| $1\ \text{yd} = 3\ \text{ft}$ | $1\ \text{yd}^3 = 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 27\ \text{ft}^3$ |
| $1\ \text{ft} = 12\ \text{in}$ | $1\ \text{ft}^3 = 12 \times 12 \times 12 = 1728\ \text{in}^3$ |
| $1\ \text{m} = 100\ \text{cm}$ | $1\ \text{m}^3 = 100 \times 100 \times 100 = 1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3$ |

:::mistake Using the length factor for cubic units
$81\ \text{ft}^3$ is **not** $81 \div 3 = 27\ \text{yd}^3$. A cubic yard holds $3 \times 3 \times 3 = 27$ cubic feet, so $81\ \text{ft}^3 = 81 \div 27 = 3\ \text{yd}^3$. In the same way, $1\ \text{m}^3$ is $1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3$, not $100\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

:::example Concrete in cubic yards
A patio slab is $20\ \text{ft}$ long, $12\ \text{ft}$ wide and $4\ \text{in}$ thick. How many cubic yards of concrete is that, to the nearest tenth?
@step Thickness in feet: $4\ \text{in} = \tfrac{4}{12} = \tfrac13\ \text{ft}$
@step $V = 20 \times 12 \times \tfrac13 = 80\ \text{ft}^3$
@step $80 \div 27 \approx 2.96$
@answer About $3.0\ \text{yd}^3$
:::

## Concrete, water and fuel

Most volume jobs follow the same plan: **sketch the shape, put every length in one unit, find the volume, then convert** to the unit you buy in.

:::example A metric garage pad
A garage pad is $6\ \text{m}$ by $4\ \text{m}$ and $10\ \text{cm}$ thick. How much concrete is needed?
@step Thickness in metres: $10\ \text{cm} = 0.1\ \text{m}$
@answer $V = 6 \times 4 \times 0.1 = 2.4\ \text{m}^3$
:::

:::example Filling a diesel tank
A rectangular transfer tank is $120\ \text{cm}$ long, $50\ \text{cm}$ wide and $40\ \text{cm}$ high inside. Diesel costs \$1.459 per litre. What does it cost to fill the empty tank?
@step $V = 120 \times 50 \times 40 = 240\,000\ \text{cm}^3$, which is $240\,000 \div 1000 = 240\ \text{L}$
@step Cost: $240 \times 1.459 = 350.16$
@answer It costs \$350.16 to fill.
:::

## Loads, trips and time

Trucks, augers and hoses move a fixed amount per load or per minute.

- **Loads** = total volume ÷ volume per load, always rounded **up**.
- **Time** = total volume ÷ rate. Check the units: litres ÷ litres per minute gives minutes.

:::figure

:::

:::example Emptying a grain bin
A grain bin is $6\ \text{m}$ across with a wall $7\ \text{m}$ high, and the grain is piled in a cone $2\ \text{m}$ above the top of the wall. A grain truck hauls $30\ \text{m}^3$ per load. How many loads empty the bin?
@step $r = 3\ \text{m}$. Cylinder: $\pi(3)^2(7) = 63\pi\ \text{m}^3$. Cone: $\tfrac13 \pi(3)^2(2) = 6\pi\ \text{m}^3$.
@step Total: $69\pi \approx 216.8\ \text{m}^3$
@step $216.8 \div 30 \approx 7.23$
@answer Round up: $8$ loads
:::

:::example Filling a pool
A round above-ground pool is $5.5\ \text{m}$ across and is filled to a depth of $1.2\ \text{m}$. A hose delivers $30\ \text{L}$ per minute. About how many hours will it take to fill?
@step $V = \pi(2.75)^2(1.2) \approx 28.51\ \text{m}^3$, which is about $28\,510\ \text{L}$
@step $28\,510 \div 30 \approx 950$ minutes
@answer $950 \div 60 \approx 15.8$ hours
:::

## Working backward: a missing dimension

Sometimes you know the volume and need a length: how thick a pad is, how tall a tank must be, or how wide a pile will spread. Put the known values into the formula, then **undo** the operations one at a time.

| Shape | Solve for | Rearranged formula |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangular prism | height | $h = \dfrac{V}{lw}$ |
| Cylinder | height | $h = \dfrac{V}{\pi r^2}$ |
| Cylinder | radius | $r = \sqrt{\dfrac{V}{\pi h}}$ |
| Cone | radius | $r = \sqrt{\dfrac{3V}{\pi h}}$ |
| Sphere | radius | $r = \sqrt[3]{\dfrac{3V}{4\pi}}$ |

:::example How thick is the pad?
A crew pours $3.6\ \text{m}^3$ of concrete for a pad that is $6\ \text{m}$ by $5\ \text{m}$. How thick is the pad, in centimetres?
@step $3.6 = 6 \times 5 \times h = 30h$
@step $h = 3.6 \div 30 = 0.12\ \text{m}$
@answer $0.12\ \text{m} = 12\ \text{cm}$
:::

:::

:::example How tall is the tank?
A cylindrical tank must hold $5000\ \text{L}$ and has an inside diameter of $1.6\ \text{m}$. How tall must it be, to the nearest hundredth of a metre?
@step $5000\ \text{L} = 5\ \text{m}^3$ and $r = 1.6 \div 2 = 0.8\ \text{m}$
@step $5 = \pi(0.8)^2 h$, so $h = \dfrac{5}{\pi(0.8)^2} \approx \dfrac{5}{2.0106} \approx 2.487$
@answer About $2.49\ \text{m}$
:::

:::check m203-vol-rearrange 2
:::

:::example A spherical tank
A spherical water tank must hold $10\,000\ \text{L}$. What radius does it need, to the nearest hundredth of a metre?
@step $10\,000\ \text{L} = 10\ \text{m}^3$
@step $10 = \tfrac43 \pi r^3$, so $r^3 = \dfrac{3 \times 10}{4\pi} \approx 2.387$
@step $r = \sqrt[3]{2.387} \approx 1.337$
@answer About $1.34\ \text{m}$
:::

:::tip Square root or cube root?
If the formula has $r^2$, finish with a square root. If it has $r^3$, finish with a cube root. Check your answer by putting it back into the original formula.
:::

:::check m203-vol-rearrange 3
:::

:::summary
- Cube the length factor for volume: $1\ \text{yd}^3 = 27\ \text{ft}^3$, $1\ \text{ft}^3 = 1728\ \text{in}^3$, $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3$.
- Plan: one unit for every length, find the volume, then convert to litres, gallons or cubic yards as the job needs.
- Loads and containers are rounded up; time = volume ÷ rate.
- To find a missing dimension, substitute and undo: divide for a height, then take a square root ($r^2$) or a cube root ($r^3$) for a radius.
:::
