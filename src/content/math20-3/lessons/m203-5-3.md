Flat-pack furniture, a bike wheel kit and a backyard deck all come with instructions that pull the object apart on paper. These drawings, called **exploded views**, show every part and where it goes. In this lesson you will read exploded views and parts lists, and use them to count, order and price the parts.

## Reading an exploded view

:::definition Exploded view
An **exploded view** is a drawing of an object with its parts pulled apart, but still lined up with where they go. Dashed lines often show how the parts join.
:::

:::definition Component
A **component** is one part of a larger object. Each component in an exploded view usually has a label, such as a letter, that matches the parts list.
:::

:::example Parts of a bookshelf
The exploded view shows a bookshelf with side panels (A), shelves (B) and a top (C). Each shelf rests on $4$ metal shelf pins. How many wooden parts and how many pins are needed?
@step Count each label: A appears $2$ times, B appears $4$ times and C appears $1$ time.
@step Wooden parts: $2 + 4 + 1 = 7$
@step Pins: $4$ shelves $\times\ 4$ pins $= 16$
@answer The bookshelf has $7$ wooden parts and needs $16$ shelf pins.
:::

## Parts lists and costs

A **parts list** goes with the exploded view. It gives the label, the name and the quantity of each component, and a store's list may give a unit price.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Label","Part","Quantity","Unit price"],"rows":[["A","Side panel","2","$18.50"],["B","Shelf","4","$7.25"],["C","Top panel","1","$11.99"],["D","Shelf pin","16","$0.10"]]}
:::

:::example Pricing the parts
What do the parts in the list above cost in all? (Ignore tax.)
@step Side panels: $2 \times 18.50 = 37.00$
@step Shelves: $4 \times 7.25 = 29.00$
@step Top panel: $1 \times 11.99 = 11.99$
@step Shelf pins: $16 \times 0.10 = 1.60$
@step Add: $37.00 + 29.00 + 11.99 + 1.60 = 79.59$
@answer The parts cost \$79.59.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the quantity
Adding only the unit prices gives \$37.84, which prices **one** of each part. Multiply each unit price by its quantity first, then add.
:::

:::example A part is missing
Mei opens the box and counts $2$ side panels, $3$ shelves, $1$ top panel and $12$ shelf pins. What will the missing parts cost at the unit prices in the list?
@step Compare with the Quantity column: $1$ shelf and $16 - 12 = 4$ pins are missing.
@step Shelf: $1 \times 7.25 = 7.25$. Pins: $4 \times 0.10 = 0.40$.
@answer The missing parts cost $7.25 + 0.40 = 7.65$, or \$7.65.
:::

## Putting the parts together in order

An exploded view also shows the **order** of assembly. A part can only go on once the part it sits on or fastens to is in place. Start with the part that holds everything up, and finish with the outside layer.

:::example Building a deck
Put these steps in order: attach the railing; set the posts on the footings; screw the deck boards to the joists; bolt the beams to the posts; pour the concrete footings; fasten the joists to the beams.
@step The footings hold everything else, so they come first. The posts sit on the footings.
@step The beams bolt to the posts, and the joists rest on the beams.
@step The deck boards are screwed to the joists, and the railing goes around the finished floor.
@answer Footings, posts, beams, joists, deck boards, railing.
:::

:::tip Read the dashed lines
In an exploded view, follow each dashed line from a part to the part it joins. The part at the other end must already be in place.
:::

## Scale and comparing costs

Exploded views in a set of plans are often drawn to scale, so you can find the real size of a part. On a drawing with a scale of $1 : 20$, a shelf drawn $4.5\ \text{cm}$ long is really $4.5 \times 20 = 90\ \text{cm}$ long.

:::example Buying for a class
A shop class is building $3$ of the bookshelves from the parts list. They buy the parts and pay $5\%$ GST on the total. What is the total cost, to the nearest cent?
@step One bookshelf: \$79.59 (from the example above).
@step Three bookshelves: $79.59 \times 3 = 238.77$
@step GST: $238.77 \times 0.05 = 11.9385$
@step Total: $238.77 + 11.9385 = 250.7085$
@answer The total cost is \$250.71.
:::

A store sells the complete kit for \$69.99. Buying the parts separately for one bookshelf costs $79.59 - 69.99 = 9.60$, or \$9.60 more.

:::summary
- An exploded view pulls an object apart on paper; dashed lines show where the parts join.
- Each component has a label that matches the parts list, which gives the name and quantity of each part.
- Total cost: multiply each quantity by its unit price, then add. Add tax once, at the end.
- Assemble in order: the part that holds everything up goes first, and the outside layer goes last.
- If the exploded view is drawn to scale, multiply a drawing length by the scale to get the real length.
:::
