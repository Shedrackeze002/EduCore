Before a sheet-metal worker cuts a furnace duct or a carpenter orders plywood for a shipping crate, they work out how much material covers the outside. That amount is the **surface area**. In this lesson you will find it for prisms and cylinders, in metric and imperial units.

## Nets and surface area

:::definition Surface area and net
The **surface area** ($SA$) of a 3-D object is the total area of all its outside faces, measured in square units such as $\text{m}^2$ or $\text{ft}^2$. A **net** is the flat pattern you get when you unfold the object. The area of the net equals the surface area.
:::

Unfold a closed box and you get six rectangles in three matching pairs: the top and bottom, the front and back, and the two ends.

Add the three pairs of faces:

$$SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh$$

:::example A plywood crate
A closed plywood crate is $1.2\ \text{m}$ long, $0.8\ \text{m}$ wide and $0.6\ \text{m}$ high. How much plywood covers all six faces?
@step Top and bottom: $2 \times 1.2 \times 0.8 = 1.92\ \text{m}^2$
@step Front and back: $2 \times 1.2 \times 0.6 = 1.44\ \text{m}^2$
@step Two ends: $2 \times 0.8 \times 0.6 = 0.96\ \text{m}^2$
@answer $SA = 1.92 + 1.44 + 0.96 = 4.32\ \text{m}^2$ of plywood
:::

:::tip One line per pair
Write one line for each pair of faces, then add. Three short lines are much easier to check than one long calculation.
:::

## Triangular prisms

A **triangular prism** has two triangular ends joined by three rectangles. Each rectangle is one side of the triangle times the length of the prism. Tents, wedges, ramps and many gift boxes are triangular prisms.

:::example A tent with a floor
An A-frame tent has a sewn-in floor. Each triangular end has a base of $6\ \text{ft}$, a height of $4\ \text{ft}$ and two sloped sides of $5\ \text{ft}$. The tent is $8\ \text{ft}$ long. How much fabric covers all five faces?
@step Two triangular ends: $2 \times \tfrac12 \times 6 \times 4 = 24\ \text{ft}^2$
@step Floor: $6 \times 8 = 48\ \text{ft}^2$
@step Two sloped sides: $2 \times 5 \times 8 = 80\ \text{ft}^2$
@answer $SA = 24 + 48 + 80 = 152\ \text{ft}^2$ of fabric
:::

:::mistake Using the height for the sloped sides
Each sloped rectangle is as wide as the **sloped side** of the triangle ($5\ \text{ft}$), not the height of the triangle ($4\ \text{ft}$). The height is used only for the area of the triangles. If the sloped side is not given, find it with the Pythagorean theorem.
:::

## Only the faces the job needs

On the job you rarely cover every face. A trough has no lid, a duct is open at both ends, and a greenhouse has no plastic on the floor. **List the faces first**, and change every measurement to the same unit before you multiply.

:::example A water trough
A steel water trough with no top is $6\ \text{ft}$ long, $2\ \text{ft}$ wide and $18\ \text{in}$ deep. How much sheet steel covers the outside, in square feet?
@step Change the depth to feet: $18 \div 12 = 1.5\ \text{ft}$.
@step Bottom only (no top): $6 \times 2 = 12\ \text{ft}^2$
@step Front and back: $2 \times 6 \times 1.5 = 18\ \text{ft}^2$. Two ends: $2 \times 2 \times 1.5 = 6\ \text{ft}^2$.
@answer $12 + 18 + 6 = 36\ \text{ft}^2$ of steel
:::

## Cylinders

Peel the label off a can and lay it flat: it is a rectangle. Its length is the distance around the can, the circumference $2\pi r$, and its width is the height $h$. So a closed cylinder unfolds into **two circles and one rectangle**.

:::definition Lateral area
The **lateral area** is the area of the sides only, without the top and bottom. For a cylinder it is the curved side: $2\pi r h$, which is the same as $\pi d h$.
:::

$$SA = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h$$

:::example A closed water tank
A closed cylindrical water tank has a diameter of $1.2\ \text{m}$ and a height of $2.5\ \text{m}$. Find its surface area to the nearest tenth of a square metre.
@step The radius is half the diameter: $r = 1.2 \div 2 = 0.6\ \text{m}$.
@step Two circles: $2\pi(0.6)^2 = 0.72\pi\ \text{m}^2$
@step Curved side: $2\pi(0.6)(2.5) = 3\pi\ \text{m}^2$
@step $SA = 0.72\pi + 3\pi = 3.72\pi \approx 11.69$
@answer About $11.7\ \text{m}^2$
:::

:::example A round duct
A round heating duct has a diameter of $8\ \text{in}$ and is $5\ \text{ft}$ long. It is open at both ends. How much sheet metal makes the curved side, to the nearest tenth of a square foot?
@step Only the lateral area counts. Change the diameter to feet: $8 \div 12 = \tfrac23\ \text{ft}$.
@step $A = \pi d h = \pi \times \tfrac23 \times 5 \approx 10.47$
@answer About $10.5\ \text{ft}^2$ of sheet metal
:::

:::mistake Diameter or radius?
Plans usually give the **diameter**, but $2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h$ uses the **radius**. Halve the diameter first. Using $1.2$ in place of $0.6$ in the tank above would make each circle four times too big.
:::

:::summary
- Surface area is the total area of the outside faces, the same as the area of the net.
- Rectangular prism: $SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh$.
- Triangular prism: two triangles plus three rectangles, each one side of the triangle times the length.
- Closed cylinder: $SA = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h$. The lateral (curved) area is $2\pi r h = \pi d h$.
- Include only the faces the job covers, put every measurement in the same unit, and halve the diameter to get the radius.
:::
