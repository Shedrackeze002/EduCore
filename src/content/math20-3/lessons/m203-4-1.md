Carpenters, crane operators and surveyors solve right triangles every day: how long a brace must be, how high a ladder reaches, how steep a ramp is. This lesson reviews the two tools you need for one right triangle, the Pythagorean theorem and the trigonometric ratios, before you chain triangles together in the next lessons.

## Naming the sides

In a right triangle, the longest side is across from the right angle. The other two sides are named from the angle you are working with, which is often called $\theta$ (theta).

:::definition Hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent
- The **hypotenuse** is across from the right angle. It is always the longest side.
- The **opposite** side is across from the angle $\theta$.
- The **adjacent** side touches the angle $\theta$ and is not the hypotenuse.
:::

If you move to the other acute angle, the opposite and adjacent sides swap. The hypotenuse never changes.

## The Pythagorean theorem

When you know two sides and want the third, and no angle is involved, use the Pythagorean theorem. If $c$ is the hypotenuse and $a$ and $b$ are the legs:
$$c^2 = a^2 + b^2$$

:::

:::example A brace for a farm gate
A gate is $6\ \text{ft}$ wide and $4\ \text{ft}$ tall. A diagonal brace runs from corner to corner. How long is the brace, to the nearest tenth of a foot?
@step The brace is the hypotenuse: $c^2 = 6^2 + 4^2 = 36 + 16 = 52$
@step $c = \sqrt{52} \approx 7.21$
@answer The brace is about $7.2\ \text{ft}$ long.
:::

:::example How high does the ladder reach?
The foot of a $6\ \text{m}$ ladder is $1.5\ \text{m}$ from a wall. How high up the wall does it reach, to the nearest tenth of a metre?
@step The ladder is the hypotenuse, so subtract: $h^2 = 6^2 - 1.5^2 = 36 - 2.25 = 33.75$
@step $h = \sqrt{33.75} \approx 5.81$
@answer The ladder reaches about $5.8\ \text{m}$ up the wall.
:::

## Finding a side with SOH CAH TOA

When an angle is involved, use a trigonometric ratio. The memory aid **SOH CAH TOA** tells you which one:

$$\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad \cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad \tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$$

Use these steps every time:

1. **Sketch** the triangle and mark the right angle.
2. **Label** the sides from the angle: opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse.
3. **Choose** the ratio that uses the side you know and the side you want.
4. **Solve**: multiply if the unknown is on top; divide if it is on the bottom.

:::figure
{"type":"rightTriangle","vertices":{"right":"B","a":"A","b":"T"},"legA":9.37,"legB":15,"sideLabels":{"rb":"15 m","ab":"L"},"angleLabels":{"a":"58°"},"rotate":0,"mirror":false}
:::

:::example A guy wire
A guy wire is attached $15\ \text{m}$ up a radio tower and makes an angle of $58^\circ$ with the ground. How long is the wire, to the nearest tenth of a metre?
@step From the $58^\circ$ angle, the $15$ m height is opposite and the wire $L$ is the hypotenuse. Use SOH.
@step $\sin 58^\circ = \dfrac{15}{L}$. The unknown is on the bottom, so $L = \dfrac{15}{\sin 58^\circ} \approx \dfrac{15}{0.8480}$
@answer $L \approx 17.7\ \text{m}$. Check: the wire is longer than the $15$ m height, as a hypotenuse must be.
:::

:::example How high is the kite?
A kite string is $80\ \text{m}$ long and makes an angle of $40^\circ$ with the ground. Ignoring sag, how high is the kite?
@step The string is the hypotenuse and the height $h$ is opposite the angle. Use SOH.
@step $\sin 40^\circ = \dfrac{h}{80}$, so $h = 80\sin 40^\circ \approx 80 \times 0.6428$
@answer $h \approx 51.4\ \text{m}$
:::

:::mistake Degree mode and dividing the wrong way
Make sure your calculator is in **degree** mode: $\sin 40^\circ \approx 0.6428$. If you get $0.7451$, it is in radian mode. Also, when the unknown is on the **bottom** of the ratio, you must divide: $L = \dfrac{15}{\sin 58^\circ}$, not $15\sin 58^\circ$.
:::

## Finding an angle

When you know two sides and want an angle, find the ratio and use the inverse key: $\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$ or $\tan^{-1}$.

:::example How steep is the ramp?
A ramp rises $0.6\ \text{m}$ over a horizontal run of $7.5\ \text{m}$. What angle does it make with the ground, to the nearest tenth of a degree?
@step The rise is opposite the angle and the run is adjacent. Use TOA.
@step $\tan\theta = \dfrac{0.6}{7.5} = 0.08$
@answer $\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.08) \approx 4.6^\circ$
:::

:::example Setting a ladder
A $7\ \text{m}$ ladder has its foot $1.8\ \text{m}$ from a wall. What angle does it make with the ground?
@step The $1.8$ m distance is adjacent to the angle and the ladder is the hypotenuse. Use CAH.
@step $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1.8}{7} \approx 0.2571$
@answer $\theta = \cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1.8}{7}\right) \approx 75.1^\circ$
:::

## Solving a whole problem

Many jobs need two or three answers from one triangle. Find each one from the information you were **given** when you can, and keep all the digits until the end.

:::example A rafter for a 6/12 roof
A roof has a 6/12 pitch: it rises $6$ in for every $12$ in of run. Each rafter covers a run of $14\ \text{ft}$. Find the angle of the roof and the length of a rafter, to the nearest tenth.
@step Angle: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{6}{12}$, so $\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.5) \approx 26.6^\circ$
@step Rafter: the run is adjacent and the rafter $L$ is the hypotenuse, so $L = \dfrac{14}{\cos\theta} \approx 15.65$
@step Check with the Pythagorean theorem: the rise is $14 \times \dfrac{6}{12} = 7$ ft, and $\sqrt{14^2 + 7^2} = \sqrt{245} \approx 15.65$.
@answer The roof makes an angle of about $26.6^\circ$, and each rafter is about $15.7\ \text{ft}$ long.
:::

:::tip The angles add to 90°
The two acute angles of a right triangle add to $90^\circ$. If one is $26.6^\circ$, the other is about $63.4^\circ$.
:::

:::summary
- The hypotenuse is across from the right angle; opposite and adjacent depend on the angle you use.
- No angle involved: use $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$.
- With an angle: label the sides, choose SOH, CAH or TOA, then multiply (unknown on top) or divide (unknown on the bottom).
- To find an angle, use $\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$ or $\tan^{-1}$ of the ratio.
- Keep all the digits until the end, and check that the hypotenuse is the longest side.
:::
