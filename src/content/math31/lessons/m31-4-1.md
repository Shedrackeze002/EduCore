In Unit 3 you found derivatives from the limit definition: $\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^2) = 2x$ and $\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^3) = 3x^2$. A pattern is hiding there. In this lesson we prove it once, for every power, so you never have to expand $(x + h)^n$ again.

## The power rule

:::definition Power rule
For any real number $n$,
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left(x^n\right) = nx^{n-1}.$$
Bring the exponent down as a coefficient, then subtract $1$ from the exponent. Two special cases: $\dfrac{d}{dx}(x) = 1$, and for a constant, $\dfrac{d}{dx}(c) = 0$ (its graph is a horizontal line).
:::

**Proof for a positive integer $n$.** Expand $(x + h)^n$ with the binomial theorem:
$$(x + h)^n = x^n + nx^{n-1}h + \tbinom{n}{2}x^{n-2}h^2 + \dots + h^n.$$
Subtract $x^n$: every remaining term contains $h$, and all but the first contain $h^2$ or a higher power. Divide by $h$:
$$\frac{(x + h)^n - x^n}{h} = nx^{n-1} + h\left[\tbinom{n}{2}x^{n-2} + \dots + h^{n-2}\right].$$
As $h \to 0$ the bracketed part is multiplied by $h \to 0$, so the limit is $nx^{n-1}$. The rule also holds for negative and fractional exponents; you will see why for some of them later in the course.

:::example Single powers
Differentiate $x^{7}$, $x^{-3}$ and $x^{2/3}$.
@step $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x^{7}\right) = 7x^{6}$
@step $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x^{-3}\right) = -3x^{-4}$ (subtract $1$: $-3 - 1 = -4$)
@step $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x^{2/3}\right) = \tfrac{2}{3}x^{-1/3}$ (subtract $1$: $\tfrac{2}{3} - 1 = -\tfrac{1}{3}$)
@answer $7x^{6}$, $-3x^{-4} = -\dfrac{3}{x^{4}}$ and $\tfrac{2}{3}x^{-1/3} = \dfrac{2}{3\sqrt[3]{x}}$
:::

## Constant multiples and sums

:::definition Constant multiple and sum rules
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left[cf(x)\right] = cf'(x), \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\left[f(x) \pm g(x)\right] = f'(x) \pm g'(x)$$
:::

Both follow from the limit laws: a constant factor comes out of a limit, and the limit of a sum is the sum of the limits. Together with the power rule, they let you differentiate any polynomial term by term.

:::example A polynomial
Find $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = 4x^{5} - 3x^{3} + 7x - 9$, then find $f'(1)$.
@step Differentiate term by term: $f'(x) = 4(5x^{4}) - 3(3x^{2}) + 7(1) - 0$.
@step Simplify: $f'(x) = 20x^{4} - 9x^{2} + 7$.
@step Substitute: $f'(1) = 20 - 9 + 7 = 18$.
@answer $f'(x) = 20x^{4} - 9x^{2} + 7$ and $f'(1) = 18$
:::

:::tip Notation
All of these mean the same thing: $f'(x)$, $y'$, $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ and $\dfrac{d}{dx}f(x)$. Use $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ when the function is written $y = \dots$.
:::

## Roots and reciprocals

The power rule only works on a power of $x$, so rewrite every root and every reciprocal as a power **before** you differentiate:
$$\sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}, \qquad \sqrt[3]{x^{2}} = x^{2/3}, \qquad \frac{1}{x^{3}} = x^{-3}, \qquad \frac{5}{\sqrt{x}} = 5x^{-1/2}.$$

:::example Rewrite, differentiate, tidy up
Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for $y = 4x^{3} - \dfrac{6}{x} + 2\sqrt{x}$.
@step Rewrite: $y = 4x^{3} - 6x^{-1} + 2x^{1/2}$.
@step Power rule: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 12x^{2} - 6(-1)x^{-2} + 2\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)x^{-1/2} = 12x^{2} + 6x^{-2} + x^{-1/2}$.
@step Write with positive exponents: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 12x^{2} + \dfrac{6}{x^{2}} + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$.
@answer $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 12x^{2} + \dfrac{6}{x^{2}} + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$
:::

There is no rule yet for products and quotients, so simplify them into a sum of powers first.

:::example Divide first
Differentiate $f(x) = \dfrac{x^{2} + 3}{\sqrt{x}}$.
@step Divide each term by $x^{1/2}$: $f(x) = x^{3/2} + 3x^{-1/2}$.
@step Power rule: $f'(x) = \tfrac{3}{2}x^{1/2} - \tfrac{3}{2}x^{-3/2}$.
@answer $f'(x) = \dfrac{3\sqrt{x}}{2} - \dfrac{3}{2x\sqrt{x}}$
:::

:::mistake Reciprocals are not powers of the denominator
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{1}{x^{3}}\right)$ is **not** $\dfrac{1}{3x^{2}}$. Rewrite first: $x^{-3}$ has derivative $-3x^{-4} = -\dfrac{3}{x^{4}}$. Watch the coefficients too: $\dfrac{1}{2x^{2}} = \tfrac{1}{2}x^{-2}$, not $2x^{-2}$.
:::

:::check m31-power-rule 2
:::

## Slopes and rates of change

The derivative gives the slope of the tangent line at every point, so you can now find tangent slopes, and the points where the tangent is horizontal, in a few lines.

:::example Horizontal tangents
Find the points on the graph of $f(x) = x^{3} - 3x + 1$ where the tangent line is horizontal.
@step A horizontal tangent has slope $0$: $f'(x) = 3x^{2} - 3 = 0$.
@step $3(x - 1)(x + 1) = 0$, so $x = -1$ or $x = 1$.
@step $f(-1) = -1 + 3 + 1 = 3$ and $f(1) = 1 - 3 + 1 = -1$.
@answer The tangent is horizontal at $(-1,\ 3)$ and $(1,\ -1)$.
:::

The derivative is also an instantaneous rate of change. If $s(t)$ is a position, $s'(t)$ is the velocity.

:::example A ball thrown upward
A ball's height is $h(t) = 1.5 + 19.6t - 4.9t^{2}$ metres after $t$ seconds. Find its velocity at $t = 1$ s and at $t = 3$ s, and when it stops rising.
@step $h'(t) = 19.6 - 9.8t$.
@step $h'(1) = 9.8$ m/s (rising) and $h'(3) = -9.8$ m/s (falling).
@step It stops rising when $h'(t) = 0$: $9.8t = 19.6$, so $t = 2$ s.
@answer $9.8$ m/s at $t = 1$, $-9.8$ m/s at $t = 3$, and the ball peaks at $t = 2$ s.
:::

:::check m31-power-rule 3
:::

:::summary
- Power rule: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x^n\right) = nx^{n-1}$ for any real $n$; the derivative of a constant is $0$.
- Constant multiples stay in front, and a sum is differentiated term by term.
- Rewrite roots and reciprocals as powers first, and simplify products and quotients into sums of powers.
- Give answers with positive exponents; the derivative at a point is the slope of the tangent there and the instantaneous rate of change.
:::
