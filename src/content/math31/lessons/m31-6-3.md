Two graphs can both be increasing and still look very different: one bends upward like a skateboard ramp, the other bends downward like the top of a hill. The first derivative tells you which way a graph is heading; the second derivative tells you which way it **bends**.

## Concavity

:::definition Concave up and concave down
The graph of $f$ is **concave up** on an interval if $f'$ is increasing there: the graph bends upward and lies above its tangent lines. It is **concave down** if $f'$ is decreasing: the graph bends downward and lies below its tangent lines.
:::

Since $f''$ is the derivative of $f'$, the sign of $f''$ tells us whether $f'$ is increasing:

- if $f''(x) > 0$ on an interval, $f$ is concave up there;
- if $f''(x) < 0$ on an interval, $f$ is concave down there.

:::example Intervals of concavity
Find where $f(x) = x^{3} - 6x^{2} + 5$ is concave up and concave down.
@step $f'(x) = 3x^{2} - 12x$ and $f''(x) = 6x - 12 = 6(x - 2)$.
@step $f''(x) < 0$ for $x < 2$ and $f''(x) > 0$ for $x > 2$.
@answer Concave down on $(-\infty, 2)$; concave up on $(2, \infty)$.
:::

:::check m31-concavity 1
:::

## Points of inflection

:::definition Point of inflection
A **point of inflection** is a point on a continuous curve where the concavity changes, from up to down or from down to up.
:::

To find them, solve $f''(x) = 0$ (and look where $f''$ does not exist), then check that $f''$ really **changes sign**.

:::example Two points of inflection
Find the points of inflection of $f(x) = x^{4} - 4x^{3}$.
@step $f'(x) = 4x^{3} - 12x^{2}$ and $f''(x) = 12x^{2} - 24x = 12x(x - 2)$.
@step Signs of $f''$: $+$ on $(-\infty, 0)$, $-$ on $(0, 2)$, $+$ on $(2, \infty)$, so the concavity changes at both $x = 0$ and $x = 2$.
@step $f(0) = 0$ and $f(2) = 16 - 32 = -16$.
@answer The points of inflection are $(0, 0)$ and $(2, -16)$.
:::

## The second derivative test

At a critical number where $f'(c) = 0$, the tangent is horizontal. If the graph is concave up there, it must be at the bottom of a valley; if it is concave down, at the top of a hill.

:::definition Second derivative test
Suppose $f'(c) = 0$.
- If $f''(c) > 0$, $f$ has a local minimum at $c$.
- If $f''(c) < 0$, $f$ has a local maximum at $c$.
- If $f''(c) = 0$, the test gives no information: use the first derivative test.
:::

:::example Classifying critical numbers
Use the second derivative test to classify the critical numbers of $f(x) = 2x^{3} - 3x^{2} - 12x + 1$.
@step $f'(x) = 6x^{2} - 6x - 12 = 6(x + 1)(x - 2)$, so the critical numbers are $-1$ and $2$.
@step $f''(x) = 12x - 6$: $f''(-1) = -18 < 0$ and $f''(2) = 18 > 0$.
@step $f(-1) = -2 - 3 + 12 + 1 = 8$ and $f(2) = 16 - 12 - 24 + 1 = -19$.
@answer Local maximum $(-1, 8)$; local minimum $(2, -19)$.
:::

:::check m31-concavity 2
:::

## Sketching a curve with f′ and f″

To sketch $y = f(x)$ by hand, gather the information first:

1. The domain and the intercepts.
2. A sign chart for $f'$: intervals of increase and decrease, and local extrema.
3. A sign chart for $f''$: concavity and points of inflection.
4. Plot the key points and join them with the right shape on each interval.

:::example A complete summary
Describe the graph of $f(x) = x^{3} - 3x$.
@step Intercepts: $f(x) = x(x^{2} - 3) = 0$ at $x = 0$ and $x = \pm\sqrt{3}$.
@step $f'(x) = 3x^{2} - 3 = 3(x + 1)(x - 1)$: increasing on $(-\infty, -1) \cup (1, \infty)$, decreasing on $(-1, 1)$; local maximum $f(-1) = 2$, local minimum $f(1) = -2$.
@step $f''(x) = 6x$: concave down on $(-\infty, 0)$, concave up on $(0, \infty)$; point of inflection $(0, 0)$.
