A car's odometer tells you how far it has gone, its speedometer tells you how fast, and the push you feel in your seat tells you how quickly the speed is changing. For an object moving along a straight line, these are position, velocity and acceleration, and each one is the derivative of the one before.

## Position, velocity and acceleration

Let $s(t)$ be the position of an object on a line at time $t$, measured from a fixed origin with a chosen positive direction.

:::definition Velocity and acceleration
**Velocity** is the rate of change of position, $v(t) = s'(t)$. **Acceleration** is the rate of change of velocity, $a(t) = v'(t) = s''(t)$. **Speed** is the size of the velocity, $|v(t)|$.
:::

Units follow from the rates: if $s$ is in metres and $t$ in seconds, $v$ is in m/s and $a$ in m/s² (metres per second, per second).

:::example Velocity and acceleration at an instant
A particle moves along a line so that its position, in metres, after $t$ seconds is $s(t) = t^{3} - 9t^{2} + 24t$, for $t \ge 0$. Find its velocity and acceleration at $t = 1$.
@step $v(t) = 3t^{2} - 18t + 24$, so $v(1) = 3 - 18 + 24 = 9$ m/s.
@step $a(t) = 6t - 18$, so $a(1) = 6 - 18 = -12$ m/s².
@answer $v(1) = 9$ m/s and $a(1) = -12$ m/s²
:::

## Direction of motion

The sign of the velocity gives the direction: $v > 0$ means the object moves in the positive direction, $v < 0$ means it moves in the negative direction, and $v = 0$ means it is **at rest** for an instant. An object can only turn around at a time when it is at rest.

:::example When does it turn around?
For $s(t) = t^{3} - 9t^{2} + 24t$, find when the particle is at rest and when it moves in the positive direction.
@step $v(t) = 3t^{2} - 18t + 24 = 3(t - 2)(t - 4)$, which is $0$ at $t = 2$ and $t = 4$.
@step Test: $v(1) = 9 > 0$, $v(3) = -3 < 0$, $v(5) = 9 > 0$.
@answer At rest at $t = 2$ s and $t = 4$ s; moving in the positive direction on $[0, 2) \cup (4, \infty)$ and in the negative direction on $(2, 4)$.
:::

The same ideas describe vertical motion. Near Earth's surface (ignoring air resistance), a ball thrown upward from $1.5$ m at $19.6$ m/s has height $h(t) = 1.5 + 19.6t - 4.9t^{2}$ metres. It reaches its highest point when $v(t) = 19.6 - 9.8t = 0$, at $t = 2$ s, where $h(2) = 1.5 + 39.2 - 19.6 = 21.1$ m.

:::example Hitting the ground
A stone is dropped from a bridge $44.1$ m above a river, so $h(t) = 44.1 - 4.9t^{2}$. When does it hit the water, and how fast is it going?
@step $h(t) = 0$: $4.9t^{2} = 44.1$, so $t^{2} = 9$ and $t = 3$ s (time is positive).
@step $v(t) = h'(t) = -9.8t$, so $v(3) = -29.4$ m/s.
@answer It hits the water after $3$ s with velocity $-29.4$ m/s: a speed of $29.4$ m/s, downward.
:::

## Speeding up or slowing down

Negative acceleration does **not** always mean slowing down. What matters is whether the acceleration pushes the same way the object is moving.

:::definition Speeding up and slowing down
An object is **speeding up** when $v(t)$ and $a(t)$ have the same sign, and **slowing down** when they have opposite signs.
:::

:::example Comparing sign charts
For $s(t) = t^{3} - 9t^{2} + 24t$, when is the particle speeding up?
@step $v(t) = 3(t - 2)(t - 4)$: positive on $[0, 2)$, negative on $(2, 4)$, positive on $(4, \infty)$.
@step $a(t) = 6(t - 3)$: negative on $[0, 3)$, positive on $(3, \infty)$.
@step Same signs on $(2, 3)$ (both negative) and on $(4, \infty)$ (both positive).
@answer Speeding up on $(2, 3) \cup (4, \infty)$; slowing down on $[0, 2) \cup (3, 4)$.
:::

:::mistake "Negative acceleration means slowing down"
On $(2, 3)$ the acceleration is negative, yet the particle is speeding up: it is moving in the negative direction and being pushed harder that way. Always compare the signs of $v$ and $a$.
:::

## Displacement and total distance

**Displacement** on $[t_1, t_2]$ is the change in position, $s(t_2) - s(t_1)$; it can be negative. **Total distance travelled** counts every metre covered, in either direction, so you must split the interval wherever the object turns around.

:::example Out and back
For $s(t) = t^{3} - 9t^{2} + 24t$, find the displacement and the total distance travelled from $t = 0$ to $t = 5$.
@step Turning points in the interval: $t = 2$ and $t = 4$.
@step Positions: $s(0) = 0$, $s(2) = 20$, $s(4) = 16$, $s(5) = 125 - 225 + 120 = 20$.
@step Displacement: $s(5) - s(0) = 20 - 0 = 20$ m.
@step Distance: $|20 - 0| + |16 - 20| + |20 - 16| = 20 + 4 + 4 = 28$ m.
@answer Displacement $20$ m; total distance $28$ m.
:::

:::tip Make a position table
List the position at the start, at every turning point and at the end. The total distance is the sum of the gaps between consecutive positions.
:::

:::

:::summary
- $v(t) = s'(t)$, $a(t) = v'(t) = s''(t)$, and speed is $|v(t)|$.
- $v > 0$: positive direction; $v < 0$: negative direction; $v = 0$: at rest (a possible turning point).
- Speeding up when $v$ and $a$ have the same sign; slowing down when they have opposite signs.
- Displacement is $s(t_2) - s(t_1)$; total distance adds the distances of each leg between turning points.
:::
