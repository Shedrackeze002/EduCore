The elevation profile of a hiking trail in Kananaskis climbs, levels off at a summit, drops into a valley and climbs again. The sign of the slope tells you whether you are going up or down, and the summits and valleys are where the slope is zero. The derivative does the same job for any function.

## Increasing and decreasing

:::definition Increasing and decreasing
$f$ is **increasing** on an interval if $f(x_1) < f(x_2)$ whenever $x_1 < x_2$ in the interval, and **decreasing** if $f(x_1) > f(x_2)$ whenever $x_1 < x_2$.
:::

A tangent line that slopes upward means the graph is rising, so the sign of $f'$ tells us the direction of the graph:

- if $f'(x) > 0$ for every $x$ in an interval, $f$ is increasing on that interval;
- if $f'(x) < 0$ for every $x$ in an interval, $f$ is decreasing on that interval.

(A proof uses the Mean Value Theorem: between any two points the average rate of change equals $f'(c)$ for some $c$, so it has the same sign as $f'$.)

Intervals of increase and decrease are intervals of $x$-values, written in interval notation such as $(-\infty, -1) \cup (3, \infty)$.

## Critical numbers

$f'$ can change sign only where it is $0$ or where it does not exist, so those values divide the number line into intervals of increase and decrease.

:::definition Critical number
A **critical number** of $f$ is a number $c$ **in the domain of $f$** where $f'(c) = 0$ or $f'(c)$ does not exist.
:::

:::example Critical numbers of a polynomial
Find the critical numbers of $f(x) = x^{3} - 3x^{2} - 9x + 2$.
@step $f'(x) = 3x^{2} - 6x - 9 = 3(x^{2} - 2x - 3) = 3(x + 1)(x - 3)$
@step A polynomial's derivative exists everywhere, so only $f'(x) = 0$ matters.
@answer The critical numbers are $-1$ and $3$.
:::

:::example Where the derivative does not exist
Find the critical numbers of $g(x) = x^{2/3}$.
@step $g'(x) = \dfrac{2}{3}x^{-1/3} = \dfrac{2}{3\sqrt[3]{x}}$, which is never $0$.
@step $g'(0)$ does not exist, but $g(0) = 0$ is defined.
@answer The only critical number is $0$, where the graph has a sharp point (a cusp).
:::

:::mistake Values outside the domain
For $h(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$, $h'(x) = -\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$ does not exist at $x = 0$, but $0$ is **not** a critical number because $h(0)$ is undefined. Always check that a candidate is in the domain.
:::

## The first derivative test

:::definition Local maximum and local minimum
$f(c)$ is a **local maximum** value if $f(c) \ge f(x)$ for all $x$ near $c$, and a **local minimum** value if $f(c) \le f(x)$ for all $x$ near $c$.
:::

**First derivative test.** At a critical number $c$: if $f'$ changes from $+$ to $-$, $f$ has a local maximum; if $f'$ changes from $-$ to $+$, a local minimum; if $f'$ does not change sign, there is no local extremum.

:::example A sign chart
Find the intervals of increase and decrease and the local extrema of $f(x) = x^{3} - 3x^{2} - 9x + 2$.
@step The critical numbers $-1$ and $3$ split the line into $(-\infty, -1)$, $(-1, 3)$ and $(3, \infty)$.
@step Test a value in each: $f'(-2) = 15 > 0$, $f'(0) = -9 < 0$, $f'(4) = 15 > 0$.
@step $f'$ changes from $+$ to $-$ at $x = -1$: local maximum $f(-1) = -1 - 3 + 9 + 2 = 7$.
@step $f'$ changes from $-$ to $+$ at $x = 3$: local minimum $f(3) = 27 - 27 - 27 + 2 = -25$.
@answer Increasing on $(-\infty, -1) \cup (3, \infty)$, decreasing on $(-1, 3)$; local maximum $(-1, 7)$, local minimum $(3, -25)$.
:::

## Absolute extrema on a closed interval

The **absolute maximum** of $f$ on an interval is the largest value of $f$ there, and the **absolute minimum** is the smallest. The **Extreme Value Theorem** guarantees both exist when $f$ is continuous on a closed interval $[a, b]$, and they must occur at a critical number or at an endpoint.

:::definition Closed interval method
To find the absolute extrema of a continuous $f$ on $[a, b]$:
1. Find the critical numbers of $f$ in $(a, b)$.
2. Evaluate $f$ at those critical numbers and at the endpoints $a$ and $b$.
3. The largest value is the absolute maximum; the smallest is the absolute minimum.
:::

:::example On a closed interval
Find the absolute maximum and minimum of $f(x) = x^{3} - 3x^{2} - 9x + 2$ on $[-2, 4]$.
@step Critical numbers in $(-2, 4)$: $-1$ and $3$.
@step $f(-2) = -8 - 12 + 18 + 2 = 0$, $f(-1) = 7$, $f(3) = -25$, $f(4) = 64 - 48 - 36 + 2 = -18$.
@answer Absolute maximum $7$ at $x = -1$; absolute minimum $-25$ at $x = 3$.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the endpoints
On $[-2, 5]$ the same function has $f(5) = 125 - 75 - 45 + 2 = 7$, which ties the local maximum. On a longer interval such as $[-2, 6]$, $f(6) = 56$ beats it, so the absolute maximum is at an endpoint. Always include the endpoints.
:::

:::check m31-extrema 3
:::

:::summary
- $f' > 0$ on an interval means $f$ increases; $f' < 0$ means $f$ decreases.
- Critical numbers: values in the domain where $f'(c) = 0$ or $f'(c)$ does not exist.
- First derivative test: $+$ to $-$ gives a local maximum, $-$ to $+$ a local minimum, no sign change gives neither.
- Closed interval method: compare $f$ at the critical numbers and the endpoints.
:::
