A woodturner shapes a table leg by spinning a block on a lathe, and a potter shapes a bowl on a wheel. Each solid is traced out by rotating a flat region about an axis. Slicing such a solid into thin discs turns its volume into an integral.

## Slicing into discs

:::definition Solid of revolution
A **solid of revolution** is the solid formed when a plane region is rotated about a line, the **axis of rotation**.
:::

Rotate the region under $y = f(x) \ge 0$, $a \le x \le b$, about the $x$-axis. A slice perpendicular to the axis at $x$ is a thin disc (dashed below) with radius $f(x)$ and thickness $\Delta x$, so its volume is $\pi[f(x)]^{2}\,\Delta x$. Adding the discs and letting $\Delta x \to 0$:

:::definition Disc method
$$V = \pi\int_a^b \big[f(x)\big]^{2}\,dx$$
The radius $f(x)$ is the distance from the axis to the curve.
:::

:::example A disc integral
The region under $y = \sqrt{x}$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$ is rotated about the $x$-axis. Find the volume.
@step Each disc has radius $\sqrt{x}$, so $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^4 \left(\sqrt{x}\right)^{2}dx = \pi\int_0^4 x\,dx$.
@step $= \pi\Big[\dfrac{x^{2}}{2}\Big]_0^4 = \pi(8 - 0)$
@answer $V = 8\pi \approx 25.13$ cubic units
:::

## Checking with a cone and a sphere

The disc method reproduces the volume formulas you already know.

- **Cone:** rotating $y = \frac{r}{h}x$, $0 \le x \le h$, gives $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^h \frac{r^{2}}{h^{2}}x^{2}\,dx = \pi\frac{r^{2}}{h^{2}}\cdot\frac{h^{3}}{3} = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h$.
- **Sphere:** rotating $y = \sqrt{r^{2} - x^{2}}$, $-r \le x \le r$, gives $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_{-r}^{r} (r^{2} - x^{2})\,dx = \pi\Big[r^{2}x - \frac{x^{3}}{3}\Big]_{-r}^{r} = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}$.

:::example A cone
The region under $y = 2x$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 3$ is rotated about the $x$-axis. Find the volume.
@step $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^3 (2x)^{2}\,dx = \pi\int_0^3 4x^{2}\,dx = \pi\Big[\frac{4x^{3}}{3}\Big]_0^3 = 36\pi$
@step Check: the solid is a cone of radius $6$ and height $3$, and $\frac{1}{3}\pi(6)^{2}(3) = 36\pi$.
@answer $V = 36\pi \approx 113.10$ cubic units
:::

:::mistake Square the radius, keep the pi
The integrand is $\pi[f(x)]^{2}$, not $\pi f(x)$ or $[f(x)]^{2}$. For $y = \sqrt{x}$, the integrand is $\pi x$, not $\pi\sqrt{x}$.
:::

:::check m31-volume-revolution 2
:::

## Rotating about the y-axis

To rotate about the $y$-axis, slice perpendicular to the $y$-axis. Each slice is a disc of radius $x = g(y)$ and thickness $\Delta y$, so
$$V = \pi\int_c^d \big[g(y)\big]^{2}\,dy.$$
First solve the equation of the curve for $x$ in terms of $y$.

:::example About the y-axis
The region in the first quadrant bounded by $y = x^{2}$, the $y$-axis and the line $y = 4$ is rotated about the $y$-axis. Find the volume.
@step Solve for $x$: $x = \sqrt{y}$, so a slice at height $y$ has radius $\sqrt{y}$.
@step $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^4 \left(\sqrt{y}\right)^{2}dy = \pi\int_0^4 y\,dy = \pi\Big[\frac{y^{2}}{2}\Big]_0^4$
@answer $V = 8\pi \approx 25.13$ cubic units
:::

## Washers

If the region does not touch the axis, each slice is a **washer**: a disc with a hole. With outer radius $R(x)$ and inner radius $r(x)$, its area is $\pi R^{2} - \pi r^{2}$.

:::definition Washer method
$$V = \pi\int_a^b \Big(\big[R(x)\big]^{2} - \big[r(x)\big]^{2}\Big)\,dx$$
:::

:::example Between two curves
The region enclosed by $y = x$ and $y = x^{2}$ is rotated about the $x$-axis. Find the volume.
@step $x = x^{2}$ gives $x = 0$ or $x = 1$. On $[0, 1]$, $x \ge x^{2}$, so $R = x$ and $r = x^{2}$.
@step $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^1 (x^{2} - x^{4})\,dx = \pi\Big[\frac{x^{3}}{3} - \frac{x^{5}}{5}\Big]_0^1 = \pi\left(\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{5}\right)$
@answer $V = \dfrac{2\pi}{15} \approx 0.42$ cubic units
:::

:::mistake Square each radius separately
The washer's area is $\pi(R^{2} - r^{2})$, not $\pi(R - r)^{2}$. At $x = \frac{1}{2}$ above, $R = \frac{1}{2}$ and $r = \frac{1}{4}$: $\pi\left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{16}\right) = \frac{3\pi}{16}$, but $\pi\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{2} = \frac{\pi}{16}$.
:::

:::tip Another axis
To rotate about another line, measure each radius from that line. Rotating the region under $y = \sqrt{x}$, $0 \le x \le 4$, about $y = -1$ gives washers with $R = \sqrt{x} + 1$ and $r = 1$:
$V = \pi\int_0^4 \left[(\sqrt{x} + 1)^{2} - 1\right]dx = \pi\int_0^4 (x + 2\sqrt{x})\,dx = \pi\left(8 + \frac{32}{3}\right) = \frac{56\pi}{3}$.
:::

:::

:::summary
- Disc method: $V = \pi\int_a^b [f(x)]^{2}\,dx$, where the radius $f(x)$ is the distance from the axis to the curve.
- About the $y$-axis, solve for $x = g(y)$ and use $V = \pi\int_c^d [g(y)]^{2}\,dy$.
- Washer method: $V = \pi\int_a^b \left([R(x)]^{2} - [r(x)]^{2}\right)dx$; square each radius separately.
- About any other line, measure each radius from that line.
- Give exact answers in terms of $\pi$, or round at the end.
:::
