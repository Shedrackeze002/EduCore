In section 3.1 we found the slope of a tangent at one point by letting a secant line shrink. Doing the same calculation at a general point $x$ produces a whole new function — the **derivative** — that gives the slope of the tangent everywhere at once.

## The definition of the derivative

:::definition Derivative
The derivative of $f$ is the function
$$f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h},$$
wherever this limit exists. If $f'(a)$ exists, $f$ is **differentiable** at $x = a$, and $f'(a)$ is the slope of the tangent at $(a, f(a))$.
:::

Other notations for the derivative of $y = f(x)$ are $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, $y'$ and $\dfrac{d}{dx}f(x)$. Finding a derivative with the limit definition is called differentiating **from first principles**. It always takes four steps:

1. Write $f(x + h)$.
2. Subtract $f(x)$ and simplify.
3. Divide by $h$ and cancel it.
4. Let $h \to 0$.

:::example A quadratic
Find $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = x^2 - 4x$, then find $f'(3)$.
@step $f(x + h) = (x + h)^2 - 4(x + h) = x^2 + 2xh + h^2 - 4x - 4h$
@step $f(x + h) - f(x) = 2xh + h^2 - 4h$
@step $\dfrac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h} = 2x + h - 4$
@step $f'(x) = \displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} (2x + h - 4) = 2x - 4$, so $f'(3) = 2(3) - 4 = 2$.
@answer $f'(x) = 2x - 4$ and $f'(3) = 2$
:::

The graph shows $f$ (blue) and $f'$ (orange). Where $f$ falls, $f'$ is negative; at the vertex, where the tangent is horizontal, $f'(2) = 0$; where $f$ rises, $f'$ is positive.

## Polynomials

:::example A cubic
Find $\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^3)$ from first principles.
@step $(x + h)^3 - x^3 = x^3 + 3x^2h + 3xh^2 + h^3 - x^3 = 3x^2h + 3xh^2 + h^3$
@step Divide by $h$: $3x^2 + 3xh + h^2$
@step Let $h \to 0$: the terms with $h$ vanish.
@answer $\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^3) = 3x^2$
:::

Notice the pattern: $x^2$ has derivative $2x$ and $x^3$ has derivative $3x^2$. Unit 4 proves the **power rule** that generalizes it. For now, the definition is the tool.

:::mistake Brackets when subtracting
Write $f(x + h) - [f(x)]$ with brackets: in $f(x + h) - (x^2 - 4x)$, the $-4x$ becomes $+4x$. Forgetting the brackets leaves terms without $h$, and the limit will not work out.
:::

## Interpreting the derivative

$f'(a)$ is the instantaneous rate of change of $f$ at $x = a$. Its units are (units of $f$) per (unit of $x$), and its sign tells whether $f$ is increasing or decreasing.

:::example A draining tank
$V(t)$ is the volume of water in a tank, in litres, $t$ minutes after a valve is opened, and $V'(10) = -15$. What does this mean?
@step The units of $V'(t)$ are litres per minute.
@step The value is negative, so the volume is decreasing.
@answer Ten minutes after the valve opens, the water is draining at $15$ L/min.
:::

:::example Marginal cost
$C(x)$ is the cost, in dollars, of making $x$ hockey jerseys, and $C'(200) = 18$. Interpret this.
@step The units of $C'(x)$ are dollars per jersey.
@answer When 200 jerseys have been made, the cost is increasing at \$18 per jersey: making the 201st jersey costs about \$18.
:::

:::mistake The derivative is not the function value
$V'(10) = -15$ does not say the tank holds $-15$ L, or that $15$ L drained during the first ten minutes. It is a rate at one instant.
:::

## Reciprocal and square-root functions

The same four steps work for other functions; the algebra in step 2 changes.

:::example A reciprocal
Find $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$.
@step $f(x + h) - f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x + h} - \dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{x - (x + h)}{x(x + h)} = \dfrac{-h}{x(x + h)}$
@step Divide by $h$: $\dfrac{-1}{x(x + h)}$
@step Let $h \to 0$: $\dfrac{-1}{x \cdot x}$
@answer $f'(x) = -\dfrac{1}{x^2}$
:::

At $x = 1$ the slope is $f'(1) = -1$, so the tangent at $(1, 1)$ is $y = -x + 2$:

:::example A square root
Find $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$.
@step Multiply by the conjugate: $\dfrac{\sqrt{x + h} - \sqrt{x}}{h} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{x + h} + \sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{x + h} + \sqrt{x}} = \dfrac{(x + h) - x}{h(\sqrt{x + h} + \sqrt{x})}$
@step Cancel $h$: $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x + h} + \sqrt{x}}$
@step Let $h \to 0$: $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x} + \sqrt{x}}$
@answer $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$ for $x > 0$
:::

:::tip The algebra of Unit 2 returns
Step 2 uses exactly the methods of section 2.2: combine fractions for reciprocals, multiply by a conjugate for square roots.
:::

A function is **not** differentiable where its graph has a corner (such as $y = |x|$ at $x = 0$), a vertical tangent, or a discontinuity. If $f$ is differentiable at $a$, it must be continuous there.

:::check m31-first-principles 3
:::

:::summary
- $f'(x) = \displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}$ gives the slope of the tangent at every $x$.
- First principles: write $f(x + h)$, subtract $f(x)$, divide by $h$, let $h \to 0$.
- $\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^2) = 2x$, $\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^3) = 3x^2$, $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right) = -\dfrac{1}{x^2}$, $\dfrac{d}{dx}\sqrt{x} = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$.
- $f'(a)$ is an instantaneous rate, in units of $f$ per unit of $x$; its sign shows increase or decrease.
:::
