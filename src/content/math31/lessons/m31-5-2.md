Populations, investments, radioactive samples and cooling cups of coffee all change at a rate proportional to their size. The function that models them, $e^x$, has a remarkable property: it is its own derivative.

## The derivative of e^x

For any base $a > 0$, the definition of the derivative gives
$$\frac{d}{dx}a^x = \lim_{h \to 0}\frac{a^{x + h} - a^x}{h} = a^x\cdot\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{a^h - 1}{h}.$$
So the derivative of $a^x$ is $a^x$ times a constant, the slope of $y = a^x$ at $x = 0$. The table estimates that constant for three bases:

The constant is about $0.693$ for base $2$ and about $1.099$ for base $3$. The number $e \approx 2.71828$ is the base that makes it exactly $1$.

:::definition The number e
$e$ is the number for which $\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{e^h - 1}{h} = 1$. Therefore
$$\frac{d}{dx}e^x = e^x.$$
:::

:::example The chain rule with e
Differentiate (a) $y = 4e^{x} - x^{3}$, (b) $y = e^{-3x}$, (c) $y = e^{x^{2} + 1}$.
@step (a) Term by term: $4e^{x} - 3x^{2}$.
@step (b) The inside is $-3x$: $e^{-3x}\cdot(-3) = -3e^{-3x}$.
@step (c) The inside is $x^{2} + 1$: $e^{x^{2} + 1}\cdot 2x$.
@answer (a) $4e^{x} - 3x^{2}$ (b) $-3e^{-3x}$ (c) $2xe^{x^{2} + 1}$
:::

## Other bases

Any base can be written with $e$: $a^x = \left(e^{\ln a}\right)^x = e^{x\ln a}$. By the chain rule, $\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{x\ln a} = e^{x\ln a}\ln a$, so:

:::definition Derivative of a^x
$$\frac{d}{dx}a^x = a^x\ln a$$
:::

This explains the table: $\ln 2 \approx 0.693$ and $\ln 3 \approx 1.099$. For example, $\dfrac{d}{dx}3^{x} = 3^{x}\ln 3$ and $\dfrac{d}{dx}5^{2x} = 5^{2x}\ln 5\cdot 2$. Leave $\ln a$ exact; type it as ln(3).

:::mistake a^x is not a power function
In $3^x$ the variable is in the exponent, so the power rule does not apply: $\dfrac{d}{dx}3^x \ne x\cdot 3^{x-1}$. The power rule is for $x^n$ with a constant exponent.
:::

## Logarithmic functions

Since $e^{\ln x} = x$ for $x > 0$, differentiate both sides with the chain rule: $e^{\ln x}\cdot\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln x = 1$, so $x\cdot\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln x = 1$. Change of base, $\log_a x = \dfrac{\ln x}{\ln a}$, handles other bases.

:::definition Derivatives of logarithms
$$\frac{d}{dx}\ln x = \frac{1}{x}, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\log_a x = \frac{1}{x\ln a}, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\ln g(x) = \frac{g'(x)}{g(x)}$$
:::

Before differentiating a logarithm of a product, quotient, power or root, **expand it** with the laws of logarithms. The derivative becomes a sum of simple fractions.

:::example Log laws first
Differentiate (a) $y = \ln(x^{2} + 1)$ and (b) $y = \ln\left(x^{3}(2x + 1)\right)$.
@step (a) $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{2x}{x^{2} + 1}$.
@step (b) Expand: $y = 3\ln x + \ln(2x + 1)$.
@step Then $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{3}{x} + \dfrac{2}{2x + 1}$.
@answer (a) $\dfrac{2x}{x^{2} + 1}$ (b) $\dfrac{3}{x} + \dfrac{2}{2x + 1}$
:::

:::mistake The derivative of ln(kx)
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln(5x) = \dfrac{5}{5x} = \dfrac{1}{x}$, not $\dfrac{5}{x}$. Indeed, $\ln(5x) = \ln 5 + \ln x$, and $\ln 5$ is a constant.
:::

:::example Products and quotients
Differentiate (a) $y = xe^{2x}$ and (b) $y = \dfrac{\ln x}{x}$.
@step (a) Product rule: $e^{2x} + x\cdot 2e^{2x} = e^{2x}(1 + 2x)$.
@step (b) Quotient rule: $\dfrac{\frac{1}{x}\cdot x - \ln x\cdot 1}{x^{2}} = \dfrac{1 - \ln x}{x^{2}}$.
@answer (a) $e^{2x}(1 + 2x)$ (b) $\dfrac{1 - \ln x}{x^{2}}$
:::

## Growth and decay

:::example A growing town
A town's population is modelled by $P(t) = 5000e^{0.03t}$, $t$ years from now. How fast is it growing after $10$ years?
@step $P'(t) = 5000(0.03)e^{0.03t} = 150e^{0.03t}$.
@step $P'(10) = 150e^{0.3} \approx 202.48$.
@answer About $202$ people per year
:::

:::example An investment
A balance of $A(t) = 1000(1.05)^{t}$ dollars grows at $5\%$ per year, compounded annually. How fast is it growing at $t = 10$?
@step $A'(t) = 1000(1.05)^{t}\ln 1.05$.
@step $A'(10) = 1000(1.05)^{10}\ln 1.05 \approx 79.47$.
@answer About \$79.47 per year
:::

:::tip e^u is never zero
When you solve $f'(x) = 0$ and the derivative has a factor $e^{u}$, divide it out: $e^{u} > 0$ for every $u$. For example, $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(xe^{-x}\right) = e^{-x}(1 - x) = 0$ only when $x = 1$.
:::

:::summary
- $\dfrac{d}{dx}e^x = e^x$ and $\dfrac{d}{dx}a^x = a^x\ln a$; with an inside function, multiply by its derivative.
- $\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln x = \dfrac{1}{x}$, $\dfrac{d}{dx}\log_a x = \dfrac{1}{x\ln a}$ and $\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln g(x) = \dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)}$.
- Expand logarithms of products, quotients, powers and roots before differentiating.
- Exponential models grow (or decay) at a rate proportional to their size.
:::
