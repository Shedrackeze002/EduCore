To differentiate $(2x + 1)^{10}$ with the power rule alone, you would have to expand ten brackets. The chain rule differentiates any composite function, such as a power, a root or a reciprocal of another function, without expanding.

## Rates that multiply

Picture three linked gears. If gear $u$ turns $3$ times as fast as gear $x$, and gear $y$ turns $2$ times as fast as gear $u$, then $y$ turns $2 \times 3 = 6$ times as fast as $x$. Rates of change multiply in the same way.

:::definition Chain rule
If $y = f(u)$ and $u = g(x)$, so that $y = f(g(x))$, then
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du}\cdot\frac{du}{dx}, \qquad \text{or} \qquad \frac{d}{dx}f(g(x)) = f'(g(x))\cdot g'(x).$$
Differentiate the **outside** function, leaving the inside alone, then multiply by the derivative of the **inside**.
:::

**Why it works.** A small change $\Delta x$ causes a change $\Delta u$, which causes a change $\Delta y$. When $\Delta u \ne 0$, $\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta u}\cdot\dfrac{\Delta u}{\Delta x}$, and as $\Delta x \to 0$ the two factors approach $\dfrac{dy}{du}$ and $\dfrac{du}{dx}$. (A complete proof also handles the case $\Delta u = 0$.)

:::example A power of a polynomial
Differentiate $y = (3x^{2} + 1)^{4}$.
@step Inside: $u = 3x^{2} + 1$, so $\dfrac{du}{dx} = 6x$. Outside: $y = u^{4}$, so $\dfrac{dy}{du} = 4u^{3}$.
@step $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 4(3x^{2} + 1)^{3}\cdot 6x$.
@answer $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 24x(3x^{2} + 1)^{3}$
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the inside
$\dfrac{d}{dx}(3x^{2} + 1)^{4} = 4(3x^{2} + 1)^{3}$ is wrong: it treats the inside as if it were $x$. The factor $6x$ from the inside function is the whole point of the chain rule.
:::

## Roots and reciprocals of functions

Combining the chain rule with the power rule gives the **general power rule**:
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left[g(x)\right]^n = n\left[g(x)\right]^{n-1}g'(x).$$
As in lesson 4.1, rewrite roots and reciprocals as powers first.

:::example A root
Find $f'(4)$ for $f(x) = \sqrt{x^{2} + 9}$.
@step Rewrite: $f(x) = (x^{2} + 9)^{1/2}$.
@step $f'(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}(x^{2} + 9)^{-1/2}\cdot 2x = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2} + 9}}$.
@step $f'(4) = \dfrac{4}{\sqrt{25}} = \dfrac{4}{5}$.
@answer $f'(4) = \dfrac{4}{5}$
:::

:::example A reciprocal
Differentiate $y = \dfrac{3}{(2x + 1)^{2}}$.
@step Rewrite: $y = 3(2x + 1)^{-2}$.
@step $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3(-2)(2x + 1)^{-3}\cdot 2$.
@answer $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{12}{(2x + 1)^{3}}$
:::

:::tip Quotient rule or chain rule?
When the numerator is a constant, the chain rule is faster than the quotient rule: $\dfrac{3}{(2x + 1)^{2}}$ is just $3(2x + 1)^{-2}$.
:::

:::check m31-chain-rule 2
:::

## Combining the rules

Most functions need several rules. Decide what the function is **as a whole** (a product, a quotient or a composition) and work inward.

:::example A product with a chain inside
Differentiate $f(x) = x^{2}(3x - 1)^{4}$.
@step It is a product: $u = x^{2}$ and $v = (3x - 1)^{4}$, so $u' = 2x$ and, by the chain rule, $v' = 4(3x - 1)^{3}\cdot 3 = 12(3x - 1)^{3}$.
@step $f'(x) = 2x(3x - 1)^{4} + 12x^{2}(3x - 1)^{3}$.
@step Factor out $2x(3x - 1)^{3}$: $f'(x) = 2x(3x - 1)^{3}\left[(3x - 1) + 6x\right]$.
@answer $f'(x) = 2x(3x - 1)^{3}(9x - 1)$
:::

The chain rule also works with a table of values. For $h(x) = f(g(x))$, the outside derivative is read at $g(a)$, which may be in a different row.

:::example A composition from a table
If $h(x) = f(g(x))$, find $h'(1)$.
@step Chain rule: $h'(1) = f'(g(1))\cdot g'(1)$.
@step Row $x = 1$: $g(1) = 4$ and $g'(1) = 5$. Row $x = 4$: $f'(4) = -2$.
@step $h'(1) = f'(4)\cdot g'(1) = (-2)(5)$.
@answer $h'(1) = -10$
:::

:::mistake Reading the wrong row
Using $f'(1) = 2$ instead of $f'(g(1)) = f'(4)$ gives $10$, which is wrong. The outside derivative is evaluated at the **output** of the inside function.
:::

## Nested functions and applications

When there are several layers, apply the chain rule once for each layer, from the outside in.

:::example Three layers
Differentiate $y = \left[(2x - 5)^{2} - 2\right]^{4}$.
@step Outside: $4\left[(2x - 5)^{2} - 2\right]^{3}$ times the derivative of the inside.
@step The inside, $(2x - 5)^{2} - 2$, has derivative $2(2x - 5)\cdot 2$ (chain rule again).
@answer $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 16(2x - 5)\left[(2x - 5)^{2} - 2\right]^{3}$
:::

:::example The speed of sound
A model for the speed of sound in air at $T$ °C is $v(T) = 20\sqrt{T + 273}$ m/s. How fast does the speed change with temperature at $16$ °C?
@step $v(T) = 20(T + 273)^{1/2}$, so $v'(T) = 20\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}(T + 273)^{-1/2}\cdot 1 = \dfrac{10}{\sqrt{T + 273}}$.
@step $v'(16) = \dfrac{10}{\sqrt{289}} = \dfrac{10}{17} \approx 0.59$.
@answer The speed increases by about $0.59$ m/s per degree Celsius.
:::

:::check m31-chain-rule 3
:::

:::summary
- Chain rule: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{dy}{du}\cdot\dfrac{du}{dx}$, or $\left[f(g(x))\right]' = f'(g(x))\,g'(x)$.
- General power rule: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left[g(x)\right]^n = n\left[g(x)\right]^{n-1}g'(x)$; rewrite roots and reciprocals as powers.
- Never forget the derivative of the inside function.
- For combinations, identify the outermost structure first; with a table, evaluate $f'$ at $g(a)$.
:::
