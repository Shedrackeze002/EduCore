The area between a revenue curve and a cost curve is a total profit; the area between the profiles of two riverbanks is a cross-section of the channel. Each is an area between two curves, and one idea finds them all: add up the heights of thin strips with a definite integral.

## Upper curve minus lower curve

On an interval where $f(x) \ge g(x)$, a thin vertical strip at $x$ (dashed below) has height $f(x) - g(x)$ and width $\Delta x$. Adding the strips and letting $\Delta x \to 0$ gives an integral.

:::definition Area between curves
If $f(x) \ge g(x)$ for $a \le x \le b$, the area of the region between the curves is
$$A = \int_a^b \big[f(x) - g(x)\big]\,dx = \int_a^b \big[(\text{upper curve}) - (\text{lower curve})\big]\,dx.$$
This works wherever the curves lie, above or below the $x$-axis: only their difference matters.
:::

## Finding the limits

Often a region is enclosed by the two curves alone. Then the limits of integration are the $x$-coordinates of the points where the curves meet.

:::example A region enclosed by two curves
Find the area of the region enclosed by $y = x^{2}$ and $y = 2x + 3$.
@step Set the curves equal: $x^{2} = 2x + 3$, so $x^{2} - 2x - 3 = 0$ and $(x + 1)(x - 3) = 0$. They meet at $x = -1$ and $x = 3$.
@step Test a point between them: at $x = 0$ the line gives $3$ and the parabola gives $0$, so the line is on top.
@step $A = \displaystyle\int_{-1}^{3} (2x + 3 - x^{2})\,dx = \Big[x^{2} + 3x - \frac{x^{3}}{3}\Big]_{-1}^{3} = 9 - \left(-\frac{5}{3}\right)$
@answer $\dfrac{32}{3} \approx 10.67$ square units
:::

## When the curves cross

If the curves cross inside the interval, the upper curve changes. Split the interval at each crossing and integrate (upper − lower) on each piece. In one formula, $A = \int_a^b |f(x) - g(x)|\,dx$.

:::example Two regions
Find the total area enclosed by $y = x^{3}$ and $y = x$.
@step $x^{3} = x$ gives $x(x - 1)(x + 1) = 0$, so $x = -1, 0, 1$.
@step On $[-1, 0]$, $x^{3} \ge x$ (at $x = -\frac{1}{2}$: $-\frac{1}{8} > -\frac{1}{2}$). On $[0, 1]$, $x \ge x^{3}$.
@step $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{0} (x^{3} - x)\,dx = \Big[\frac{x^{4}}{4} - \frac{x^{2}}{2}\Big]_{-1}^{0} = \frac{1}{4}$ and $\displaystyle\int_0^1 (x - x^{3})\,dx = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{4}$.
@answer $A = \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ square units
:::

## Integrating with respect to y

Some regions are easier to slice into horizontal strips: when the boundaries are given as $x = g(y)$, or when a vertical strip would meet different curves in different parts of the region.

:::definition Area with respect to y
If the curve $x = f(y)$ is to the right of $x = g(y)$ for $c \le y \le d$, then
$$A = \int_c^d \big[f(y) - g(y)\big]\,dy = \int_c^d \big[(\text{right curve}) - (\text{left curve})\big]\,dy.$$
:::

:::example Horizontal strips
Find the area of the region enclosed by $x = y^{2}$ and $x = y + 2$.
@step $y^{2} = y + 2$ gives $(y - 2)(y + 1) = 0$, so the curves meet at $y = -1$ and $y = 2$.
@step At $y = 0$ the line gives $x = 2$ and the parabola gives $x = 0$, so the line is on the right.
@step $A = \displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2} (y + 2 - y^{2})\,dy = \Big[\frac{y^{2}}{2} + 2y - \frac{y^{3}}{3}\Big]_{-1}^{2} = \frac{10}{3} - \left(-\frac{7}{6}\right)$
@answer $\dfrac{9}{2}$ square units
:::

:::summary
- Area between curves: $A = \int_a^b \big[(\text{upper}) - (\text{lower})\big]\,dx$, wherever the curves lie.
- For an enclosed region, solve $f(x) = g(x)$ to find the limits, and test a point to see which curve is on top.
- If the curves cross inside the interval, split at each crossing and add the areas of the pieces.
- For curves given as $x = g(y)$, integrate (right − left) with respect to $y$.
:::
