Adding up thousands of thin rectangles is slow, even for a computer. The fundamental theorem of calculus replaces that limit of sums with one subtraction, using an antiderivative. It ties together the two big ideas of calculus: rates of change (derivatives) and accumulation (integrals).

## Definite integrals as net area

The definite integral $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ is the limit of Riemann sums. Where $f$ is negative, each product $f(x_{i}^{*})\,\Delta x$ is negative, so area below the $x$-axis counts as negative.

:::definition Definite integral and net area
In $\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$, the numbers $a$ and $b$ are the **limits of integration** and $f$ is the **integrand**. A definite integral is a number, the **net area** between the graph and the $x$-axis from $x = a$ to $x = b$:
$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = (\text{area above the } x\text{-axis}) - (\text{area below the } x\text{-axis}).$$
:::

## The fundamental theorem of calculus

:::definition Fundamental theorem of calculus
If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and $F$ is any antiderivative of $f$, then
$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a) = \Big[F(x)\Big]_a^b.$$
:::

Why is it true? Let $A(x)$ be the net area under $f$ from $a$ to $x$. Moving from $x$ to $x + h$ adds a thin strip of width $h$ and height about $f(x)$, so
$$\frac{A(x + h) - A(x)}{h} \approx f(x), \qquad\text{and as } h \to 0, \quad A'(x) = f(x).$$
So $A$ is an antiderivative of $f$, and $A(x) = F(x) + C$. Since $A(a) = 0$, $C = -F(a)$, and the whole area is $A(b) = F(b) - F(a)$.

:::example Using the theorem
Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^3 (3x^{2} - 2x)\,dx$.
@step An antiderivative is $F(x) = x^{3} - x^{2}$. No $+ C$ is needed: it would cancel in $F(b) - F(a)$.
@step $F(3) = 27 - 9 = 18$ and $F(1) = 1 - 1 = 0$.
@answer $\displaystyle\int_1^3 (3x^{2} - 2x)\,dx = 18 - 0 = 18$
:::

:::example Other functions
Evaluate (a) $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi} \sin x\,dx$ and (b) $\displaystyle\int_1^{e} \frac{1}{x}\,dx$.
@step (a) $\Big[-\cos x\Big]_0^{\pi} = -\cos\pi - (-\cos 0) = 1 + 1 = 2$
@step (b) $\Big[\ln x\Big]_1^{e} = \ln e - \ln 1 = 1 - 0 = 1$
@answer (a) $2$ (b) $1$
:::

:::mistake Subtract all of F(a)
Put $F(a)$ in brackets. With $F(x) = x^{3} - x^{2}$, $F(-1) = -1 - 1 = -2$, so $\int_{-1}^{3} (3x^{2} - 2x)\,dx = 18 - (-2) = 20$, not $18 - 2$.
:::

## Properties of definite integrals

:::definition Properties
For continuous $f$ and $g$ and a constant $k$:
- $\int_a^a f(x)\,dx = 0$ and $\int_b^a f(x)\,dx = -\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$
- $\int_a^b k\,f(x)\,dx = k\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ and $\int_a^b \big(f(x) \pm g(x)\big)\,dx = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx \pm \int_a^b g(x)\,dx$
- $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx + \int_b^c f(x)\,dx = \int_a^c f(x)\,dx$
:::

:::example Using the properties
Given $\int_0^4 f(x)\,dx = 10$ and $\int_0^4 g(x)\,dx = -3$, find $\int_0^4 \big(2f(x) - 5g(x) + 1\big)\,dx$.
@step Split the integral: $2\int_0^4 f(x)\,dx - 5\int_0^4 g(x)\,dx + \int_0^4 1\,dx$.
@step $\int_0^4 1\,dx = 4$ (a rectangle of height $1$ and width $4$), so the total is $2(10) - 5(-3) + 4 = 20 + 15 + 4$.
@answer $39$
:::

## Net area and total area

:::example Net area versus total area
For $f(x) = x^{2} - 2x$ on $[0, 3]$, find the definite integral and the total area between the graph and the $x$-axis.
@step $F(x) = \frac{1}{3}x^{3} - x^{2}$, so $\int_0^3 f(x)\,dx = F(3) - F(0) = (9 - 9) - 0 = 0$.
@step $f(x) = x(x - 2) = 0$ at $x = 2$. Then $\int_0^2 f(x)\,dx = \frac{8}{3} - 4 = -\frac{4}{3}$ and $\int_2^3 f(x)\,dx = 0 - \left(-\frac{4}{3}\right) = \frac{4}{3}$.
@step Total area $= \left|-\frac{4}{3}\right| + \left|\frac{4}{3}\right| = \frac{8}{3}$
@answer The integral is $0$, but the total area is $\frac{8}{3}$ square units.
:::

## Average value

:::definition Average value
The **average value** of $f$ on $[a, b]$ is
$$f_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx.$$
It is the height of the rectangle on $[a, b]$ that has the same area as the region under the curve.
:::

For $f(x) = x^{2}$ on $[0, 3]$, $\int_0^3 x^{2}\,dx = 9$, so $f_{\text{avg}} = \frac{9}{3} = 3$. The rectangle of height $3$ and width $3$ has the same area as the shaded region:

:::example Average velocity
A drone's velocity is $v(t) = 3t^{2} + 2t$ m/s, $t$ seconds after take-off. Find its average velocity over the first $2$ s.
@step $\int_0^2 (3t^{2} + 2t)\,dt = \Big[t^{3} + t^{2}\Big]_0^2 = 8 + 4 = 12$, so the drone moves $12$ m.
@step $v_{\text{avg}} = \dfrac{12}{2 - 0} = 6$
@answer $6$ m/s
:::

:::summary
- $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ is a number: the net area, with area below the $x$-axis counted as negative.
- Fundamental theorem: $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a)$ for any antiderivative $F$ of $f$.
- Integrals add over adjacent intervals, change sign when the limits are reversed, and split over sums and constant multiples.
- For the total area, split where $f(x) = 0$ and add the absolute values of the pieces.
- Average value: $f_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$.
:::
