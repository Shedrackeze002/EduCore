Zoom in far enough on any smooth curve and it starts to look like a straight line: its tangent line. In this lesson you will write equations of tangent and normal lines, find where a tangent has a given slope, and use the tangent line to estimate values such as $\sqrt{26}$ without a calculator.

## The equation of a tangent line

In Unit 3 you saw that the derivative $f'(a)$ is the slope of the tangent line at the point $(a, f(a))$. A point and a slope are all you need to write the equation of a line.

:::definition Tangent line
The tangent line to the graph of $f$ at $x = a$ is the line through $(a, f(a))$ with slope $f'(a)$:
$$y - f(a) = f'(a)(x - a).$$
:::

:::example A cubic
Find an equation of the tangent line to $f(x) = x^{3} - 3x + 1$ at $x = 2$.
@step Point: $f(2) = 8 - 6 + 1 = 3$, so the line passes through $(2, 3)$.
@step Slope: $f'(x) = 3x^{2} - 3$, so $m = f'(2) = 12 - 3 = 9$.
@step Point-slope form: $y - 3 = 9(x - 2)$.
@answer $y = 9x - 15$
:::

:::mistake Using f′(x) instead of f′(a)
The slope of a tangent line is a **number**, $f'(a)$. Writing $y - 3 = (3x^{2} - 3)(x - 2)$ gives a curve, not a line. Evaluate the derivative at the point first.
:::

## Normal lines

:::definition Normal line
The **normal line** to a curve at a point is the line through that point that is perpendicular to the tangent line. When $f'(a) \ne 0$, its slope is the negative reciprocal $-\dfrac{1}{f'(a)}$. (When $f'(a) = 0$ the tangent is horizontal and the normal is the vertical line $x = a$.)
:::

:::example Tangent and normal together
For $f(x) = x^{2} - 4x + 5$ at $x = 3$, find the tangent line and the normal line.
@step $f(3) = 9 - 12 + 5 = 2$, and $f'(x) = 2x - 4$, so $f'(3) = 2$.
@step Tangent: $y - 2 = 2(x - 3)$, so $y = 2x - 4$.
@step Normal slope: $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. Normal: $y - 2 = -\dfrac{1}{2}(x - 3)$, so $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{7}{2}$.
@answer Tangent $y = 2x - 4$; normal $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{7}{2}$
:::

## Horizontal and parallel tangents

To find where the tangent has a particular slope, set the derivative equal to that slope and solve for $x$. A **horizontal** tangent has slope $0$; a tangent **parallel** to a given line has the same slope as that line.

:::example Horizontal tangents
Find the points on $y = x^{3} - 6x^{2} + 9x + 1$ where the tangent line is horizontal.
@step $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^{2} - 12x + 9 = 3(x - 1)(x - 3)$
@step $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0$ when $x = 1$ or $x = 3$.
@step $y(1) = 1 - 6 + 9 + 1 = 5$ and $y(3) = 27 - 54 + 27 + 1 = 1$.
@answer The tangent is horizontal at $(1, 5)$ and $(3, 1)$.
:::

:::example A parallel tangent
Where is the tangent to $y = x^{2}$ parallel to the line $y = 4x - 1$?
@step The line has slope $4$, so solve $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x = 4$: $x = 2$.
@answer At $(2, 4)$; the tangent there is $y = 4x - 4$.
:::

:::tip Rewrite the line first
If the line is given in general form, such as $8x - 2y + 3 = 0$, solve for $y$ ($y = 4x + \tfrac{3}{2}$) to read its slope.
:::

## Linear approximation

Close to $x = a$, the tangent line stays very close to the curve, so its height is a good estimate of $f(x)$.

:::definition Linear approximation
The **linearization** of $f$ at $a$ is the tangent-line function
$$L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a).$$
For $x$ near $a$, the **linear approximation** is $f(x) \approx L(x)$.
:::

:::example Estimating a square root
Use a linear approximation to estimate $\sqrt{26}$.
@step Choose $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ and $a = 25$, a nearby perfect square: $f(25) = 5$.
@step $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$, so $f'(25) = \dfrac{1}{10}$, and $L(x) = 5 + \dfrac{1}{10}(x - 25)$.
@step $L(26) = 5 + \dfrac{1}{10}(1) = 5.1$
@answer $\sqrt{26} \approx 5.1$ (a calculator gives $5.0990\ldots$)
:::

The same idea estimates the change in a quantity: $\Delta f \approx f'(a)\,\Delta x$. For a cube with edges of $10$ cm, $V = s^{3}$ and $V'(10) = 3(10)^{2} = 300$, so lengthening each edge by $0.1$ cm adds about $300(0.1) = 30$ cm³: $V(10.1) \approx 1030$ cm³ (the exact volume is $1030.301$ cm³).

:::mistake Straying too far from a
The approximation is only good **near** $a$. Using $a = 25$ to estimate $\sqrt{40}$ gives $L(40) = 6.5$, while $\sqrt{40} \approx 6.32$. Choose $a$ close to $x$, with $f(a)$ and $f'(a)$ easy to find.
:::

:::summary
- Tangent line at $x = a$: $y - f(a) = f'(a)(x - a)$.
- Normal line: through the same point, with slope $-\dfrac{1}{f'(a)}$.
- Horizontal tangents: solve $f'(x) = 0$; tangents parallel to a line: solve $f'(x) = m$.
- Linear approximation: $f(x) \approx f(a) + f'(a)(x - a)$ for $x$ near $a$, and $\Delta f \approx f'(a)\,\Delta x$.
:::
