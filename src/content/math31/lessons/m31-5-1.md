Tides, springs, sound waves and alternating current all rise and fall periodically, and they are modelled with sine and cosine. To find how fast they change we need the derivatives of the trigonometric functions. Throughout calculus, angles are measured in **radians**.

## Two special limits

The derivative of $\sin x$ depends on two limits, both with $h$ in radians:
$$\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{\sin h}{h} = 1, \qquad \lim_{h \to 0}\frac{\cos h - 1}{h} = 0.$$
A table of values makes them believable (a geometric proof uses areas in the unit circle):

These limits, and every derivative in this lesson, are true only in radians. In degrees, $\dfrac{\sin h^\circ}{h} \to \dfrac{\pi}{180}$, and every derivative picks up an extra factor of $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$. Set your calculator to radian mode.

## The derivatives of sine and cosine

Use the sum identity $\sin(x + h) = \sin x\cos h + \cos x\sin h$ in the definition:
$$\frac{\sin(x + h) - \sin x}{h} = \sin x\cdot\frac{\cos h - 1}{h} + \cos x\cdot\frac{\sin h}{h}.$$
As $h \to 0$ this approaches $\sin x\cdot 0 + \cos x\cdot 1 = \cos x$. The same method with $\cos(x + h) = \cos x\cos h - \sin x\sin h$ gives the derivative of cosine.

:::definition Derivatives of sine and cosine
$$\frac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x, \qquad \frac{d}{dx}\cos x = -\sin x$$
:::

:::example An exact derivative value
Find $f'\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)$ for $f(x) = 4\sin x + 2\cos x$.
@step $f'(x) = 4\cos x - 2\sin x$.
@step $f'\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = 4\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) - 2\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$.
@answer $f'\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = 2 - \sqrt{3}$
:::

## The other four functions

Write the other functions in terms of sine and cosine and use the quotient rule. For tangent:
$$\frac{d}{dx}\tan x = \frac{d}{dx}\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} = \frac{\cos x\cos x - \sin x(-\sin x)}{\cos^{2} x} = \frac{1}{\cos^{2} x} = \sec^{2} x.$$
For secant, $\dfrac{d}{dx}(\cos x)^{-1} = -(\cos x)^{-2}(-\sin x) = \dfrac{1}{\cos x}\cdot\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x} = \sec x\tan x$.

:::definition The six derivatives
$$\begin{aligned} (\sin x)' &= \cos x & (\cos x)' &= -\sin x \\ (\tan x)' &= \sec^{2} x & (\cot x)' &= -\csc^{2} x \\ (\sec x)' &= \sec x\tan x & (\csc x)' &= -\csc x\cot x \end{aligned}$$
The derivatives of the "co-" functions (cosine, cotangent, cosecant) all have a negative sign.
:::

:::example Term by term
Differentiate $y = 3\sin x - 2\tan x + x^{2}$.
@step Use $(\sin x)' = \cos x$, $(\tan x)' = \sec^{2} x$ and the power rule on each term.
@answer $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3\cos x - 2\sec^{2} x + 2x$
:::

## With the chain, product and quotient rules

:::example Four combinations
Differentiate (a) $\sin(3x^{2})$, (b) $\sin^{3} x$, (c) $x^{2}\cos x$, (d) $\dfrac{\sin x}{1 + \cos x}$.
@step (a) Chain rule with inside $3x^{2}$: $\cos(3x^{2})\cdot 6x = 6x\cos(3x^{2})$.
@step (b) $\sin^{3} x = (\sin x)^{3}$, so $3\sin^{2} x\cdot\cos x$.
@step (c) Product rule: $2x\cos x + x^{2}(-\sin x) = 2x\cos x - x^{2}\sin x$.
@step (d) Quotient rule: $\dfrac{\cos x(1 + \cos x) - \sin x(-\sin x)}{(1 + \cos x)^{2}} = \dfrac{\cos x + \cos^{2} x + \sin^{2} x}{(1 + \cos x)^{2}} = \dfrac{1 + \cos x}{(1 + \cos x)^{2}}$.
@answer (a) $6x\cos(3x^{2})$ (b) $3\sin^{2} x\cos x$ (c) $2x\cos x - x^{2}\sin x$ (d) $\dfrac{1}{1 + \cos x}$
:::

:::mistake Where does the chain go?
$\sin(3x^{2})$ (the sine of $3x^2$) and $\sin^{3} x$ (the cube of $\sin x$) have different outside functions. In $\sin(3x^{2})$ the outside is sine; in $\sin^{3} x$ the outside is the cube.
:::

## Applications

:::example Horizontal tangents
Find all $x$ in $0 \le x < 2\pi$ where $f(x) = x + 2\cos x$ has a horizontal tangent.
@step $f'(x) = 1 - 2\sin x = 0$, so $\sin x = \tfrac{1}{2}$.
@step On the unit circle, $\sin x = \tfrac{1}{2}$ in quadrants I and II.
@answer $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$
:::

:::example A mass on a spring
A mass on a spring has position $s(t) = 5\cos 2t$ cm after $t$ seconds. Find its velocity at $t = \frac{\pi}{4}$ s and at $t = 1$ s.
@step $v(t) = s'(t) = -5\sin 2t\cdot 2 = -10\sin 2t$.
@step $v\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = -10\sin\frac{\pi}{2} = -10$ cm/s, and $v(1) = -10\sin 2 \approx -9.09$ cm/s (radians).
@answer $-10$ cm/s and about $-9.09$ cm/s
:::

:::summary
- In radians, $\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{\sin h}{h} = 1$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{\cos h - 1}{h} = 0$; these give $(\sin x)' = \cos x$.
- Learn the six derivatives; the "co-" functions have negative derivatives.
- Combine them with the chain, product and quotient rules: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\sin(g(x)) = \cos(g(x))\,g'(x)$.
- Use exact unit-circle values at special angles, and radian mode for decimals.
:::
