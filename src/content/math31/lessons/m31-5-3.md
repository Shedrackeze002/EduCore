The circle $x^{2} + y^{2} = 25$ is not the graph of a function, and solving for $y$ gives two messy formulas. Yet at the point $(3, 4)$ the circle clearly has a tangent line. Implicit differentiation finds its slope without solving for $y$. The second half of the lesson differentiates derivatives themselves.

## Implicit differentiation

An equation such as $x^{2} + y^{2} = 25$ defines $y$ **implicitly** as one or more functions of $x$. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a function of $x$. By the chain rule,
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left(y^{n}\right) = ny^{n-1}\frac{dy}{dx},$$
just as $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left[g(x)\right]^n = n\left[g(x)\right]^{n-1}g'(x)$.

:::definition Implicit differentiation
1. Differentiate every term with respect to $x$; each time you differentiate an expression in $y$, multiply by $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.
2. Collect the terms containing $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ on one side and factor it out.
3. Divide. The answer usually contains both $x$ and $y$.
:::

:::example A circle
Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for $x^{2} + y^{2} = 25$ and the slope of the tangent at $(3, 4)$.
@step Differentiate: $2x + 2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0$.
@step Solve: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{2x}{2y} = -\dfrac{x}{y}$.
@step At $(3, 4)$: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{3}{4}$.
@answer $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{x}{y}$; the tangent at $(3, 4)$ has slope $-\dfrac{3}{4}$.
:::

## Products and mixed terms

A term such as $xy$ is a product of two functions of $x$, so it needs the product rule: $\dfrac{d}{dx}(xy) = 1\cdot y + x\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.

:::example A tilted ellipse
Find the slope of the tangent to $x^{2} + xy + y^{2} = 7$ at the point $(1, 2)$.
@step Check the point: $1 + 2 + 4 = 7$ ✓
@step Differentiate: $2x + \left(y + x\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right) + 2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0$.
@step Collect: $(x + 2y)\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -(2x + y)$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{2x + y}{x + 2y}$.
@step At $(1, 2)$: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{2 + 2}{1 + 4}$.
@answer The slope is $-\dfrac{4}{5}$.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting dy/dx
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(y^{2}\right)$ is $2y\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, not $2y$, and $\dfrac{d}{dx}(xy)$ is $y + x\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, not $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$. Every time you differentiate $y$, the chain rule adds a factor of $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.
:::

The method works with any functions. For $e^{y} + xy = e$ at the point $(0, 1)$: $e^{y}\dfrac{dy}{dx} + y + x\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{y}{e^{y} + x} = -\dfrac{1}{e}$ at $(0, 1)$.

:::check m31-implicit 2
:::

## Higher derivatives

The derivative $f'$ is a function, so it has a derivative of its own.

:::definition Higher derivatives
The **second derivative** is $f''(x) = \dfrac{d}{dx}f'(x)$, also written $\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}$. Continuing gives the third derivative $f'''(x)$, and the $n$th derivative $f^{(n)}(x)$.
:::

:::example Differentiating again and again
Find all the derivatives of $f(x) = x^{4} - 3x^{2} + 5x$.
@step $f'(x) = 4x^{3} - 6x + 5$
@step $f''(x) = 12x^{2} - 6$ and $f'''(x) = 24x$
@step $f^{(4)}(x) = 24$ and $f^{(5)}(x) = 0$
@answer Every derivative after the fourth is $0$.
:::

If $s(t)$ is position, then velocity is $v(t) = s'(t)$ and **acceleration** is $a(t) = v'(t) = s''(t)$. For $s(t) = t^{3} - 6t^{2} + 9t$ metres: $v(t) = 3t^{2} - 12t + 9$ and $a(t) = 6t - 12$, so at $t = 3$ s the acceleration is $6$ m/s².

:::check m31-higher-derivative 1
:::

:::example Patterns
Find $\dfrac{d^{27}}{dx^{27}}\sin x$ and the $n$th derivative of $e^{2x}$.
@step The derivatives of $\sin x$ are $\cos x$, $-\sin x$, $-\cos x$, $\sin x$, and then they repeat every $4$.
@step $27 = 4(6) + 3$, so the $27$th derivative equals the third: $-\cos x$.
@step Each derivative of $e^{2x}$ multiplies by $2$: $2e^{2x}$, $4e^{2x}$, $8e^{2x}$, …
@answer $\dfrac{d^{27}}{dx^{27}}\sin x = -\cos x$ and $\dfrac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}e^{2x} = 2^{n}e^{2x}$
:::

:::tip Simplify before differentiating again
Tidy each derivative (factor, combine fractions) before you differentiate it again. For $y = xe^{x}$, writing $y' = (x + 1)e^{x}$ makes $y'' = (x + 2)e^{x}$ quick.
:::

## Second derivatives implicitly

Differentiate $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ again, remembering that $y$ is still a function of $x$, then substitute the expression for $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.

:::example Concavity of a circle
Find $\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}$ for $x^{2} + y^{2} = 25$ at the point $(3, 4)$.
@step From before, $y' = -\dfrac{x}{y}$.
@step Quotient rule: $y'' = -\dfrac{1\cdot y - x\cdot y'}{y^{2}} = -\dfrac{y + \frac{x^{2}}{y}}{y^{2}} = -\dfrac{y^{2} + x^{2}}{y^{3}}$.
@step On the circle $x^{2} + y^{2} = 25$, so $y'' = -\dfrac{25}{y^{3}}$.
@step At $(3, 4)$: $y'' = -\dfrac{25}{64}$.
@answer $\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = -\dfrac{25}{64}$ at $(3, 4)$
:::

The negative value says the slope is decreasing there: the upper half of the circle bends downward.

:::summary
- Implicit differentiation: differentiate both sides with respect to $x$, multiplying by $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ each time you differentiate an expression in $y$; then solve for $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.
- Use the product rule on terms such as $xy$; substitute both coordinates to find a slope at a point.
- $f''$, $f'''$ and $f^{(n)}$ are found by differentiating repeatedly; acceleration is the second derivative of position.
- For $\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}$ implicitly, differentiate $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ and substitute $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ back in.
:::
