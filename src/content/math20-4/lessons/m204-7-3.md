Real rooms and yards are not always simple rectangles. An L-shaped kitchen or a lawn with a flower bed is made of simpler shapes. Once you know the area, you can work out how much flooring or sod to buy, and what it will cost.

## Adding the parts

:::definition Composite shape
A **composite shape** is a shape made from two or more simple shapes, such as rectangles, triangles and half circles.
:::

To find the area of a composite shape, split it into simple shapes. Find the area of each part. Then add the areas.

:::example An L-shaped kitchen floor
The kitchen floor shown is split into two rectangles by the dashed line. What is its total area?
@step Left rectangle: $3 \times 6 = 18\ \text{m}^2$
@step Right rectangle: $4 \times 2 = 8\ \text{m}^2$
@step Add: $18 + 8 = 26$
@answer The kitchen floor has an area of $26\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Counting a part twice
The parts must not overlap. If two parts share a piece, that piece gets counted twice and the area is too big. Split the shape with one line.
:::

## Taking away a part

Sometimes a part is cut out of a shape. Find the area of the whole shape. Then subtract the part that is not included.

:::

:::example Mowing around a flower bed
A lawn is $12\ \text{m}$ by $8\ \text{m}$. A flower bed that is $3\ \text{m}$ by $2\ \text{m}$ is in the lawn. What area of grass needs to be mowed?
@step Whole lawn: $12 \times 8 = 96\ \text{m}^2$
@step Flower bed: $3 \times 2 = 6\ \text{m}^2$
@step Subtract: $96 - 6 = 90$
@answer There is $90\ \text{m}^2$ of grass to mow.
:::

:::example A stage with a rounded front
A stage is a $6\ \text{m}$ by $4\ \text{m}$ rectangle with a half circle on one end. What is its area, to the nearest tenth?
@step Rectangle: $6 \times 4 = 24\ \text{m}^2$
@step The half circle's diameter is $4\ \text{m}$, so its radius is $2\ \text{m}$. Half circle: $\pi \times 2 \times 2 \div 2 \approx 6.28\ \text{m}^2$
@step Add: $24 + 6.28 = 30.28$
@answer The stage has an area of about $30.3\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting to halve the circle
A half circle is only half of $\pi \times r \times r$. Using the full circle adds too much area.
:::

## How much material to buy

Flooring comes in boxes, sod comes in rolls, and grass seed comes in bags. Each one covers a certain area. Divide the area by what one box, roll or bag covers. Then **round up**, because you cannot buy part of a box.

:::figure

:::

:::example Boxes of flooring
A bedroom floor is $3.6\ \text{m}$ by $3\ \text{m}$. One box of laminate flooring covers $2.2\ \text{m}^2$. How many boxes are needed?
@step Area: $3.6 \times 3 = 10.8\ \text{m}^2$
@step $10.8 \div 2.2 \approx 4.91$
@answer Round up: buy $5$ boxes.
:::

:::mistake Rounding down
$4$ boxes cover only $4 \times 2.2 = 8.8\ \text{m}^2$. That is not enough for $10.8\ \text{m}^2$. When you buy materials, always round up.
:::

:::

## Adding GST and extra for waste

In this lesson, GST of $5\%$ is added to the price. To add GST, find $5\%$ of the cost ($\times 0.05$), then add it on. Installers often buy $10\%$ extra to allow for cutting and waste. To add $10\%$, multiply the area by $1.1$ **before** you divide.

:::example Flooring with waste and GST
The bedroom above is $10.8\ \text{m}^2$. Add $10\%$ for waste. Each box covers $2.2\ \text{m}^2$ and costs \$39.99. What is the total cost with GST?
@step Add waste: $10.8 \times 1.1 = 11.88\ \text{m}^2$
@step Boxes: $11.88 \div 2.2 = 5.4$, so round up to $6$ boxes.
@step Cost before tax: $6 \times 39.99 = 239.94$
@step GST: $239.94 \times 0.05 \approx 12.00$
@answer Total: $239.94 + 12.00 = 251.94$. The flooring costs \$251.94.
:::

:::mistake Adding the waste to the boxes
Add the $10\%$ to the **area** before you divide. If you add it after rounding, you may buy the wrong number of boxes.
:::

:::tip Make a list of steps
Area → add waste (if asked) → divide → round up → multiply by the price → add GST. Do one step at a time.
:::

:::summary
- A composite shape is made of simple shapes. Add the parts, or subtract a part that is cut out.
- A half circle has half the area of a full circle.
- To find how much to buy, divide the area by what one item covers, then always round up.
- To add $10\%$ for waste, multiply the area by $1.1$ first.
- To add $5\%$ GST, find $5\%$ of the cost and add it on.
:::
