A couch, a laptop or a washing machine can cost a lot. Many stores let you take it home now and pay a little each month. That can help, but paying over time almost always costs more than paying cash.

## Instalment plans

When you pay over time, you make a **down payment** first. Then you make the same payment every month (or every week) until the plan ends.

:::definition Down payment and instalment
A **down payment** is the money you pay at the start. An **instalment** is one of the regular payments after that. A plan made of instalments is an **instalment plan**.
$$\text{total cost} = \text{down payment} + \text{number of payments} \times \text{each payment}$$
:::

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Payment plan for a TV","Amount"],"rows":[["Cash price","$800.00"],["Down payment","$100.00"],["Monthly payment","$65.00"],["Number of payments","12"]]}
:::

:::example The total cost of a plan
Use the TV plan in the table. What is the total cost of the plan?
@step All the monthly payments: $12 \times 65.00 = 780.00$
@step Add the down payment: $100.00 + 780.00 = 880.00$
@answer The plan costs \$880.00 in all.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the down payment
The down payment is part of the cost. If you only add up the monthly payments, you get \$780.00, which is \$100.00 too low.
:::

## The finance charge

The extra money you pay for the chance to pay over time is called the **finance charge**.

:::definition Finance charge
The **finance charge** is the extra cost of paying over time.
$$\text{finance charge} = \text{total cost of the plan} - \text{cash price}$$
:::

:::example The TV again
The TV plan costs \$880.00. The cash price is \$800.00.
@step $880.00 - 800.00 = 80.00$
@answer The finance charge is \$80.00. Paying over time costs \$80.00 more.
:::

:::check m204-instalment 1
:::

:::example No money down
A laptop costs \$949.99 cash. The store also offers \$0 down and 24 monthly payments of \$46.50.
@step Total cost: $24 \times 46.50 = 1{,}116.00$
@step Finance charge: $1{,}116.00 - 949.99 = 166.01$
@answer The plan costs \$1,116.00, which is \$166.01 more than cash.
:::

:::note Rent-to-own stores
Some stores let you pay **every week**. The payment looks small, but it adds up. 78 weekly payments of \$19.99 is $78 \times 19.99 = 1{,}559.22$ dollars. For a washer with a cash price of \$899.99, that is \$659.23 extra.
:::

:::

## Comparing payment plans

Sometimes the down payment is a **percent** of the cash price. Find it first, then add the monthly payments.

:::example 20% down
An e-bike costs \$1,500 cash. The plan is 20% down, then 12 monthly payments of \$109.99.
@step Down payment: $0.2 \times 1{,}500 = 300.00$
@step Monthly payments: $12 \times 109.99 = 1{,}319.88$
@step Total cost: $300.00 + 1{,}319.88 = 1{,}619.88$
@step Finance charge: $1{,}619.88 - 1{,}500.00 = 119.88$
@answer The plan costs \$1,619.88, which is \$119.88 more than cash.
:::

To compare two plans, find the total cost of each one. The plan with the smaller total costs less.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Plan","Down payment","Monthly payment","Number of payments"],"rows":[["Plan A","$200.00","$85.00","12"],["Plan B","$0.00","$54.99","24"]]}
:::

:::example Which plan costs less?
Use the table to compare the two plans.
@step Plan A: $200.00 + 12 \times 85.00 = 200.00 + 1{,}020.00 = 1{,}220.00$
@step Plan B: $24 \times 54.99 = 1{,}319.76$
@step Difference: $1{,}319.76 - 1{,}220.00 = 99.76$
@answer Plan A costs \$99.76 less, even though Plan B has the smaller monthly payment.
:::

:::tip Look at the total, not the monthly payment
A small monthly payment often means **more** payments. Always find the total cost before you sign.
:::

:::check m204-instalment 3
:::

:::summary
- Total cost of a plan $=$ down payment $+$ number of payments $\times$ each payment.
- Finance charge $=$ total cost of the plan $-$ cash price.
- A percent down payment is a percent of the cash price.
- To compare plans, compare their total costs. Saving up and paying cash is usually cheapest.
:::
