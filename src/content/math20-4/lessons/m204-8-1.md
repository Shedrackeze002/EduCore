Before a deck, a garage or a kitchen is built, someone draws a plan. The plan is much smaller than the real thing, but every length is shrunk in the same way. If you can read the scale, you can find any real length from the plan.

## What a scale means

:::definition Scale drawing and scale
A **scale drawing** is an accurate drawing of a real object, made bigger or smaller. A **floor plan** is a scale drawing of a building, seen from above. The **scale** tells how a length on the drawing compares with the real length.
:::

A scale can be written in words: "$1\ \text{cm}$ represents $1.5\ \text{m}$". That means every centimetre on the plan stands for $1.5$ metres in real life.

:::example The size of a living room
On this floor plan, $1\ \text{cm}$ represents $1.5\ \text{m}$. How long and how wide is the real living room?
@step Each centimetre is $1.5\ \text{m}$, so multiply.
@step Length: $6 \times 1.5 = 9$
@step Width: $4 \times 1.5 = 6$
@answer The living room is $9\ \text{m}$ long and $6\ \text{m}$ wide.
:::

## Measuring a plan with a ruler

Sometimes a plan does not show the lengths. Then you measure the drawing with a ruler and use the scale.

:::figure
{"type":"ruler","system":"metric","length":10,"start":0,"end":4.5}
:::

:::example A wall on a garage plan
Amara measures a wall on a garage plan. It is $4.5\ \text{cm}$ long, as shown. On the plan, $1\ \text{cm}$ represents $2\ \text{m}$. How long is the real wall?
@step Read the ruler: the wall is $4.5\ \text{cm}$ on the plan.
@step $4.5 \times 2 = 9$
@answer The real wall is $9\ \text{m}$ long.
:::

:::mistake Start at zero
Line up the start of the object with the $0$ mark, not with the end of the ruler. Otherwise every measurement is a little too long.
:::

## Ratio scales

:::definition Ratio scale
A scale can also be a **ratio**, like $1 : 50$. It means $1$ unit on the drawing is $50$ of the **same** unit in real life. So $1\ \text{cm}$ on the plan is $50\ \text{cm}$ in real life.
:::

:::example A kitchen wall
A kitchen plan uses a scale of $1 : 50$. A wall is $7.2\ \text{cm}$ long on the plan. How long is the real wall, in metres?
@step $7.2 \times 50 = 360$, so the wall is $360\ \text{cm}$.
@step There are $100\ \text{cm}$ in $1\ \text{m}$: $360 \div 100 = 3.6$
@answer The real wall is $3.6\ \text{m}$ long.
:::

To go the other way, from a real length to the drawing, **divide** by the scale.

:::example Drawing a fence
On a yard plan, $1\ \text{cm}$ represents $0.5\ \text{m}$. A real fence is $4.5\ \text{m}$ long. How long is it on the plan?
@step $4.5 \div 0.5 = 9$
@answer The fence is $9\ \text{cm}$ long on the plan.
:::

:::tip Check the size
A drawing is smaller than the real thing. If your real length comes out smaller than the drawing, you divided when you should have multiplied.
:::

## Finding the scale

To find the scale, put both lengths in the same unit. Then divide the real length by the drawing length.

:::example A model truck
A model pickup truck is $24\ \text{cm}$ long. The real truck is $6\ \text{m}$ long. What is the scale?
@step Change $6\ \text{m}$ to centimetres: $6 \times 100 = 600\ \text{cm}$.
@step $600 \div 24 = 25$
@answer The real truck is $25$ times as long. The scale is $1 : 25$.
:::

You can also use a scale to find an area. Find the real length and width first, then multiply.

:::example Flooring for a room
On a plan, a room is $5\ \text{cm}$ by $4\ \text{cm}$, and $1\ \text{cm}$ represents $1.5\ \text{m}$. How much flooring does the room need?
@step Real length: $5 \times 1.5 = 7.5\ \text{m}$. Real width: $4 \times 1.5 = 6\ \text{m}$.
@step Area: $7.5 \times 6 = 45$
@answer The room needs $45\ \text{m}^2$ of flooring.
:::

:::check m204-scale 3
:::

:::summary
- A scale compares a length on a drawing with the real length.
- "$1\ \text{cm}$ represents $2\ \text{m}$": multiply the drawing length by $2$ to get metres.
- $1 : 50$ means $1\ \text{cm}$ on the drawing is $50\ \text{cm}$ in real life. Divide centimetres by $100$ to get metres.
- From real length to drawing length, divide. To find a scale, divide the real length by the drawing length (same units).
:::
