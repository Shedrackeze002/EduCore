When you save money in a bank, the bank pays you for using it. When you borrow money, you pay the lender. That payment is called **interest**. Knowing how it works helps your savings grow and keeps loans from costing too much.

## What is interest?

:::definition Principal
The **principal** is the amount of money you save or borrow at the start.
:::

:::definition Interest
**Interest** is the extra money paid for using money. A bank pays you interest on savings. You pay interest on a loan.
:::

:::definition Interest rate
The **interest rate** is the percent of the principal paid as interest. In this lesson, every rate is a percent **per year**.
:::

:::example One year of interest
Ama puts \$1000 in a savings account that pays 3% per year. How much interest does she earn in one year?
@step Write the rate as a decimal: $3\% = 0.03$
@step $1000 \times 0.03 = 30$
@answer Ama earns \$30 in interest in one year.
:::

With **simple interest**, the interest is the same every year. Ama earns \$30 each year, so after 4 years she has earned \$120 and has \$1120 in the account.

## The simple interest formula

:::definition Simple interest
**Simple interest** is interest paid only on the principal. It is found with the formula $I = Prt$.
:::

$$I = Prt$$

- $I$ is the interest in dollars.
- $P$ is the principal.
- $r$ is the rate per year, written as a decimal.
- $t$ is the time in years.

:::example A loan for tools
Kwame borrows \$2500 for tools. The loan charges 6% per year simple interest. How much interest does he pay in 3 years?
@step $r = 6\% = 0.06$ and $t = 3$
@step $I = Prt = 2500 \times 0.06 \times 3 = 450$
@answer Kwame pays \$450 in interest.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting to change the percent
Using $r = 6$ gives $2500 \times 6 \times 3 = 45\,000$. That is far too much! Always change the percent to a decimal: $6\% = 0.06$.
:::

## Months and the total amount

The formula needs time in **years**. If the time is in months, divide by 12: $t = \text{months} \div 12$.

The **total amount** is the principal plus the interest: $A = P + I$. For a loan, it is what you pay back. For savings, it is what you have at the end.

:::example A car loan for 18 months
Jordan borrows \$3600 for 18 months at 7.5% per year simple interest. Find the interest and the total to pay back.
@step $r = 7.5\% = 0.075$ and $t = 18 \div 12 = 1.5$ years
@step $I = 3600 \times 0.075 \times 1.5 = 405$
@step $A = P + I = 3600 + 405 = 4005$
@answer The interest is \$405. Jordan pays back \$4005 in total.
:::

:::example Savings for 8 months
Lena saves \$1850 for 8 months at 2.5% per year simple interest. Find the interest to the nearest cent, and the total.
@step $I = 1850 \times 0.025 \times \dfrac{8}{12} \approx 30.833$
@step $A = 1850 + 30.83 = 1880.83$
@answer Lena earns about \$30.83, so she has \$1880.83 at the end.
:::

:::mistake Using months for t
For 18 months, $t$ is $1.5$, not $18$. Using $18$ makes the interest 12 times too big.
:::

:::

## Finding the rate or the time

Sometimes you know the interest and need the rate or the time. Put what you know into $I = Prt$. Then divide.

:::example Finding the rate
Mei put \$2000 in a savings account. After 3 years, it earned \$150 in simple interest. What is the rate?
@step $150 = 2000 \times r \times 3$, so $150 = 6000 \times r$
@step $r = 150 \div 6000 = 0.025$
@answer The rate is $0.025 \times 100 = 2.5\%$ per year.
:::

:::example Finding the time
Diego borrowed \$4000 at 5% per year simple interest. He paid \$300 in interest. How many months did he have the loan?
@step $300 = 4000 \times 0.05 \times t$, so $300 = 200 \times t$
@step $t = 300 \div 200 = 1.5$ years
@answer $1.5 \times 12 = 18$, so Diego had the loan for 18 months.
:::

:::check m204-simple-interest 3
:::

:::summary
- Principal $P$: the money saved or borrowed. Interest $I$: the extra money paid for using it.
- Simple interest: $I = Prt$, with $r$ as a decimal and $t$ in years.
- Months to years: $t = \text{months} \div 12$.
- Total amount: $A = P + I$.
- To find $r$ or $t$, put the numbers into $I = Prt$ and divide.
:::
