A credit card lets you buy now and pay later. If you pay the whole bill on time, it costs nothing extra. If you do not, the bank charges interest, and it adds up fast.

## How a credit card bill works

:::definition Credit
**Credit** means buying something now with borrowed money and paying it back later. Credit cards and loans are both credit.
:::

:::definition Balance
The **balance** is the amount you owe. The **statement balance** is what you owe on the monthly bill. The **unpaid balance** is the part you still owe after you make a payment.
:::

:::definition Minimum payment
The **minimum payment** is the smallest amount you must pay by the due date. It is often only a small percent of the balance.
:::

For most cards, if you pay the **full statement balance** by the due date, you pay no interest. If you pay less, the card charges interest on the unpaid balance. Each card has its own rules, so read your statement.

:::example Interest for one month
Tariq has an unpaid balance of \$400. His card charges 2% per month on the unpaid balance. How much interest is he charged?
@step $2\% = 0.02$
@step $400 \times 0.02 = 8$
@answer Tariq is charged \$8 in interest this month.
:::

## Yearly rates and monthly interest

Most cards show the rate **per year**. For one month of simple interest, multiply by the yearly rate and divide by 12.

$$\text{interest for one month} = \text{unpaid balance} \times \text{yearly rate} \div 12$$

:::example A card that charges 19.99% per year
Nadia has an unpaid balance of \$1250. Her card charges 19.99% per year. Find the interest for one month, to the nearest cent.
@step $19.99\% = 0.1999$
@step $1250 \times 0.1999 \div 12 \approx 20.823$
@answer Nadia is charged about \$20.82 for one month.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting to divide by 12
$1250 \times 0.1999 \approx 249.88$ is the interest for a whole **year**. For one month, divide by 12.
:::

:::

## The cost of a loan

A loan is paid back in equal monthly payments. The payments add up to more than you borrowed.

:::definition Cost of borrowing
The **cost of borrowing** is the extra money you pay for a loan.
$$\text{cost of borrowing} = \text{total paid} - \text{amount borrowed}$$
:::

:::example A loan for a used car
Marcus borrows \$9000 for a used car. He pays it back with 48 monthly payments of \$219.75. Find the total paid and the cost of borrowing.
@step Total paid: $219.75 \times 48 = 10\,548$
@step Cost of borrowing: $10\,548 - 9000 = 1548$
@answer Marcus pays \$10,548 in total. Borrowing costs him \$1548 extra.
:::

## Why paying the full balance matters

The less you leave unpaid, the less interest you pay. Paying the full balance means no interest at all.

:::example Three ways to pay a \$900 bill
Kiona’s statement balance is \$900. Her card charges 2% per month on the unpaid balance. Compare paying the minimum of \$27, paying \$500, and paying all \$900.
@step Pay \$27: unpaid $900 - 27 = 873$, interest $873 \times 0.02 = 17.46$
@step Pay \$500: unpaid $900 - 500 = 400$, interest $400 \times 0.02 = 8$
@step Pay \$900: unpaid $0$, interest $0$
@answer The interest is \$17.46, \$8 or \$0. Paying in full saves the most.
:::

:::note The minimum payment trap
Nadia’s balance was \$1250. She pays the 3% minimum, \$37.50. Next month the card adds about \$20.20 in interest. Her balance only drops by \$17.30. Paying only the minimum can take years and cost hundreds of dollars in interest.
:::

:::tip Use credit wisely
Only charge what you can pay off this month. If you already carry a balance, pay as much as you can and stop using the card until it is paid off.
:::

:::summary
- A credit card is a loan. Pay the full statement balance by the due date and you pay no interest.
- Interest for one month $=$ unpaid balance $\times$ monthly rate, or unpaid balance $\times$ yearly rate $\div 12$.
- Total paid on a loan $=$ payment $\times$ number of payments.
- Cost of borrowing $=$ total paid $-$ amount borrowed.
- Paying only the minimum keeps you in debt longer and costs much more.
:::
