Fencing a dog run, putting trim around a window and edging a flower bed are all the same kind of job. Each one needs the distance around a shape. If you measure it right, you buy the right amount and do not waste money.

## Perimeter of a rectangle

:::definition Perimeter
The **perimeter** of a shape is the distance all the way around its outside edge. It is a length, so it is measured in units like metres (m) or feet (ft).
:::

A rectangle has four sides: two lengths and two widths. To find its perimeter, add all four sides.

:::example Fencing a dog run
Amara is fencing the dog run shown above. It is $8\ \text{m}$ long and $5\ \text{m}$ wide. How much fencing does she need?
@step Add all four sides: $8 + 5 + 8 + 5 = 26$
@step Or use the shortcut $P = 2(l + w) = 2 \times 13 = 26$
@answer Amara needs $26\ \text{m}$ of fencing.
:::

:::tip Trace with your finger
Start at one corner. Trace all the way around the shape and say each side as you add it. Stop when you are back where you started.
:::

:::mistake Adding only two sides
$8 + 5 = 13$ is only halfway around the dog run. A rectangle has **four** sides.
:::

## Other shapes with straight sides

For any shape with straight sides, add the lengths of all the sides. A triangle has $3$ sides. An L-shaped room has $6$ sides.

:::

:::example Baseboard for an L-shaped room
Tariq is putting baseboard around the L-shaped room shown. Every side is labelled. What is the perimeter?
@step Count the sides: there are $6$.
@step Add them: $10 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 6 + 7 = 34$
@answer The perimeter is $34\ \text{m}$.
:::

:::mistake Missing a side
An L-shape has $6$ sides, not $4$. Count the sides before you add, and check that every side is used once.
:::

In a **regular** shape, all the sides are the same length. Multiply one side by the number of sides.

:::example A gazebo floor
A gazebo floor is a regular hexagon. A hexagon has $6$ sides. Each side is $2.5\ \text{m}$. What is the perimeter?
@step $6 \times 2.5 = 15$
@answer The perimeter is $15\ \text{m}$.
:::

:::

## Circumference of a circle

:::definition Circumference, diameter and radius
The **circumference** is the distance around a circle. The **diameter** is the distance across the circle through its centre. The **radius** is the distance from the centre to the edge. The diameter is $2$ times the radius.
:::

:::

For every circle, the circumference is a little more than $3$ times the diameter. That number is called $\pi$ (pi). It is about $3.14$. Use the $\pi$ key on your calculator.
$$C = \pi \times d$$

:::example Edging a round flower bed
A round flower bed has a diameter of $3\ \text{m}$. How much edging goes around it, to the nearest tenth of a metre?
@step $C = \pi \times 3 \approx 9.42$
@answer About $9.4\ \text{m}$ of edging is needed.
:::

:::example Banding a round table
A round table has a radius of $60\ \text{cm}$. How much edge banding goes around it, to the nearest tenth?
@step Diameter: $2 \times 60 = 120\ \text{cm}$
@step $C = \pi \times 120 \approx 376.99$
@answer About $377.0\ \text{cm}$ of banding is needed.
:::

:::mistake Using the radius in place of the diameter
$\pi \times 60 \approx 188.5\ \text{cm}$ only goes halfway around the table. If you are given the radius, double it first.
:::

:::

## The cost of edging and fencing

Edging, fencing and trim are often sold by the metre or by the foot. Find the perimeter first. Then multiply by the price for one unit.

:::figure

:::

:::example Edging a garden
A rectangular garden is $6.5\ \text{m}$ long and $4\ \text{m}$ wide. Garden edging costs \$3.49 per metre. What will the edging cost?
@step Perimeter: $6.5 + 4 + 6.5 + 4 = 21\ \text{m}$
@step Cost: $21 \times 3.49 = 73.29$
@answer The edging costs \$73.29.
:::

:::mistake Finding the area by mistake
$6.5 \times 4 = 26$ is the area of the garden, not the distance around it. Edging goes around the edge, so add the sides.
:::

:::check m204-perimeter 3
:::

:::summary
- Perimeter is the distance around a shape. Add all the sides.
- Rectangle: $P = l + w + l + w$, or $P = 2(l + w)$.
- Regular shape: multiply one side by the number of sides.
- Circle: the circumference is $C = \pi \times d$. If you know the radius, double it first.
- To find a cost, multiply the perimeter by the price for one metre or one foot.
:::
