How much carpet covers a floor? How much sod covers a lawn? How much pizza is in a large pizza? These questions are about area. Area tells you how much flat surface a shape covers.

## Area of a rectangle

:::definition Area
**Area** is the amount of surface a shape covers. It is measured in square units, such as square metres ($\text{m}^2$) or square feet ($\text{ft}^2$).
:::

A square metre is a square that is $1\ \text{m}$ on each side. The rectangle below is $4\ \text{m}$ long and $3\ \text{m}$ wide. It covers $12$ squares, so its area is $12\ \text{m}^2$.

:::figure

:::

You do not need to count squares. Multiply the length by the width:
$$A = l \times w$$

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":340,"yMin":0,"yMax":241,"step":1000},"polylines":[{"points":[[84.2,35],[312.8,35],[312.8,235],[84.2,235],[84.2,35]],"color":"brand"}],"points":[{"x":179.1,"y":11,"label":"4 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":32.2,"y":123,"label":"3.5 m","color":"muted","open":true}],"hideTickLabels":true,"caption":"bedroom floor"}
:::

:::example Carpet for a bedroom
A bedroom floor is $4\ \text{m}$ long and $3.5\ \text{m}$ wide. What is its area?
@step $A = l \times w$
@step $A = 4 \times 3.5 = 14$
@answer The floor has an area of $14\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Mixing up area and perimeter
Perimeter goes **around** a shape, so you add the sides. Area **covers** a shape, so you multiply. Area is always in square units, like $\text{m}^2$.
:::

## Triangles and parallelograms

The **height** of a shape is the straight-up distance from the base to the top. It meets the base at a right angle (a square corner). In the pictures, the height is the dashed line.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":340,"yMin":0,"yMax":208,"step":1000},"polylines":[{"points":[[45,35],[295,35],[120,201.7],[45,35]],"color":"brand"},{"points":[[129,35],[129,44],[120,44]],"color":"muted"}],"points":[{"x":135.4,"y":11,"label":"b = 6 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":126,"y":73,"label":"h = 4 m","color":"muted","open":true}],"hideTickLabels":true,"caption":"triangular flower bed","segments":[{"from":[120,201.7],"to":[120,35],"dashed":true,"color":"muted"}]}
:::

A triangle is half of a rectangle with the same base and height:
$$A = b \times h \div 2$$

:::example A triangular flower bed
A flower bed is a triangle with a base of $6\ \text{m}$ and a height of $4\ \text{m}$. What is its area?
@step $A = 6 \times 4 \div 2$
@step $A = 24 \div 2 = 12$
@answer The flower bed has an area of $12\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::definition Parallelogram
A **parallelogram** is a four-sided shape whose opposite sides are parallel. It looks like a rectangle that has been pushed over.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":340,"yMin":0,"yMax":125,"step":1000},"polylines":[{"points":[[53.9,35],[237.3,35],[303.9,118.3],[120.6,118.3],[53.9,35]],"color":"brand"},{"points":[[129.6,35],[129.6,44],[120.6,44]],"color":"muted"}],"points":[{"x":103.4,"y":11,"label":"b = 5.5 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":126.6,"y":64.7,"label":"h = 2.5 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":41.1,"y":80,"label":"3.2 m","color":"muted","open":true}],"hideTickLabels":true,"caption":"parallelogram-shaped parking stall","segments":[{"from":[120.6,118.3],"to":[120.6,35],"dashed":true,"color":"muted"}]}
:::

If you cut the triangle off one end of a parallelogram and move it to the other end, you get a rectangle. So:
$$A = b \times h$$

:::example A parking stall
A parking stall is a parallelogram with a base of $5.5\ \text{m}$ and a height of $2.5\ \text{m}$. Its slanted sides are $3.2\ \text{m}$ long. What is its area?
@step $A = b \times h = 5.5 \times 2.5 = 13.75$
@step The slanted side is not the height, so $3.2\ \text{m}$ is not used.
@answer The stall has an area of $13.75\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Using the slanted side as the height
The height must meet the base at a square corner. A slanted side is longer than the height, so using it gives an area that is too big.
:::

## Area of a circle

To find the area of a circle, you need its **radius**: the distance from the centre to the edge.
$$A = \pi \times r \times r$$
This is often written $A = \pi r^2$.

:::

:::example A circular patio
A circular patio has a radius of $3\ \text{m}$. What is its area, to the nearest tenth?
@step $A = \pi \times 3 \times 3 = \pi \times 9$
@step $\pi \times 9 \approx 28.27$
@answer The patio has an area of about $28.3\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::example A pizza
A pizza has a diameter of $10\ \text{in}$. What is its area, to the nearest tenth?
@step The radius is half of the diameter: $10 \div 2 = 5\ \text{in}$
@step $A = \pi \times 5 \times 5 = \pi \times 25 \approx 78.54$
@answer The pizza has an area of about $78.5\ \text{in}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Using the diameter
$\pi \times 10 \times 10 \approx 314\ \text{in}^2$ is $4$ times too big. Always halve the diameter to get the radius first.
:::

:::summary
- Area is the surface a shape covers. It is measured in square units.
- Rectangle: $A = l \times w$.
- Triangle: $A = b \times h \div 2$. Parallelogram: $A = b \times h$. The height meets the base at a square corner.
- Circle: $A = \pi \times r \times r$. If you know the diameter, halve it first.
:::
