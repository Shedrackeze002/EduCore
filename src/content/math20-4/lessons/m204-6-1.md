At work you might cut rope in metres, weigh flour in kilograms, or measure a board in feet and inches. Canada uses SI units, but many tools, recipes and products still use imperial units. Changing from one unit to another helps you get the job right.

## SI units

:::definition SI
**SI** is the system of units used in Canada. It is also called the metric system. Its units go up and down by $10$, $100$ or $1000$.
:::

Here are the SI facts you will use most. The word *kilo* means one thousand. *Centi* means one hundredth. *Milli* means one thousandth.

To change units, ask one question first: is the new unit **bigger** or **smaller**?

- Big unit to small unit: you need **more** of them. **Multiply.**
- Small unit to big unit: you need **fewer** of them. **Divide.**

:::example Rope for a job site
A piece of rope is $2.4\ \text{m}$ long. How many centimetres is that?
@step $1\ \text{m} = 100\ \text{cm}$. Centimetres are smaller, so multiply.
@step $2.4 \times 100 = 240$
@answer The rope is $240\ \text{cm}$ long.
:::

:::example Flour at a bakery
A bag of flour has a mass of $750\ \text{g}$. What is its mass in kilograms?
@step $1\ \text{kg} = 1000\ \text{g}$. Kilograms are bigger, so divide.
@step $750 \div 1000 = 0.75$
@answer The flour has a mass of $0.75\ \text{kg}$.
:::

:::mistake Multiplying when you should divide
$750 \times 1000 = 750\,000$, but a bag of flour cannot have a mass of $750\,000\ \text{kg}$. That is heavier than a loaded truck! Always check that your answer makes sense.
:::

## Imperial units

:::definition Imperial units
**Imperial** units are an older system of units. They are still used in the United States and on many Canadian job sites. Imperial units include the inch (in), the foot (ft), the yard (yd), the ounce (oz) and the pound (lb).
:::

:::definition Foot, yard and pound
A **foot** is $12$ inches. A **yard** is $3$ feet. A **pound** is a unit of weight equal to $16$ ounces.
:::

Use the same rule: big unit to small unit, multiply. Small unit to big unit, divide.

:::example A board in inches
A board is $8\ \text{ft}$ long. How many inches is that?
@step $1\ \text{ft} = 12\ \text{in}$. Inches are smaller, so multiply.
@step $8 \times 12 = 96$
@answer The board is $96\ \text{in}$ long.
:::

:::example Cheese at a deli
A block of cheese weighs $40\ \text{oz}$. How many pounds is that?
@step $1\ \text{lb} = 16\ \text{oz}$. Pounds are bigger, so divide.
@step $40 \div 16 = 2.5$
@answer The cheese weighs $2.5\ \text{lb}$.
:::

:::mistake Using 10 or 100 like in SI
Imperial units do not go up by $10$ or $100$. A board $8\ \text{ft}$ long is $96\ \text{in}$, not $800\ \text{in}$. Use $12$, $3$ or $16$.
:::

## Mixed units

Imperial measurements often use two units, such as $5\ \text{ft}\ 8\ \text{in}$ or $7\ \text{lb}\ 6\ \text{oz}$. To write the whole amount in the small unit, change the big unit first, then add.

:::example Height in inches
Maya is $5\ \text{ft}\ 8\ \text{in}$ tall. A form at a clinic asks for her height in inches.
@step Change the feet: $5 \times 12 = 60\ \text{in}$
@step Add the extra inches: $60 + 8 = 68$
@answer Maya is $68\ \text{in}$ tall.
:::

To go the other way, divide by $12$. The whole number is the feet. The remainder is the inches.

:::example A window in feet and inches
A window is $75\ \text{in}$ wide. Write this in feet and inches.
@step $12$ goes into $75$ six times: $6 \times 12 = 72$.
@step Inches left over: $75 - 72 = 3$
@answer The window is $6\ \text{ft}\ 3\ \text{in}$ wide.
:::

:::mistake 5 ft 8 in is not 5.8 ft
A foot has $12$ inches, not $10$. So $8\ \text{in}$ is $\tfrac{8}{12}$ of a foot, not $0.8$ of a foot. Change everything to inches instead.
:::

:::tip Pounds and ounces work the same way
$1\ \text{lb}\ 4\ \text{oz} = 16 + 4 = 20\ \text{oz}$. Just use $16$ instead of $12$.
:::

:::summary
- SI units go up and down by $10$, $100$ or $1000$: $1\ \text{km} = 1000\ \text{m}$, $1\ \text{m} = 100\ \text{cm}$, $1\ \text{kg} = 1000\ \text{g}$, $1\ \text{L} = 1000\ \text{mL}$.
- Imperial facts: $1\ \text{ft} = 12\ \text{in}$, $1\ \text{yd} = 3\ \text{ft}$, $1\ \text{lb} = 16\ \text{oz}$.
- Big unit to small unit: multiply. Small unit to big unit: divide.
- For mixed units like $5\ \text{ft}\ 8\ \text{in}$, change the big unit first, then add the rest.
- Check that your answer makes sense.
:::
