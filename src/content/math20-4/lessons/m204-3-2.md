Not every job pays by the hour. Some jobs pay a set amount for the year. Others pay you for what you sell or what you make, and some jobs come with tips.

## Salary

A salary is the same no matter how many hours you work in a week. It is split into equal pays through the year.

:::definition Salary
A **salary** is a set amount of pay for a whole year. Each pay is the salary divided by the number of pays in a year.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["How often you are paid","Pays in a year"],"rows":[["Weekly","52"],["Every two weeks","26"],["Twice a month","24"],["Monthly","12"]]}
:::

:::example Pay every two weeks
Priya earns a salary of \$39,000 a year. She is paid every two weeks. How much is each pay?
@step Every two weeks means 26 pays a year.
@step $39{,}000 \div 26 = 1{,}500.00$
@answer Each pay is \$1,500.00.
:::

:::mistake Every two weeks is not twice a month
"Every two weeks" gives 26 pays. "Twice a month" gives only 24 pays. A salary of \$42,000 paid twice a month is $42{,}000 \div 24 = 1{,}750.00$ dollars each pay.
:::

## Commission

People who sell things are often paid a **commission**. The more they sell, the more they earn.

:::definition Commission
A **commission** is pay that is a percent of your sales.
$$\text{commission} = \text{commission rate} \times \text{sales}$$
:::

:::example Selling furniture
Tariq earns 5% commission. Last week he sold \$8,400 worth of furniture.
@step $5\% = 0.05$
@step $0.05 \times 8{,}400 = 420.00$
@answer Tariq earned \$420.00 in commission.
:::

Some jobs pay a **base salary plus commission**, so you earn something even in a slow week.

:::example Base salary plus commission
Mei earns \$400 a week plus 3% of her sales. This week she sold \$6,500 worth of shoes.
@step Commission: $0.03 \times 6{,}500 = 195.00$
@step Total pay: $400.00 + 195.00 = 595.00$
@answer Mei earned \$595.00 this week.
:::

## Piecework

Some jobs pay for the work you finish, not for your time. Work faster, and you earn more.

:::definition Piecework
With **piecework**, you are paid for each piece of work you finish, such as each tree planted or each box packed.
$$\text{pay} = \text{pay per piece} \times \text{number of pieces}$$
:::

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Day","Boxes packed"],"rows":[["Monday","120"],["Tuesday","150"],["Wednesday","135"]]}
:::

:::example Packing boxes
Owen packs boxes at a warehouse for \$0.60 per box. The table shows his work this week.
@step Total boxes: $120 + 150 + 135 = 405$
@step Pay: $0.60 \times 405 = 243.00$
@answer Owen earned \$243.00.
:::

:::tip Pay per hour
To compare piecework with an hourly job, divide the pay by the hours worked. A tree planter who plants 1500 trees at \$0.16 each earns $1500 \times 0.16 = 240.00$ dollars. In an 8-hour day, that is $240.00 \div 8 = 30.00$ dollars per hour.
:::

## Tips

:::definition Tips
**Tips** are extra money customers give for good service. Many workplaces share the tips among the staff.
:::

When tips are shared **equally**, divide the total by the number of people. Four servers share \$384 in tips: $384 \div 4 = 96.00$, so each server gets \$96.00.

Some places use a **tip pool** shared by hours worked. People who work more hours get a bigger share.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Name","Hours worked"],"rows":[["Ava","8"],["Ben","6"],["Carlos","6"],["Dina","4"]]}
:::

:::example A tip pool
The staff in the table share \$360 in tips by hours worked. How much does Ben get?
@step Total hours: $8 + 6 + 6 + 4 = 24$
@step Tips per hour: $360 \div 24 = 15.00$
@step Ben’s share: $6 \times 15.00 = 90.00$
@answer Ben gets \$90.00.
:::

:::mistake Sharing a pool equally
Dividing \$360 by 4 people gives \$90.00 each. That happens to match Ben, but it is not fair to Ava, who worked 8 hours and should get $8 \times 15.00 = 120.00$ dollars.
:::

:::summary
- Salary: each pay $=$ yearly salary $\div$ number of pays (52, 26, 24 or 12).
- Commission $=$ rate $\times$ sales. With a base salary, add the base and the commission.
- Piecework pay $=$ pay per piece $\times$ number of pieces.
- Tips shared equally: total $\div$ number of people. A tip pool by hours: find the tips per hour, then multiply by your hours.
:::
