Your first pay cheque may be smaller than you expected. Some money is taken off before you get paid. Your **pay stub** shows where that money goes.

## Reading a pay stub

Each time you are paid, you get a pay stub. It lists your gross pay, the money taken off, and what is left for you.

:::definition Pay stub, deduction and net pay
A **pay stub** is a record of one pay. A **deduction** is money taken off your gross pay. **Net pay** is the money you take home.
$$\text{net pay} = \text{gross pay} - \text{total deductions}$$
:::

Most deductions pay for things that help you now or later.

:::definition CPP and EI
**CPP** (Canada Pension Plan) saves money for your pension when you retire. **EI** (Employment Insurance) can pay you for a while if you lose your job.
:::

- **Income tax** pays for things like schools, hospitals and roads.
- **Union dues** pay for a union, if your workplace has one.
- A **health plan** can pay for things like dental care or glasses.

:::example Net pay from a pay stub
Find the total deductions and the net pay on the pay stub above.
@step Total deductions: $42.00 + 14.00 + 84.00 = 140.00$
@step Net pay: $840.00 - 140.00 = 700.00$
@answer The deductions total \$140.00, and the net pay is \$700.00.
:::

:::mistake Subtracting only one deduction
Every deduction comes off your pay. Add them **all** first, then subtract the total from the gross pay.
:::

## Deductions from percents

Many deductions are a **percent** of your gross pay. Change the percent to a decimal and multiply. Round each deduction to the nearest cent.

The real rates for CPP, EI and income tax change from year to year. In this course, always use the rates given in the question.

:::example Three percent deductions
Noor’s gross pay is \$1,234.60. Suppose 5% is taken for CPP, 2% for EI and 10% for income tax.
@step CPP: $0.05 \times 1{,}234.60 = 61.73$
@step EI: $0.02 \times 1{,}234.60 = 24.692$, which rounds to \$24.69.
@step Income tax: $0.1 \times 1{,}234.60 = 123.46$
@step Total deductions: $61.73 + 24.69 + 123.46 = 209.88$
@step Net pay: $1{,}234.60 - 209.88 = 1{,}024.72$
@answer Noor’s net pay is \$1,024.72.
:::

:::tip A quick check
The percents add to $5 + 2 + 10 = 17\%$. And $0.17 \times 1{,}234.60 \approx 209.88$, so the total looks right. Because each deduction is rounded, the two ways can be a cent or two apart.
:::

:::

## From hours to net pay

To find net pay from a timecard, find the gross pay first. Then find the deductions.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Pay stub","Amount"],"rows":[["Hours worked","36"],["Hourly wage","$17.50"],["Gross pay","?"],["CPP (5% of gross pay)","?"],["EI (2% of gross pay)","?"],["Income tax (10% of gross pay)","?"],["Union dues","$15.00"],["Net pay","?"]]}
:::

:::example The whole pay stub
Use the pay stub above to find the gross pay and the net pay.
@step Gross pay: $36 \times 17.50 = 630.00$
@step CPP: $0.05 \times 630.00 = 31.50$. EI: $0.02 \times 630.00 = 12.60$. Income tax: $0.1 \times 630.00 = 63.00$.
@step Total deductions: $31.50 + 12.60 + 63.00 + 15.00 = 122.10$
@step Net pay: $630.00 - 122.10 = 507.90$
@answer The gross pay is \$630.00, and the net pay is \$507.90.
:::

:::mistake Taking deductions off the hourly wage
Deductions are a percent of your **gross pay** for the whole pay period, not of your hourly wage. Find the gross pay first.
:::

:::check m204-net-pay 3
:::

:::summary
- A pay stub shows gross pay, each deduction and net pay.
- Net pay $=$ gross pay $-$ total deductions.
- Common deductions are CPP, EI, income tax, union dues and health plans.
- For a percent deduction, multiply the gross pay by the percent as a decimal, and round to the nearest cent.
- Find the gross pay first, then the deductions, then the net pay.
:::
