Truck drivers, delivery workers, hikers and farm workers all read maps. A map tells you how far it is to a place and which way to go. A phone is handy, but a paper map still works when there is no signal.

## Map scale

:::definition Map scale
The **map scale** tells how a distance on the map compares with the real distance. A scale of "$1\ \text{cm}$ represents $5\ \text{km}$" means each centimetre on the map stands for $5$ kilometres on the ground.
:::

:::example To the campground
On a park map, $1\ \text{cm}$ represents $5\ \text{km}$. The campground is $7\ \text{cm}$ from the park gate on the map. How far is it really?
@step Each centimetre is $5\ \text{km}$, so multiply.
@step $7 \times 5 = 35$
@answer The campground is $35\ \text{km}$ from the gate.
:::

Many maps use a ratio scale, like $1 : 250\,000$. That means $1\ \text{cm}$ on the map is $250\,000\ \text{cm}$ on the ground. Change it to kilometres: $1\ \text{km} = 100\,000\ \text{cm}$, so $250\,000 \div 100\,000 = 2.5\ \text{km}$.

:::example Reading a ratio scale
A county map has a scale of $1 : 250\,000$. Two towns are $6\ \text{cm}$ apart on the map. How far apart are they?
@step $1\ \text{cm}$ represents $250\,000 \div 100\,000 = 2.5\ \text{km}$.
@step $6 \times 2.5 = 15$
@answer The towns are $15\ \text{km}$ apart.
:::

## Compass directions

:::definition Compass directions
The four main **compass** directions are **north** (N), south (S), east (E) and west (W). On most maps, north is at the top. Between them are northeast (NE), northwest (NW), southeast (SE) and southwest (SW).
:::

:::example Directions to the arena
Use the grid map. Which direction is the Arena from the School? How do you get there?
@step Start at the School in B2. The Arena is in E4.
@step To get there, you go right (east) and up (north). So the Arena is **northeast** of the School.
@step Across: B to E is $3$ squares east. Up: row 2 to row 4 is $2$ squares north.
@answer The Arena is northeast (NE) of the School. Go 3 squares east, then 2 squares north.
:::

:::mistake From where?
"The Arena from the School" means you **start at the School**. Going the other way, from the Arena to the School, is southwest.

:::summary
- Multiply a map distance by the scale to get the real distance.
- For a ratio scale like $1 : 250\,000$, change to kilometres: divide by $100\,000$.
- For a route, add the legs first, then use the scale.
- Grid squares are named letter first, then number, like B2.
- North is up, south is down, east is right and west is left.
:::
