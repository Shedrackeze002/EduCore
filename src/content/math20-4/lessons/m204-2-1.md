Stores sell the same food in many sizes. The biggest box is not always the best deal. The **unit price** tells you which size really costs less.

## What is a unit price?

When you buy a pack of something, you pay for many units at once. The unit price is the cost of just **one** unit.

:::definition Unit price
The **unit price** is the cost of one unit, such as one item, one kilogram or one litre.
$$\text{unit price} = \text{price} \div \text{number of units}$$
:::

Many stores print the unit price in small type on the shelf tag.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Item","Price","Unit price"],"rows":[["Granola bars, box of 8","$4.00","$0.50 per bar"]]}
:::

:::example Price for one item
A box of 8 granola bars costs \$4.00. What is the price for one bar?
@step Unit price $=$ price $\div$ number of units
@step $4.00 \div 8 = 0.50$
@answer The unit price is \$0.50 per bar.
:::

:::mistake Dividing the wrong way
$8 \div 4.00 = 2$ tells you how many bars you get for one dollar. It is not the price. For a unit price, the **money goes first**: price $\div$ amount.
:::

:::

## Different sizes and units

Small things, like cereal or shampoo, are often priced **per 100 g** or **per 100 mL**. First count the groups of 100. Then divide the price by that number.

:::example Price per 100 g
A $750\ \text{g}$ box of cereal costs \$5.49. Find the unit price per $100\ \text{g}$.
@step Groups of $100\ \text{g}$: $750 \div 100 = 7.5$
@step $5.49 \div 7.5 = 0.732$
@answer The unit price is about \$0.73 per $100\ \text{g}$.
:::

:::tip Rounding to the nearest cent
Look at the third digit after the decimal point. In $0.732$ it is $2$, so round down to $0.73$. If it is $5$ or more, round up.
:::

:::check m204-unit-price 2
:::

Sometimes the size and the unit price use different units. Change the size first.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Big unit","Small unit"],"rows":[["1 kg","1000 g"],["1 L","1000 mL"]]}
:::

:::example Grams to kilograms
A $500\ \text{g}$ pack of ground beef costs \$6.49. What is the price per kilogram?
@step Change to kilograms: $500\ \text{g} \div 1000 = 0.5\ \text{kg}$
@step $6.49 \div 0.5 = 12.98$
@answer The beef costs \$12.98 per kilogram.
:::

:::example Litres to groups of 100 mL
A $2\ \text{L}$ jug of apple juice costs \$3.79. Find the unit price per $100\ \text{mL}$.
@step Change to millilitres: $2\ \text{L} = 2000\ \text{mL}$. That is $2000 \div 100 = 20$ groups of $100\ \text{mL}$.
@step $3.79 \div 20 = 0.1895$
@answer The unit price is about \$0.19 per $100\ \text{mL}$.
:::

:::check m204-unit-price 3
:::

## Finding the best buy

To compare sizes fairly, find the unit price of each size in the **same** unit. The lowest unit price wins.

:::definition Best buy
The **best buy** is the choice with the lowest unit price, as long as you need that much and will use it.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Size","Price"],"rows":[["1 kg","$2.99"],["4 kg","$9.99"],["8 kg","$21.99"]]}
:::

:::example Three bags of rice
A store sells rice in the three sizes shown in the table. Which is the best buy?
@step $1\ \text{kg}$: \$2.99 per kilogram
@step $4\ \text{kg}$: $9.99 \div 4 = 2.4975$, about \$2.50 per kilogram
@step $8\ \text{kg}$: $21.99 \div 8 = 2.74875$, about \$2.75 per kilogram
@answer The $4\ \text{kg}$ bag is the best buy. The biggest bag is **not** the cheapest per kilogram.
:::

:::tip A quick check
If one size is exactly double another, compare the big price with **two** small ones. A $2\ \text{L}$ jug costs \$5.50 and a $4\ \text{L}$ jug costs \$9.00. Two small jugs cost $2 \times 5.50 = 11.00$ dollars, so the $4\ \text{L}$ jug is the better buy.
:::

:::mistake Bigger is always cheaper
Stores know shoppers think this. A middle size or a sale item can be cheaper per unit. Always check.
:::

## Deals, coupons and common sense

A deal like "3 for \$12" or a coupon changes what you really pay. Work out what you pay and what you get. Then find the unit price.

:::example Is the deal better?
Juice boxes come in packs of 8. The 8-pack is on sale: 3 for \$12. A 24-pack costs \$10.99. Which is the better buy?
@step Deal: \$12 for $3 \times 8 = 24$ juice boxes. $12 \div 24 = 0.50$, so \$0.50 per box.
@step 24-pack: $10.99 \div 24 \approx 0.458$, about \$0.46 per box.
@answer The 24-pack is the better buy. A sale sign does not always mean the lowest price.
:::

:::example Using a coupon
A $300\ \text{g}$ bag of coffee costs \$6.49. A $900\ \text{g}$ bag costs \$16.99. Kai has a coupon for \$2.00 off the small bag.
@step With the coupon, the small bag costs $6.49 - 2.00 = 4.49$ dollars. That is $4.49 \div 3 \approx 1.50$, about \$1.50 per $100\ \text{g}$.
@step Big bag: $16.99 \div 9 \approx 1.89$, about \$1.89 per $100\ \text{g}$.
@answer With the coupon, the small bag is the better buy.
:::

:::note The lowest unit price is not always the best choice
- **Need:** will you use it all? A huge bag is not a deal if half of it goes in the garbage.
- **Waste:** fresh food can spoil before you finish it.
- **Quality and space:** a cheap brand may not work as well, and you need room to store a big pack.
:::

:::check m204-best-buy 3
:::

:::summary
- Unit price $=$ price $\div$ number of units. Put the money first.
- For small items, divide by the number of groups of $100\ \text{g}$ or $100\ \text{mL}$.
- If the units do not match, change them first: $1\ \text{kg} = 1000\ \text{g}$ and $1\ \text{L} = 1000\ \text{mL}$.
- The best buy has the lowest unit price. The biggest size is not always cheapest.
- For deals and coupons, find what you really pay and what you really get. Also think about need, waste and quality.
:::
