Railway tracks, the rungs of a ladder and the lines in a parking lot are parallel. When a road, a brace or a stair stringer crosses them, the angles it makes follow rules that you can prove and then use to build things accurately.

## Transversals and angle pairs

:::definition Parallel lines and transversals
- **Parallel lines** are lines in the same plane that never meet. Matching arrowheads on lines show that they are parallel, and $m \parallel n$ means "line $m$ is parallel to line $n$".
- A **transversal** is a line that crosses two or more other lines.
:::

A transversal crossing two lines makes eight angles. Angles between the two lines are **interior** angles; angles outside them are **exterior** angles.

:::definition Angle pairs
- **Corresponding angles** are in matching positions at the two intersections, such as $\angle 2$ and $\angle 6$.
- **Alternate interior angles** are between the lines, on opposite sides of the transversal, such as $\angle 3$ and $\angle 6$.
- **Alternate exterior angles** are outside the lines, on opposite sides of the transversal, such as $\angle 1$ and $\angle 8$.
- **Same-side interior angles** are between the lines, on the same side of the transversal, such as $\angle 4$ and $\angle 6$.
- **Vertically opposite angles** are across from each other at one intersection, such as $\angle 1$ and $\angle 4$.
:::

## Angle properties of parallel lines

When a transversal crosses **parallel** lines:

- corresponding angles are equal;
- alternate interior angles are equal, and so are alternate exterior angles;
- same-side interior angles are supplementary (they add to $180^\circ$).

Vertically opposite angles are equal for any two crossing lines, and angles that form a straight line add to $180^\circ$.

:::example Angles with reasons
In the diagram above, $m \parallel n$ and $\angle 2 = 60^\circ$. Find $\angle 6$, $\angle 7$ and $\angle 5$, giving a reason for each.
@step $\angle 6 = 60^\circ$: corresponding angles are equal.
@step $\angle 7 = 60^\circ$: alternate exterior angles are equal.
@step $\angle 3 = 60^\circ$ (vertically opposite $\angle 2$), and $\angle 3$ and $\angle 5$ are same-side interior angles, so $\angle 5 = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ$.
@answer $\angle 6 = 60^\circ$, $\angle 7 = 60^\circ$ and $\angle 5 = 120^\circ$.
:::

:::mistake Same-side interior angles are not equal
Corresponding and alternate angles are equal, but same-side interior angles **add to** $180^\circ$. If one of them is acute, the other must be obtuse.
:::

## Using algebra and extra lines

:::example Angles with algebra
Two parallel fence rails are crossed by a diagonal brace. A pair of alternate interior angles measure $(3x + 10)^\circ$ and $(5x - 30)^\circ$. Find $x$ and the angle.
@step Alternate interior angles are equal: $3x + 10 = 5x - 30$.
@step Subtract $3x$ and add $30$: $40 = 2x$, so $x = 20$.
@step $3(20) + 10 = 70$ and $5(20) - 30 = 70$.
@answer $x = 20$ and each angle measures $70^\circ$.
:::

## Proving that lines are parallel

Each property has a **converse**. If a transversal crosses two lines and any one of these is true, the lines are parallel:

- a pair of corresponding angles are equal;
- a pair of alternate interior (or alternate exterior) angles are equal;
- a pair of same-side interior angles are supplementary.

:::example Are the lines parallel?
In the diagram, the angles marked $72^\circ$ and $66^\circ$ are corresponding angles. Is $m \parallel n$?
@step For the lines to be parallel, corresponding angles must be equal.
@step $72^\circ \ne 66^\circ$.
@answer No, $m$ and $n$ are not parallel, even though they might look close.
:::

You can also write the reasoning as a **two-column proof**, with each statement beside its reason. In the first diagram, suppose you only know that $\angle 2 = \angle 7$.

| Statement | Reason |
|---|---|
| $\angle 2 = \angle 7$ | Given |
| $\angle 7 = \angle 6$ | Vertically opposite angles are equal |
| $\angle 2 = \angle 6$ | Transitive property |
| $m \parallel n$ | Corresponding angles are equal |

:::summary
- A transversal crossing two lines forms corresponding, alternate interior, alternate exterior, same-side interior and vertically opposite angles.
- With parallel lines, corresponding and alternate angles are equal, and same-side interior angles add to $180^\circ$.
- An extra parallel line through a point can create the angle pairs you need.
- The converses prove lines are parallel: equal corresponding or alternate angles, or supplementary same-side interior angles.
- Give a reason for every statement, as in a two-column proof.
:::
