Every triangle, from a roof truss to the brace on a gate, has angles that add to $180^\circ$. With the parallel-line properties from Lesson 2.1, you can prove why, and then use the result to find unknown angles.

## The angle sum of a triangle

:::definition Interior angle
An **interior angle** of a triangle is an angle inside the triangle, formed by two of its sides.
:::

To prove the angle sum, draw line $m$ through vertex $A$, parallel to side $BC$ (which lies on line $n$).

| Statement | Reason |
|---|---|
| $\angle 1 = \angle 4$ | Alternate interior angles ($m \parallel n$) |
| $\angle 2 = \angle 5$ | Alternate interior angles ($m \parallel n$) |
| $\angle 1 + \angle 3 + \angle 2 = 180^\circ$ | Angles on a straight line |
| $\angle 4 + \angle 3 + \angle 5 = 180^\circ$ | Substitute equal angles |

So the three interior angles of $\triangle ABC$ ($\angle 4$, $\angle 3$ and $\angle 5$) add to $180^\circ$. The same argument works for any triangle.

:::example Finding the third angle
Two angles of a triangle measure $48^\circ$ and $77^\circ$. Find the third angle.
@step The angles add to $180^\circ$: $x + 48^\circ + 77^\circ = 180^\circ$.
@answer $x = 180^\circ - 48^\circ - 77^\circ = 55^\circ$
:::

:::example An isosceles triangle
In the diagram, $CA = CB$ and $\angle C = 40^\circ$. Find $x$.
@step $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ are opposite the equal sides, so both are $x$.
@step $x + x + 40^\circ = 180^\circ$, so $2x = 140^\circ$.
@answer $x = 70^\circ$

:::example Angles as expressions
The angles of a triangle measure $x^\circ$, $(2x + 15)^\circ$ and $(3x - 9)^\circ$. Find each angle.
@step $x + (2x + 15) + (3x - 9) = 180$
@step $6x + 6 = 180$, so $6x = 174$ and $x = 29$.
@step The angles are $29^\circ$, $2(29) + 15 = 73^\circ$ and $3(29) - 9 = 78^\circ$.
@answer $29^\circ$, $73^\circ$ and $78^\circ$ (check: they add to $180^\circ$).
:::

## The exterior angle property

:::definition Exterior angle
An **exterior angle** of a triangle is formed by one side and the extension of the side next to it. The two interior angles that are not next to it are its **non-adjacent** (or remote) interior angles.
:::

In the diagram, $\angle CBD$ is an exterior angle. It forms a straight angle with $\angle ABC$, so $\angle CBD = 180^\circ - \angle ABC$. The angle sum gives $\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ - \angle ABC$ too. So:

$$\text{exterior angle} = \text{sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles}$$

:::example Using the exterior angle property
In the diagram, find $x$.
@step The non-adjacent interior angles are $\angle A = 55^\circ$ and $\angle C = 50^\circ$.
@answer $x = 55^\circ + 50^\circ = 105^\circ$
:::

:::example An exterior angle with algebra
An exterior angle measures $(4x - 10)^\circ$, and its non-adjacent interior angles measure $(x + 20)^\circ$ and $(2x + 5)^\circ$. Find $x$ and the exterior angle.
@step $4x - 10 = (x + 20) + (2x + 5)$, so $4x - 10 = 3x + 25$.
@step $x = 35$
@answer The exterior angle is $4(35) - 10 = 130^\circ$ (check: $55^\circ + 75^\circ = 130^\circ$).
:::

:::mistake Adding the wrong angles
The exterior angle equals the two interior angles that are **not** next to it. The interior angle next to it is its supplement, not part of the sum.
:::

## Combining angle properties

Many problems need several properties in a row. Name the property you use at each step.

:::example A line parallel to one side
In $\triangle ABC$, $D$ is on $AB$ and $E$ is on $AC$ with $DE \parallel BC$. $\angle A = 50^\circ$ and $\angle ADE = 70^\circ$. Find $\angle ABC$ and $\angle ACB$.
@step $\angle ABC = \angle ADE = 70^\circ$: corresponding angles ($DE \parallel BC$).
@step In $\triangle ADE$: $\angle AED = 180^\circ - 50^\circ - 70^\circ = 60^\circ$.
@step $\angle ACB = \angle AED = 60^\circ$: corresponding angles.
@answer $\angle ABC = 70^\circ$ and $\angle ACB = 60^\circ$
:::

:::tip The exterior angles add to 360°
If you draw one exterior angle at each vertex of a triangle, each pairs with an interior angle to make $180^\circ$. That is $3 \times 180^\circ = 540^\circ$ in all, and the interior angles use $180^\circ$, so the exterior angles add to $360^\circ$.
:::

:::summary
- The interior angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$; parallel lines prove it.
- In an isosceles triangle, the angles opposite the equal sides are equal.
- An exterior angle equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles.
- With expressions, write an equation from an angle property and solve it.
- In multi-step problems, give the property that justifies each step.
:::
