Which is easier to estimate: $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ or $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$? They are the same number, but the second one is simply half of $1.414$, about $0.707$. In this lesson you will divide radicals and learn to move a radical out of the denominator.

## Dividing radicals

For $a \ge 0$ and $b > 0$,

$$\frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}} = \sqrt{\frac{a}{b}}.$$

With numbers in front, divide the numbers in front and divide the radicands, then simplify.

:::example Dividing radicals
Simplify (a) $\dfrac{\sqrt{72}}{\sqrt{2}}$ (b) $\dfrac{12\sqrt{10}}{3\sqrt{2}}$.
@step (a) $\sqrt{\dfrac{72}{2}} = \sqrt{36} = 6$
@step (b) $\dfrac{12}{3}\sqrt{\dfrac{10}{2}} = 4\sqrt{5}$
@answer (a) $6$ (b) $4\sqrt{5}$
:::

:::example A design problem
A rectangular rooftop solar array has an area of $12\sqrt{10}$ m² and a width of $3\sqrt{2}$ m. How long is it?
@step Length $= \dfrac{\text{area}}{\text{width}} = \dfrac{12\sqrt{10}}{3\sqrt{2}}$
@step $= \dfrac{12}{3}\sqrt{\dfrac{10}{2}} = 4\sqrt{5}$
@answer The array is $4\sqrt{5}$ m long, about $8.9$ m.
:::

## Why rationalize?

:::definition Rationalize the denominator
To **rationalize the denominator** is to rewrite a fraction so that there is no radical in the denominator, without changing its value.
:::

There are good reasons for this convention:

- It is easier to estimate: $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ is half of $1.414$.
- It is easier to compare and combine answers when they are all written the same way.
- Before calculators, dividing by a long decimal such as $1.414\ldots$ by hand was hard work.

## Rationalizing a monomial denominator

Multiply the numerator and the denominator by the radical in the denominator. Since $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} = 1$, the value does not change, and $\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3} = 3$ clears the radical.

:::example Rationalizing
Rationalize (a) $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{3}}$ (b) $\dfrac{10}{\sqrt{6}}$.
@step (a) $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{3}} \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{3}$
@step (b) $\dfrac{10}{\sqrt{6}} \times \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{6}} = \dfrac{10\sqrt{6}}{6}$
@step Reduce (b) by dividing the numerator and denominator by $2$: $\dfrac{5\sqrt{6}}{3}$
@answer (a) $\dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{3}$ (b) $\dfrac{5\sqrt{6}}{3}$
:::

:::mistake Changing the value
Multiplying **only** the denominator by $\sqrt{3}$ changes the number: $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{5}{3}$, which is not equal to $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{3}}$. Always multiply the top and bottom by the same radical.
:::

:::tip Reducing
Only reduce numbers that are **outside** the radical with each other: $\dfrac{10\sqrt{6}}{6} = \dfrac{5\sqrt{6}}{3}$, but you cannot cancel the $6$ under the radical with the $6$ in the denominator.
:::

## Two terms in the numerator

When the numerator has two terms, multiply **each** term by the radical, then divide each term by the new denominator.

:::example A binomial numerator
Simplify $\dfrac{6 + \sqrt{10}}{\sqrt{2}}$ so that the denominator has no radical.
@step $\dfrac{6 + \sqrt{10}}{\sqrt{2}} \times \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{6\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{20}}{2}$
@step $\sqrt{20} = 2\sqrt{5}$, so the numerator is $6\sqrt{2} + 2\sqrt{5}$.
@step Divide each term by $2$: $\dfrac{6\sqrt{2}}{2} + \dfrac{2\sqrt{5}}{2}$
@answer $3\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{5}$
:::

:::check m202-radical-divide 3
:::

:::summary
- $\dfrac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{a}{b}}$; divide the numbers in front and the radicands separately.
- Rationalize a monomial denominator by multiplying the numerator **and** the denominator by the radical.
- Reduce only the numbers outside the radicals.
- With two terms on top, multiply each term, then divide each term by the denominator.
:::
