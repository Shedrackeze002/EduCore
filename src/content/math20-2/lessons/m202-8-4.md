If you double every length of a box, does it hold twice as much? It holds **eight** times as much. Lengths, areas and volumes grow at different rates, and knowing how lets designers, farmers and manufacturers predict paint, material and capacity for objects of any size.

## Similar objects

:::definition Similar objects
Two 2-D shapes or 3-D objects are **similar** if every length on one is the same multiple $k$ of the corresponding length on the other. The number $k$ is the **scale factor**.
:::

Heights, widths, radii, diagonals and edge lengths are all lengths, so every one of them is multiplied by $k$. Areas and volumes are not lengths, so they change differently.

## Area and surface area: multiply by k²

A $2\ \text{cm}$ by $3\ \text{cm}$ rectangle has an area of $6\ \text{cm}^2$. Enlarge it with $k = 3$: it becomes $6\ \text{cm}$ by $9\ \text{cm}$, with an area of $54\ \text{cm}^2$. That is $9 = 3^2$ times the original area, because **both** dimensions were multiplied by $3$.

:::definition Areas of similar shapes
If two shapes or objects are similar with scale factor $k$, then
$$\text{new area} = k^2 \times \text{original area}$$
The same rule holds for the **surface areas** of similar 3-D objects.
:::

:::example Surface area of a larger tank
A cylindrical water tank is $1.2\ \text{m}$ tall and has a surface area of $9.5\ \text{m}^2$. A similar tank is $1.8\ \text{m}$ tall. What is its surface area, to the nearest tenth of a square metre?
@step $k = \dfrac{1.8}{1.2} = 1.5$
@step $k^2 = 1.5^2 = 2.25$
@step Surface area: $9.5 \times 2.25 = 21.375$
@answer The larger tank has a surface area of about $21.4\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Multiplying an area by k
The larger tank is $1.5$ times as tall, but its surface area is **not** $1.5$ times as big. Area grows in two directions, so multiply by $k^2$.
:::

## Volume: multiply by k³

A cube with $2\ \text{cm}$ edges has a volume of $8\ \text{cm}^3$. With $k = 3$, the edges become $6\ \text{cm}$ and the volume becomes $216\ \text{cm}^3$, which is $27 = 3^3$ times as much. Length, width and height were **each** multiplied by $3$.

:::definition Volumes of similar objects
If two objects are similar with scale factor $k$, then
$$\text{new volume} = k^3 \times \text{original volume}$$
Capacity is a volume, and objects made of the same material have masses in the same ratio as their volumes.
:::

:::example A bigger can
A juice can holds $355\ \text{mL}$. A similar can has every length $1.2$ times as long. How much does it hold, to the nearest tenth of a millilitre?
@step $k^3 = 1.2^3 = 1.728$
@step $355 \times 1.728 = 613.44$
@answer The larger can holds about $613.4\ \text{mL}$. A $20\%$ increase in every length gives about $73\%$ more juice.
:::

## Working back to the scale factor

If you know two areas or two volumes, you can work back to $k$. Divide to get the ratio, then undo the power.

- Area ratio $= k^2$, so $k = \sqrt{\text{area ratio}}$.
- Volume ratio $= k^3$, so $k = \sqrt[3]{\text{volume ratio}}$.

:::example From areas to a length
Two similar photos have areas of $150\ \text{cm}^2$ and $384\ \text{cm}^2$. The smaller photo is $10\ \text{cm}$ wide. How wide is the larger one?
@step $k^2 = \dfrac{384}{150} = 2.56$
@step $k = \sqrt{2.56} = 1.6$
@answer The larger photo is $10 \times 1.6 = 16\ \text{cm}$ wide.
:::

:::example From volumes to a surface area
Two similar water bottles hold $250\ \text{cm}^3$ and $2000\ \text{cm}^3$. The small bottle has a surface area of $240\ \text{cm}^2$. What is the surface area of the large bottle?
@step $k^3 = \dfrac{2000}{250} = 8$, so $k = \sqrt[3]{8} = 2$.
@step Surface areas scale by $k^2 = 4$.
@answer The large bottle has a surface area of $240 \times 4 = 960\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::summary
- Similar objects: every length is multiplied by the same scale factor $k$.
- Areas and surface areas are multiplied by $k^2$; volumes, capacities and masses (same material) by $k^3$.
- To find $k$ from areas, take the square root of the area ratio. From volumes, take the cube root of the volume ratio.
- Always find $k$ from **lengths** first, then square or cube it as the question needs.
:::
