Stop signs, honeycomb cells, floor tiles and gazebo floors are all polygons. Designers and builders need their angles to cut and join the pieces. One idea, splitting a polygon into triangles, gives every angle you need.

## Polygons and the interior angle sum

:::definition Polygon, convex and regular
- A **polygon** is a closed figure made of straight line segments that meet only at their endpoints.
- A polygon is **convex** if every interior angle is less than $180^\circ$.
- A **regular polygon** has all sides equal and all angles equal.
:::

Each triangle has angles adding to $180^\circ$, so the pentagon's angles add to $3 \times 180^\circ = 540^\circ$. In general, a polygon with $n$ sides splits into $n - 2$ triangles.

| Sides $n$ | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Triangles | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
| Angle sum | $180^\circ$ | $360^\circ$ | $540^\circ$ | $720^\circ$ | $900^\circ$ | $1080^\circ$ |

$$S = 180^\circ(n - 2)$$

:::example Sum of the interior angles
Find the sum of the interior angles of a convex 12-sided polygon (a dodecagon).
@step $n = 12$, so it splits into $12 - 2 = 10$ triangles.
@answer $S = 180^\circ(10) = 1800^\circ$
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the 2
The sum is not $180^\circ \times n$. From one vertex you can draw diagonals only to the vertices that are not its neighbours, which makes $n - 2$ triangles. Check with a triangle: $n = 3$ must give $180^\circ$.
:::

## Regular polygons

In a regular polygon all $n$ interior angles are equal, so each one is the sum divided by $n$:

$$\text{each interior angle} = \frac{180^\circ(n - 2)}{n}$$

An **exterior angle** of a polygon is formed by one side and the extension of the next side. Walking around any convex polygon, you turn through all the exterior angles and finish facing the way you started, so the exterior angles (one at each vertex) add to $360^\circ$. For a regular polygon, each exterior angle is $\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$.

:::example A regular octagon
A stop sign is shaped like a regular octagon. Find each interior angle and each exterior angle.
@step Sum: $180^\circ(8 - 2) = 1080^\circ$, so each interior angle is $1080^\circ \div 8 = 135^\circ$.
@step Each exterior angle: $360^\circ \div 8 = 45^\circ$ (check: $135^\circ + 45^\circ = 180^\circ$).
@answer Interior $135^\circ$, exterior $45^\circ$
:::

:::example Finding the number of sides
Each interior angle of a regular polygon measures $156^\circ$. How many sides does it have?
@step Each exterior angle is $180^\circ - 156^\circ = 24^\circ$.
@step The exterior angles add to $360^\circ$: $n = 360^\circ \div 24^\circ = 15$.
@answer The polygon has $15$ sides (check: $\dfrac{180^\circ(13)}{15} = 156^\circ$).
:::

:::example A missing angle
Find $x$ in the convex pentagon above.
@step A pentagon's angles add to $180^\circ(5 - 2) = 540^\circ$.
@step The known angles add to $100^\circ + 120^\circ + 95^\circ + 110^\circ = 425^\circ$.
@answer $x = 540^\circ - 425^\circ = 115^\circ$
:::

:::example Angles as expressions
The angles of a convex pentagon measure $2x^\circ$, $2x^\circ$, $(3x - 10)^\circ$, $(3x + 20)^\circ$ and $110^\circ$. Find $x$ and the largest angle.
@step $2x + 2x + (3x - 10) + (3x + 20) + 110 = 540$
@step $10x + 120 = 540$, so $x = 42$.
@step The angles are $84^\circ$, $84^\circ$, $116^\circ$, $146^\circ$ and $110^\circ$.
@answer $x = 42$ and the largest angle is $146^\circ$.
:::

:::tip Will it tile?
Regular polygons cover a floor with no gaps only if their interior angles fit exactly around a point ($360^\circ$). Three regular hexagons fit: $3 \times 120^\circ = 360^\circ$. Regular pentagons do not: $360^\circ \div 108^\circ$ is not a whole number.
:::

:::summary
- An $n$-sided convex polygon splits into $n - 2$ triangles, so its interior angles add to $180^\circ(n - 2)$.
- Each interior angle of a regular polygon is $\dfrac{180^\circ(n - 2)}{n}$.
- The exterior angles of a convex polygon, one at each vertex, add to $360^\circ$; each one in a regular polygon is $\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$.
- An interior angle and its exterior angle add to $180^\circ$, which helps you find $n$.
- For a missing angle, subtract the known angles from the angle sum.
:::
