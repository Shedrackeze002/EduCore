Blueprints, site plans, road maps and model kits are all drawn to scale. A **scale diagram** is similar to the real object: every length is multiplied by the same number. Knowing that number lets a builder read real lengths off a plan, and lets you fit a whole town onto one page.

## Scale factor

:::definition Scale diagram and scale factor
A **scale diagram** is a drawing that is similar to the original object. The **scale factor** $k$ compares corresponding lengths:
$$k = \dfrac{\text{length on the diagram}}{\text{corresponding length on the original}}$$
If $k > 1$, the diagram is an **enlargement**. If $0 < k < 1$, it is a **reduction**.
:::

:::example Finding a scale factor
A $4\ \text{cm}$ by $2.5\ \text{cm}$ logo is redrawn as a $6\ \text{cm}$ by $3.75\ \text{cm}$ scale diagram. What is the scale factor? Is it an enlargement or a reduction?
@step $k = \dfrac{6}{4} = 1.5$
@step Check with the other pair of sides: $\dfrac{3.75}{2.5} = 1.5$
@answer $k = 1.5$. Since $k > 1$, the diagram is an enlargement.
:::

## Same units first

A scale factor has no units, so both lengths must be in the **same unit** before you divide.

:::example A canoe
A $5.4\ \text{m}$ canoe is drawn $9\ \text{cm}$ long. What scale factor was used?
@step Same units: $5.4\ \text{m} = 540\ \text{cm}$.
@step $k = \dfrac{9}{540} = \dfrac{1}{60} \approx 0.0167$
@answer $k = \dfrac{1}{60}$, a reduction. Every length on the drawing is $\dfrac{1}{60}$ of the real length.
:::

:::mistake Mixing units
Dividing $9 \div 5.4$ gives $1.67$, which says the drawing is bigger than the canoe. Convert first, then divide.
:::

## Scales written as ratios

Plans and maps often give the scale as a ratio. A scale of $1 : 50$ means $1$ unit on the diagram stands for $50$ of the same unit in real life. It is the same as a scale factor of $k = \dfrac{1}{50}$.

:::example A floor plan
A floor plan has a scale of $1 : 50$. A wall is $8.6\ \text{cm}$ long on the plan. How long is the real wall, in metres?
@step Real length: $8.6 \times 50 = 430\ \text{cm}$
@step $430\ \text{cm} = 4.3\ \text{m}$
@answer The wall is $4.3\ \text{m}$ long.
:::

:::example A hiking map
A trail on a $1 : 50\,000$ topographic map measures $7.4\ \text{cm}$, as shown on the ruler. How long is the real trail?
@step $7.4 \times 50\,000 = 370\,000\ \text{cm}$
@step $1\ \text{km} = 100\,000\ \text{cm}$, so $370\,000\ \text{cm} = 3.7\ \text{km}$
@answer The trail is $3.7\ \text{km}$ long.

## Finding and using a scale

To write a scale as $1 : n$, put both lengths in the same unit, then divide the real length by the diagram length.

:::example A road map
Two towns are $12.6\ \text{km}$ apart. On a map, they are $8.4\ \text{cm}$ apart. Write the scale as $1 : n$, then find the real distance for $5.2\ \text{cm}$ on the same map.
@step $12.6\ \text{km} = 12.6 \times 100\,000 = 1\,260\,000\ \text{cm}$
@step $n = \dfrac{1\,260\,000}{8.4} = 150\,000$, so the scale is $1 : 150\,000$.
@step $5.2\ \text{cm}$ on the map: $5.2 \times 150\,000 = 780\,000\ \text{cm} = 7.8\ \text{km}$
@answer The scale is $1 : 150\,000$, and $5.2\ \text{cm}$ stands for $7.8\ \text{km}$.
:::

:::tip Will it fit?
To choose a scale for a drawing, divide each real length by the space you have on the page. A larger $n$ in $1 : n$ makes a smaller drawing.
:::

:::summary
- A scale diagram is similar to the original: $k = \dfrac{\text{diagram length}}{\text{original length}}$.
- $k > 1$ is an enlargement; $0 < k < 1$ is a reduction.
- Put both lengths in the same unit before dividing.
- A scale of $1 : n$ means real length $= n \times$ diagram length, in the same unit. Convert to metres or kilometres at the end.
- To find $n$, divide the real length by the diagram length (same units).
:::
