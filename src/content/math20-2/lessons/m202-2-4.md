Roof trusses, bridge supports and quilt patterns use many identical triangles. Builders need to know that two triangles match exactly, and they can be sure by checking just three pairs of parts.

## Congruent triangles

:::definition Congruent
Two figures are **congruent** if they have exactly the same size and shape. In congruent triangles, all three pairs of corresponding sides and all three pairs of corresponding angles are equal.
:::

The statement $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$ means "triangle $ABC$ is congruent to triangle $DEF$". The order of the letters shows which parts match: $A$ matches $D$, $B$ matches $E$ and $C$ matches $F$.

:::example Corresponding parts
$\triangle PQR \cong \triangle XYZ$. List the equal sides and the equal angles.
@step Match the letters in order: $P \leftrightarrow X$, $Q \leftrightarrow Y$, $R \leftrightarrow Z$.
@step Sides: $PQ = XY$, $QR = YZ$, $PR = XZ$.
@answer Angles: $\angle P = \angle X$, $\angle Q = \angle Y$, $\angle R = \angle Z$.
:::

## Three ways to prove triangles congruent

You do not need all six pairs of parts. Any one of these is enough:

:::definition SSS, SAS and ASA
- **SSS** (side-side-side): all three pairs of corresponding sides are equal.
- **SAS** (side-angle-side): two pairs of corresponding sides are equal, and the angles **between** those sides (the included angles) are equal.
- **ASA** (angle-side-angle): two pairs of corresponding angles are equal, and the sides **between** those angles (the included sides) are equal.
:::

In diagrams, matching tick marks show equal sides and matching arcs show equal angles.

## When there is not enough information

Two other patterns do **not** prove congruence:

- **SSA**: two pairs of sides and an angle that is **not** between them. Two different triangles can fit this information.
- **AAA**: three pairs of equal angles. The triangles have the same shape but can be different sizes.

In this diagram, $AB = DE$, $BC = EF$ and $\angle A = \angle D$, but $\angle A$ is not between the two marked sides. The two triangles are clearly different, so SSA is not enough.

:::mistake Using an angle that is not included
"Two sides and an angle" proves congruence only when the angle is formed by those two sides. Always check where the angle is before choosing SAS.
:::

## Two-column congruence proofs

A congruence proof lists three pairs of equal parts, each with a reason, then names the condition.

:::example A proof with a midpoint
Given: $E$ is the midpoint of $AC$ and of $BD$. Prove: $\triangle AEB \cong \triangle CED$.
@step $AE = CE$ and $BE = DE$, by the definition of midpoint.
@step $\angle AEB = \angle CED$, because vertically opposite angles are equal.
@step The equal angles are between the equal sides.
@answer $\triangle AEB \cong \triangle CED$ by SAS.
:::

| Statement | Reason |
|---|---|
| $AE = CE$ | Definition of midpoint |
| $BE = DE$ | Definition of midpoint |
| $\angle AEB = \angle CED$ | Vertically opposite angles are equal |
| $\triangle AEB \cong \triangle CED$ | SAS |

:::tip Reasons you will use often
A side shared by both triangles is equal to itself (**common side**). Midpoints and angle bisectors create equal parts. Parallel lines create equal alternate angles, and crossing lines create equal vertically opposite angles.
:::

:::summary
- Congruent triangles have the same size and shape; the order of the letters shows which parts match.
- SSS, SAS and ASA each prove two triangles are congruent.
- In SAS the angle must be between the two sides; in ASA the side must be between the two angles.
- SSA and AAA do not prove congruence.
- In a two-column proof, give a reason for each pair of equal parts, then name the condition.
:::
