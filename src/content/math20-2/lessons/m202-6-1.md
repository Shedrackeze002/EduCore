A soccer ball kicked down the field, water arcing from a fountain, the cable of a suspension bridge: each traces the same curve, a **parabola**. In this lesson you will read the key features of a parabola from its graph and say what they mean in a real situation.

## Quadratic functions and parabolas

:::definition Quadratic function
A **quadratic function** can be written in **standard form** $y = ax^2 + bx + c$, where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are real numbers and $a \ne 0$. The highest power of $x$ is $2$.
:::

:::definition Parabola
The graph of a quadratic function is a symmetric U-shaped curve called a **parabola**. It **opens up** when $a > 0$ and **opens down** when $a < 0$.
:::

The table shows some values of $y = x^2 - 2x - 3$. The $y$-values decrease, turn around at $x = 1$, then repeat in reverse order. That repeat is the symmetry of the parabola.

| $x$ | $-2$ | $-1$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $y$ | $5$ | $0$ | $-3$ | $-4$ | $-3$ | $0$ | $5$ |

:::example Up or down?
Does the graph of $y = -2x^2 + 3x + 1$ open up or down?
@step The coefficient of $x^2$ is $a = -2$.
@step $a < 0$, so the parabola opens down.
@answer It opens down, like an upside-down U, so its vertex is its highest point.
:::

## Vertex, axis of symmetry and intercepts

:::definition Features of a parabola
- The **vertex** is the turning point: the lowest point of a parabola that opens up, or the highest point of one that opens down.
- The **axis of symmetry** is the vertical line through the vertex. The two halves of the parabola are mirror images across it.
- The **$x$-intercepts** are where the graph meets the $x$-axis. They are also called the **zeros** of the function, because $y = 0$ there.
- The **$y$-intercept** is where the graph crosses the $y$-axis (where $x = 0$).
:::

:::example Reading the graph of $y = x^2 - 2x - 3$
Use the graph above to state the vertex, the axis of symmetry, the zeros and the $y$-intercept.
@step The parabola opens up, so the vertex is its lowest point: $(1, -4)$.
@step The axis of symmetry is the vertical line through the vertex: $x = 1$.
@step The graph crosses the $x$-axis at $(-1, 0)$ and $(3, 0)$, so the zeros are $-1$ and $3$.
@step The graph crosses the $y$-axis at $(0, -3)$.
@answer Vertex $(1, -4)$; axis of symmetry $x = 1$; zeros $-1$ and $3$; $y$-intercept $-3$.
:::

The axis of symmetry is exactly halfway between the zeros: $\dfrac{-1 + 3}{2} = 1$.

:::definition Maximum and minimum values
If a parabola opens up, the $y$-coordinate of its vertex is the **minimum value** of the function. If it opens down, the $y$-coordinate of its vertex is the **maximum value**.
:::

So $y = x^2 - 2x - 3$ has a minimum value of $-4$. A parabola does not have to cross the $x$-axis twice: it can touch it once (at the vertex) or miss it completely. A quadratic function has two, one or no zeros.

:::mistake The axis of symmetry is a vertical line
The axis of symmetry above is $x = 1$, not $y = 1$ and not just "$1$". A vertical line has an equation of the form $x = $ a number. Also keep the vertex coordinates in order: $(x, y)$.
:::

## Domain and range

:::definition Domain and range
The **domain** of a function is the set of all its $x$-values. The **range** is the set of all its $y$-values.
:::

Any real number can be substituted into $y = ax^2 + bx + c$, so the domain of every quadratic function is $\{x \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$. The range starts or stops at the vertex.

:::example Domain and range from a graph
The graph shows $y = -x^2 - 2x + 3$. State its domain and range.
@step The parabola keeps going left and right forever, so the domain is $\{x \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$.
@step It opens down, and its highest point is the vertex $(-1, 4)$. The greatest $y$-value is $4$.
@step Every $y$-value at or below $4$ is reached.
@answer Domain: $\{x \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$. Range: $\{y \mid y \le 4,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$. The maximum value is $4$.
:::

:::mistake Use the y-coordinate of the vertex for the range
The range is about $y$-values. For the vertex $(-1, 4)$ the range is $y \le 4$, not $y \le -1$. And check the direction: a parabola that opens down has a range of the form $y \le$ a number.

:::example The height of a ball
Use the graph to describe the ball's flight.
@step **Maximum height:** the vertex is $(1, 20)$, so the ball reaches a maximum height of $20$ m after $1$ s.
@step **Starting height:** the vertical intercept is $(0, 15)$, so the ball started $15$ m above the ground.
@step **Landing:** the height is $0$ at $t = 3$, so the ball hits the ground after $3$ s.
@step **At $15$ m:** a horizontal line at height $15$ meets the graph at $t = 0$ (on the way up) and at $t = 2$ (on the way down).
@answer The ball rises to $20$ m after $1$ s, is back at $15$ m after $2$ s and lands after $3$ s.
:::

:::tip Domain and range in context
Time starts at $0$ and ends when the ball lands, so the domain is $\{t \mid 0 \le t \le 3,\ t \in \mathbb{R}\}$. The height runs from $0$ (on landing) up to the maximum, so the range is $\{h \mid 0 \le h \le 20,\ h \in \mathbb{R}\}$.
:::

The same reading skills work for a business. If a graph shows profit against price, the vertex gives the best price and the greatest profit, and the zeros are the **break-even** prices, where the profit is $0$.

:::summary
- A quadratic function $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ ($a \ne 0$) has a parabola as its graph. It opens up if $a > 0$ and down if $a < 0$.
- The vertex is the turning point. Its $y$-coordinate is the minimum value (opens up) or the maximum value (opens down).
- The axis of symmetry is the vertical line through the vertex, halfway between the zeros.
- The zeros ($x$-intercepts) are where $y = 0$; there can be two, one or none. The $y$-intercept is where $x = 0$.
- Domain: $\{x \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$. Range: $y \ge$ or $y \le$ the $y$-coordinate of the vertex. In context, both are limited to values that make sense.
:::
