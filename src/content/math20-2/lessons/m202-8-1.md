Is the big bag of coffee really the better deal? Which family vehicle is cheaper to drive? Who ran faster in training? Each question compares **rates**, and the fairest way to compare rates is to bring them to the same base.

## Rates and unit rates

:::definition Rate
A **rate** compares two quantities measured in different units, such as $90\ \text{km}$ in $1.5\ \text{h}$, or \$17.49 for $750\ \text{g}$.
:::

:::definition Unit rate
A **unit rate** is a rate whose second quantity is one unit, such as $60$ km/h or \$18.50 per hour. For prices and fuel, we often use a convenient base instead of one unit: price per $100\ \text{g}$, or litres per $100\ \text{km}$.
:::

To find a unit rate, divide the first quantity by the number of base units in the second.

:::example Unit price
A $750\ \text{g}$ bag of coffee beans costs \$17.49. What is the unit price per $100\ \text{g}$, to the nearest cent?
@step $750\ \text{g}$ is $7.5$ portions of $100\ \text{g}$.
@step $17.49 \div 7.5 = 2.332$
@answer The unit price is about \$2.33 per $100\ \text{g}$.
:::

## Comparing rates to make decisions

Once two options are written as rates with the same base, you can compare them directly.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Package","Price"],"rows":[["400 g","$7.89"],["1 kg","$17.49"]]}
:::

:::example The better buy
A store sells coffee in the two sizes shown. Which is the better buy?
@step $400\ \text{g}$: $7.89 \div 4 \approx 1.97$, so about \$1.97 per $100\ \text{g}$.
@step $1\ \text{kg} = 1000\ \text{g}$: $17.49 \div 10 \approx 1.75$, so about \$1.75 per $100\ \text{g}$.
@answer The $1\ \text{kg}$ bag has the lower unit price, so it is the better buy, as long as you will use it all before it goes stale.
:::

:::tip A good decision uses more than one number
The lowest unit price is the best value only if you need that much. A real decision also weighs storage, waste and how much money you have today.
:::

:::example Fuel consumption
Car A used $42.6\ \text{L}$ of gas to travel $520\ \text{km}$. Car B used $31.9\ \text{L}$ to travel $410\ \text{km}$. Which car is more fuel-efficient?
@step Car A: $42.6 \div 5.2 \approx 8.2$ L/100 km
@step Car B: $31.9 \div 4.1 \approx 7.8$ L/100 km
@answer Car B uses less fuel for every $100\ \text{km}$, so it is more fuel-efficient.
:::

:::mistake Is a bigger rate better?
It depends on the rate. A **higher** speed in km/h means faster, but a **higher** fuel consumption in L/100 km means more fuel used, which is worse. Always ask what the rate measures before you decide.
:::

## Rates as slopes

When one quantity changes at a constant rate, its graph is a straight line, and the **slope** of the line is the rate.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":6,"yMin":0,"yMax":120,"xStep":1,"yStep":20,"xLabel":"Time (h)","yLabel":"Distance (km)"},"polylines":[{"points":[[0,0],[6,108]]}],"points":[{"x":1,"y":18,"label":"(1, 18)"},{"x":4,"y":72,"label":"(4, 72)"}]}
:::

:::example Reading a rate from a graph
The graph shows the distance a cyclist travels. What is the rate, and what does it mean?
@step Use two points on the line: $(1, 18)$ and $(4, 72)$.
@step Slope $= \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \dfrac{72 - 18}{4 - 1} = \dfrac{54}{3} = 18$
@step The units are the vertical units over the horizontal units: kilometres per hour.
@answer The rate is $18$ km/h: the cyclist travels $18\ \text{km}$ every hour.
:::

## Comparing rates on one graph

When a rate changes, the graph is made of segments with different slopes.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":4.5,"yMin":0,"yMax":40,"xStep":0.5,"yStep":5,"xLabel":"Time (h)","yLabel":"Distance from home (km)"},"polylines":[{"points":[[0,0],[1,15],[1.5,15],[2.5,35],[4.5,0]]}],"segmentLabels":["A","B","C","D"]}
:::

- A **steeper** segment shows a **greater** rate.
- A **horizontal** segment shows a rate of $0$: nothing is changing.
- A segment that goes **down** shows a negative rate: the quantity is decreasing.

:::example A bike ride
The graph shows Mei's distance from home on a bike ride. Describe each segment.
@step A: $15 \div 1 = 15$ km/h, riding away from home.
@step B: horizontal, so $0$ km/h. Mei is stopped.
@step C: $(35 - 15) \div 1 = 20$ km/h, riding away again. This is the steepest segment.
@step D: $(0 - 35) \div 2 = -17.5$ km/h. Mei rides home at $17.5$ km/h.
@answer Mei rides fastest during segment C, at $20$ km/h.
:::

:::summary
- A rate compares quantities in different units; a unit rate has a base of one unit (or a handy base such as $100\ \text{g}$ or $100\ \text{km}$).
- Compare options by writing their rates with the same base.
- Decide what "better" means: a lower price per $100\ \text{g}$ and a lower L/100 km are better; a higher speed is faster.
- On a graph, the slope is the rate. Steeper means faster; horizontal means zero; downward means decreasing.
:::
