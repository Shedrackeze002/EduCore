If you know where a parabola crosses the $x$-axis, you already know a lot about it. Factored form puts the zeros right in the equation, which makes it the quickest way to sketch a parabola or to build a model from measurements.

## Factored form

:::definition Factored form
A quadratic function is in **factored form** when it is written as
$$y = a(x - r)(x - s),$$
where $a \ne 0$. The zeros are $r$ and $s$, and $a$ is the same number as the $a$ in standard form.
:::

Why are $r$ and $s$ the zeros? When $x = r$, the factor $(x - r)$ is $0$, and anything multiplied by $0$ is $0$. This is the **zero product property**.

:::example Reading the zeros
Find the zeros of $y = 2(x - 1)(x + 3)$ and say which way the parabola opens.
@step Set each factor equal to $0$: $x - 1 = 0$ gives $x = 1$; $x + 3 = 0$ gives $x = -3$.
@step $a = 2 > 0$, so the parabola opens up.
@answer The zeros are $1$ and $-3$, and the parabola opens up, as the graph shows.
:::

:::mistake The sign flips
In $(x + 3)$ the zero is $-3$, not $3$. Ask "what value of $x$ makes this bracket $0$?" It can help to read $(x + 3)$ as $(x - (-3))$.

## Axis, vertex and y-intercept

From $y = a(x - r)(x - s)$:

- **Axis of symmetry:** halfway between the zeros, $x = \dfrac{r + s}{2}$.
- **Vertex:** substitute the axis value into the equation.
- **$y$-intercept:** substitute $x = 0$.

:::example Everything from factored form
For $y = -(x + 2)(x - 4)$, find the zeros, the axis of symmetry, the vertex, the $y$-intercept and the range.
@step Zeros: $x = -2$ and $x = 4$.
@step Axis of symmetry: $x = \dfrac{-2 + 4}{2} = 1$.
@step Vertex: $y = -(1 + 2)(1 - 4) = -(3)(-3) = 9$, so the vertex is $(1, 9)$.
@step $y$-intercept: $y = -(0 + 2)(0 - 4) = -(2)(-4) = 8$.
@step $a = -1 < 0$, so the parabola opens down and $9$ is the maximum value.
@answer Zeros $-2$ and $4$; axis $x = 1$; vertex $(1, 9)$; $y$-intercept $8$; range $\{y \mid y \le 9,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$.
:::

:::tip Standard form or factored form?
Expanding factored form gives standard form: $2(x - 1)(x + 3) = 2(x^2 + 2x - 3) = 2x^2 + 4x - 6$. Standard form shows the $y$-intercept ($c = -6$); factored form shows the zeros. Both forms have the same $a$.
:::

## Writing an equation from the zeros

If you know the zeros and one more point, you can find $a$ and write the equation.

:::example Finding $a$
A parabola has zeros $-1$ and $5$ and passes through $(1, -16)$. Write its equation in factored form and find the vertex.
@step Start with $y = a(x + 1)(x - 5)$.
@step Substitute $(1, -16)$: $-16 = a(1 + 1)(1 - 5) = a(2)(-4) = -8a$.
@step $a = \dfrac{-16}{-8} = 2$, so $y = 2(x + 1)(x - 5)$.
@step Axis: $x = \dfrac{-1 + 5}{2} = 2$. Vertex: $y = 2(2 + 1)(2 - 5) = 2(3)(-3) = -18$.
@answer $y = 2(x + 1)(x - 5)$, with vertex $(2, -18)$.
:::

:::check m202-factored-form 3
:::

:::example A park entrance arch
A parabolic arch is $40$ m wide at the ground and $10$ m high at its centre. Write an equation for the arch, and find its height $5$ m from one base.
@step Put the origin at the left base. The zeros are $0$ and $40$, so $y = ax(x - 40)$.
@step The top is halfway across, at $(20, 10)$. Substitute: $10 = a(20)(20 - 40) = -400a$, so $a = -\dfrac{10}{400} = -0.025$.
@step At $x = 5$: $y = -0.025(5)(5 - 40) = -0.025(5)(-35) = 4.375$.
@answer $y = -0.025x(x - 40)$. The arch is about $4.4$ m high, $5$ m from the base.
:::

:::tip Choose a convenient origin
With the origin at one base, one zero is $0$ and the equation is simply $y = ax(x - w)$, where $w$ is the width at the ground.
:::

:::check m202-factored-form 3
:::

:::summary
- Factored form: $y = a(x - r)(x - s)$. The zeros are $r$ and $s$; watch the signs.
- Axis of symmetry: $x = \dfrac{r + s}{2}$. Substitute it to find the vertex.
- $y$-intercept: substitute $x = 0$.
- The sign of $a$ tells whether the parabola opens up ($a > 0$) or down ($a < 0$).
- To find $a$, substitute one known point that is not a zero, then solve for $a$.
:::
