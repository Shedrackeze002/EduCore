Try to factor $2x^2 + 3x - 4$ and you will get stuck: no pair of integers works. The **quadratic formula** solves every quadratic equation, whether it factors or not.

## The quadratic formula

:::definition Quadratic formula
The roots of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where $a \ne 0$, are
$$x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}.$$
The symbol $\pm$ means "plus or minus": use $+$ for one root and $-$ for the other.
:::

To use the formula:

1. Write the equation in standard form and identify $a$, $b$ and $c$, with their signs.
2. Calculate $b^2 - 4ac$.
3. Substitute into the formula and work out both roots.

:::example Two rational roots
Solve $2x^2 - x - 3 = 0$.
@step $a = 2$, $b = -1$ and $c = -3$.
@step $b^2 - 4ac = (-1)^2 - 4(2)(-3) = 1 + 24 = 25$
@step $x = \dfrac{1 \pm \sqrt{25}}{4} = \dfrac{1 \pm 5}{4}$
@step $x = \dfrac{1 + 5}{4} = \dfrac{3}{2}$ or $x = \dfrac{1 - 5}{4} = -1$
@answer $x = \dfrac{3}{2}$ or $x = -1$
:::

When $b^2 - 4ac$ is a perfect square, as here, the equation could also be factored: $2x^2 - x - 3 = (2x - 3)(x + 1)$.

:::note Where does the formula come from?
The formula comes from rearranging $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ step by step until $x$ is alone, a method called completing the square. You do not need the derivation in this course, but you can test the formula on an equation you have already solved by factoring.
:::

## Roots that are not whole numbers

:::example Rounding to the nearest hundredth
Solve $2x^2 + 3x - 4 = 0$. Round the roots to the nearest hundredth.
@step $a = 2$, $b = 3$ and $c = -4$.
@step $b^2 - 4ac = 3^2 - 4(2)(-4) = 9 + 32 = 41$
@step $x = \dfrac{-3 \pm \sqrt{41}}{4}$
@step $x = \dfrac{-3 + \sqrt{41}}{4} \approx 0.85$ or $x = \dfrac{-3 - \sqrt{41}}{4} \approx -2.35$
@answer $x \approx 0.85$ or $x \approx -2.35$
:::

Sometimes you are asked for **exact** roots. Then leave the square root in the answer and simplify it.

:::example Exact roots
Solve $x^2 - 4x - 1 = 0$. Give exact roots.
@step $b^2 - 4ac = (-4)^2 - 4(1)(-1) = 16 + 4 = 20$
@step $x = \dfrac{4 \pm \sqrt{20}}{2} = \dfrac{4 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2}$, because $\sqrt{20} = \sqrt{4}\sqrt{5} = 2\sqrt{5}$.
@step Divide each term of the numerator by $2$: $x = 2 \pm \sqrt{5}$.
@answer $x = 2 + \sqrt{5}$ or $x = 2 - \sqrt{5}$ (about $4.24$ and $-0.24$)
:::

:::mistake Three common slips
- The sign of $-b$: if $b = -4$, then $-b = 4$.
- Squaring a negative on a calculator: enter $(-4)^2 = 16$, not $-4^2$, which a calculator reads as $-16$.
- The fraction bar: divide the whole numerator, $-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}$, by $2a$, not just the square root.
:::

:::check m202-quad-formula 2
:::

## How many roots?

The expression $b^2 - 4ac$ under the square root tells you how many real roots there are.

| $b^2 - 4ac$ | Real roots | Graph of $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ |
|---|---|---|
| positive | two | crosses the $x$-axis twice |
| zero | one | touches the $x$-axis at the vertex |
| negative | none | does not meet the $x$-axis |

:::example No real roots
Solve $x^2 + 2x + 5 = 0$.
@step $b^2 - 4ac = 2^2 - 4(1)(5) = 4 - 20 = -16$
@step No real number is the square root of $-16$.
@answer There are no real roots. The graph of $y = x^2 + 2x + 5$ stays above the $x$-axis; its vertex is $(-1, 4)$.
:::

## Choosing a method

:::example Rearrange, then use the formula
Solve $x(x - 8) = 5$. Round to the nearest hundredth.
@step Expand and rearrange: $x^2 - 8x - 5 = 0$.
@step $b^2 - 4ac = (-8)^2 - 4(1)(-5) = 64 + 20 = 84$
@step $x = \dfrac{8 \pm \sqrt{84}}{2}$
@answer $x \approx 8.58$ or $x \approx -0.58$
:::

:::tip Which method?
- **Graphing** shows how many roots there are and gives estimates.
- **Factoring** is fastest when the equation factors easily.
- **The quadratic formula** always works, and gives exact or decimal answers.
:::

:::check m202-quad-formula 3
:::

:::summary
- For $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$: $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$.
- Write the equation in standard form first, and keep the signs of $a$, $b$ and $c$.
- Exact roots keep the square root (simplified); decimal roots are rounded at the end.
- $b^2 - 4ac > 0$: two real roots. $b^2 - 4ac = 0$: one. $b^2 - 4ac < 0$: none.
:::
