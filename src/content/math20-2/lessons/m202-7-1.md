When does a kicked ball land? At what price does a business break even? Questions like these lead to **quadratic equations**. In this lesson you will solve them in two ways: by reading a graph and by factoring.

## Roots and zeros

:::definition Quadratic equation and root
A **quadratic equation** can be written as $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where $a \ne 0$. A **root** (or solution) is a value of $x$ that makes the equation true.
:::

The roots of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are the zeros of the function $y = ax^2 + bx + c$, the $x$-intercepts of its graph. A parabola can cross the $x$-axis twice, touch it once or miss it, so a quadratic equation has two, one or no real roots.

Use the graph of $y = x^2 - 2x - 8$ to solve $x^2 - 2x - 8 = 0$.
@step The graph crosses the $x$-axis at $(-2, 0)$ and $(4, 0)$.
@step Check $x = 4$: $4^2 - 2(4) - 8 = 16 - 8 - 8 = 0$ ✓. Check $x = -2$: $(-2)^2 - 2(-2) - 8 = 4 + 4 - 8 = 0$ ✓.

## Solving by factoring

:::definition Zero product property
If $mn = 0$, then $m = 0$ or $n = 0$ (or both).
:::

To solve a quadratic equation by factoring:

1. Write it in standard form, with $0$ on one side.
2. Factor the other side.
3. Set each factor equal to $0$ and solve.
4. Check each root in the original equation.

:::example A trinomial
Solve $x^2 + 2x - 15 = 0$.
@step Two integers with a product of $-15$ and a sum of $2$: $5$ and $-3$.
@step $(x + 5)(x - 3) = 0$
@step $x + 5 = 0$ or $x - 3 = 0$
@answer $x = -5$ or $x = 3$
:::

:::example Rearrange first
Solve $x^2 = 6x - 8$.
@step Standard form: $x^2 - 6x + 8 = 0$.
@step Product $8$ and sum $-6$: $-2$ and $-4$. So $(x - 2)(x - 4) = 0$.
@answer $x = 2$ or $x = 4$
:::

:::mistake Get $0$ on one side first
$(x - 2)(x + 1) = 4$ does **not** mean $x - 2 = 4$. The zero product property only works when the product is $0$. Expand and rearrange: $x^2 - x - 2 = 4$, so $x^2 - x - 6 = 0$. Then $(x - 3)(x + 2) = 0$, and $x = 3$ or $x = -2$.
:::

## Special cases

:::example A common factor
Solve $3x^2 = 12x$.
@step Standard form: $3x^2 - 12x = 0$.
@step Remove the common factor: $3x(x - 4) = 0$.
@answer $x = 0$ or $x = 4$
:::

:::mistake Do not divide by $x$
Dividing both sides of $3x^2 = 12x$ by $3x$ gives only $x = 4$; the root $x = 0$ is lost. Factor instead.
:::

:::example A difference of squares
Solve $x^2 - 49 = 0$.
@step $x^2 - 49 = (x - 7)(x + 7)$, so $(x - 7)(x + 7) = 0$.
@answer $x = 7$ or $x = -7$
:::

:::example A perfect square
Solve $x^2 - 6x + 9 = 0$.
@step $(x - 3)(x - 3) = 0$, and both factors give $x = 3$.
@answer There is one root, $x = 3$. The graph of $y = x^2 - 6x + 9$ touches the $x$-axis only at its vertex, $(3, 0)$.
:::

## When a is not 1

To factor $ax^2 + bx + c$ when $a \ne 1$, find two integers with a product of $ac$ and a sum of $b$. Use them to split the middle term, then factor by grouping. This is called **decomposition**.

:::example Factoring by decomposition
Solve $2x^2 - 5x - 3 = 0$.
@step $ac = 2(-3) = -6$. Two integers with a product of $-6$ and a sum of $-5$: $-6$ and $1$.
@step Split the middle term: $2x^2 - 6x + x - 3 = 0$.
@step Group: $2x(x - 3) + 1(x - 3) = 0$, so $(2x + 1)(x - 3) = 0$.
@step $2x + 1 = 0$ gives $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$; $x - 3 = 0$ gives $x = 3$.
@answer $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ or $x = 3$
:::

:::check m202-quad-solve-factor 3
:::

:::summary
- The roots of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are the $x$-intercepts of the graph of $y = ax^2 + bx + c$. There can be two, one or no real roots.
- To solve $f(x) = k$ by graphing, find where the graph meets the line $y = k$.
- To solve by factoring: standard form, factor, set each factor equal to $0$, solve, check.
- Never divide both sides by $x$: factor out the common factor instead.
- When $a \ne 1$, factor by decomposition: two integers with a product of $ac$ and a sum of $b$.
:::
