Fold a parabola along its axis of symmetry and the two halves match exactly. That symmetry is a powerful tool: from just the zeros, or just the equation, you can find the vertex and the greatest or least value of a quadratic model.

## The axis of symmetry from the zeros

Points on a parabola at the same height are the same distance from the axis of symmetry. The zeros are two such points (both have $y = 0$), so the axis is halfway between them.

:::definition Axis of symmetry from the zeros
If a parabola has zeros $r$ and $s$, its axis of symmetry is
$$x = \dfrac{r + s}{2}.$$
The vertex lies on this line. Substitute this $x$-value into the equation to find the $y$-coordinate of the vertex.
:::

:::example Zeros, axis and vertex
The graph of $y = x^2 - 4x - 12$ has zeros $-2$ and $6$. Find the axis of symmetry and the vertex.
@step $x = \dfrac{-2 + 6}{2} = \dfrac{4}{2} = 2$
@step Substitute $x = 2$: $y = (2)^2 - 4(2) - 12 = 4 - 8 - 12 = -16$.
@answer The axis of symmetry is $x = 2$ and the vertex is $(2, -16)$.
:::

The same idea works for any two points with the same $y$-coordinate. If $(1, 5)$ and $(7, 5)$ are on a parabola, its axis of symmetry is $x = \dfrac{1 + 7}{2} = 4$.

## The axis of symmetry from standard form

When the zeros are not easy to find, use the equation instead.

:::definition Axis of symmetry from $y = ax^2 + bx + c$
For $y = ax^2 + bx + c$, the axis of symmetry is
$$x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}.$$
:::

**Why it works.** The $y$-intercept is $(0, c)$. Find the other point on the parabola with $y = c$:
$$ax^2 + bx + c = c \quad\Rightarrow\quad ax^2 + bx = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x(ax + b) = 0,$$
so $x = 0$ or $x = -\dfrac{b}{a}$. The axis is halfway between these two points: $x = \dfrac{1}{2}\left(0 - \dfrac{b}{a}\right) = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$.

:::example Vertex from standard form
Find the axis of symmetry and the vertex of $y = 2x^2 + 12x + 7$.
@step $a = 2$ and $b = 12$, so $x = -\dfrac{12}{2(2)} = -\dfrac{12}{4} = -3$.
@step Substitute $x = -3$: $y = 2(-3)^2 + 12(-3) + 7 = 18 - 36 + 7 = -11$.
@answer The axis of symmetry is $x = -3$ and the vertex is $(-3, -11)$. Since $a > 0$, $-11$ is the minimum value.
:::

:::mistake Watch the signs in $-b/(2a)$
For $y = x^2 - 6x + 2$, $b = -6$, so $x = -\dfrac{-6}{2(1)} = 3$, not $-3$. Write $b$ with its sign, then apply the negative in front. Do not forget the $2$ in $2a$.
:::

## Maximum and minimum values

The vertex gives the greatest or least value of a quadratic function:

- $a < 0$: the parabola opens down, and the $y$-coordinate of the vertex is the **maximum** value.
- $a > 0$: the parabola opens up, and the $y$-coordinate of the vertex is the **minimum** value.

:::example A pop fly
A baseball's height, $h$ metres, $t$ seconds after it is hit is modelled by $h = -4.9t^2 + 19.6t + 1.5$. Find its maximum height.
@step $a = -4.9 < 0$, so there is a maximum.
@step $t = -\dfrac{19.6}{2(-4.9)} = \dfrac{19.6}{9.8} = 2$
@step $h = -4.9(2)^2 + 19.6(2) + 1.5 = -19.6 + 39.2 + 1.5 = 21.1$
@answer The ball reaches a maximum height of $21.1$ m, $2$ s after it is hit.
:::

## Solving optimization problems

Many real decisions ask for the **best** value: the most area, the greatest revenue, the least cost. When the quantity is modelled by a quadratic function, the best value is at the vertex.

1. Choose a variable and write an equation for the quantity.
2. Find the vertex with $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$ (or halfway between the zeros).
3. Answer the question that was asked, with units.

:::example Fencing a corral
Kalinda has $60$ m of fencing for a rectangular corral along a river, so only three sides need fencing. What dimensions give the greatest area?
@step Let $x$ be the length of each side that meets the river. The side parallel to the river is $60 - 2x$.
@step $A = x(60 - 2x) = -2x^2 + 60x$
@step $x = -\dfrac{60}{2(-2)} = 15$, and the other side is $60 - 2(15) = 30$.
@step $A = 15 \times 30 = 450$
@answer A corral $15$ m by $30$ m, with the $30$ m side along the river, gives the greatest area, $450\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

The graph shows the area for every possible value of $x$. The vertex, $(15, 450)$, is the highest point.

:::example Setting a ticket price
A theatre sells $300$ tickets at \$10 each. For each \$1 increase in price, it expects to sell $15$ fewer tickets. What price gives the maximum revenue?
@step Let $x$ be the number of \$1 increases. Price: $10 + x$ dollars. Tickets sold: $300 - 15x$.
@step $R = (10 + x)(300 - 15x) = -15x^2 + 150x + 3000$
@step $x = -\dfrac{150}{2(-15)} = 5$
@step Price $= 10 + 5 = 15$ dollars; tickets sold $= 300 - 15(5) = 225$; revenue $= 15 \times 225 = 3375$ dollars.
@answer A price of \$15 gives the maximum revenue, \$3375.
:::

:::mistake Answer the question that was asked
In the theatre example, $x = 5$ is the number of \$1 increases, not the price. Go back to the question: it asks for the price, which is \$15.
:::

:::summary
- The axis of symmetry is halfway between the zeros: $x = \dfrac{r + s}{2}$.
- For $y = ax^2 + bx + c$, the axis of symmetry is $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$.
- Substitute the axis value into the equation to find the $y$-coordinate of the vertex.
- $a < 0$: the vertex gives the maximum value. $a > 0$: it gives the minimum value.
- In optimization problems, write the quadratic model, find the vertex, and answer in context with units.
:::
