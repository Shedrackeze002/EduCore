Quadratic equations turn up wherever areas, falling objects or profits are involved. Solving the equation is only part of the job: you also have to decide which roots make sense in the situation.

## A plan for problems

1. Define a variable, with units.
2. Write an equation that models the situation.
3. Rearrange it into standard form and solve it, by factoring or with the quadratic formula.
4. Check each root in context. **Reject** roots that are impossible, such as a negative length or time.
5. Answer the question in a sentence, with units.

:::example Dimensions of a rectangle
A rectangular garden is $3$ m longer than it is wide. Its area is $70\ \text{m}^2$. Find its dimensions.
@step Let the width be $w$ metres. The length is $w + 3$ metres.
@step $w(w + 3) = 70$, so $w^2 + 3w - 70 = 0$.
@step $(w + 10)(w - 7) = 0$, so $w = -10$ or $w = 7$.
@step A width cannot be negative, so reject $w = -10$.
@answer The garden is $7$ m wide and $10$ m long. Check: $7 \times 10 = 70$ ✓
:::

:::

## Projectiles

The height of an object thrown or kicked into the air is often modelled by $h = -4.9t^2 + vt + h_0$, where $t$ is the time in seconds, $v$ is the starting upward speed in metres per second and $h_0$ is the starting height in metres. The $-4.9$ comes from gravity, which near Earth's surface is about $9.8$ metres per second squared.

:::example When does it land?
Diego kicks a ball. Its height is $h = -4.9t^2 + 12t + 1.5$. When does the ball hit the ground? Round to the nearest hundredth of a second.
@step The ball hits the ground when $h = 0$: $-4.9t^2 + 12t + 1.5 = 0$.
@step $b^2 - 4ac = 12^2 - 4(-4.9)(1.5) = 144 + 29.4 = 173.4$
@step $t = \dfrac{-12 \pm \sqrt{173.4}}{-9.8}$, so $t \approx -0.12$ or $t \approx 2.57$.
@step Time after the kick cannot be negative, so reject $t \approx -0.12$.
@answer The ball hits the ground after about $2.57$ s.
:::

:::example Two answers that both make sense
When is the same ball $5$ m above the ground? Round to the nearest hundredth of a second.
@step $-4.9t^2 + 12t + 1.5 = 5$, so $-4.9t^2 + 12t - 3.5 = 0$.
@step $b^2 - 4ac = 12^2 - 4(-4.9)(-3.5) = 144 - 68.6 = 75.4$
@step $t = \dfrac{-12 \pm \sqrt{75.4}}{-9.8}$, so $t \approx 0.34$ or $t \approx 2.11$.
@answer Both roots make sense: the ball is $5$ m high on the way up (after about $0.34$ s) and on the way down (after about $2.11$ s).
:::

## Borders and frames

:::example A frame of uniform width
A $20$ cm by $30$ cm photo has a frame of uniform width. The framed photo covers $1200\ \text{cm}^2$. How wide is the frame?
@step Let the frame be $x$ cm wide. It adds $x$ on **both** sides, so the outside measures $(20 + 2x)$ cm by $(30 + 2x)$ cm.
@step $(20 + 2x)(30 + 2x) = 1200$, so $4x^2 + 100x + 600 = 1200$.
@step $4x^2 + 100x - 600 = 0$. Divide by $4$: $x^2 + 25x - 150 = 0$.
@step $(x + 30)(x - 5) = 0$, so $x = -30$ or $x = 5$. Reject $x = -30$.
@answer The frame is $5$ cm wide. Check: $30 \times 40 = 1200$ ✓
:::

:::mistake A border goes on both sides
A border $x$ wide adds $2x$ to each dimension, not $x$. Sketch the shape and label all four sides before you write the equation.
:::

:::check m202-quad-apply 2
:::

## When both roots make sense

Not every problem has a root to reject.

:::example Break-even prices
A school club's profit, $P$ dollars, from selling T-shirts at $x$ dollars each is modelled by $P = -5x^2 + 150x - 1000$. At what prices does the club break even?
@step Break even means $P = 0$: $-5x^2 + 150x - 1000 = 0$.
@step Divide by $-5$: $x^2 - 30x + 200 = 0$.
@step $(x - 10)(x - 20) = 0$, so $x = 10$ or $x = 20$.
@answer Both prices make sense: the club breaks even at \$10 and at \$20, and makes a profit at any price in between.
:::

:::example Using the Pythagorean theorem
A diagonal path across a rectangular garden plot is $13$ m long. One side of the plot is $7$ m longer than the other. Find the side lengths.
@step Let the shorter side be $x$ metres. Then $x^2 + (x + 7)^2 = 13^2$.
@step $2x^2 + 14x + 49 = 169$, so $2x^2 + 14x - 120 = 0$. Divide by $2$: $x^2 + 7x - 60 = 0$.
@step $(x + 12)(x - 5) = 0$, so $x = -12$ or $x = 5$. Reject $x = -12$.
@answer The sides are $5$ m and $12$ m. Check: $5^2 + 12^2 = 169 = 13^2$ ✓
:::

:::check m202-quad-apply 3
:::

:::summary
- Define a variable, write an equation, rearrange it into standard form, then solve.
- Check every root in context; reject impossible ones, such as negative lengths or times.
- Projectiles: $h = -4.9t^2 + vt + h_0$. Set $h = 0$ for landing, or set $h$ equal to a height to find when the object is there.
- A uniform border of width $x$ adds $2x$ to each dimension.
- Sometimes both roots are valid: two break-even prices, or two times at the same height.
:::
