Not every triangle has a right angle. Surveyors mapping a lake, pilots plotting a course and engineers designing a bridge truss all work with triangles that have no $90^\circ$ angle. The **sine law** lets you solve them whenever you know a side and the angle across from it.

## Naming the parts of a triangle

:::definition Oblique triangle
An **oblique triangle** is a triangle with no right angle. In this unit every triangle is **acute**: all three angles are less than $90^\circ$.
:::

Name the vertices with capital letters. Name each side with the lower-case letter of the angle **opposite** it: side $a$ is across from $\angle A$, side $b$ is across from $\angle B$, and side $c$ is across from $\angle C$.

A side and the angle opposite it make a **matching pair**. The sine law is built from matching pairs.

:::tip Biggest across from biggest
In any triangle, the longest side is opposite the largest angle, and the shortest side is opposite the smallest angle. Use this to check that your answers make sense.
:::

## Where the sine law comes from

Draw the height $h$ from $C$ straight down to side $AB$. It splits $\triangle ABC$ into two right triangles.

In the left right triangle, $\sin A = \dfrac{h}{b}$, so $h = b \sin A$. In the right one, $\sin B = \dfrac{h}{a}$, so $h = a \sin B$.

Both expressions equal $h$, so $a \sin B = b \sin A$. Divide both sides by $\sin A \sin B$:

$$\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B}$$

Drawing the height from a different vertex brings in side $c$ and $\angle C$ in the same way.

:::definition The sine law
In any $\triangle ABC$,
$$\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B} = \dfrac{c}{\sin C} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \dfrac{\sin A}{a} = \dfrac{\sin B}{b} = \dfrac{\sin C}{c}$$
Use the form with the unknown on top: sides on top to find a side, sines on top to find an angle.
:::

## Finding a side

To find a side, you need one complete matching pair and the angle opposite the side you want. Then you write two ratios from the sine law and solve.

:::example Two angles and a side opposite one of them
In acute $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A = 48^\circ$, $\angle B = 65^\circ$ and $a = 12\ \text{cm}$. Determine $b$, to the nearest tenth of a centimetre.
@step $a$ and $\angle A$ are a matching pair, and $b$ is opposite $\angle B$.
@step $\dfrac{b}{\sin 65^\circ} = \dfrac{12}{\sin 48^\circ}$
@step Multiply both sides by $\sin 65^\circ$: $b = \dfrac{12 \sin 65^\circ}{\sin 48^\circ} \approx \dfrac{12(0.9063)}{0.7431} \approx 14.63$
@answer $b \approx 14.6\ \text{cm}$. Check: $b$ is opposite the larger angle ($65^\circ > 48^\circ$), so it should be longer than $a$. It is.
:::

:::example Two angles and the side between them
In acute $\triangle PQR$, $\angle P = 54^\circ$, $\angle Q = 71^\circ$ and $r = 15\ \text{m}$. Determine $p$, to the nearest tenth of a metre.
@step Side $r$ is opposite $\angle R$, which is not given. Angle sum: $\angle R = 180^\circ - 54^\circ - 71^\circ = 55^\circ$.
@step Now $r$ and $\angle R$ are a matching pair: $\dfrac{p}{\sin 54^\circ} = \dfrac{15}{\sin 55^\circ}$
@step $p = \dfrac{15 \sin 54^\circ}{\sin 55^\circ} \approx 14.81$
@answer $p \approx 14.8\ \text{m}$
:::

:::mistake Mixing up the pairs
Each ratio must use a side and the angle **opposite** it. Writing $\dfrac{p}{\sin 54^\circ} = \dfrac{15}{\sin 71^\circ}$ pairs side $r$ with $\angle Q$, which is not across from it. Name each side by its opposite angle before you substitute.
:::

## Finding an angle

To find an angle, use the form with the sines on top, so the unknown is in a numerator. Solve for the sine of the angle, then use $\sin^{-1}$ on your calculator.

:::example Two sides and an angle opposite one of them
In acute $\triangle ABC$, $a = 11.2\ \text{cm}$, $b = 9.4\ \text{cm}$ and $\angle A = 72^\circ$. Determine $\angle B$, to the nearest degree.
@step $a$ and $\angle A$ are a matching pair, and $\angle B$ is opposite $b$.
@step $\dfrac{\sin B}{9.4} = \dfrac{\sin 72^\circ}{11.2}$
@step Multiply both sides by $9.4$: $\sin B = \dfrac{9.4 \sin 72^\circ}{11.2} \approx 0.7982$
@step $\angle B = \sin^{-1}(0.7982) \approx 53.0^\circ$
@answer $\angle B \approx 53^\circ$
:::

:::note Why there is only one answer
Here the known angle is opposite the **longer** known side ($a > b$), so $\angle B$ must be smaller than $\angle A$. That fixes the shape of the triangle, so there is exactly one answer. Every triangle you solve in this course is set up so that the given information fixes one triangle.
:::

Once you know two angles, the angle sum gives the third, and the sine law can then give the last side. Keep full calculator values until the very end, and round only your final answers.

:::check m202-sine-law 3
:::

:::summary
- Label each side with the lower-case letter of the opposite angle. A side and its opposite angle are a matching pair.
- Sine law: $\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B} = \dfrac{c}{\sin C}$. Put the unknown on top.
- You need one complete matching pair. If the known side has no known opposite angle, use the angle sum first.
- To find an angle, solve for its sine, then use $\sin^{-1}$.
- Check: the largest angle is always opposite the longest side.
:::
