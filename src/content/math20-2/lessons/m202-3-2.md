The sine law needs a matching pair: a known side and the known angle opposite it. When you know two sides and the angle between them, or all three sides, there is no matching pair. The **cosine law** handles these cases, and it turns out to be the Pythagorean theorem with a correction for angles that are not $90^\circ$.

## When the sine law cannot start

Look at the information in each case below. Neither one contains a side together with its opposite angle, so every sine law ratio would have two unknowns.

- **SAS:** two sides and the angle between them, such as $a$, $b$ and $\angle C$.
- **SSS:** all three sides, $a$, $b$ and $c$, and no angles.

:::definition Contained angle
The **contained angle** of two sides is the angle between them, at the vertex where they meet. In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle C$ is the contained angle of sides $a$ and $b$.
:::

## The cosine law

Draw the height $h$ from $C$ to side $AB$. It splits side $c$ into two parts: $x$ next to $A$, and $c - x$ next to $B$.

In the left right triangle, $x = b \cos A$ and $h^2 = b^2 - x^2$. In the right triangle, the Pythagorean theorem gives

$$a^2 = h^2 + (c - x)^2 = (b^2 - x^2) + (c^2 - 2cx + x^2) = b^2 + c^2 - 2cx$$

Replace $x$ with $b \cos A$ to get $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc \cos A$.

:::definition The cosine law
In any $\triangle ABC$,
$$c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$$
The same pattern works for every side: $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$ and $b^2 = a^2 + c^2 - 2ac\cos B$. The side on the left is opposite the angle on the right.
:::

:::tip The Pythagorean connection
If $\angle C = 90^\circ$, then $\cos C = 0$ and the cosine law becomes $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$. The term $-2ab\cos C$ corrects the Pythagorean theorem for other angles.
:::

:::example Two sides and the contained angle
In acute $\triangle ABC$, $a = 9\ \text{cm}$, $b = 11\ \text{cm}$ and $\angle C = 54^\circ$. Determine $c$, to the nearest tenth of a centimetre.
@step $\angle C$ is the contained angle of $a$ and $b$, so use $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$.
@step $c^2 = 9^2 + 11^2 - 2(9)(11)\cos 54^\circ$
@step $c^2 \approx 81 + 121 - 116.38 = 85.62$
@step $c \approx \sqrt{85.62} \approx 9.25$
@answer $c \approx 9.3\ \text{cm}$
:::

:::mistake Order of operations
Multiply $2ab\cos C$ **before** you subtract. A common error is to work left to right: $(81 + 121 - 198) \times \cos 54^\circ$. That gives a tiny, wrong answer. On a calculator, type the whole right side in one line, or find $2ab\cos C$ first.
:::

## Finding an angle (SSS)

To find an angle, rearrange the cosine law so the cosine is alone:

$$\cos C = \dfrac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab}$$

The side opposite the angle you want is the one that is subtracted.

:::example Three sides
In acute $\triangle DEF$, $d = 7\ \text{m}$, $e = 9\ \text{m}$ and $f = 10\ \text{m}$. Determine the measure of the largest angle, to the nearest degree.
@step The largest angle is opposite the longest side, $f = 10$, so find $\angle F$.
@step $\cos F = \dfrac{d^2 + e^2 - f^2}{2de} = \dfrac{7^2 + 9^2 - 10^2}{2(7)(9)} = \dfrac{30}{126} \approx 0.2381$
@step $\angle F = \cos^{-1}(0.2381) \approx 76.2^\circ$
@answer The largest angle is $\angle F \approx 76^\circ$.
:::

## Using both laws

Once the cosine law gives the third side, you have a matching pair, so the sine law can finish the job.

:::example Solving further
In acute $\triangle PQR$, $p = 14\ \text{m}$, $q = 17\ \text{m}$ and $\angle R = 58^\circ$. Determine $r$, to the nearest tenth of a metre, and $\angle P$, to the nearest degree.
@step Cosine law: $r^2 = 14^2 + 17^2 - 2(14)(17)\cos 58^\circ \approx 196 + 289 - 252.24 = 232.76$
@step $r \approx \sqrt{232.76} \approx 15.26$
@step Now $r$ and $\angle R$ are a matching pair. Sine law: $\sin P = \dfrac{14 \sin 58^\circ}{15.26} \approx 0.7782$, so $\angle P \approx 51.1^\circ$.
@answer $r \approx 15.3\ \text{m}$ and $\angle P \approx 51^\circ$. Check: $p$ is the shortest side and $\angle P$ is the smallest angle ($\angle Q \approx 71^\circ$).
:::

:::check m202-cosine-law 3
:::

:::summary
- Use the cosine law when there is no matching pair: two sides and the contained angle (SAS), or three sides (SSS).
- Side: $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$. Angle: $\cos C = \dfrac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab}$.
- The side on its own is always opposite the angle in the formula.
- Find $2ab\cos C$ before subtracting, and keep full calculator values until the end.
- After the cosine law gives a side, the sine law can find the remaining angles.
:::
