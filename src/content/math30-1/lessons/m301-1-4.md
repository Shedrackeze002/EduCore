A thermostat converts degrees Celsius to Fahrenheit; a second formula converts back. A function and its inverse undo each other, and their graphs are mirror images in the line $y = x$.

## The inverse of a relation

:::definition Inverse
The inverse of a relation interchanges the $x$- and $y$-coordinates of every point: $(x, y) \to (y, x)$. The inverse of a function $f$ is written $f^{-1}$. Its graph is the reflection of the graph of $f$ in the line $y = x$.
:::

Interchanging coordinates swaps the roles of input and output, so the domain of $f^{-1}$ is the range of $f$, and the range of $f^{-1}$ is the domain of $f$. Points on the line $y = x$ are invariant.

:::example A set of points
Find the inverse of $\{(1, 4), (2, 6), (3, 6)\}$. Is the inverse a function?
@step Interchange the coordinates: $\{(4, 1), (6, 2), (6, 3)\}$.
@step The inverse contains $(6, 2)$ and $(6, 3)$: one input with two outputs.
@answer $\{(4, 1), (6, 2), (6, 3)\}$; it is not a function.
:::

## Finding an inverse algebraically

Write $y = f(x)$, interchange $x$ and $y$, then solve for $y$.

:::example A linear function
Find the inverse of $f(x) = 3x - 4$.
@step $y = 3x - 4$
@step Interchange: $x = 3y - 4$
@step Solve: $3y = x + 4$, so $y = \dfrac{x + 4}{3}$.
@step Verify: $f\left(\dfrac{x + 4}{3}\right) = 3 \cdot \dfrac{x + 4}{3} - 4 = x$, so $f$ undoes $f^{-1}$.
@answer $f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{x + 4}{3}$
:::

## When is the inverse a function?

:::definition Horizontal line test
If no horizontal line crosses the graph of $f$ more than once, then the inverse of $f$ is a function. Reflecting in $y = x$ turns horizontal lines into vertical lines, so this is the vertical line test applied to the inverse.
:::

The parabola $y = x^2 - 3$ (grey) fails the test: $y = 1$ crosses it at $x = -2$ and $x = 2$. Its inverse, $y = \pm\sqrt{x + 3}$ (coloured), fails the vertical line test.

## Inverses of quadratic functions

:::example A restricted quadratic
Find the inverse of $f(x) = (x - 2)^2 + 1$, $x \ge 2$, and state its domain and range.
@step Interchange: $x = (y - 2)^2 + 1$, so $(y - 2)^2 = x - 1$.
@step $y - 2 = \pm\sqrt{x - 1}$.
@step The range of $f^{-1}$ is the domain of $f$, so $y \ge 2$: choose the positive root.
@step The domain of $f^{-1}$ is the range of $f$: $x \ge 1$.
@answer $f^{-1}(x) = \sqrt{x - 1} + 2$, domain $\{x \mid x \ge 1\}$, range $\{y \mid y \ge 2\}$
:::

:::summary
- The inverse interchanges coordinates, $(x, y) \to (y, x)$, and reflects the graph in $y = x$.
- Domain and range swap; points on $y = x$ are invariant.
- To find $f^{-1}(x)$: write $y = f(x)$, interchange $x$ and $y$, solve for $y$.
- The inverse is a function only if $f$ passes the horizontal line test; restrict the domain of a quadratic at its vertex.
:::
