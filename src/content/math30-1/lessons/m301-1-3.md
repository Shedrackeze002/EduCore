Engineers who design a cable, an arch or a signal rarely use just one transformation. They stretch, flip and shift a basic shape all at once, and one equation, $y - k = af(b(x - h))$, keeps track of everything.

## The general transformation

:::definition General transformation form
$y - k = af(b(x - h))$, also written $y = af(b(x - h)) + k$. It applies to $y = f(x)$:
- a vertical stretch by a factor of $|a|$ about the $x$-axis (and a reflection in the $x$-axis if $a < 0$),
- a horizontal stretch by a factor of $\frac{1}{|b|}$ about the $y$-axis (and a reflection in the $y$-axis if $b < 0$),
- a translation of $h$ units horizontally and $k$ units vertically.
:::

The mapping combines all of these:

$$(x, y) \to \left(\frac{x}{b} + h,\ ay + k\right)$$

**Order matters.** Apply the stretches and reflections first, then the translations. To see why, follow the $y$-coordinate $5$ under $y = 2f(x) + 3$. The equation multiplies the output by $2$ and then adds $3$, giving $2(5) + 3 = 13$. Translating first would give $2(5 + 3) = 16$, which is a different point. Stretches and reflections are multiplications, and they must happen before the additions, exactly as in the order of operations.

## Image points

:::example Applying the mapping
The point $(6, -2)$ is on the graph of $y = f(x)$. Find the corresponding point on the graph of $y = 3f\left(-\frac{1}{2}(x - 1)\right) + 4$.
@step $a = 3$, $b = -\frac{1}{2}$, $h = 1$, $k = 4$.
@step $x$: $\dfrac{6}{-\frac{1}{2}} + 1 = -12 + 1 = -11$
@step $y$: $3(-2) + 4 = -2$
@answer $(-11, -2)$
:::

:::example Describing the transformations
Describe how the graph of $y + 2 = -\frac{1}{2}f(3(x + 4))$ is related to the graph of $y = f(x)$.
@step $a = -\frac{1}{2}$: a vertical stretch by a factor of $\frac{1}{2}$ about the $x$-axis and a reflection in the $x$-axis.
@step $b = 3$: a horizontal stretch by a factor of $\frac{1}{3}$ about the $y$-axis.
@step $h = -4$ and $k = -2$: a translation of 4 units left and 2 units down.
@answer Stretch vertically by $\frac{1}{2}$, reflect in the $x$-axis, stretch horizontally by $\frac{1}{3}$, then translate 4 left and 2 down.
:::

## Factor b first

The value of $h$ can only be read once the expression inside the brackets has the form $b(x - h)$. If it does not, factor out $b$ first.

:::example An unfactored equation
The point $(8, 5)$ is on the graph of $y = f(x)$. Find its image on $y = f(2x - 6) + 1$.
@step Factor: $2x - 6 = 2(x - 3)$, so $y = f(2(x - 3)) + 1$ with $b = 2$, $h = 3$, $k = 1$.
@step $x$: $\frac{8}{2} + 3 = 7$; $y$: $5 + 1 = 6$.
@answer $(7, 6)$
:::

:::mistake Reading h too early
In $y = f(2x - 6)$ the horizontal translation is **not** 6 units right. Factoring gives $f(2(x - 3))$: the graph is squeezed by a factor of $\frac{1}{2}$ and then moved 3 units right.
:::

:::check m301-combined-transform 2
:::

## Writing equations

From a description, read off $a$, $b$, $h$ and $k$ and substitute into $y = af(b(x - h)) + k$. For example, if $y = \sqrt{x}$ is stretched vertically by a factor of $3$, reflected in the $y$-axis and translated 2 units right and 5 units up, then $a = 3$, $b = -1$, $h = 2$ and $k = 5$, so $y = 3\sqrt{-(x - 2)} + 5$.

From a graph, find the image of a key point of the base function (the vertex of $y = |x|$ or $y = x^2$, the endpoint of $y = \sqrt{x}$) to get $h$ and $k$, then substitute one more point to find $a$.

:::summary
- $y - k = af(b(x - h))$ maps $(x, y) \to \left(\frac{x}{b} + h,\ ay + k\right)$.
- Stretch and reflect first, then translate.
- Factor the inside to $b(x - h)$ before reading $h$.
- To write an equation from a graph, get $h$ and $k$ from a key point and $a$ from one more point.
:::
