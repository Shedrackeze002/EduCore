A sound engineer who delays a recording by two seconds and turns the volume up by a fixed amount has moved the whole waveform without changing its shape. That is a translation, and every function you meet this year can be shifted the same way.

## Translating a graph

:::definition Translation
A transformation that slides every point of a graph the same distance in the same direction. The shape and orientation do not change.
:::

The graph of $y - k = f(x - h)$ is the graph of $y = f(x)$ translated

- $h$ units horizontally (right if $h > 0$, left if $h < 0$), and
- $k$ units vertically (up if $k > 0$, down if $k < 0$).

The same equation is often written $y = f(x - h) + k$. Both forms say the same thing: replace $x$ with $x - h$ and $y$ with $y - k$.

Why does $x - h$ move the graph to the **right** when $h > 0$? On the image, the output that $f$ produced at $x = 0$ now appears where $x - h = 0$, that is, at $x = h$.

## Image points and mapping notation

:::definition Image point
The point a given point moves to under a transformation. The image of $A$ is written $A'$ ("$A$ prime").
:::

:::definition Mapping
A rule that describes how every point moves. For $y - k = f(x - h)$, the mapping is $(x, y) \to (x + h,\ y + k)$.
:::

:::example Finding an image point
The point $(4, -1)$ is on the graph of $y = f(x)$. Find the corresponding point on the graph of $y + 5 = f(x - 3)$.
@step Compare with $y - k = f(x - h)$: $h = 3$ and $k = -5$.
@step Mapping: $(x, y) \to (x + 3,\ y - 5)$.
@step $(4, -1) \to (4 + 3,\ -1 - 5)$.
@answer $(7, -6)$
:::

## Writing the equation of a translated function

To write the equation, start from the base function and substitute: $x - h$ for $x$, then add $k$. This works for any base function, such as $y = x^2$, $y = |x|$, $y = \sqrt{x}$, $y = x^3$ or $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$.

:::mistake The sign of h
In $y = f(x + 3)$, the graph moves 3 units **left**, not right: $x + 3 = x - (-3)$, so $h = -3$. The vertical shift reads the way it looks only when $k$ is on the right side: $y = f(x) + 3$ moves up 3, and so does $y - 3 = f(x)$.
:::

## Domain and range after a translation

A horizontal translation shifts every $x$-value by $h$, so it shifts the domain. A vertical translation shifts every $y$-value by $k$, so it shifts the range.

:::example Domain and range
$y = f(x)$ has domain $\{x \mid -3 \le x \le 5,\ x \in \mathbb{R}\}$ and range $\{y \mid -2 \le y \le 4,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$. Find the domain and range of $y = f(x + 1) - 6$.
@step $h = -1$ and $k = -6$.
@step Domain: $-3 - 1 = -4$ and $5 - 1 = 4$.
@step Range: $-2 - 6 = -8$ and $4 - 6 = -2$.
@answer Domain $\{x \mid -4 \le x \le 4,\ x \in \mathbb{R}\}$; range $\{y \mid -8 \le y \le -2,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$
:::

:::tip Reading h and k from two graphs
Pick a corner or another clear point on the original graph and find its image. The change in $x$ is $h$ and the change in $y$ is $k$. Check with a second point: in a translation every point moves by the same amount.
:::

:::check m301-translate 3
:::

:::summary
- $y - k = f(x - h)$, or $y = f(x - h) + k$, translates $y = f(x)$ by $h$ units horizontally and $k$ units vertically.
- Mapping: $(x, y) \to (x + h,\ y + k)$.
- $f(x - 3)$ moves right 3; $f(x + 3)$ moves left 3.
- A translation shifts the domain by $h$ and the range by $k$; the shape does not change.
:::
