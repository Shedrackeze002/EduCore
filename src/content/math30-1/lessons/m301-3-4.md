An engineer who knows where a beam's deflection is zero, or a designer who needs a box with an exact volume, is solving a polynomial equation. The factored form of a polynomial connects those equations to the graph: every factor is an $x$-intercept, and its exponent tells you the shape of the graph there.

## Zeros, roots and x-intercepts

For a polynomial function $y = P(x)$, the **zeros** of the function, the **roots** of the equation $P(x) = 0$ and the **$x$-intercepts** of the graph are the same numbers. Once $P(x)$ is factored, the zero product property finds them: a product is zero only when one of its factors is zero.

:::example Solving by factoring
Solve $x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x - 6 = 0$.
@step Possible integral zeros: $\pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \pm 6$. Test: $P(1) = -8$, but $P(-1) = -1 + 2 + 5 - 6 = 0$, so $x + 1$ is a factor.
@step Divide by $x + 1$:
$$\begin{array}{r|rrrr} -1 & 1 & 2 & -5 & -6 \\ & & -1 & -1 & 6 \\ \hline & 1 & 1 & -6 & \boxed{0} \end{array}$$
@step $x^2 + x - 6 = (x + 3)(x - 2)$, so $(x + 3)(x + 1)(x - 2) = 0$.
@answer $x = -3$, $x = -1$ or $x = 2$
:::

:::

## Multiplicity and the shape at each zero

:::definition Multiplicity
If $(x - a)^n$ is a factor of $P(x)$, but $(x - a)^{n+1}$ is not, then $a$ is a zero of **multiplicity** $n$.
:::

The multiplicity controls what the graph does at the zero:

| Multiplicity | Sign of $P(x)$ at the zero | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| $1$ | changes | crosses the $x$-axis, like a line |
| $2$ | does not change | touches the $x$-axis and turns back, like a parabola |
| $3$ | changes | crosses with a flattened bend, like $y = x^3$ |

**Why.** Near $x = a$, the other factors stay close to a fixed non-zero number, so the graph looks like a multiple of $(x - a)^n$. The power $(x - a)^2$ is never negative, so the function cannot change sign there; $(x - a)^1$ and $(x - a)^3$ both change sign.

## Sketching from factored form

To sketch $y = P(x)$ from its factored form:

1. Find the zeros and their multiplicities.
2. Find the $y$-intercept by substituting $x = 0$.
3. Find the degree (add the exponents) and the sign of the leading coefficient (multiply the number in front by the coefficient of $x$ in each factor, each raised to its exponent). These give the end behaviour.
4. Draw the curve from the left end to the right end through the intercepts, with the right shape at each zero.

:::example Sketching a cubic
Sketch $y = -(x + 2)(x - 1)^2$.
@step Zeros: $-2$ (multiplicity $1$, crosses) and $1$ (multiplicity $2$, touches).
@step $y$-intercept: $-(2)(-1)^2 = -2$.
@step Degree $1 + 2 = 3$ (odd) and leading coefficient $-1$ (negative): the graph extends from quadrant II to quadrant IV.
@step Start high on the left, cross at $-2$, pass through $(0, -2)$, rise to touch the axis at $1$, and fall to the right.
@answer The graph is shown below.
:::

## Inequalities and applications

The zeros split the number line into intervals, and $P(x)$ keeps the same sign throughout each one. Test one value in each interval.

:::example Solving a polynomial inequality
Solve $x^3 - x^2 - 6x \ge 0$.
@step Let $P(x) = x^3 - x^2 - 6x$. Factor: $P(x) = x(x^2 - x - 6) = x(x + 2)(x - 3)$. The zeros are $-2$, $0$ and $3$.
@step Test each interval: $P(-3) = -18$, $P(-1) = 4$, $P(1) = -6$, $P(4) = 24$.
@step $P(x) \ge 0$ on $[-2, 0]$ and on $[3, \infty)$; the zeros are included because of the "$\ge$".
@answer $x \in [-2, 0] \cup [3, \infty)$
:::

| Interval | $(-\infty, -2)$ | $(-2, 0)$ | $(0, 3)$ | $(3, \infty)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sign of $x(x + 2)(x - 3)$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |

:::example A box with a given volume
A box has height $x$ cm, width $x + 2$ cm and length $x + 5$ cm. Its volume is $120$ cm³. Find its dimensions.
@step $x(x + 2)(x + 5) = 120$ expands to $x^3 + 7x^2 + 10x - 120 = 0$.
@step Test positive factors of $120$: $P(3) = 27 + 63 + 30 - 120 = 0$. Divide by $x - 3$:
$$\begin{array}{r|rrrr} 3 & 1 & 7 & 10 & -120 \\ & & 3 & 30 & 120 \\ \hline & 1 & 10 & 40 & \boxed{0} \end{array}$$
@step $x^2 + 10x + 40 = 0$ has discriminant $100 - 160 < 0$, so $x = 3$ is the only real solution.
@answer $3$ cm by $5$ cm by $8$ cm (check: $3 \times 5 \times 8 = 120$)
:::

:::mistake Keep only answers that make sense
In a context, reject zeros that give negative or zero lengths. For an open box cut from a $30$ cm by $20$ cm sheet, $V(x) = x(30 - 2x)(20 - 2x)$ only makes sense for $0 < x < 10$.
:::

:::check m301-poly-equation 3
:::

:::summary
- Zeros of $P$, roots of $P(x) = 0$ and $x$-intercepts are the same numbers; factor and use the zero product property.
- Multiplicity $1$: crosses. Multiplicity $2$: touches and turns back. Multiplicity $3$: crosses with a flattened bend.
- Sketch from factored form with the zeros, the $y$-intercept and the end behaviour (degree = sum of exponents).
- Equation from a graph: $y = a(\dots)$ with a factor for each zero, then substitute a point to find $a$.
- Inequalities: the zeros split the number line; test the sign in each interval. In contexts, keep only sensible answers.
:::
