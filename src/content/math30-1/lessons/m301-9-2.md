A playlist of 10 songs on shuffle can play in $3\,628\,800$ different orders. Counting arrangements like this is so common that mathematicians gave it its own notation, the factorial, and its own name: a permutation.

## Factorial notation

:::definition Factorial
For a natural number $n$, $n! = n(n - 1)(n - 2) \cdots (3)(2)(1)$. By definition, $0! = 1$.
:::

$n!$ counts the ways to arrange $n$ different objects in a row: $n$ choices for the first place, $n - 1$ for the second, and so on. Notice that $n! = n \times (n - 1)!$. Putting $n = 1$ gives $1! = 1 \times 0!$, so $0!$ must equal $1$ for the pattern to hold.

That same pattern lets you cancel factorials without multiplying them out.

:::example Cancelling factorials
Simplify (a) $\dfrac{10!}{8!}$ and (b) $\dfrac{(n + 1)!}{(n - 1)!}$.
@step (a) $10! = 10 \times 9 \times 8!$, so $\dfrac{10 \times 9 \times 8!}{8!} = 10 \times 9 = 90$
@step (b) $(n + 1)! = (n + 1)(n)(n - 1)!$, so $\dfrac{(n + 1)(n)(n - 1)!}{(n - 1)!} = n(n + 1)$
@answer (a) $90$; (b) $n(n + 1) = n^2 + n$
:::

:::mistake Dividing the numbers inside the factorials
$\dfrac{10!}{5!}$ is **not** $2!$. Write out the larger factorial until you reach the smaller one: $\dfrac{10!}{5!} = 10 \times 9 \times 8 \times 7 \times 6 = 30\,240$.
:::

## Permutations of n objects taken r at a time

A **permutation** is an arrangement of objects in which order matters. Often only some of the objects are arranged: 12 runners in a race, but only 3 medals.

:::definition The number of permutations
The number of permutations of $n$ different objects taken $r$ at a time is
$${}_{n}P_{r} = \frac{n!}{(n - r)!}, \qquad 0 \le r \le n$$
:::

Here is why. By the counting principle there are $n(n - 1)(n - 2)\cdots(n - r + 1)$ ways to fill $r$ places. Multiplying the top and bottom by $(n - r)!$ completes the top to $n!$, which gives $\dfrac{n!}{(n - r)!}$.

:::example Medals in a race
12 skiers race at Nakiska. In how many ways can the gold, silver and bronze medals be awarded?
@step The medals are different, so order matters: ${}_{12}P_{3}$.
@step ${}_{12}P_{3} = \dfrac{12!}{9!} = 12 \times 11 \times 10$
@answer $1320$ ways.
:::

## Solving equations with permutations

To find $n$, rewrite the notation as a product of factors in $n$, then solve. The answer must be a natural number big enough for the notation to make sense.

:::example Solving for n
Solve ${}_{n}P_{2} = 56$.
@step ${}_{n}P_{2} = \dfrac{n!}{(n - 2)!} = n(n - 1)$
@step $n(n - 1) = 56$, so $n^2 - n - 56 = 0$
@step $(n - 8)(n + 7) = 0$, so $n = 8$ or $n = -7$.
@answer $n$ is a natural number, so $n = 8$. (Check: $8 \times 7 = 56$.)
:::

:::mistake Keeping the negative root
$n = -7$ solves the quadratic, but $-7$ objects makes no sense, and $(-7)!$ is not defined. Always reject roots that are not natural numbers.
:::

## Arrangements with identical objects

If some objects are identical, swapping them does not create a new arrangement, so $n!$ counts each arrangement several times.

:::definition Permutations with identical objects
The number of arrangements of $n$ objects, where $a$ are alike, $b$ are alike, $c$ are alike, and so on, is
$$\frac{n!}{a!\,b!\,c!\cdots}$$
:::

:::example MISSISSIPPI
How many different arrangements of the letters of MISSISSIPPI are there?
@step 11 letters: I appears 4 times, S appears 4 times, P appears twice, M once.
@step $\dfrac{11!}{4!\,4!\,2!} = \dfrac{39\,916\,800}{24 \times 24 \times 2}$
@answer $34\,650$ arrangements.
:::

## Restrictions and routes

Handle the restriction first, then arrange everything else.

- **Must be together:** tape them into one block, arrange the blocks, then multiply by the arrangements inside the block.
- **Must not be together:** count all arrangements, then subtract those where they are together.

:::example Seating with a restriction
Six friends, including Mei and Owen, sit in a row. How many seatings have Mei and Owen (a) together and (b) apart?
@step (a) Treat Mei and Owen as one unit: 5 units, $5! = 120$. They can switch places: $2 \times 120 = 240$
@step (b) All seatings: $6! = 720$. Apart: $720 - 240 = 480$
@answer (a) $240$; (b) $480$
:::

On a grid of streets, every shortest route from A to B is a string of E (east) and N (north) moves. Counting routes is counting arrangements of identical letters.

@step Each route is an arrangement of EEEENNN: 7 moves with 4 Es alike and 3 Ns alike.
@step $\dfrac{7!}{4!\,3!} = \dfrac{5040}{24 \times 6}$

:::summary
- $n! = n(n - 1)\cdots(2)(1)$ and $0! = 1$. Cancel factorials by writing the larger one down to the smaller one.
- ${}_{n}P_{r} = \dfrac{n!}{(n - r)!}$ counts ordered arrangements of $r$ objects chosen from $n$.
- To solve for $n$, expand the notation, solve, and reject values that are not natural numbers.
- With identical objects, divide $n!$ by the factorial of each group of alike objects.
- For "together", use a block; for "apart", subtract "together" from the total. Grid routes are arrangements of Es and Ns.
:::
