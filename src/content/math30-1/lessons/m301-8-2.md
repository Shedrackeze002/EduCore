An engineer reading a sensor curve, or a student checking a calculator screen, needs to know at a glance whether a graph matches its equation. Every feature of a rational function, from its asymptotes to a single missing point, can be read from the factored equation.

## A sketching checklist

To sketch $y = f(x)$ or to match it with a graph, work through the features in order.

1. **Factor** the numerator and the denominator completely.
2. **Domain:** exclude every zero of the denominator.
3. **Points of discontinuity:** for each cancelled factor, find $y$ from the simplified function.
4. **Vertical asymptotes:** the zeros of the denominator that remain.
5. **Horizontal asymptote:** compare the degrees.
6. **Intercepts:** $x$-intercepts from the simplified numerator, and $f(0)$.
7. **Behaviour near each vertical asymptote:** test a value just to the left and just to the right.

:::example Sketching with a hole
Sketch $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 - x - 6}$.
@step Factor: $f(x) = \dfrac{(x - 2)(x + 2)}{(x - 3)(x + 2)} = \dfrac{x - 2}{x - 3}$, $x \ne -2$.
@step Hole: $\dfrac{-2 - 2}{-2 - 3} = \dfrac{4}{5}$, so an open circle at $\left(-2, \tfrac{4}{5}\right)$.
@step Vertical asymptote $x = 3$; horizontal asymptote $y = \dfrac{1}{1} = 1$.
@step $x$-intercept $2$; $y$-intercept $f(0) = \dfrac{-2}{-3} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
@step Near $x = 3$: $f(2.9) = \dfrac{0.9}{-0.1} = -9$ and $f(3.1) = \dfrac{1.1}{0.1} = 11$. The graph drops down on the left of the asymptote and rises on the right.
@answer The graph below
:::

## Graphs with a point of discontinuity

A point of discontinuity looks like a single missing point on an otherwise smooth curve. The rest of the graph is the graph of the simplified function, so the two graphs match everywhere except at the hole.

:::mistake Holes are not asymptotes
For $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 - x - 6}$, the factor $x + 2$ cancels. There is no vertical asymptote at $x = -2$: the graph passes right through that $x$-value, with only one point missing. Drawing the simplified graph without the open circle is also a mistake.
:::

When you match an equation with a graph, look for the features that tell the choices apart: the location of the hole (both coordinates), the vertical and horizontal asymptotes, the intercepts, and which side of the asymptotes each branch is on.

:::check m301-rational-graph 2
:::

## Two vertical asymptotes

With two vertical asymptotes, the graph has three branches. A sign chart shows where the graph is above or below the $x$-axis.

:::example Using a sign chart
Sketch $f(x) = \dfrac{x - 1}{x^2 - 4}$.
@step Factor: $f(x) = \dfrac{x - 1}{(x - 2)(x + 2)}$. Nothing cancels.
@step Vertical asymptotes $x = -2$ and $x = 2$. Degree $1$ over degree $2$, so the horizontal asymptote is $y = 0$.
@step $x$-intercept $1$; $y$-intercept $f(0) = \dfrac{-1}{-4} = \dfrac{1}{4}$.
@step Signs: for $x < -2$, $f(-3) = -0.8 < 0$; for $-2 < x < 1$, $f(0) > 0$; for $1 < x < 2$, $f(1.5) \approx -0.29 < 0$; for $x > 2$, $f(3) = 0.4 > 0$.
@step So the graph approaches $y = 0$ from below on the far left, comes down from above just right of $x = -2$, crosses the $x$-axis at $1$, drops toward $x = 2$, and comes down from above on the right.
@answer The graph below
:::

## Writing an equation from a graph

Read the features and build the equation from them.

- A vertical asymptote $x = h$ gives a factor $(x - h)$ in the denominator.
- An $x$-intercept $r$ gives a factor $(x - r)$ in the numerator.
- A point of discontinuity at $x = a$ gives $(x - a)$ in both.
- The horizontal asymptote (or one point on the graph) gives the constant in front.

:::example From a graph to an equation
Write an equation for the graph above. It has a vertical asymptote $x = -2$, a horizontal asymptote $y = 2$, an $x$-intercept of $1$, and a point of discontinuity at $(4, 1)$.
@step Start with $f(x) = \dfrac{a(x - 1)(x - 4)}{(x + 2)(x - 4)}$.
@step The degrees are equal, so the horizontal asymptote is $y = a$: $a = 2$.
@step Check the hole: the simplified function is $\dfrac{2(x - 1)}{x + 2}$, and $\dfrac{2(4 - 1)}{4 + 2} = 1$. ✓
@answer $f(x) = \dfrac{2(x - 1)(x - 4)}{(x + 2)(x - 4)}$
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the constant
$f(x) = \dfrac{(x - 1)(x - 4)}{(x + 2)(x - 4)}$ has the right asymptote, intercept and hole location, but its horizontal asymptote is $y = 1$ and its hole is at $\left(4, \tfrac{1}{2}\right)$. Always check the horizontal asymptote or a point.
:::

:::summary
- Factor first, then find the domain, holes, vertical asymptotes, horizontal asymptote and intercepts.
- Test values on each side of a vertical asymptote, or use a sign chart, to place the branches.
- A point of discontinuity is an open circle on the graph of the simplified function, not an asymptote.
- To write an equation, turn each feature into a factor and use the horizontal asymptote or a point to find the constant.
:::
