Hold a magnifying glass over a page and slowly lift it. At one height the image blurs away completely, because the image has moved infinitely far from the lens. The image distance is a **rational function** of the object distance, and that blow-up is a vertical asymptote.

## Rational functions and y = a/(x − h) + k

:::definition Rational function
A function of the form $f(x) = \dfrac{p(x)}{q(x)}$, where $p(x)$ and $q(x)$ are polynomials and $q(x) \ne 0$. In this course the numerator and denominator are monomials, binomials or trinomials.
:::

The simplest rational function is $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$. It is undefined at $x = 0$, and as $|x|$ grows, $\dfrac{1}{x}$ gets closer and closer to $0$. So its graph has a **vertical asymptote** $x = 0$ and a **horizontal asymptote** $y = 0$.

:::definition Asymptotes
A vertical asymptote $x = a$ is a vertical line the graph approaches as $f(x)$ grows without bound near $x = a$. A horizontal asymptote $y = b$ is a horizontal line the graph approaches as $|x|$ grows without bound.
:::

Transformations move the asymptotes with the graph: $y = \dfrac{a}{x - h} + k$ has the vertical asymptote $x = h$ and the horizontal asymptote $y = k$.

## Vertical asymptotes and points of discontinuity

A rational function is undefined wherever its denominator is $0$. What the graph does there depends on the numerator, so **always factor first**.

:::definition Point of discontinuity
A single point missing from a graph, drawn as an open circle (a "hole"). It occurs at $x = a$ when $(x - a)$ is a factor of both the numerator and the denominator and cancels completely.
:::

- A factor of the denominator that **cancels** gives a point of discontinuity.
- A factor of the denominator that **remains** after cancelling gives a vertical asymptote.

:::example A hole and an asymptote
Find the points of discontinuity and the vertical asymptotes of $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - x - 6}{x^2 - 9}$.
@step Factor: $f(x) = \dfrac{(x - 3)(x + 2)}{(x - 3)(x + 3)}$.
@step The factor $x - 3$ cancels: $f(x) = \dfrac{x + 2}{x + 3}$, $x \ne 3$.
@step Hole at $x = 3$. Its $y$-coordinate comes from the simplified function: $\dfrac{3 + 2}{3 + 3} = \dfrac{5}{6}$.
@step The factor $x + 3$ remains, so $x = -3$ is a vertical asymptote.
@answer Point of discontinuity $\left(3, \tfrac{5}{6}\right)$; vertical asymptote $x = -3$
:::

## Horizontal asymptotes

The horizontal asymptote describes the **end behaviour**: what $f(x)$ approaches as $|x|$ grows. Divide the numerator and denominator by the highest power of $x$ in the denominator:
$$\frac{2x^2 + 3x - 1}{x^2 - 4} = \frac{2 + \frac{3}{x} - \frac{1}{x^2}}{1 - \frac{4}{x^2}} \to \frac{2 + 0 - 0}{1 - 0} = 2.$$

| $x$ | $10$ | $100$ | $1000$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\dfrac{2x^2 + 3x - 1}{x^2 - 4}$ | $2.385$ | $2.031$ | $2.003$ |

Comparing the degree of the numerator, $n$, with the degree of the denominator, $d$:

- $n < d$: the horizontal asymptote is $y = 0$.
- $n = d$: the horizontal asymptote is $y = \dfrac{\text{leading coefficient of the numerator}}{\text{leading coefficient of the denominator}}$.
- $n > d$: there is no horizontal asymptote.

:::example Comparing degrees
Find the horizontal asymptote of $g(x) = \dfrac{3x - 6}{x^2 + 2x}$ and of $h(x) = \dfrac{4x^2 - 1}{2x^2 + x - 3}$.
@step $g$: degree $1$ over degree $2$, so $y = 0$.
@step $h$: both have degree $2$, so $y = \dfrac{4}{2} = 2$.
@answer $g$: $y = 0$; $h$: $y = 2$
:::

In context, the asymptotes have meaning. For a lens with focal length $10$ cm, the image distance is $d_i = \dfrac{10d}{d - 10}$. As the object approaches $d = 10$ (the vertical asymptote), the image runs off to infinity; for very distant objects, $d_i$ approaches $10$ cm (the horizontal asymptote).

:::tip A graph can cross its horizontal asymptote
A vertical asymptote is never crossed, but a horizontal asymptote only describes the ends of the graph. For example, $y = \dfrac{x - 1}{x^2 - 4}$ crosses $y = 0$ at $x = 1$.
:::

## Intercepts and domain

- **Domain:** exclude every zero of the original denominator, including the ones that cancel.
- **$x$-intercepts:** the zeros of the numerator that are left after cancelling.
- **$y$-intercept:** $f(0)$, if $0$ is in the domain.

:::example Putting it together
Analyze $f(x) = \dfrac{2x^2 - 2x - 12}{x^2 + x - 2}$.
@step Factor: $f(x) = \dfrac{2(x - 3)(x + 2)}{(x + 2)(x - 1)} = \dfrac{2(x - 3)}{x - 1}$, $x \ne -2$.
@step Domain: $\{x \mid x \ne -2,\ x \ne 1,\ x \in \mathbb{R}\}$.
@step Hole at $x = -2$: $y = \dfrac{2(-5)}{-3} = \dfrac{10}{3}$. Vertical asymptote $x = 1$.
@step Horizontal asymptote: equal degrees, so $y = \dfrac{2}{1} = 2$.
@step $x$-intercept $3$ (not $-2$, which cancelled). $y$-intercept: $f(0) = \dfrac{-12}{-2} = 6$.
@answer Hole $\left(-2, \tfrac{10}{3}\right)$; $x = 1$; $y = 2$; $x$-intercept $3$; $y$-intercept $6$
:::

:::mistake A cancelled zero is not an intercept
The numerator $2x^2 - 2x - 12$ is $0$ at $x = -2$, but $f(-2)$ is undefined. The graph has a hole there, not an $x$-intercept.
:::

:::summary
- A rational function is a quotient of polynomials; it is undefined where the denominator is $0$.
- $y = \dfrac{a}{x - h} + k$ has asymptotes $x = h$ and $y = k$.
- Factor first: a cancelled factor gives a point of discontinuity, a remaining denominator factor gives a vertical asymptote.
- Horizontal asymptote: $y = 0$ if the numerator has the lower degree, the ratio of leading coefficients if the degrees are equal, none if the numerator has the higher degree.
- $x$-intercepts come from the simplified numerator; the domain comes from the original denominator.
:::
