You already know that $47 \div 5 = 9$ remainder $2$, because $47 = 5 \times 9 + 2$. Dividing a polynomial by a binomial $x - a$ works the same way, and the remainder turns out to be something you can predict without dividing at all.

## Long division of polynomials

:::definition Division statement
When a polynomial $P(x)$ is divided by $x - a$, the result is a **quotient** $Q(x)$ and a **remainder** $R$, a number, such that
$$P(x) = (x - a)Q(x) + R$$
The quotient has degree one less than $P(x)$.
:::

Long division of polynomials follows the same cycle as long division of numbers: divide the leading terms, multiply, subtract, bring down the next term, and repeat.

:::example Long division
Divide $x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x + 7$ by $x - 2$.
@step Divide the leading terms: $x^3 \div x = x^2$. Multiply: $x^2(x - 2) = x^3 - 2x^2$. Subtract from $x^3 + 2x^2$ to get $4x^2$, and bring down $-5x$: $4x^2 - 5x$.
@step $4x^2 \div x = 4x$. Multiply: $4x(x - 2) = 4x^2 - 8x$. Subtract to get $3x$, and bring down $+7$: $3x + 7$.
@step $3x \div x = 3$. Multiply: $3(x - 2) = 3x - 6$. Subtract: $7 - (-6) = 13$. There are no terms left to bring down.
@step The quotient is $x^2 + 4x + 3$ and the remainder is $13$.
@answer $x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x + 7 = (x - 2)(x^2 + 4x + 3) + 13$
:::

:::tip The division statement as a fraction
Dividing both sides by $x - a$ gives $\dfrac{P(x)}{x - a} = Q(x) + \dfrac{R}{x - a}$, with the restriction $x \ne a$. For the example, $\dfrac{x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x + 7}{x - 2} = x^2 + 4x + 3 + \dfrac{13}{x - 2}$, $x \ne 2$.
:::

## Synthetic division

**Synthetic division** is a shortcut for dividing by $x - a$ that keeps only the coefficients:

1. Write the coefficients of $P(x)$ in descending order of degree, with $0$ for any missing power of $x$. Write $a$ to the left.
2. Bring down the first coefficient.
3. Multiply it by $a$, write the product under the next coefficient, and add. Repeat to the last column.
4. The last number is the remainder. The other numbers are the coefficients of the quotient, starting one degree lower than $P(x)$.

Here is the long division above, done synthetically with $a = 2$:
$$\begin{array}{r|rrrr} 2 & 1 & 2 & -5 & 7 \\ & & 2 & 8 & 6 \\ \hline & 1 & 4 & 3 & \boxed{13} \end{array}$$
The bottom row reads $x^2 + 4x + 3$, remainder $13$, as before.

:::example Synthetic division with a missing term
Divide $2x^3 - 7x + 3$ by $x + 2$.
@step There is no $x^2$ term, so the coefficients are $2, 0, -7, 3$. Since $x + 2 = x - (-2)$, use $a = -2$.
@step Bring down $2$; $2(-2) = -4$, and $0 + (-4) = -4$; $-4(-2) = 8$, and $-7 + 8 = 1$; $1(-2) = -2$, and $3 + (-2) = 1$:
$$\begin{array}{r|rrrr} -2 & 2 & 0 & -7 & 3 \\ & & -4 & 8 & -2 \\ \hline & 2 & -4 & 1 & \boxed{1} \end{array}$$
@step The quotient is $2x^2 - 4x + 1$ and the remainder is $1$.
@answer $2x^3 - 7x + 3 = (x + 2)(2x^2 - 4x + 1) + 1$
:::

:::mistake Placeholders and the sign of a
Leaving out the $0$ for a missing power pushes every coefficient into the wrong column. And to divide by $x + 2$, use $a = -2$: the value of $x$ that makes the divisor zero.
:::

## The remainder theorem

:::definition Remainder theorem
When a polynomial $P(x)$ is divided by $x - a$, the remainder is $P(a)$.
:::

**Why it works.** The division statement $P(x) = (x - a)Q(x) + R$ is true for every value of $x$. Substitute $x = a$:
$$P(a) = (a - a)Q(a) + R = 0 \cdot Q(a) + R = R$$
Check it on the first example: $P(2) = 8 + 8 - 10 + 7 = 13$, which is the remainder we found by dividing.

:::example Using the remainder theorem
Find the remainder when $P(x) = x^4 - 3x^2 + 2x - 5$ is divided by $x + 1$.
@step $x + 1 = x - (-1)$, so the remainder is $P(-1)$.
@step $P(-1) = (-1)^4 - 3(-1)^2 + 2(-1) - 5 = 1 - 3 - 2 - 5 = -9$
@answer The remainder is $-9$.
:::

:::tip Which method?
If you only need the remainder, use the remainder theorem. If you also need the quotient, use synthetic division: its last number is $P(a)$ anyway.
:::

## Working backward: unknown coefficients

The remainder theorem turns information about a remainder into an equation. One unknown needs one remainder; two unknowns need two.

:::example One unknown
When $P(x) = x^3 + kx^2 - 4x + 1$ is divided by $x - 2$, the remainder is $5$. Find $k$.
@step By the remainder theorem, $P(2) = 5$.
@step $P(2) = (2)^3 + k(2)^2 - 4(2) + 1 = 8 + 4k - 8 + 1 = 4k + 1$
@step $4k + 1 = 5$, so $4k = 4$.
@answer $k = 1$
:::

:::example Two unknowns
When $P(x) = x^3 + mx^2 + nx - 2$ is divided by $x - 1$ the remainder is $4$, and when it is divided by $x + 1$ the remainder is $-4$. Find $m$ and $n$.
@step $P(1) = 1 + m + n - 2 = 4$, so $m + n = 5$.
@step $P(-1) = -1 + m - n - 2 = -4$, so $m - n = -1$.
@step Add the equations: $2m = 4$, so $m = 2$. Then $n = 5 - 2 = 3$.
@answer $m = 2$, $n = 3$
:::

The division statement also works backward: if you know the divisor, the quotient and the remainder, then $P(x) = (x - a)Q(x) + R$. Multiply out and add the remainder.

:::summary
- Division statement: $P(x) = (x - a)Q(x) + R$, where the quotient $Q(x)$ has degree one less than $P(x)$.
- Synthetic division: coefficients in descending order with $0$ placeholders, $a$ on the left; bring down, multiply, add. The last number is the remainder.
- Remainder theorem: the remainder on division by $x - a$ is $P(a)$. For $x + 2$, use $a = -2$.
- A given remainder gives an equation for an unknown coefficient; two remainders give a system for two unknowns.
:::
