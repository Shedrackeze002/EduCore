What happens to a graph when you take the square root of every output? Values between 0 and 1 grow, values above 1 shrink, and negative values disappear completely. That one idea explains every graph of $y = \sqrt{f(x)}$.

## From y = f(x) to y = √f(x)

For every point $(x, y)$ on $y = f(x)$ with $y \ge 0$, the graph of $y = \sqrt{f(x)}$ has the point $(x, \sqrt{y})$. Where $f(x) < 0$ there is no point at all.

Compare $y$ and $\sqrt{y}$:

- If $y = 0$ or $y = 1$, then $\sqrt{y} = y$: these are the **invariant points**.
- If $0 < y < 1$, then $\sqrt{y} > y$: the graph of $\sqrt{f(x)}$ is above the graph of $f$.
- If $y > 1$, then $\sqrt{y} < y$: the graph of $\sqrt{f(x)}$ is below the graph of $f$.

## Domain and range

The domain of $y = \sqrt{f(x)}$ is every $x$ with $f(x) \ge 0$. The range is the set of square roots of the non-negative values of $f$.

:::example Domain and range
State the domain and range of $y = \sqrt{2x - 4}$.
@step $2x - 4 \ge 0$ gives $x \ge 2$.
@step On that domain, $2x - 4$ takes every value $\ge 0$, so its square root takes every value $\ge 0$.
@answer Domain $\{x \mid x \ge 2,\ x \in \mathbb{R}\}$; range $\{y \mid y \ge 0,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$
:::

:::check m301-sqrt-of-function 2
:::

## Square roots of quadratic functions

For a quadratic, find the zeros and the vertex first.

If the parabola opens up and crosses the $x$-axis, $f(x) \ge 0$ outside the zeros, so $y = \sqrt{f(x)}$ has two branches. For $f(x) = x^2 - 4$, the domain is $\{x \mid x \le -2 \text{ or } x \ge 2,\ x \in \mathbb{R}\}$ and the range is $\{y \mid y \ge 0,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$.

:::mistake √f(x) is not |f(x)|
Taking the absolute value **reflects** the negative part of a graph above the $x$-axis. Taking the square root **removes** it: where $f(x) < 0$, $\sqrt{f(x)}$ is undefined. The graph of $y = \sqrt{x^2 - 4}$ has no points at all between $x = -2$ and $x = 2$.
:::

Plot the invariant points where $f(x) = 0$ and $f(x) = 1$. Mark where $f$ is negative (no graph there). Then take square roots of a few easy values, such as $4 \to 2$ and $9 \to 3$, and join the points with a smooth curve that meets the $x$-axis vertically.
:::

:::summary
- $(x, y)$ on $y = f(x)$ with $y \ge 0$ corresponds to $(x, \sqrt{y})$ on $y = \sqrt{f(x)}$.
- Invariant points occur where $f(x) = 0$ or $f(x) = 1$.
- $\sqrt{f(x)}$ is above $f$ where $0 < f(x) < 1$ and below it where $f(x) > 1$.
- The domain is where $f(x) \ge 0$; the range is the square roots of the non-negative values of $f$.
:::
