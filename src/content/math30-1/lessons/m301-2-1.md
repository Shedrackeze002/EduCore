In shallow water, the speed of a wave can be modelled by $v = \sqrt{9.8d}$, where $d$ is the depth in metres. Formulas with square roots like this describe pendulums, falling objects and stopping distances, and their graphs are all transformations of one curve: $y = \sqrt{x}$.

## The base radical function

:::definition Radical function
A function with the independent variable under a radical sign, such as $y = \sqrt{x}$ or $y = 2\sqrt{3 - x} + 1$. In this unit the radical is a square root.
:::

The graph of $y = \sqrt{x}$ starts at the endpoint $(0, 0)$ and rises ever more slowly. Perfect squares give convenient key points.

| $x$ | $0$ | $1$ | $4$ | $9$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $y = \sqrt{x}$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ |

A square root of a negative number is not real, so the domain is $\{x \mid x \ge 0,\ x \in \mathbb{R}\}$. A principal square root is never negative, so the range is $\{y \mid y \ge 0,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$.

## Transforming y = √x

The graph of $y = a\sqrt{b(x - h)} + k$ is the graph of $y = \sqrt{x}$ transformed by the mapping $(x, y) \to \left(\frac{x}{b} + h,\ ay + k\right)$. The endpoint $(0, 0)$ moves to $(h, k)$.

:::example Graphing with key points
Graph $y = 2\sqrt{-(x - 3)} + 1$.
@step $a = 2$, $b = -1$, $h = 3$, $k = 1$: stretch vertically by $2$, reflect in the $y$-axis, translate 3 right and 1 up.
@step Mapping: $(x, y) \to (-x + 3,\ 2y + 1)$.
@step $(0, 0) \to (3, 1)$, $(1, 1) \to (2, 3)$, $(4, 2) \to (-1, 5)$, $(9, 3) \to (-6, 7)$.
@answer Endpoint $(3, 1)$; the graph opens to the left through $(2, 3)$ and $(-1, 5)$.
:::

## Domain and range

The endpoint $(h, k)$ and the signs of $a$ and $b$ decide everything:

- $b > 0$: the graph opens to the right, domain $x \ge h$. $b < 0$: it opens to the left, domain $x \le h$.
- $a > 0$: the graph rises from the endpoint, range $y \ge k$. $a < 0$: it falls, range $y \le k$.

:::example Reading domain and range
State the domain and range of $y = -3\sqrt{2(x + 1)} + 4$.
@step The endpoint is $(-1, 4)$.
@step $b = 2 > 0$, so the domain is $\{x \mid x \ge -1,\ x \in \mathbb{R}\}$, or $[-1, \infty)$.
@step $a = -3 < 0$, so the range is $\{y \mid y \le 4,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$, or $(-\infty, 4]$.
@answer Domain $[-1, \infty)$; range $(-\infty, 4]$
:::

:::mistake A hidden reflection
For $y = \sqrt{6 - 2x}$, the domain is not $x \ge 3$. Factor the radicand: $6 - 2x = -2(x - 3)$, so $b < 0$ and the graph opens to the left. Solving $6 - 2x \ge 0$ gives $-2x \ge -6$, so $x \le 3$ (dividing by a negative reverses the inequality).
:::

:::tip Two stretches, one graph
$\sqrt{4x} = 2\sqrt{x}$ for $x \ge 0$, so a horizontal stretch by a factor of $\frac{1}{4}$ gives the same graph as a vertical stretch by a factor of $2$. This is why one radical graph can have more than one correct equation.
:::

:::summary
- $y = \sqrt{x}$ has endpoint $(0, 0)$, domain $x \ge 0$ and range $y \ge 0$; use the key points $(0, 0)$, $(1, 1)$, $(4, 2)$ and $(9, 3)$.
- $y = a\sqrt{b(x - h)} + k$ maps $(x, y) \to \left(\frac{x}{b} + h,\ ay + k\right)$; the endpoint is $(h, k)$.
- The sign of $b$ gives the domain ($x \ge h$ or $x \le h$); the sign of $a$ gives the range ($y \ge k$ or $y \le k$).
- Factor the radicand before reading $h$.
:::
