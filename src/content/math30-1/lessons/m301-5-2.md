Real waves are rarely as simple as $y = \sin x$. A tide is taller, a Ferris wheel is raised off the ground, and a daylight cycle starts in March, not January. Four parameters stretch and slide the basic curve to fit any of them.

## The four parameters

:::definition Sinusoidal function
$$y = a\sin b(x - c) + d \qquad \text{or} \qquad y = a\cos b(x - c) + d$$
- $|a|$ is the **amplitude** (a vertical stretch); $a < 0$ also reflects the graph in the $x$-axis.
- $\dfrac{2\pi}{|b|}$ is the **period** (a horizontal stretch by a factor of $\frac{1}{|b|}$).
- $c$ is the **phase shift**, a horizontal translation: right when $c > 0$, left when $c < 0$.
- $d$ is the **vertical displacement**. The **midline** is $y = d$, the maximum is $d + |a|$ and the minimum is $d - |a|$.
:::

## Factor b first

The phase shift is read from $b(x - c)$, so $b$ must be factored out of the brackets before you can see $c$.

:::example Hidden phase shift
For $y = 2\sin(3x - \pi) + 4$, determine the period, phase shift and range.
@step Factor out $3$: $3x - \pi = 3\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$, so $y = 2\sin 3\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) + 4$.
@step Period $= \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$; phase shift $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ to the right.
@step The graph runs from $4 - 2 = 2$ to $4 + 2 = 6$.
@answer Period $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, phase shift $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ right, range $\{y \mid 2 \le y \le 6,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$
:::

:::example In degrees
State the period and phase shift of $y = \cos(2x + 60^\circ)$.
@step $2x + 60^\circ = 2(x + 30^\circ)$
@answer Period $\dfrac{360^\circ}{2} = 180^\circ$; phase shift $30^\circ$ to the left
:::

:::mistake Reading the shift before factoring
In $y = 2\sin(3x - \pi) + 4$ the graph does **not** move $\pi$ to the right. The shift is $\frac{\pi}{3}$, because $3x - \pi = 0$ when $x = \frac{\pi}{3}$.
:::

:::check m301-sinusoid-equation 2
:::

## Writing an equation from a graph

Read the features in this order:

1. $a = \dfrac{\max - \min}{2}$ and $d = \dfrac{\max + \min}{2}$.
2. Measure the period, then $b = \dfrac{2\pi}{\text{period}}$.
3. For cosine with $a > 0$, $c$ is the $x$-value of a maximum. For sine with $a > 0$, $c$ is where the curve crosses the midline going up.

:::example Cosine and sine forms
Write an equation for the graph above as a cosine function and as a sine function.
@step Maximum $2$, minimum $-4$: $a = \dfrac{2 - (-4)}{2} = 3$ and $d = \dfrac{2 + (-4)}{2} = -1$.
@step Maximums at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$: the period is $\pi$, so $b = \dfrac{2\pi}{\pi} = 2$.
@step Cosine: a maximum at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ gives $c = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Sine: the curve crosses the midline $y = -1$ going up at $x = 0$, so $c = 0$.
@answer $y = 3\cos 2\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) - 1$, or equally $y = 3\sin 2x - 1$

## Writing an equation from a description

:::example From characteristics
A sine function has an amplitude of $4$, a period of $4\pi$, a phase shift of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to the right and a vertical displacement of $3$ up. Write its equation.
@step $a = 4$, $d = 3$ and $c = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
@step $b = \dfrac{2\pi}{4\pi} = \dfrac{1}{2}$
@answer $y = 4\sin\dfrac{1}{2}\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) + 3$, typed as `4sin((1/2)(x - pi/2)) + 3`
:::

:::summary
- In $y = a\sin b(x - c) + d$: amplitude $|a|$, period $\frac{2\pi}{|b|}$, phase shift $c$, midline $y = d$.
- The range is $d - |a| \le y \le d + |a|$.
- Factor $b$ out of the brackets before reading the phase shift.
- From a graph: $a$ and $d$ from the maximum and minimum, $b$ from the period, $c$ from a maximum (cosine) or an upward midline crossing (sine).
:::
