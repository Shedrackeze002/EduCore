A mirror flips an image, and a zoom lens stretches it. Graphs can be flipped and stretched too: reflections and stretches change the orientation or the proportions of a graph while keeping its basic shape.

## Reflections in the axes

:::definition Reflection
A transformation that flips a graph over a line, called the line of reflection. Each point and its image are the same distance from the line, on opposite sides.
:::

- $y = -f(x)$ is a reflection in the **$x$-axis**: $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$. Every output changes sign.
- $y = f(-x)$ is a reflection in the **$y$-axis**: $(x, y) \to (-x, y)$. Every input changes sign.

The grey graph below is $y = f(x)$, through $(-4, 1)$, $(-2, 3)$, $(1, 3)$ and $(3, -1)$. The coloured graph is $y = -f(x)$.

:::definition Invariant point
A point that does not move under a transformation: it is its own image.
:::

In a reflection in the $x$-axis, $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$ leaves a point unchanged only when $y = 0$. So the invariant points are the $x$-intercepts, such as $(2.5, 0)$ above. In a reflection in the $y$-axis, the invariant points are on the $y$-axis.

:::example Reflecting a point
The point $(-5, 2)$ is on the graph of $y = f(x)$. Find the corresponding points on $y = -f(x)$ and on $y = f(-x)$.
@step $y = -f(x)$: $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$, so $(-5, 2) \to (-5, -2)$.
@step $y = f(-x)$: $(x, y) \to (-x, y)$, so $(-5, 2) \to (5, 2)$.
@answer $(-5, -2)$ on $y = -f(x)$; $(5, 2)$ on $y = f(-x)$
:::

:::check m301-reflect 1
:::

:::example Reflecting an equation
Given $f(x) = x^2 - 3x + 1$, write $y = -f(x)$ and $y = f(-x)$.
@step $y = -f(x) = -(x^2 - 3x + 1) = -x^2 + 3x - 1$
@step $y = f(-x) = (-x)^2 - 3(-x) + 1 = x^2 + 3x + 1$
@answer $y = -x^2 + 3x - 1$ and $y = x^2 + 3x + 1$
:::

## Vertical stretches

:::definition Stretch
A transformation that multiplies the distance of every point from an axis by a constant, the stretch factor. A factor greater than 1 pulls the graph away from the axis; a factor between 0 and 1 squeezes it toward the axis.
:::

$y = af(x)$ is a **vertical stretch by a factor of $|a|$ about the $x$-axis**: $(x, y) \to (x, ay)$. If $a < 0$, the graph is also reflected in the $x$-axis.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":-8,"xMax":8,"yMin":-8,"yMax":8},"polylines":[{"points":[[-4,1],[-2,3],[1,3],[3,-1]],"color":"muted"},{"points":[[-4,2],[-2,6],[1,6],[3,-2]],"color":"brand"}],"points":[{"x":-2,"y":3,"color":"muted","label":"(-2, 3)"},{"x":-2,"y":6,"label":"(-2, 6)"}]}
:::

Above, $y = 2f(x)$ doubles every $y$-coordinate: $(-2, 3) \to (-2, 6)$. Points on the $x$-axis do not move.

## Horizontal stretches

$y = f(bx)$ is a **horizontal stretch by a factor of $\frac{1}{|b|}$ about the $y$-axis**: $(x, y) \to \left(\frac{x}{b}, y\right)$. If $b < 0$, the graph is also reflected in the $y$-axis.

Why the reciprocal? The image reaches the output $f(6)$ when $bx = 6$, that is, at $x = \frac{6}{b}$. For $y = f(2x)$ that happens at $x = 3$: everything arrives twice as soon, so the graph is squeezed to half its width.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":-8,"xMax":8,"yMin":-8,"yMax":8},"polylines":[{"points":[[-4,1],[-2,3],[1,3],[3,-1]],"color":"muted"},{"points":[[-2,1],[-1,3],[0.5,3],[1.5,-1]],"color":"brand"}],"points":[{"x":-4,"y":1,"color":"muted","label":"(-4, 1)"},{"x":-2,"y":1,"label":"(-2, 1)"}]}
:::

:::example Stretching a point
The point $(6, -4)$ is on the graph of $y = f(x)$. Find the corresponding point on $y = f(3x)$, on $y = \frac{1}{2}f(x)$ and on $y = f\left(\frac{1}{2}x\right)$.
@step $y = f(3x)$: divide $x$ by $3$: $(2, -4)$, a horizontal stretch by a factor of $\frac{1}{3}$.
@step $y = \frac{1}{2}f(x)$: multiply $y$ by $\frac{1}{2}$: $(6, -2)$.
@step $y = f\left(\frac{1}{2}x\right)$: divide $x$ by $\frac{1}{2}$, that is, multiply by $2$: $(12, -4)$.
@answer $(2, -4)$, $(6, -2)$ and $(12, -4)$
:::

:::mistake The horizontal factor
$y = f(3x)$ is a horizontal stretch by a factor of $\frac{1}{3}$, **not** $3$. Numbers inside the brackets act on $x$ "backwards": multiplying $x$ by $3$ inside the function divides every $x$-coordinate of the graph by $3$.
:::

:::check m301-stretch 2
:::

## Invariant points of stretches and reflections

| Transformation | Mapping | Invariant points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $y = -f(x)$ or $y = af(x)$ | $(x, y) \to (x, ay)$ | on the $x$-axis ($y = 0$) |
| $y = f(-x)$ or $y = f(bx)$ | $(x, y) \to \left(\frac{x}{b}, y\right)$ | on the $y$-axis ($x = 0$) |

:::example Invariant points
Let $f(x) = x^2 - x - 6$. Find the invariant points for $y = 4f(x)$ and for $y = f(2x)$.
@step $f(x) = (x - 3)(x + 2)$, so the $x$-intercepts are $(-2, 0)$ and $(3, 0)$; the $y$-intercept is $(0, -6)$.
@step A vertical stretch keeps the points with $y = 0$: $(-2, 0)$ and $(3, 0)$.
@step A horizontal stretch keeps the point with $x = 0$: $(0, -6)$.
@answer $y = 4f(x)$: $(-2, 0)$ and $(3, 0)$; $y = f(2x)$: $(0, -6)$
:::

:::check m301-reflect 3
:::

:::check m301-stretch 3
:::

:::summary
- $y = -f(x)$ reflects in the $x$-axis; $y = f(-x)$ reflects in the $y$-axis.
- $y = af(x)$: vertical stretch by $|a|$, $(x, y) \to (x, ay)$.
- $y = f(bx)$: horizontal stretch by $\frac{1}{|b|}$, $(x, y) \to \left(\frac{x}{b}, y\right)$.
- Vertical changes leave $x$-intercepts invariant; horizontal changes leave the $y$-intercept invariant.
:::
