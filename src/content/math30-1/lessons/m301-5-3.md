Sine and cosine stay between $-1$ and $1$, but the tangent of an angle can be any real number. Its graph is a row of identical branches separated by vertical asymptotes.

## Tangent on the unit circle

For $P(\theta) = (x, y)$ on the unit circle,
$$\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} = \frac{y}{x}$$
That is the **slope of the terminal arm**, the line from the origin through $P(\theta)$.

- When $y = 0$ ($\theta = 0, \pi, 2\pi, \ldots$) the arm is horizontal, so $\tan\theta = 0$.
- When $x = 0$ ($\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2}, \ldots$) the arm is vertical, so $\tan\theta$ is undefined.
- As $\theta$ approaches $\frac{\pi}{2}$ from below, the arm gets steeper and $\tan\theta$ grows without bound.

:::example Tangent as a slope
The terminal arm of $\theta$ meets the unit circle at $P\left(-\dfrac{8}{17}, \dfrac{15}{17}\right)$. Determine $\tan\theta$.
@step $\tan\theta = \dfrac{y}{x} = \dfrac{15/17}{-8/17} = \dfrac{15}{-8}$
@answer $\tan\theta = -\dfrac{15}{8}$, the slope of the terminal arm
:::

:::definition Asymptote
An **asymptote** is a line that a graph approaches more and more closely without reaching it. The graph of $y = \tan x$ has a vertical asymptote at every value where $\cos x = 0$.
:::

The dashed lines are the asymptotes $x = \pm\frac{\pi}{2}$ and $x = \pm\frac{3\pi}{2}$. Between them, each branch rises from $-\infty$ through a zero to $+\infty$.

| Feature | $y = \tan x$ |
|---|---|
| Domain | $\left\{x \mid x \ne \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi,\ x \in \mathbb{R},\ n \in \mathbb{I}\right\}$ |
| Range | $\{y \mid y \in \mathbb{R}\}$ |
| Period | $\pi$ (or $180^\circ$) |
| Zeros | $x = n\pi$, $n \in \mathbb{I}$ |
| Asymptotes | $x = \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$, $n \in \mathbb{I}$ |
| Amplitude | none: there is no maximum or minimum |

The period is $\pi$, not $2\pi$: the terminal arms of $\theta$ and $\theta + \pi$ point in opposite directions along the same line, so they have the same slope.

:::mistake Treating tangent like sine
$y = \tan x$ has no amplitude and its range is all real numbers, not $-1 \le y \le 1$. Its period is $\pi$, half the period of sine and cosine.
