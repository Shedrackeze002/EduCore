A cup of tea left on a counter cools toward room temperature, not toward $0\,^{\circ}\text{C}$. Its graph is an exponential decay curve that has been moved up so that its asymptote sits at room temperature. Transformations let one family of curves model all of these situations.

## The transformation equation

Every transformation you have studied applies to exponential functions. Starting from $y = c^{x}$, the general transformed function is
$$y = a(c)^{b(x - h)} + k$$

| Parameter | Effect on the graph of $y = c^{x}$ |
|---|---|
| $\lvert a \rvert$ | vertical stretch by a factor of $\lvert a \rvert$ |
| $a < 0$ | reflection in the $x$-axis |
| $\lvert b \rvert$ | horizontal stretch by a factor of $\frac{1}{\lvert b \rvert}$ |
| $b < 0$ | reflection in the $y$-axis |
| $h$ | horizontal translation ($h > 0$ right, $h < 0$ left) |
| $k$ | vertical translation ($k > 0$ up, $k < 0$ down) |

Only $k$ moves the horizontal asymptote: it becomes $y = k$.

:::example Describe the transformations
Describe how $y = -2^{x + 2} + 3$ is related to $y = 2^{x}$, and state its asymptote, domain and range.
@step Compare with $y = a(2)^{x - h} + k$: $a = -1$, $h = -2$, $k = 3$.
@step $a = -1$: reflection in the $x$-axis. $h = -2$: translation 2 units left. $k = 3$: translation 3 units up.
@step The asymptote $y = 0$ moves up to $y = 3$. The reflection puts the graph below it.
@answer Reflect in the $x$-axis, then translate 2 left and 3 up. Asymptote $y = 3$; domain $\{x \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$; range $\{y \mid y < 3,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$.
:::

The parent $y = 2^{x}$ is grey; $y = -2^{x + 2} + 3$ is purple. The points $(1, 2)$ and $(2, 4)$ map to $(-1, 1)$ and $(0, -1)$.

## Image points

Each point $(x, y)$ on $y = c^{x}$ moves to a point on $y = a(c)^{b(x - h)} + k$ by the mapping
$$(x, y) \to \left(\frac{x}{b} + h,\ ay + k\right)$$

Stretches and reflections are applied first, then the translations.

:::example Find an image point
The point $(2, 4)$ is on $y = 2^{x}$. Find its image on $y = -3(2)^{\frac{1}{2}(x + 1)} + 5$.
@step Read the parameters: $a = -3$, $b = \frac{1}{2}$, $h = -1$, $k = 5$.
@step $x$: $\dfrac{2}{\frac{1}{2}} + (-1) = 4 - 1 = 3$.
@step $y$: $-3(4) + 5 = -7$.
@step Check: $-3(2)^{\frac{1}{2}(3 + 1)} + 5 = -3(2)^{2} + 5 = -7$ ✓
@answer $(3, -7)$
:::

:::mistake The horizontal stretch factor
A value of $b = \frac{1}{2}$ is a horizontal stretch by a factor of $2$, not $\frac{1}{2}$: the $x$-coordinates are **divided** by $b$. Similarly $b = 3$ compresses the graph to one third of its width.
:::

:::

## Writing the equation

To write an equation from a description, read off each parameter and substitute into $y = a(c)^{b(x - h)} + k$.

:::example From a description
The graph of $y = 3^{x}$ is stretched vertically by a factor of $2$, stretched horizontally by a factor of $\frac{1}{2}$, reflected in the $x$-axis and translated 4 units left and 1 unit up. Write the equation.
@step Vertical stretch by $2$ with a reflection in the $x$-axis: $a = -2$.
@step Horizontal stretch by $\frac{1}{2}$: $b = \dfrac{1}{\frac{1}{2}} = 2$.
@step Translations: $h = -4$ and $k = 1$.
@answer $y = -2(3)^{2(x + 4)} + 1$
:::

To write an equation from a graph, use the asymptote for $k$ and the points to find $a$ and $h$. The vertical distance from the asymptote is multiplied by the base each time $x$ increases by $1$.

:::example From a graph
The graph above is a transformation of $y = 2^{x}$. Write its equation.
@step The asymptote is $y = -2$, so $k = -2$.
@step The points $(1, 0)$, $(2, 2)$ and $(3, 6)$ are $2$, $4$ and $8$ units above the asymptote. The distance doubles each step, which matches the base $2$.
@step Take $h = 1$. At $x = h$ the point is $a$ units from the asymptote, so $a = 2$.
@step Check $(3, 6)$: $2(2)^{3 - 1} - 2 = 8 - 2 = 6$ ✓
@answer $y = 2(2)^{x - 1} - 2$
:::

:::tip More than one correct equation
Because $2(2)^{x - 1} = 2^{1} \cdot 2^{x - 1} = 2^{x}$, the same graph is also $y = 2^{x} - 2$. Choosing a different point as the "starting point" changes $a$ and $h$ but not the graph.
:::

:::summary
- $y = a(c)^{b(x - h)} + k$: $a$ and $b$ stretch and reflect; $h$ and $k$ translate.
- The horizontal asymptote is $y = k$. The range is $y > k$ when $a > 0$ and $y < k$ when $a < 0$; the domain is always all real numbers.
- Mapping rule: $(x, y) \to \left(\frac{x}{b} + h,\ ay + k\right)$.
- From a graph, find $k$ from the asymptote, then use the distances from the asymptote to find $a$ and $h$.
:::
