A physics student drops a stone from a bridge and times its fall at 2.5 s. If the model $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{d}{4.9}}$ describes the fall, how high is the bridge? Answering means solving a radical equation, and a graph shows both the answer and the traps.

## Two graphical methods

To solve an equation such as $\sqrt{x + 3} = 2$ graphically, use either method:

1. **Intersection:** graph $y = \sqrt{x + 3}$ and $y = 2$. The solution is the $x$-coordinate of each intersection point.
2. **Related function:** move everything to one side and graph $y = \sqrt{x + 3} - 2$. The solutions are its $x$-intercepts.

## Extraneous roots

Algebraically, you isolate the radical and square both sides. Squaring can create roots that do not satisfy the original equation.

:::definition Extraneous root
A value produced by the solving process (such as squaring both sides) that does not satisfy the original equation.
:::

:::example Squaring both sides
Solve $\sqrt{x + 7} = x + 1$.
@step Square: $x + 7 = x^2 + 2x + 1$, so $x^2 + x - 6 = 0$.
@step Factor: $(x + 3)(x - 2) = 0$, so $x = -3$ or $x = 2$.
@step Check $x = 2$: $\sqrt{9} = 3$ and $2 + 1 = 3$ ✓.
@step Check $x = -3$: $\sqrt{4} = 2$ but $-3 + 1 = -2$ ✗, so $x = -3$ is extraneous.
@answer $x = 2$
:::

:::

:::example Rounding the solution
Solve $\sqrt{x + 4} = x - 1$, to the nearest hundredth.
@step The graphs intersect once, near $x = 3.8$.
@step Square: $x + 4 = x^2 - 2x + 1$, so $x^2 - 3x - 3 = 0$.
@step $x = \dfrac{3 \pm \sqrt{21}}{2}$, so $x \approx 3.79$ or $x \approx -0.79$.
@step At $x \approx -0.79$ the right side $x - 1$ is negative, so that root is extraneous.
@answer $x \approx 3.79$
:::

:::example The falling stone
Using $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{d}{4.9}}$, find the height $d$ when $t = 2.5$ s.
@step $\sqrt{\dfrac{d}{4.9}} = 2.5$
@step Square both sides: $\dfrac{d}{4.9} = 6.25$, so $d = 30.625$.
@answer The bridge is about $30.6$ m high.
:::

:::tip Count first
Before you solve, count the intersection points on the graph. If the algebra gives more roots than the graph shows, the extra ones are extraneous.
:::

:::summary
- Solve $A = B$ graphically from the intersections of $y = A$ and $y = B$, or from the $x$-intercepts of $y = A - B$.
- Squaring both sides can introduce extraneous roots; check every root in the original equation.
- A square root is never negative, so a root that makes the other side negative is extraneous.
- Use the graph to predict the number of solutions and the formula for accurate values.
