Before electronic calculators, scientists and engineers multiplied large numbers with tables of logarithms and slide rules. The trick was a law you will prove in this lesson: the logarithm of a product is the sum of the logarithms, so multiplication becomes addition.

## The product and quotient laws

For $c > 0$, $c \ne 1$, and $M, N > 0$:

:::definition Product law
$\log_{c} MN = \log_{c} M + \log_{c} N$
:::

:::definition Quotient law
$\log_{c} \dfrac{M}{N} = \log_{c} M - \log_{c} N$
:::

**Proof.** Let $m = \log_{c} M$ and $n = \log_{c} N$, so $M = c^{m}$ and $N = c^{n}$. Then $MN = c^{m}c^{n} = c^{m + n}$, so $\log_{c} MN = m + n = \log_{c} M + \log_{c} N$. Similarly $\dfrac{M}{N} = c^{m - n}$, which gives the quotient law. The laws of logarithms are the exponent laws written in logarithmic form.

:::example Combine, then evaluate
Evaluate $\log_{2} 40 - \log_{2} 5$ and $\log 4 + \log 25$.
@step Neither $\log_{2} 40$ nor $\log_{2} 5$ is a whole number, but $\log_{2} 40 - \log_{2} 5 = \log_{2} \dfrac{40}{5} = \log_{2} 8 = 3$.
@step $\log 4 + \log 25 = \log (4 \times 25) = \log 100 = 2$.
@answer $3$ and $2$
:::

:::mistake Laws that do not exist
$\log(M + N)$ is **not** $\log M + \log N$, and $\dfrac{\log M}{\log N}$ is **not** $\log \dfrac{M}{N}$. For example, $\log 10 + \log 10 = 2$, but $\log 20 \approx 1.301$.
:::

## The power law and change of base

:::definition Power law
$\log_{c} M^{P} = P\log_{c} M$
:::

**Proof.** Let $m = \log_{c} M$, so $M = c^{m}$. Then $M^{P} = \left(c^{m}\right)^{P} = c^{mP}$, so $\log_{c} M^{P} = mP = P\log_{c} M$.

:::example Use the power law first
Evaluate $2\log_{6} 3 + \log_{6} 4$.
@step Power law: $2\log_{6} 3 = \log_{6} 3^{2} = \log_{6} 9$.
@step Product law: $\log_{6} 9 + \log_{6} 4 = \log_{6} 36$.
@answer $2$, because $6^{2} = 36$.
:::

Calculators have keys only for common logarithms (and natural logarithms, which you will meet in calculus). The **change of base formula** lets you evaluate a logarithm with any base:
$$\log_{c} x = \frac{\log x}{\log c}$$

**Proof.** Let $y = \log_{c} x$, so $c^{y} = x$. Take the common logarithm of both sides: $\log c^{y} = \log x$. By the power law, $y\log c = \log x$, so $y = \dfrac{\log x}{\log c}$.

:::example Change of base
Evaluate $\log_{3} 50$ to the nearest hundredth.
@step $\log_{3} 50 = \dfrac{\log 50}{\log 3} \approx \dfrac{1.6990}{0.4771}$
@step $\approx 3.56$. Check: $3^{3.56} \approx 50$, and $3^{3} = 27 < 50 < 81 = 3^{4}$ ✓
@answer $\log_{3} 50 \approx 3.56$
:::

## Expanding and condensing

The laws work in both directions. **Expanding** splits one logarithm into several; **condensing** combines several into one. Assume every variable is positive so that each logarithm is defined.

:::example Expand
Expand $\log_{3} \dfrac{x^{2}\sqrt{y}}{z^{4}}$.
@step Quotient and product laws: $\log_{3} x^{2} + \log_{3} \sqrt{y} - \log_{3} z^{4}$.
@step Power law, with $\sqrt{y} = y^{\frac{1}{2}}$:
@answer $2\log_{3} x + \frac{1}{2}\log_{3} y - 4\log_{3} z$
:::

:::example Condense
Write $3\log x - \frac{1}{2}\log y + \log z$ as a single logarithm.
@step Power law: $\log x^{3} - \log \sqrt{y} + \log z$.
@step Added terms multiply in the numerator; the subtracted term divides.
@answer $\log \dfrac{x^{3}z}{\sqrt{y}}$
:::

:::example In terms of given values
Given $\log_{5} 2 = p$ and $\log_{5} 3 = q$, write $\log_{5} 72$ and $\log_{5} 30$ in terms of $p$ and $q$.
@step $72 = 2^{3} \times 3^{2}$, so $\log_{5} 72 = 3\log_{5} 2 + 2\log_{5} 3 = 3p + 2q$.
@step $30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5$, so $\log_{5} 30 = \log_{5} 2 + \log_{5} 3 + \log_{5} 5 = p + q + 1$.
@answer $\log_{5} 72 = 3p + 2q$ and $\log_{5} 30 = p + q + 1$
:::

:::tip Factor into primes
To write a logarithm in terms of given values, factor the argument into the given numbers (and the base, whose logarithm is $1$). A fraction gives negative coefficients; a square root gives a factor of $\frac{1}{2}$.
:::

:::summary
- Product law: $\log_{c} MN = \log_{c} M + \log_{c} N$. Quotient law: $\log_{c} \frac{M}{N} = \log_{c} M - \log_{c} N$. Power law: $\log_{c} M^{P} = P\log_{c} M$.
- Each law comes from an exponent law; the arguments must be positive.
- Change of base: $\log_{c} x = \dfrac{\log x}{\log c}$.
- To evaluate a combination, condense it into one logarithm first.
:::
