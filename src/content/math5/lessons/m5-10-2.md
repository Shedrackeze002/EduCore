A fence goes around a garden, and grass covers the inside. The fence is the **perimeter** and the grass is the **area**. In this lesson you will find both for rectangles.

## The area of a rectangle

A rectangle $5\ \text{cm}$ long and $3\ \text{cm}$ wide is covered by $3$ rows of $5$ square centimetres.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":8,"yMin":0,"yMax":5,"step":1},"hideTickLabels":true,"hideAxes":true,"polylines":[{"points":[[1,1],[6,1],[6,4],[1,4],[1,1]],"fill":true}],"caption":"A 5 by 3 rectangle on centimetre grid paper"}
:::

$$3 \times 5 = 15, \text{ so the area is } 15\ \text{cm}^2$$

You do not need to count every square. Multiply:

$$\text{Area of a rectangle} = \text{length} \times \text{width}$$

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":340,"yMin":0,"yMax":188,"step":340},"hideTickLabels":true,"polylines":[{"points":[[31.85,35.3],[261.85,35.3],[261.85,179.05],[31.85,179.05],[31.85,35.3]],"fill":true}],"caption":"A rectangle, 24 m by 15 m","points":[{"x":124.45,"y":12,"label":"24 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":271.85,"y":94.88,"label":"15 m","color":"muted","open":true}]}
:::

:::example A community garden
A community garden is a rectangle $24\ \text{m}$ long and $15\ \text{m}$ wide. What is its area?
@step Area $=$ length $\times$ width $= 24 \times 15$
@step $24 \times 10 = 240$ and $24 \times 5 = 120$. Add: $240 + 120 = 360$.
@answer The area is $360\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

## Finding a missing side

If you know the area and one side, divide to find the other side.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":340,"yMin":0,"yMax":120,"step":340},"hideTickLabels":true,"polylines":[{"points":[[33.35,35.3],[263.35,35.3],[263.35,111.2],[33.35,111.2],[33.35,35.3]],"fill":true}],"caption":"A rectangle, ? cm by 6 cm","points":[{"x":125.95,"y":12,"label":"? cm","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":270.35,"y":34.95,"label":"6 cm","color":"muted","open":true}]}
:::

:::example A paper banner
A rectangular banner has an area of $156\ \text{cm}^2$. It is $6\ \text{cm}$ wide. How long is it?
@step Length $\times 6 = 156$, so length $= 156 \div 6$.
@step $156 \div 6 = 26$
@step Check: $26 \times 6 = 156$ ✓
@answer The banner is $26\ \text{cm}$ long.
:::

## Perimeter and area

:::definition Perimeter
The **perimeter** is the distance all the way around a shape. Add the lengths of all the sides. Perimeter is a length, so it uses $\text{cm}$ or $\text{m}$.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":340,"yMin":0,"yMax":212,"step":340},"hideTickLabels":true,"polylines":[{"points":[[56.75,35.3],[286.75,35.3],[286.75,179.05],[56.75,179.05],[56.75,35.3]],"fill":true}],"caption":"A rectangle, 8 m by 5 m","points":[{"x":152.95,"y":12,"label":"8 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":293.75,"y":94.88,"label":"5 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":152.95,"y":186.75,"label":"8 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":21.15,"y":94.88,"label":"5 m","color":"muted","open":true}]}
:::

:::example A patio
A patio is a rectangle $8\ \text{m}$ by $5\ \text{m}$. Find its perimeter and its area.
@step Perimeter $= 8 + 5 + 8 + 5 = 26$, so it is $26\ \text{m}$.
@step Area $= 8 \times 5 = 40$, so it is $40\ \text{m}^2$.
@answer Perimeter $26\ \text{m}$, area $40\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Mixing up perimeter and area
Perimeter is the fence around the outside: add the sides and use $\text{m}$. Area is the grass inside: multiply and use $\text{m}^2$.
:::

## Same area, different perimeters

Rectangles can have the same area but different perimeters. Here are all the rectangles with whole-number sides and an area of $24\ \text{m}^2$:

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Length (m)","Width (m)","Area (m²)","Perimeter (m)"],"rows":[["24","1","24","50"],["12","2","24","28"],["8","3","24","22"],["6","4","24","20"]]}
:::

The long, thin rectangle has the greatest perimeter. The rectangle closest to a square has the least perimeter.

:::tip The square wins
Among all rectangles with the same area, the **square** has the least perimeter. For an area of $36\ \text{m}^2$, a $6\ \text{m}$ by $6\ \text{m}$ square needs only $24\ \text{m}$ of fence.
:::

:::example The least fencing
Ayesha wants a rectangular rabbit pen with an area of $36\ \text{m}^2$ and whole-metre sides. Which pen uses the least fencing?
@step The choices are $1$ by $36$, $2$ by $18$, $3$ by $12$, $4$ by $9$ and $6$ by $6$.
@step Their perimeters are $74$, $40$, $30$, $26$ and $24$ metres.
@answer The $6\ \text{m}$ by $6\ \text{m}$ square uses the least fencing: $24\ \text{m}$.
:::

## Solving area problems

Some shapes are made of rectangles. Split the shape into rectangles, find each area, then add.

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":340,"yMin":0,"yMax":238,"step":340},"hideTickLabels":true,"polylines":[{"points":[[65.5,35.3],[278,35.3],[278,141.55],[193,141.55],[193,205.3],[65.5,205.3],[65.5,35.3]],"fill":true}],"caption":"An L-shaped room","points":[{"x":149.35,"y":12,"label":"10 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":285,"y":76.13,"label":"5 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":110.45,"y":213,"label":"6 m","color":"muted","open":true},{"x":29.9,"y":108,"label":"8 m","color":"muted","open":true}]}
:::

:::example An L-shaped room
Find the area of the L-shaped room.
@step The bottom is $10\ \text{m}$ and the top is $6\ \text{m}$, so the step on the right is $10 - 6 = 4$ metres wide.
@step Split it with a vertical line. Left rectangle: $6 \times 8 = 48$. Right rectangle: $4 \times 5 = 20$.
@step Add: $48 + 20 = 68$.
@answer The area of the room is $68\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::check m5-rectangle-area 3
:::

:::summary
- Area of a rectangle $=$ length $\times$ width, in square units.
- To find a missing side, divide the area by the side you know.
- Perimeter is the distance around: add all the sides, in units of length.
- Rectangles with the same area can have different perimeters. The square has the least perimeter.
- Split shapes made of rectangles into rectangles, then add their areas.
:::
