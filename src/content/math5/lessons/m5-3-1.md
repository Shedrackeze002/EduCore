A coach has $84$ players. Can she make teams of $3$ with nobody left out? You could divide, but there is a faster way. In this lesson you will learn quick tests that tell you whether one number divides another.

## What divisible means

:::definition Divisible
A number is **divisible** by another number when it can be divided with a **remainder** of $0$. The **remainder** is the amount left over after dividing.
:::

Put $12$ counters in rows of $4$. You get $3$ full rows and nothing left over. So $12$ is divisible by $4$. Put $14$ counters in rows of $4$. You get $3$ rows and $2$ left over. So $14$ is **not** divisible by $4$.

:::example Divide to check
Is $56$ divisible by $7$? Is $58$ divisible by $7$?
@step $7 \times 8 = 56$, so $56 \div 7 = 8$ with remainder $0$.
@step $58 - 56 = 2$, so $58 \div 7 = 8$ R $2$.
@answer $56$ is divisible by $7$. $58$ is not.
:::

You can always divide to check. For $2$, $3$ and $5$ there are faster tests.

## The tests for 2 and 5

Look at the multiples of $2$ and of $5$. What do you notice about the last digits?

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Multiples of 2","Multiples of 5"],"rows":[["2","5"],["4","10"],["6","15"],["8","20"],["10","25"],["12","30"],["14","35"],["16","40"]]}
:::

The multiples of $2$ always end in $0$, $2$, $4$, $6$ or $8$. The multiples of $5$ always end in $0$ or $5$.

:::definition Divisibility tests for 2 and 5
- A number is divisible by $2$ when its last digit is **even**: $0$, $2$, $4$, $6$ or $8$.
- A number is divisible by $5$ when its last digit is $0$ or $5$.
- (A number that ends in $0$ is divisible by $10$ too.)
:::

:::example Using the tests
Is $345$ divisible by $2$? Is it divisible by $5$?
@step The last digit is $5$. It is odd, so $345$ is not divisible by $2$.
@step The last digit is $5$, so $345$ is divisible by $5$.
@answer Not by $2$, but yes by $5$.
:::

:::mistake Look only at the last digit
$345$ has a $4$ in it, but that does not make it even. Only the **last** digit matters for $2$ and $5$.
:::

## The test for 3

The last digit does not help for $3$: $13$ is not divisible by $3$, but $12$ is. Instead, add up the digits.

:::definition Divisibility test for 3
A number is divisible by $3$ when the **sum of its digits** is divisible by $3$.
:::

:::example Using the test for 3
Is $471$ divisible by $3$?
@step Add the digits: $4 + 7 + 1 = 12$.
@step $12$ is in the $3$ times table: $12 = 3 \times 4$.
@step So $471$ is divisible by $3$. Check: $471 \div 3 = 157$.
@answer Yes, $471$ is divisible by $3$.
:::

:::mistake The last digit does not work for 3
$23$ ends in $3$, but $23$ is not divisible by $3$ ($2 + 3 = 5$). Always use the digit sum.
:::

:::check m5-divisibility 2
:::

## Other numbers, and zero

For $4$, $6$, $7$, $8$ and $9$, you can divide to check. If the remainder is $0$, the number is divisible.

What about $0$? Division asks, "What times the divisor gives the dividend?" So $8 \div 0 = \square$ would mean $\square \times 0 = 8$. But any number times $0$ is $0$, never $8$.

:::example Dividing with zero
What are $8 \div 0$ and $0 \div 8$?
@step $8 \div 0$ would need $\square \times 0 = 8$. No number works.
@step $0 \div 8$ needs $\square \times 8 = 0$. That is $0$, because $0 \times 8 = 0$.
@answer $8 \div 0$ is not possible. $0 \div 8 = 0$.
:::

:::tip Division by zero
We can never divide **by** $0$. So no number is divisible by $0$. But $0$ divided by another number is $0$.
:::

:::check m5-divisibility 3
:::

:::summary
- A number is divisible by another when the remainder is $0$.
- Test for $2$: the last digit is even. Test for $5$: the last digit is $0$ or $5$.
- Test for $3$: the sum of the digits is divisible by $3$.
- For other numbers, divide and look at the remainder.
- Division by $0$ is not possible.
:::
