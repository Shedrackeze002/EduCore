A bag has $3$ red marbles and $5$ blue marbles. "There are $3$ red marbles for every $5$ blue ones." A **ratio** is a short way to say that.

## What is a ratio?

:::definition Ratio
A **ratio** compares two amounts in a situation. We can write it with a colon, $3 : 5$, or as a fraction, $\dfrac{3}{5}$. Both are read "$3$ to $5$".
:::

The shaded counters are the red marbles. The white counters are the blue marbles.

The ratio of red marbles to blue marbles is $3 : 5$. The **order** matters. The ratio of blue marbles to red marbles is $5 : 3$.

:::example Order the numbers
A garden bed has $4$ tomato plants and $7$ pepper plants. What is the ratio of pepper plants to tomato plants?
@step The question names pepper plants first, so their number goes first.
@step Pepper plants: $7$. Tomato plants: $4$.
@answer The ratio is $7 : 4$, or $\dfrac{7}{4}$.
:::

:::mistake Keep the order of the words
"Pepper plants to tomato plants" is $7 : 4$, not $4 : 7$. Write the numbers in the same order as the words.
:::

## Part-part and part-whole ratios

The $8$ marbles in the bag are the **whole**. The red marbles and the blue marbles are the two **parts**.

:::definition Part-part ratio
A **part-part ratio** compares one part with another part, such as red to blue, $3 : 5$.
:::

:::definition Part-whole ratio
A **part-whole ratio** compares one part with the whole, such as red to all the marbles, $3 : 8$.
:::

As a fraction, a part-whole ratio tells what fraction of the whole the part is. $3 : 8$ is $\dfrac{3}{8}$, and $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the marbles are red.

:::example A chess club
A chess club has $7$ girls and $5$ boys. Write the part-part ratio of girls to boys and the part-whole ratio of girls to all the members.
@step Part-part: girls to boys is $7 : 5$.
@step The whole is $7 + 5 = 12$ members.
@step Part-whole: girls to all the members is $7 : 12$, or $\dfrac{7}{12}$.
@answer $7 : 5$ and $7 : 12$
:::

:::mistake Find the whole first
In a part-whole ratio, the second number is the **whole**. For the chess club it is $7 + 5 = 12$, not $5$.
:::

:::

## More parts, and amounts you measure

A whole can have more than two parts. Ratios can also compare amounts you measure, like cups or litres.

:::example Three kinds of fruit
A fruit bowl has $4$ apples, $3$ pears and $5$ bananas. Write the ratio of pears to all the fruit, and the ratio of fruit that is not bananas to bananas.
@step The whole is $4 + 3 + 5 = 12$ pieces of fruit.
@step Pears to all the fruit: $3 : 12$, or $\dfrac{3}{12}$.
@step Not bananas: $4 + 3 = 7$. Not bananas to bananas: $7 : 5$.
@answer $3 : 12$ and $7 : 5$
:::

:::example A punch recipe
A punch recipe mixes $2$ cups of juice with $3$ cups of water. What is the part-whole ratio of juice to all the punch?
@step The whole is $2 + 3 = 5$ cups of punch.
@step Juice to all the punch: $2 : 5$.
@answer The ratio is $2 : 5$, or $\dfrac{2}{5}$.
:::

:::tip Name the parts
Before you write a ratio, say what each number stands for. "Juice to all the punch" tells you the first number is the juice and the second is the whole.
:::

:::

:::summary
- A ratio compares two amounts. Write it with a colon, $3 : 5$, or as a fraction, $\dfrac{3}{5}$.
- The order of the numbers matches the order of the words.
- A part-part ratio compares two parts. A part-whole ratio compares a part with the whole.
- To write a part-whole ratio, add the parts to find the whole first.
- As a fraction, a part-whole ratio tells what fraction of the whole the part is.
:::
