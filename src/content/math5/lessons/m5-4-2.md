A class picks $367$ apples at an orchard and packs them in bags of $7$. How many bags can they fill, and how many apples are left? Dividing a big number in your head is hard. The standard algorithm lets you divide one place at a time.

## Words for division

:::definition Dividend, divisor, quotient and remainder
In $367 \div 7 = 52$ R $3$:
- the **dividend** is the number being divided: $367$.
- the **divisor** is the number you divide by: $7$.
- the **quotient** is the answer: $52$.
- the **remainder** is what is left over: $3$. It is always less than the divisor.
:::

We write the long division like this. The quotient goes on top. The remainder is written after an R.

:::example Divide 367 by 7
Find $367 \div 7$.
@step $7$ does not go into $3$, so start with the first two digits, $36$.
@step How many $7$s are in $36$? $7 \times 5 = 35$, and $7 \times 6 = 42$ is too big. Write $5$ above the $6$. Subtract: $36 - 35 = 1$.
@step Bring down the $7$ to make $17$. How many $7$s are in $17$? $7 \times 2 = 14$. Write $2$ above the $7$. Subtract: $17 - 14 = 3$.
@step $$\begin{array}{r}\phantom{0}\,5\,2\mathrlap{\ \text{R}\,3} \\ 7\,\overline{\smash{)}\,3\,6\,7} \\ \underline{-\,3\,5}\,\phantom{0} \\ 1\,7 \\ \underline{-\,1\,4} \\ 3\end{array}$$
@answer $367 \div 7 = 52$ R $3$. The class fills $52$ bags with $3$ apples left over.
:::

:::check m5-divide 1
:::

## The standard algorithm, step by step

Repeat four steps for each place, from left to right:

1. **Divide**: how many times does the divisor go in?
2. **Multiply**: the divisor times that digit.
3. **Subtract**: what is left must be less than the divisor.
4. **Bring down** the next digit.

:::example Divide 852 by 4
Find $852 \div 4$.
@step Hundreds: $8 \div 4 = 2$. Write $2$. Multiply: $4 \times 2 = 8$. Subtract: $8 - 8 = 0$. Bring down the $5$.
@step Tens: $5 \div 4 = 1$ with some left. Write $1$. $4 \times 1 = 4$. Subtract: $5 - 4 = 1$. Bring down the $2$ to make $12$.
@step Ones: $12 \div 4 = 3$. Write $3$. $4 \times 3 = 12$. Subtract: $12 - 12 = 0$.
@step $$\begin{array}{r}2\,1\,3 \\ 4\,\overline{\smash{)}\,8\,5\,2} \\ \underline{-\,8}\,\phantom{0}\,\phantom{0} \\ 5\,\phantom{0} \\ \underline{-\,4}\,\phantom{0} \\ 1\,2 \\ \underline{-\,1\,2} \\ 0\end{array}$$
@answer $852 \div 4 = 213$
:::

:::tip Check by multiplying
Multiply the quotient by the divisor, then add the remainder. You should get the dividend: $7 \times 52 + 3 = 364 + 3 = 367$.
:::

:::mistake A remainder that is too big
If you subtract and get a number as big as the divisor (or bigger), the digit in the quotient is too small. Try the next digit up.
:::

:::check m5-divide 2
:::

## Zeros in the quotient

Sometimes the divisor does not go into the number you have. Then you write $0$ in the quotient and bring down the next digit.

:::example Divide 618 by 6
Find $618 \div 6$.
@step Hundreds: $6 \div 6 = 1$. Write $1$. Subtract: $6 - 6 = 0$. Bring down the $1$.
@step Tens: $6$ does not go into $1$. Write $0$ in the quotient. Bring down the $8$ to make $18$.
@step Ones: $18 \div 6 = 3$. Write $3$. Subtract: $18 - 18 = 0$.
@step $$\begin{array}{r}1\,0\,3 \\ 6\,\overline{\smash{)}\,6\,1\,8} \\ \underline{-\,6}\,\phantom{0}\,\phantom{0} \\ 1\,\phantom{0} \\ \underline{-\,0}\,\phantom{0} \\ 1\,8 \\ \underline{-\,1\,8} \\ 0\end{array}$$
@step Check: $6 \times 103 = 618$.
@answer $618 \div 6 = 103$
:::

:::mistake Skipping the zero
If you forget the $0$, you get $13$. But $6 \times 13 = 78$, not $618$. Every place after the first needs a digit in the quotient.
:::

:::check m5-divide 3
:::

:::summary
- In $\text{dividend} \div \text{divisor} = \text{quotient}$ R $\text{remainder}$, the remainder is less than the divisor.
- Divide, multiply, subtract, bring down. Repeat for each place.
- If the divisor does not go in, write $0$ in the quotient.
- Check: divisor $\times$ quotient $+$ remainder $=$ dividend.
:::
