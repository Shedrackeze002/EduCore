A balance scale stays level when both sides weigh the same. An equation works the same way: both sides have the same value. In this lesson you will find the unknown number that keeps an equation balanced.

## Equations and solutions

:::definition Equation
An **equation** says that two expressions have the same value. It has an equals sign, such as $n + 7 = 15$.
:::

:::definition Solution
The **solution** of an equation is the value of the variable that makes both sides equal. For $n + 7 = 15$, the solution is $n = 8$, because $8 + 7 = 15$.
:::

:::definition Inverse operations
**Inverse operations** undo each other. Adding and subtracting are inverse operations. Multiplying and dividing are inverse operations.
:::

To keep an equation balanced, always do the **same thing to both sides**.

:::example Solve by undoing
Solve $n + 7 = 15$.
@step $7$ is added to $n$. The inverse of adding $7$ is subtracting $7$.
@step Subtract $7$ from both sides: $n = 15 - 7 = 8$.
@step Check: $8 + 7 = 15$. Both sides are equal.
@answer $n = 8$
:::

## Equations with one operation

:::example Undo multiplying
Solve $6k = 42$.
@step $6k$ means $6 \times k$. The inverse of multiplying by $6$ is dividing by $6$.
@step Divide both sides by $6$: $k = 42 \div 6 = 7$.
@step Check: $6 \times 7 = 42$.
@answer $k = 7$
:::

:::example Undo dividing
Solve $\dfrac{m}{3} = 5$.
@step $\dfrac{m}{3}$ means $m \div 3$. The inverse of dividing by $3$ is multiplying by $3$.
@step Multiply both sides by $3$: $m = 5 \times 3 = 15$.
@step Check: $15 \div 3 = 5$.
@answer $m = 15$
:::

:::mistake Use the inverse
For $n + 7 = 15$, do not add $7$ to $15$. That gives $22$, and $22 + 7$ is not $15$. Do the **opposite** of what is done to the variable.
:::

In $3n + 4$, the variable is multiplied first and then $4$ is added. To solve, undo in the **reverse order**: undo the adding first, then the multiplying.

:::example Two steps
Solve $3n + 4 = 19$.
@step Undo the adding: subtract $4$ from both sides. $3n = 19 - 4 = 15$.
@step Undo the multiplying: divide both sides by $3$. $n = 15 \div 3 = 5$.
@step Check: $3 \times 5 + 4 = 15 + 4 = 19$. Both sides are equal.
@answer $n = 5$. Each bag has $5$ marbles.
:::

:::example A quotient and a subtraction
Solve $\dfrac{x}{4} - 2 = 6$.
@step Undo the subtracting: add $2$ to both sides. $\dfrac{x}{4} = 6 + 2 = 8$.
@step Undo the dividing: multiply both sides by $4$. $x = 8 \times 4 = 32$.
@step Check: $32 \div 4 - 2 = 8 - 2 = 6$.
@answer $x = 32$
:::

:::mistake Undo the adding first
In $3n + 4 = 19$, do not divide by $3$ first: $19 \div 3$ does not come out even, and the $4$ is still there. Take away the $4$ first.
:::

:::tip Always check
To **verify** a solution, substitute it into the equation. If both sides have the same value, your solution is correct.
:::

## Solving problems with equations

1. Choose a letter for the unknown number.
2. Write an equation that tells the story in order.
3. Solve it with inverse operations.
4. Check your answer in the story, then answer in a sentence.

:::example Stickers
Ella had some stickers. Her friend gave her $8$ more. Now she has $23$. How many did she have at first?
@step Let $s$ be the stickers she had at first. Equation: $s + 8 = 23$.
@step Subtract $8$ from both sides: $s = 23 - 8 = 15$.
@step Check: $15 + 8 = 23$.
@answer Ella had $15$ stickers at first.
:::

:::example Museum tickets
Hamid's family buys $4$ tickets to a museum and pays \$7 for parking. They pay \$43 in all. Each ticket costs the same. How much does one ticket cost?
@step Let $t$ be the cost of one ticket in dollars. Equation: $4t + 7 = 43$.
@step Subtract $7$ from both sides: $4t = 36$.
@step Divide both sides by $4$: $t = 36 \div 4 = 9$.
@step Check: $4 \times 9 + 7 = 36 + 7 = 43$.
@answer One ticket costs \$9.
:::

:::summary
- An equation says two expressions are equal. Its solution makes both sides equal.
- Inverse operations undo each other: adding and subtracting, multiplying and dividing.
- Do the same thing to both sides to keep the equation balanced.
- With two operations, undo the adding or subtracting first, then the multiplying or dividing.
- Check a solution by substituting it into the equation.
:::
