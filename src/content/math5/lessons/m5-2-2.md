Before you hand in an answer, ask yourself: does this make sense? A quick estimate can catch a big mistake. In this lesson you will estimate, then solve problems with money and measurements.

## Estimating

:::definition Estimate
An **estimate** is a number that is close to the exact answer and easy to work out. To estimate a sum or difference, round each number first. Then add or subtract the rounded numbers.
:::

:::example Estimate a sum
Estimate $4896 + 3127$ by rounding each number to the nearest thousand.
@step $4896$ rounds to $5000$.
@step $3127$ rounds to $3000$.
@step $5000 + 3000 = 8000$.
@answer The estimate is $8000$. (The exact sum is $8023$.)
:::

The estimate, $8000$, is very close to the exact answer, $8023$. An estimate will not give the exact answer, but it tells you about how big the answer should be.

## Is the answer reasonable?

An answer is **reasonable** when it is close to your estimate. If it is far away, look for a mistake.

:::example Checking a classmate's work
Diego says $6254 - 2981 = 4273$. Is that reasonable?
@step Round: $6254$ is about $6000$, and $2981$ is about $3000$.
@step Estimate: $6000 - 3000 = 3000$.
@step $4273$ is far from $3000$. Diego made a mistake.
@answer Not reasonable. (The exact answer is $3273$. Diego forgot that he regrouped $1$ thousand.)
:::

:::mistake Estimating with the wrong operation
For a difference, subtract the rounded numbers. $6000 + 3000 = 9000$ is not an estimate of $6254 - 2981$.
:::

## Problems with money

Money has two decimal places. The digits after the point are cents: \$12.45 is $12$ dollars and $45$ cents. Line up the decimal points, just like any decimal.

:::example Change from a purchase
Hana buys a sketchbook for \$12.45 and markers for \$8.79. She pays with \$50.00. How much change does she get?
@step First find the total: $12.45 + 8.79 = 21.24$.
@step $$\begin{array}{r}\overset{1}{1}\,\overset{1}{2}.\,\overset{1}{4}\,5 \\ +\ \phantom{0}\,8.\,7\,9 \\ \hline 2\,1.\,2\,4\end{array}$$
@step Then subtract from the money she paid: $50.00 - 21.24 = 28.76$.
@step $$\begin{array}{r}\overset{4}{\cancel{5}}\,\overset{9}{\cancel{0}}.\,\overset{9}{\cancel{0}}\,\overset{10}{\cancel{0}} \\ -\ 2\,1.\,2\,4 \\ \hline 2\,8.\,7\,6\end{array}$$
@step Check with an estimate: about \$12 + \$9 = \$21 spent, and \$50 − \$21 = \$29. Close to \$28.76.
@answer Hana gets \$28.76 in change.
:::

:::tip Key words
"Total", "in all" and "altogether" often mean add. "How much more", "how much is left" and "change" often mean subtract. Always think about what is happening in the story.
:::

## Problems with measurements

Metric measurements use decimals too: $2.5$ km, $0.875$ kg, $1.25$ L. Some problems take two steps.

:::example A hiking trail
A trail is $12.5$ km long. Kateri hikes $4.75$ km on Saturday and $3.875$ km on Sunday. How much of the trail is left?
@step First add the two days: $4.75 + 3.875 = 8.625$ km. (Write $4.75$ as $4.750$.)
@step Then subtract from the whole trail: $12.500 - 8.625 = 3.875$ km.
@step Check: $8.625 + 3.875 = 12.5$. Estimate: $13 - 9 = 4$, which is close.
@answer There is $3.875$ km of the trail left.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the second step
After you add the two days, you are not done. The question asks how much is **left**, so you still need to subtract.
:::

:::

:::summary
- Estimate by rounding each number, then add or subtract the rounded numbers.
- An answer is reasonable when it is close to the estimate.
- Money has two decimal places. Line up the decimal points to add or subtract it.
- Read the question carefully. Some problems need two steps.
:::
