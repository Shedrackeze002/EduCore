Ella says $2 + 3 \times 4 = 20$. Jun says it is $14$. They used the same numbers, so why are the answers different? Everyone needs to follow the same order. In this lesson you will learn that order, and how parentheses change it.

## Numerical expressions and the order of operations

:::definition Numerical expression
A **numerical expression** is a group of numbers and operations, such as $2 + 3 \times 4$. It has no equals sign. It stands for one number, called its **value**.
:::

:::definition Parentheses
**Parentheses** $(\ )$ group numbers and operations together. The work inside the parentheses is done first. In $(2 + 3) \times 4$, you add $2 + 3$ before you multiply.
:::

:::definition Order of operations
The **order of operations** tells you which operation to do first:
1. Work inside the **parentheses**.
2. **Multiply and divide**, from left to right.
3. **Add and subtract**, from left to right.
:::

:::example Without parentheses
Evaluate $2 + 3 \times 4$.
@step There are no parentheses. Multiply first: $3 \times 4 = 12$.
@step Then add: $2 + 12 = 14$.
@answer $2 + 3 \times 4 = 14$. Jun is right.
:::

:::example With parentheses
Evaluate $(2 + 3) \times 4$.
@step Parentheses first: $2 + 3 = 5$.
@step Then multiply: $5 \times 4 = 20$.
@answer $(2 + 3) \times 4 = 20$
:::

The numbers are the same, but the parentheses change the order. That changes the value.

:::mistake Not always left to right
Do not just work from left to right. In $2 + 3 \times 4$, the multiplying comes before the adding, even though the $+$ comes first.
:::

## Two steps after the parentheses

When there is more to do after the parentheses, keep following the order. Write the expression again after each step.

:::example Multiply, then add
Evaluate $5 \times (13 - 7) + 4$.
@step Parentheses first: $13 - 7 = 6$. Now it is $5 \times 6 + 4$.
@step Multiply before adding: $5 \times 6 = 30$. Now it is $30 + 4$.
@step Add: $30 + 4 = 34$.
@answer $5 \times (13 - 7) + 4 = 34$
:::

:::example Divide, then subtract
Evaluate $20 - (6 + 9) \div 3$.
@step Parentheses first: $6 + 9 = 15$. Now it is $20 - 15 \div 3$.
@step Divide before subtracting: $15 \div 3 = 5$. Now it is $20 - 5$.
@answer $20 - (6 + 9) \div 3 = 15$
:::

:::tip One step at a time
Do one operation, then write what is left. Short steps help you keep the order right.
:::

## Writing an expression for a problem

Parentheses show which part of a problem to work out first.

:::example Packs of stickers
Kai buys $4$ packs of stickers. Each pack has $6$ star stickers and $3$ heart stickers. How many stickers does Kai buy?
@step Each pack has $6 + 3$ stickers. There are $4$ packs.
@step Expression: $4 \times (6 + 3)$.
@step Parentheses first: $6 + 3 = 9$. Then multiply: $4 \times 9 = 36$.
@answer Kai buys $36$ stickers.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the parentheses
$4 \times 6 + 3 = 27$ counts the heart stickers in only one pack. The parentheses make sure every pack gets both kinds: $4 \times (6 + 3) = 36$.
:::

:::example Money left
Liam has \$50. He buys a book for \$12 and a game for \$15. How many dollars does he have left?
@step Liam spends $12 + 15$ dollars, so the expression is $50 - (12 + 15)$.
@step Parentheses first: $12 + 15 = 27$. Then subtract: $50 - 27 = 23$.
@answer Liam has \$23 left.
:::

:::check m5-order-of-operations 3
:::

:::summary
- A numerical expression stands for one number, its value.
- Order of operations: parentheses first, then multiply and divide from left to right, then add and subtract from left to right.
- Parentheses change the order, so they can change the value.
- In a problem, put the part you must work out first in parentheses.
:::
