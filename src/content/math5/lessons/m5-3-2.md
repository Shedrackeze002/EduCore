Twelve chairs can stand in one long row, in $2$ rows of $6$, or in $3$ rows of $4$. The numbers $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $6$ and $12$ all fit into $12$ exactly. They are the factors of $12$. In this lesson you will find all the factors of a number.

## Factors and factor pairs

:::definition Factor
A **factor** of a number divides that number with no remainder. $4$ is a factor of $12$ because $12 \div 4 = 3$.
:::

:::definition Factor pair
A **factor pair** is two factors that multiply to give the number. $3 \times 4 = 12$, so $3$ and $4$ are a factor pair of $12$.
:::

Each way to arrange $12$ counters in equal rows gives a factor pair: $1 \times 12$, $2 \times 6$ and $3 \times 4$.

:::example A missing partner
$4 \times \square = 36$. What is the other factor?
@step Division undoes multiplication: $\square = 36 \div 4$.
@step $36 \div 4 = 9$. Check: $4 \times 9 = 36$.
@answer The missing factor is $9$. So $4$ and $9$ are a factor pair of $36$.
:::

:::tip 1 and the number itself
Every number has $1$ and itself as factors: $36 = 1 \times 36$. A factor is never bigger than the number.
:::

## Finding all the factors

Work in order. Check $1$, then $2$, then $3$, and so on. Use the divisibility tests for $2$, $3$ and $5$. Divide to check the other numbers. Each time you find a factor, write its partner too.

:::example All the factors of 48
Find all the factors of $48$.
@step $1$: every number is divisible by $1$. $48 = 1 \times 48$.
@step $2$: the last digit, $8$, is even. $48 = 2 \times 24$.
@step $3$: the digit sum is $4 + 8 = 12$, which is in the $3$ times table. $48 = 3 \times 16$.
@step $4$: divide: $48 \div 4 = 12$. $48 = 4 \times 12$.
@step $5$: the last digit is not $0$ or $5$. Not a factor.
@step $6$: divide: $48 \div 6 = 8$. $48 = 6 \times 8$.
@step $7$: $48 \div 7 = 6$ R $6$. Not a factor.
@step The next number is $8$, and $8$ is already a partner ($6 \times 8$). Stop.
@answer $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $6$, $8$, $12$, $16$, $24$ and $48$
:::

:::tip When to stop
Stop when the number you are checking times itself is bigger than your number. For $48$, $7 \times 7 = 49$ is bigger than $48$, so every factor bigger than $7$ is already a partner.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the partners
When you find $3$, write $16$ too. If you only list $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$ and $6$, you miss half of the factors of $48$.
:::

## Factors in problems

Many problems about equal rows or equal groups are really asking for factors.

:::example Rows of chairs
Mr. Okafor has $36$ chairs. He wants equal rows with no chairs left over. How many chairs could be in each row?
@step Any number of chairs in a row must be a factor of $36$.
@step Factor pairs of $36$: $1 \times 36$, $2 \times 18$, $3 \times 12$, $4 \times 9$, $6 \times 6$.
@step $5$ is not a factor (the last digit is $6$). Since $6 \times 6 = 36$, write $6$ only once.
@answer $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $6$, $9$, $12$, $18$ or $36$ chairs in each row
:::

:::mistake Writing a factor twice
$6 \times 6 = 36$ is one factor pair, but $6$ is only one factor. List it once.
:::

:::check m5-factors 3
:::

:::summary
- A factor of a number divides it with no remainder.
- Two factors that multiply to give the number are a factor pair.
- To find all the factors, check $1, 2, 3, \ldots$ in order, using the divisibility tests, and write each factor with its partner.
- Stop when the number you are checking times itself is bigger than your number.
:::
