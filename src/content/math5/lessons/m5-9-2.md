A polygon on a coordinate grid is a set of points joined in order. If you know the coordinates of its corners, you can draw it, and you can describe it to someone who cannot see it.

## Vertices and coordinates

:::definition Vertex
A **vertex** is a corner of a polygon, where two sides meet. The plural of vertex is **vertices**.
:::

To describe a polygon on a grid, give the coordinates of each vertex. Read each vertex like a point: across first, then up.

:::example The vertices of a triangle
Write the coordinates of the vertices of triangle $ABC$.
@step $A$ is $1$ across and $1$ up: $A(1,\ 1)$.
@step $B$ is $7$ across and $2$ up: $B(7,\ 2)$.
@step $C$ is $3$ across and $6$ up: $C(3,\ 6)$.
@answer $A(1,\ 1)$, $B(7,\ 2)$ and $C(3,\ 6)$.
:::

## Drawing a polygon from coordinates

To draw a polygon from its vertices:

1. Plot each vertex: across first, then up.
2. Join the vertices **in order** with straight lines.
3. Join the last vertex back to the first one.

:::example A rectangle
Draw the polygon with vertices $A(1,\ 1)$, $B(6,\ 1)$, $C(6,\ 4)$ and $D(1,\ 4)$.
@step Plot the four points.
@step Join $A$ to $B$, $B$ to $C$, $C$ to $D$, and $D$ back to $A$.
@answer The polygon is a rectangle $5$ units wide and $3$ units tall.
:::

:::figure
{"type":"plane","grid":{"xMin":0,"xMax":10,"yMin":0,"yMax":10,"step":1},"polylines":[{"points":[[1,1],[6,1],[6,4],[1,4],[1,1]],"fill":true}],"points":[{"x":1,"y":1,"label":"A","color":"brand"},{"x":6,"y":1,"label":"B","color":"brand"},{"x":6,"y":4,"label":"C","color":"brand"},{"x":1,"y":4,"label":"D","color":"brand"}],"caption":"Rectangle ABCD"}
:::

:::mistake Joining the points out of order
If you join $A$ to $C$ and then to $B$, the sides cross and you get a bow-tie, not a rectangle. Always follow the order of the vertices.
:::

## Using what you know about shapes

The sides of a rectangle on a grid often run along grid lines. Then vertices that are straight across from each other share their **second** number, and vertices straight up from each other share their **first** number.

:::example The missing corner
Three vertices of a rectangle are $(2,\ 1)$, $(7,\ 1)$ and $(7,\ 5)$. Where is the fourth vertex?
@step $(7,\ 5)$ is straight up from $(7,\ 1)$. So the fourth vertex is straight up from $(2,\ 1)$: its first number is $2$.
@step It is straight across from $(7,\ 5)$: its second number is $5$.
@answer The fourth vertex is $(2,\ 5)$.
:::

:::figure

:::

:::tip Building a rectangle
A rectangle with bottom-left vertex $(2,\ 3)$ that is $4$ units wide and $2$ units tall has vertices $(2,\ 3)$, $(6,\ 3)$, $(6,\ 5)$ and $(2,\ 5)$. Wide adds to the first number. Tall adds to the second number.
:::

:::

:::summary
- A vertex is a corner of a polygon. Describe a polygon by giving the coordinates of its vertices.
- To draw a polygon, plot the vertices and join them in order, then join the last vertex back to the first.
- On a rectangle with sides along grid lines, vertices straight up from each other share the first number, and vertices straight across share the second number.
:::
