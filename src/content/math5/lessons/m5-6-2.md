Have you seen a sign that says "$50\%$ off"? A **percentage** is a ratio that compares a part with a whole of $100$. In this lesson you will write one part-whole relationship in four ways: as a ratio, a fraction, a decimal number and a percentage.

## Percent means out of 100

:::definition Percentage
A **percentage** compares a part with a whole of $100$. The symbol $\%$ means "out of $100$". $35\%$ means $35$ out of $100$.
:::

## Four ways to show the same thing

Fractions, decimals, ratios and percentages can all show the same part-whole relationship.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Ratio","Fraction","Decimal","Percentage"],"rows":[["35 : 100","$\\dfrac{35}{100}$","0.35","35%"],["7 : 10","$\\dfrac{7}{10}$","0.7","70%"],["9 : 20","$\\dfrac{9}{20}$","0.45","45%"]]}
:::

:::example Seven out of ten
Kiona made $7$ of her $10$ free throws. What percentage did she make?
@step The part-whole ratio is $7 : 10$, or $\dfrac{7}{10}$.
@step Make the whole $100$: $10 \times 10 = 100$, so multiply the part by $10$ too: $7 \times 10 = 70$.
@step $\dfrac{7}{10} = \dfrac{70}{100} = 0.7$
@answer Kiona made $70\%$ of her free throws.
:::

:::mistake Percent is out of 100
$\dfrac{7}{10}$ is **not** $7\%$. $7\%$ means $7$ out of $100$. Change the whole to $100$ first: $\dfrac{7}{10} = \dfrac{70}{100} = 70\%$.
:::

## Making the whole 100

When the whole is not $100$, multiply the part and the whole by the same number to make the whole $100$. These wholes are friendly:

- $10 \times 10 = 100$ and $20 \times 5 = 100$
- $25 \times 4 = 100$ and $50 \times 2 = 100$
- $4 \times 25 = 100$ and $5 \times 20 = 100$

:::example A class survey
In a class of $20$ students, $9$ have a pet. Write the part-whole ratio of students with a pet to all the students as a fraction, a decimal number and a percentage.
@step Fraction: $\dfrac{9}{20}$.
@step Make the whole $100$: $20 \times 5 = 100$, so $9 \times 5 = 45$. That gives $\dfrac{9}{20} = \dfrac{45}{100}$.
@step Decimal: $45$ hundredths is $0.45$.
@answer $\dfrac{9}{20} = 0.45 = 45\%$
:::

:::tip Quarters
$\dfrac{1}{4} = 25\%$, $\dfrac{2}{4} = 50\%$ and $\dfrac{3}{4} = 75\%$, because $4 \times 25 = 100$.
:::

## The rest of the whole

The whole is always $100\%$. If you know one part, subtract from $100\%$ to find the rest.

:::example The bus and the rest
In a class of $25$ students, $8$ take the bus. The rest walk. What percentage take the bus? What percentage walk?
@step Bus: $\dfrac{8}{25}$. Make the whole $100$: $25 \times 4 = 100$, so $8 \times 4 = 32$. That is $\dfrac{32}{100} = 32\%$.
@step Walk: $100\% - 32\% = 68\%$.
@step Check: $25 - 8 = 17$ students walk, and $\dfrac{17}{25} = \dfrac{68}{100}$.
@answer $32\%$ take the bus and $68\%$ walk.
:::

:::check m5-ratio-percent 3
:::

:::summary
- A percentage compares a part with a whole of $100$. $\%$ means "out of $100$".
- The same part-whole relationship can be a ratio ($9 : 20$), a fraction ($\dfrac{9}{20}$), a decimal ($0.45$) and a percentage ($45\%$).
- To change a fraction to a percentage, make the whole $100$ by multiplying the part and the whole by the same number.
- The parts of a whole add up to $100\%$.
:::
