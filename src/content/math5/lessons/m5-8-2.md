A stop sign, a honeycomb cell and a patch on a soccer ball are all regular polygons. Their equal sides and equal angles give them a lot of symmetry.

## Regular polygons

:::definition Polygon
A **polygon** is a closed 2-D shape with straight sides. Triangles, squares, pentagons and hexagons are polygons.
:::

:::definition Regular polygon
A **regular polygon** has all its sides equal **and** all its angles equal. An equilateral triangle and a square are regular polygons.
:::

Look at this regular hexagon. It has 6 equal sides and 6 lines of symmetry. It also fits on itself 6 times in a full turn.

In a regular polygon, the number of sides, the number of lines of symmetry and the order of rotation symmetry are all the same.

:::figure

:::example A stop sign
A stop sign is a regular octagon. How many lines of symmetry does it have? What is the smallest turn that makes it fit on itself?
@step A regular octagon has $8$ sides, so it has $8$ lines of symmetry and an order of rotation symmetry of $8$.
@step It fits $8$ times in a full turn of $360^\circ$. Share the turn equally: $360^\circ \div 8 = 45^\circ$.
@answer $8$ lines of symmetry. The smallest turn is $45^\circ$.
:::

## Polygons that are not regular

When the sides or angles are **not** all equal, a polygon has fewer symmetries than sides. A rectangle has 4 sides, but only 2 lines of symmetry. Its long sides and short sides are different.

:::figure

:::

This rhombus has 4 equal sides, but its angles are not all equal. It has only $2$ lines of symmetry, and its order of rotation symmetry is $2$.

:::mistake Counting sides is not enough
"A rectangle has 4 sides, so it has 4 lines of symmetry" is wrong. Only a **regular** polygon is sure to have as many symmetries as sides. Check other shapes fold by fold.
:::

For triangles, the lines of symmetry match the equal sides. A triangle with 3 equal sides has 3 lines of symmetry. A triangle with exactly 2 equal sides has 1. A triangle with no equal sides has none.

:::figure

:::

:::example An isosceles triangle
This triangle has exactly 2 equal sides. How many lines of symmetry does it have?
@step Fold it along the line from the top vertex to the middle of the bottom side. The two equal sides land on each other.
@step No other fold works, because the third side is a different length.
@answer It has $1$ line of symmetry.
:::

## Circles

A circle is not a polygon. Every straight line through its centre is a line of symmetry, so a circle has **infinitely many** lines of symmetry. It also fits on itself after **any** turn, so it has infinitely many rotation symmetries.

## Sorting shapes by their symmetries

Every shape belongs to one of four groups. Ask two questions: Does it have a line of symmetry? Does it have rotation symmetry (an order of $2$ or more)?

:::example Sorting a parallelogram
Which group does this parallelogram belong to?
@step No fold line makes the two halves match, so it has $0$ lines of symmetry.
@step A half turn makes it fit on itself, so its order of rotation symmetry is $2$.
@answer It has rotation symmetry but no lines of symmetry.
:::

:::summary
- A regular polygon has all sides equal and all angles equal.
- A regular polygon with $n$ sides has $n$ lines of symmetry and an order of rotation symmetry of $n$.
- Polygons that are not regular have fewer symmetries than sides. A triangle with 3 equal sides has 3 lines of symmetry, with exactly 2 equal sides it has 1, and with no equal sides it has none.
- A circle has infinitely many lines of symmetry and infinitely many rotation symmetries.
- Sort shapes by asking: Does it have a line of symmetry? Does it have rotation symmetry?
:::
