A school orders $26$ boxes of granola bars. Each box holds $347$ grams. How many grams is that in all? You need to multiply $347 \times 26$. The standard algorithm breaks this big job into small steps you already know.

## Multiplying by a one-digit number

:::definition Product
The **product** is the answer to a multiplication. In $347 \times 6 = 2082$, the product is $2082$.
:::

Multiply each digit by the one-digit number, starting with the **ones**. When a product is $10$ or more, write the ones digit and **carry** the tens digit to the next place. Add the carry **after** you multiply the next digit.

:::example Multiply by 6
Find $347 \times 6$.
@step Ones: $6 \times 7 = 42$. Write $2$ and carry $4$.
@step Tens: $6 \times 4 = 24$, plus the carried $4$ is $28$. Write $8$ and carry $2$.
@step Hundreds: $6 \times 3 = 18$, plus the carried $2$ is $20$. Write $20$.
@step $$\begin{array}{r}\phantom{0}\,\overset{2}{3}\,\overset{4}{4}\,7 \\ \underline{\times\ \phantom{0}\,\phantom{0}\,\phantom{0}\,6} \\ 2\,0\,8\,2\end{array}$$
@answer $347 \times 6 = 2082$
:::

:::mistake Adding the carry too soon
Multiply first, then add the carry. In the tens, $6 \times 4 = 24$, then $24 + 4 = 28$. Do **not** add $4 + 4$ first and then multiply.
:::

:::

## Multiplying by a two-digit number

To multiply by $26$, think of $26$ as $20 + 6$. Multiply by the $6$, then by the $20$, then add. The two answers are called **partial products**.

:::figure

:::

The area model shows the pieces. The bottom row adds to $1800 + 240 + 42 = 2082$. That is $347 \times 6$. The top row adds to $6000 + 800 + 140 = 6940$. That is $347 \times 20$.

:::example Multiply by 26
Find $347 \times 26$.
@step Multiply by the ones digit: $347 \times 6 = 2082$. This is the first partial product.
@step Multiply by the tens digit. The $2$ stands for $20$, so write a $0$ in the ones place first. Then $347 \times 2 = 694$ goes in front of it: $6940$.
@step Add the partial products: $2082 + 6940 = 9022$.
@step $$\begin{array}{rl}\phantom{0}\,3\,4\,7 & \\ \underline{\times\ \phantom{0}\,\phantom{0}\,2\,6} & \\ 2\,0\,8\,2 & \leftarrow 347 \times 6 \\ \underline{+\ 6\,9\,4\,0} & \leftarrow 347 \times 20 \\ 9\,0\,2\,2 & \end{array}$$
@answer $347 \times 26 = 9022$. The school orders $9022$ grams of granola bars.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the zero
The $2$ in $26$ means $2$ tens. If you forget the $0$, you get $694$ instead of $6940$, and the answer is far too small: $2082 + 694 = 2776$.
:::

:::check m5-multiply 2
:::

## Checking with an estimate

Round each number to its greatest place, then multiply. If your answer is close to the estimate, it is **reasonable**.

:::example Is 9022 reasonable?
Estimate $347 \times 26$.
@step $347$ rounds to $300$. $26$ rounds to $30$.
@step $300 \times 30$: multiply $3 \times 3 = 9$, then write the three zeros: $9000$.
@answer The estimate is $9000$. The answer $9022$ is close, so it is reasonable.
:::

:::tip Count the zeros
To multiply numbers that end in zeros, multiply the front digits. Then write all the zeros: $400 \times 30$ is $4 \times 3 = 12$ with three zeros, $12\,000$.
:::

:::check m5-multiply 3
:::

:::summary
- Multiply from right to left. Carry the tens digit and add it after the next multiplication.
- For a two-digit number, find two partial products: one for the ones digit and one for the tens digit (with a $0$ in the ones place). Then add them.
- Check a product with an estimate: round each number, then multiply.
:::
