A woodworker making an octagonal mirror frame, a crew building a gazebo, and a tile setter laying a patterned floor all need the angles of regular polygons, and they need them before the first cut. In this lesson you will use polygon angles to plan real jobs.

## Perimeters and corner angles

A regular polygon has $n$ equal sides, so its perimeter is $n$ times the side length, and every corner has the same interior angle.

:::example A hexagonal sandbox
A sandbox is a regular hexagon with sides of $1.2\ \text{m}$. How much lumber goes around the outside, and what angle is each corner?
@step Perimeter: $6 \times 1.2 = 7.2\ \text{m}$.
@step Corner angle: $\dfrac{180^\circ(6 - 2)}{6} = \dfrac{720^\circ}{6} = 120^\circ$.
@answer About $7.2\ \text{m}$ of lumber, and each corner is $120^\circ$.
:::

## Mitre cuts

The pieces of a frame meet at **mitre joints**, where both ends are cut at the same angle. A mitre saw's scale reads $0^\circ$ for a square ($90^\circ$) cut.

At each corner, the frame turns through one exterior angle, $\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$. The two pieces at the joint share that turn equally.

:::definition Mitre angle
For a frame shaped like a regular polygon with $n$ sides, set the mitre saw to half an exterior angle:
$$\text{mitre angle} = \dfrac{180^\circ}{n}$$
:::

:::example Picture frames and an octagonal mirror
What saw setting is needed for (a) a square picture frame and (b) a regular octagonal mirror frame?
@step (a) $n = 4$: $180^\circ \div 4 = 45^\circ$.
@step (b) $n = 8$: $180^\circ \div 8 = 22.5^\circ$.
@answer (a) $45^\circ$; (b) $22.5^\circ$
:::

:::mistake Using the whole exterior angle
Setting the saw to $45^\circ$ for an octagon (the full exterior angle) makes each joint turn $90^\circ$, and the frame closes up as a square. Each of the two pieces at a joint supplies only **half** the turn.
:::

:::

## Central angles

Lines from the centre of a regular polygon to its corners split the full $360^\circ$ around the centre into $n$ equal **central angles**. They also split the polygon into $n$ identical isosceles triangles.

:::example An octagonal gazebo floor
An octagonal gazebo floor is built from 8 triangular sections that meet at the centre. Find the angle of each section at the centre and its other two angles.
@step Central angle: $360^\circ \div 8 = 45^\circ$.
@step Each section is isosceles: $\dfrac{180^\circ - 45^\circ}{2} = 67.5^\circ$.
@answer $45^\circ$ at the centre and $67.5^\circ$ at each outside corner. Two sections meet at each corner: $2 \times 67.5^\circ = 135^\circ$, the interior angle of an octagon.
:::

## Tiling

Tiles fit around a point with no gaps only when the angles meeting there add to exactly $360^\circ$. On their own, only equilateral triangles ($6 \times 60^\circ$), squares ($4 \times 90^\circ$) and regular hexagons ($3 \times 120^\circ$) work. Regular pentagons leave a gap: $3 \times 108^\circ = 324^\circ$, so $36^\circ$ is left over.

Different regular polygons can work together. Two regular octagons leave room for a square.

:::example Octagons and squares
Two regular octagons meet at a corner. What angle is left, and which regular polygon fills it?
@step Each octagon angle is $135^\circ$: $360^\circ - 2(135^\circ) = 90^\circ$.
@step A regular polygon with a $90^\circ$ interior angle is a square.
@answer A $90^\circ$ gap is left, and a square fills it.
:::

:::summary
- Perimeter of a regular polygon $=$ number of sides $\times$ side length.
- Mitre saw setting for a regular $n$-sided frame: $\dfrac{180^\circ}{n}$, half an exterior angle.
- Central angle: $\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$. The triangles from the centre are isosceles.
- Tiles fit around a point when the angles add to exactly $360^\circ$.
:::
