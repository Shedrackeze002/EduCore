Sometimes no side comes with its opposite angle. A pipe fitter knows two pipe runs and the angle between them, or a framer knows all three members of a truss but no angles. With no matching pair, the sine law cannot start. The **cosine law** handles these cases.

## When to use the cosine law

Use the cosine law when you know:

- **two sides and the angle between them** (the contained angle), and you want the third side, or
- **all three sides**, and you want an angle.

:::definition The cosine law
In any $\triangle ABC$,
$$c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$$
The side on the left is opposite the angle in the cosine. The same pattern works for any side: $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$ and $b^2 = a^2 + c^2 - 2ac\cos B$.
:::

:::tip The Pythagorean theorem with a correction
If $\angle C = 90^\circ$, then $\cos C = 0$ and the cosine law becomes $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$. The term $-2ab\cos C$ corrects for the angle not being $90^\circ$.
:::

:::example Two sides and the contained angle
In $\triangle ABC$, $b = 12\ \text{m}$, $c = 9\ \text{m}$ and $\angle A = 58^\circ$. Determine $a$, to the nearest tenth of a metre.
@step $\angle A$ is between sides $b$ and $c$, so use $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$.
@step $a^2 = 12^2 + 9^2 - 2(12)(9)\cos 58^\circ$
@step $a^2 \approx 144 + 81 - 114.46 = 110.54$
@step $a \approx \sqrt{110.54} \approx 10.514$
@answer $a \approx 10.5\ \text{m}$
:::

:::mistake Subtracting before multiplying by the cosine
Do not work out $144 + 81 - 216 = 9$ and then multiply by $\cos 58^\circ$. The cosine belongs to the term $2bc\cos A$ only. Find $2(12)(9)\cos 58^\circ$ first, then subtract it from $144 + 81$.
:::

:::example An obtuse contained angle
In $\triangle ABC$, $a = 6.5\ \text{m}$, $b = 4.8\ \text{m}$ and $\angle C = 124^\circ$. Determine $c$, to the nearest tenth of a metre.
@step $c^2 = 6.5^2 + 4.8^2 - 2(6.5)(4.8)\cos 124^\circ$
@step $2(6.5)(4.8)\cos 124^\circ \approx -34.89$, so $c^2 \approx 42.25 + 23.04 + 34.89 = 100.18$
@step $c \approx \sqrt{100.18} \approx 10.009$
@answer $c \approx 10.0\ \text{m}$, longer than both given sides.

## Finding an angle

Rearrange the cosine law to get the cosine of the angle on its own:
$$\cos C = \dfrac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab}$$
The side **opposite** the angle you want is the one that is subtracted.

:::example Three sides
In $\triangle PQR$, $p = 6\ \text{cm}$, $q = 8\ \text{cm}$ and $r = 12\ \text{cm}$. Determine $\angle P$ and the largest angle, to the nearest tenth of a degree.
@step $\angle P$: $\cos P = \dfrac{8^2 + 12^2 - 6^2}{2(8)(12)} = \dfrac{172}{192} \approx 0.8958$, so $\angle P = \cos^{-1}(0.8958) \approx 26.38^\circ$.
@step The largest angle is opposite the longest side, $r = 12\ \text{cm}$.
@step $\cos R = \dfrac{6^2 + 8^2 - 12^2}{2(6)(8)} = \dfrac{-44}{96} \approx -0.4583$
@step $\angle R = \cos^{-1}(-0.4583) \approx 117.28^\circ$
@answer $\angle P \approx 26.4^\circ$ and the largest angle is $\angle R \approx 117.3^\circ$.
:::

:::tip A negative cosine means obtuse
If the cosine of an angle is negative, the angle is obtuse. The $\cos^{-1}$ key gives the correct obtuse angle directly, so the cosine law is the safest way to find the largest angle of a triangle.
:::

:::summary
- Use the cosine law for two sides and the contained angle (find the third side) or for three sides (find an angle).
- $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$. The side on the left is opposite the angle in the cosine.
- Work out $2ab\cos C$ before subtracting. If the angle is obtuse, this term is negative, so the total gets bigger.
- To find an angle: $\cos C = \dfrac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab}$, then use $\cos^{-1}$.
- A negative cosine means an obtuse angle. The largest angle is opposite the longest side.
:::
