On the job, nobody hands you a labelled triangle. You get a truss drawing, a survey sketch or a set of directions, and you have to find the triangle, decide which law fits, and solve it. This lesson puts the sine law and the cosine law to work.

## Choosing a law

Before you calculate anything, sort out what you know.

| You know | Use |
|---|---|
| A side and its opposite angle (a matching pair), plus one more side or angle | The sine law |
| Two angles and any side | The angle sum, then the sine law |
| Two sides and the angle **between** them | The cosine law |
| All three sides | The cosine law |
| A right angle | SOH CAH TOA or the Pythagorean theorem |

:::example Which law?
A framer knows that two rafters are $4.2\ \text{m}$ and $5.5\ \text{m}$ long and that they meet at the peak at $112^\circ$. She wants the length of the bottom chord. Which law should she use?
@step The $112^\circ$ angle is between the two known rafters.
@step No side is known together with its opposite angle, so there is no matching pair.
@answer Two sides and the contained angle: use the cosine law.
:::

:::mistake Using the sine law without a matching pair
The sine law needs a side **and** the angle opposite it. Two sides and the angle between them is not enough for the sine law, no matter how the ratios are written.
:::

## A plan for workplace problems

1. **Sketch** the triangle and label the vertices.
2. **Mark** every known side and angle, and mark the unknown with $x$ or $?$.
3. **Decide** which law fits (use the table above).
4. **Solve**, keeping full calculator values until the end.
5. **Answer** in a sentence with units, rounded as the question asks, and check that it makes sense.

:::example The bottom chord of a truss
The framer's rafters are $TL = 4.2\ \text{m}$ and $TR = 5.5\ \text{m}$, and $\angle T = 112^\circ$. How long is the bottom chord $LR$, to the nearest tenth of a metre?
@step $LR^2 = 4.2^2 + 5.5^2 - 2(4.2)(5.5)\cos 112^\circ$
@step $2(4.2)(5.5)\cos 112^\circ \approx -17.31$, so $LR^2 \approx 17.64 + 30.25 + 17.31 = 65.20$
@step $LR \approx \sqrt{65.20} \approx 8.074$
@answer The bottom chord is about $8.1\ \text{m}$ long.
:::

:::example Two fire lookout towers
Fire lookout towers $A$ and $B$ are $12.5\ \text{km}$ apart. Both spot smoke at $F$. At $A$, the angle between the line to $B$ and the line to the fire is $52^\circ$. At $B$, the angle is $63^\circ$. How far is the fire from tower $A$, to the nearest tenth of a kilometre?
@step The known side $AB$ is opposite $\angle F$: $\angle F = 180^\circ - 52^\circ - 63^\circ = 65^\circ$.
@step $AF$ is opposite $\angle B$: $\dfrac{AF}{\sin 63^\circ} = \dfrac{12.5}{\sin 65^\circ}$
@step $AF = \dfrac{12.5 \sin 63^\circ}{\sin 65^\circ} \approx 12.289$
@answer The fire is about $12.3\ \text{km}$ from tower $A$.
:::

## Turns and angles of elevation

Directions often give a **turn**, measured from the straight-ahead direction. The angle inside the triangle and the turn together make a straight line, so the inside angle is $180^\circ$ minus the turn.

:::example A survey boat
A survey boat leaves a dock $D$ and travels $9\ \text{km}$ to point $T$. It turns $50^\circ$ to the right and travels $5\ \text{km}$ to point $E$. How far is the boat from the dock, to the nearest tenth of a kilometre?
@step Inside angle at $T$: $\angle DTE = 180^\circ - 50^\circ = 130^\circ$.
@step Two sides and the contained angle: $DE^2 = 9^2 + 5^2 - 2(9)(5)\cos 130^\circ$
@step $2(9)(5)\cos 130^\circ \approx -57.85$, so $DE^2 \approx 81 + 25 + 57.85 = 163.85$ and $DE \approx 12.800$
@answer The boat is about $12.8\ \text{km}$ from the dock.
:::

To find the height of a tower you cannot reach, measure the angle of elevation from two points in line with its base. The angle of elevation at the closer point $B$ is outside $\triangle ABT$, so the angle inside the triangle at $B$ is $180^\circ$ minus it.

:::example The height of a tower
From $A$, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is $28^\circ$. After walking $40\ \text{m}$ toward the tower to $B$, it is $47^\circ$. How tall is the tower, to the nearest tenth of a metre?
@step In $\triangle ABT$: $\angle ABT = 180^\circ - 47^\circ = 133^\circ$, so $\angle T = 180^\circ - 28^\circ - 133^\circ = 19^\circ$.
@step Sine law: $BT = \dfrac{40 \sin 28^\circ}{\sin 19^\circ} \approx 57.680$
@step Right triangle: $h = BT \sin 47^\circ \approx 57.680 \times 0.7314 \approx 42.185$
@answer The tower is about $42.2\ \text{m}$ tall.
:::

:::summary
- Matching pair: sine law. Two angles and a side: angle sum, then sine law. Two sides and the contained angle, or three sides: cosine law.
- Sketch, mark what you know, decide, solve, and answer with units.
- A turn is measured from straight ahead: the angle inside the triangle is $180^\circ$ minus the turn.
- Some problems need two steps, such as the sine law in an oblique triangle and then SOH CAH TOA in a right triangle.
- Keep full calculator values until the end, then round.
:::
