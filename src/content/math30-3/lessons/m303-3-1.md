Carpenters, welders and tile setters rely on a few dependable facts about shapes: the angles of a triangle always add to the same total, and the sides and angles of a parallelogram, rhombus, trapezoid or kite always follow the same rules. Knowing those rules lets you find a missing angle or check a frame without measuring everything.

## Angles in triangles

The three interior angles of any triangle add to $180^\circ$. If you know two angles, subtract them from $180^\circ$ to get the third.

:::definition Isosceles triangle
An **isosceles triangle** has two equal sides. The angles opposite the equal sides (the **base angles**) are also equal. An **equilateral triangle** has three equal sides and three $60^\circ$ angles.
:::

:::example A gable roof truss
A gable truss has two equal rafters, so it is an isosceles triangle. The angle at the peak is $110^\circ$. What angle does each rafter make with the bottom chord?
@step The two base angles are equal: call each one $x$.
@step $x + x + 110^\circ = 180^\circ$, so $2x = 70^\circ$.
@answer $x = 35^\circ$. Each rafter meets the bottom chord at $35^\circ$.

## The quadrilateral family

Every quadrilateral can be split by one diagonal into two triangles, so its four angles add to $2 \times 180^\circ = 360^\circ$.

:::definition Special quadrilaterals
- **Parallelogram:** both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
- **Rectangle:** a parallelogram with four right angles.
- **Rhombus:** a parallelogram with four equal sides.
- **Square:** a rectangle that is also a rhombus.
- **Trapezoid:** exactly one pair of parallel sides. In an **isosceles trapezoid**, the two non-parallel sides are equal.
- **Kite:** two pairs of equal sides that are next to each other, and no parallel sides.
:::

On a diagram, matching tick marks show equal sides and matching arrowheads show parallel sides.

:::mistake Picking a name that is too general (or too specific)
A square is also a rectangle, a rhombus and a parallelogram. When a question asks for the **most specific** name, check every property given. Four equal sides with no right angles is a rhombus, not a square.
:::

## Parallelograms, rectangles and rhombuses

In every parallelogram:

- opposite sides are equal and opposite angles are equal,
- angles next to each other (consecutive angles) add to $180^\circ$,
- the diagonals **bisect** each other (they cut each other in half).

:::example One angle gives all four
$ABCD$ is a parallelogram with $\angle A = 64^\circ$. Find the other three angles.
@step $\angle B$ is next to $\angle A$: $180^\circ - 64^\circ = 116^\circ$.
@step $\angle C$ is opposite $\angle A$: $64^\circ$. $\angle D$ is opposite $\angle B$: $116^\circ$.
@answer $\angle B = 116^\circ$, $\angle C = 64^\circ$, $\angle D = 116^\circ$. Check: $64 + 116 + 64 + 116 = 360$.
:::

:::example Diagonals of a rhombus
$PQRS$ is a rhombus with $\angle PQR = 128^\circ$. Its diagonals meet at $E$. Find $\angle PQE$ and $\angle QPE$.
@step Diagonal $QS$ bisects $\angle Q$: $\angle PQE = 128^\circ \div 2 = 64^\circ$.
@step The diagonals are perpendicular, so $\angle PEQ = 90^\circ$.
@step In $\triangle PQE$: $\angle QPE = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 64^\circ = 26^\circ$.
@answer $\angle PQE = 64^\circ$ and $\angle QPE = 26^\circ$
:::

:::mistake Equal diagonals in every parallelogram
Only rectangles (and squares) have equal diagonals. In a slanted parallelogram one diagonal is longer than the other, even though each one is still cut in half by the other.
:::

## Trapezoids and kites

In a trapezoid, the two angles along each slanted side lie between the parallel sides, so they add to $180^\circ$ (they are **co-interior** angles). In an isosceles trapezoid, the two angles on each parallel side are also equal.

Trapezoid $ABCD$ has $AB \parallel DC$, $\angle A = 68^\circ$ and $\angle B = 57^\circ$. Find $\angle D$ and $\angle C$.
@step $\angle A$ and $\angle D$ are co-interior: $\angle D = 180^\circ - 68^\circ = 112^\circ$.

:::example A kite-shaped tile
In kite $KLMN$, $KL = KN$ and $ML = MN$. $\angle K = 70^\circ$ and $\angle M = 124^\circ$. Find $\angle L$.
@step $\angle L = \angle N$, and the four angles add to $360^\circ$.
@step $2\angle L = 360^\circ - 70^\circ - 124^\circ = 166^\circ$
@answer $\angle L = 83^\circ$

:::summary
- Triangle angles add to $180^\circ$. An isosceles triangle has equal base angles.
- Quadrilateral angles add to $360^\circ$.
- Parallelogram: opposite sides and angles equal, consecutive angles add to $180^\circ$, diagonals bisect each other.
- Rectangle: equal diagonals. Rhombus: diagonals cross at $90^\circ$ and bisect the corner angles.
- Trapezoid: angles along each slanted side add to $180^\circ$. Kite: the angles between the unequal sides are equal.
:::
