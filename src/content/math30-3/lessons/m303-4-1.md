A CNC plasma cutter moves its torch around a grid, a sign maker flips a logo so it reads from the other side of a window, and a tile setter turns the same tile to make a pattern. Each of these is a **transformation**: a rule that moves every point of a shape.

## Translations: sliding a shape

:::definition Transformation, image and translation
A **transformation** moves or changes a shape. The new shape is the **image**. The image of point $A$ is called $A'$ ("A prime").

A **translation** slides every point the same distance in the same direction. The shape keeps its size and still faces the same way.
:::

A translation of $a$ units across and $b$ units up or down can be written with **mapping notation**:

$$(x, y) \to (x + a,\ y + b)$$

Right is $+$ and left is $-$ for $a$; up is $+$ and down is $-$ for $b$.

:::example Moving a part on a CNC table
A triangular gusset plate has corners $A(1, 2)$, $B(4, 2)$ and $C(1, 4)$. The program translates it $5$ units left and $3$ units down. Find the image of each corner.
@step The rule is $(x, y) \to (x - 5,\ y - 3)$.
@step $A(1, 2) \to (1 - 5,\ 2 - 3) = (-4, -1)$
@step $B(4, 2) \to (4 - 5,\ 2 - 3) = (-1, -1)$
@step $C(1, 4) \to (1 - 5,\ 4 - 3) = (-4, 1)$
@answer The image is $A'(-4, -1)$, $B'(-1, -1)$, $C'(-4, 1)$.
:::

:::mistake Changing the wrong coordinate
Left and right change only $x$. Up and down change only $y$. Moving $(1, 2)$ "$5$ left" gives $(-4, 2)$, not $(1, -3)$.
:::

## Reflections: flipping a shape

:::definition Reflection
A **reflection** flips a shape over a **line of reflection** (a mirror line). Each image point is the same distance from the line as the original point, on the other side. The image faces the opposite way.
:::

| Line of reflection | Rule |
|---|---|
| the $x$-axis | $(x, y) \to (x, -y)$ |
| the $y$-axis | $(x, y) \to (-x, y)$ |
| the vertical line $x = k$ | $(x, y) \to (2k - x,\ y)$ |
| the horizontal line $y = k$ | $(x, y) \to (x,\ 2k - y)$ |

For a line that is not an axis, counting squares is often easiest: count from the point to the line, then count the same distance past it.

:::example A logo reflected in a window line
A sign maker reflects the logo $ABC$ in the dashed line $x = 1$. Where does $A(4, 1)$ go?
@step $A$ is $4 - 1 = 3$ units right of the line.
@step The image is $3$ units left of the line: $x = 1 - 3 = -2$. The $y$-coordinate stays $1$.
@step Check with the rule: $2(1) - 4 = -2$.
@answer $A'$ is at $(-2, 1)$.
:::

:::mistake Which sign changes?
A reflection in the $x$-axis keeps $x$ and changes the sign of $y$. The axis you reflect in is the coordinate that stays the same: $(3, 5) \to (3, -5)$.
:::

:::

## Rotations about the origin

:::definition Rotation
A **rotation** turns a shape about a fixed point, the **centre of rotation**. A rotation is described by its angle and direction: **clockwise** (the way a clock's hands move) or **counterclockwise**. The image faces the same way; it is turned, not flipped.
:::

For rotations about the origin $(0, 0)$:

| Rotation about the origin | Rule |
|---|---|
| $90^\circ$ counterclockwise | $(x, y) \to (-y, x)$ |
| $180^\circ$ (either direction) | $(x, y) \to (-x, -y)$ |
| $270^\circ$ counterclockwise, the same as $90^\circ$ clockwise | $(x, y) \to (y, -x)$ |

:::example A quarter turn of a tile
A tile setter rotates triangle $ABC$ by $90^\circ$ clockwise about the origin. Find the image of $B(5, 1)$.
@step A $90^\circ$ clockwise turn uses $(x, y) \to (y, -x)$: swap the coordinates, then change the sign of the new second coordinate.
@step $B(5, 1) \to (1, -5)$
@step Check the quadrant: a quarter turn clockwise moves a point in quadrant I to quadrant IV. $(1, -5)$ is in quadrant IV.
@answer $B'$ is at $(1, -5)$.
:::

:::mistake Mixing up the two quarter turns
$(x, y) \to (-y, x)$ is counterclockwise and $(x, y) \to (y, -x)$ is clockwise. If you are unsure, check the quadrant: counterclockwise goes I → II → III → IV, and clockwise goes the other way.
:::

:::check m303-transform-2d 2
:::

## Describing a transformation from a diagram

When you see a shape and its image, ask two questions.

1. **Is the image flipped?** Trace $A \to B \to C$ and $A' \to B' \to C'$. If one goes clockwise and the other counterclockwise, the shape was reflected. The line of reflection is halfway between each point and its image.
2. **If it is not flipped, was it slid or turned?** In a translation every point moves the same amount in the same direction. In a rotation the shape turns to face a new direction.

Then test a rule with two vertices.

:::example Identify the transformation
A patio slab $ABCD$ on a landscape plan was moved to $A'B'C'D'$. Describe the transformation.
@step $A \to B \to C \to D$ goes counterclockwise, and so does $A' \to B' \to C' \to D'$. The slab was not flipped.
@step $A(2, 1) \to A'(-2, -1)$ and $C(5, 3) \to C'(-5, -3)$: both coordinates change sign.
@step That is the rule $(x, y) \to (-x, -y)$.
@answer The slab was rotated $180^\circ$ about the origin.
:::

:::tip Undo it to work backward
To find an original point from its image, undo the move: translate back, reflect again in the same line, or rotate the same angle the other way.
:::

## Two transformations in a row

Patterns in tiles, quilts and logos often use one move after another. Apply them **in order**, writing down the point after each step.

:::example A two-step tile pattern
A corner of a tile is at $P(3, 2)$. It is reflected in the $y$-axis, and then the image is translated $4$ units down. Where does it end up?
@step Reflect in the $y$-axis: $(3, 2) \to (-3, 2)$.
@step Translate $4$ units down: $(-3, 2) \to (-3,\ 2 - 4) = (-3, -2)$.
@answer The corner ends up at $(-3, -2)$.
:::

:::check m303-transform-2d 3
:::

:::summary
- Translation: $(x, y) \to (x + a,\ y + b)$. The shape slides and faces the same way.
- Reflection: in the $x$-axis $(x, -y)$; in the $y$-axis $(-x, y)$; in $x = k$, $x' = 2k - x$; in $y = k$, $y' = 2k - y$. The image is flipped.
- Rotation about the origin: $90^\circ$ counterclockwise $(-y, x)$; $180^\circ$ $(-x, -y)$; $90^\circ$ clockwise (or $270^\circ$ counterclockwise) $(y, -x)$.
- To identify a transformation: check whether it is flipped, then test a rule with two vertices.
- For two transformations, apply them in order.
:::
