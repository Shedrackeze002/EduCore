A surveyor marking a property corner, a framer laying out a roof truss and a crew planning a pipeline around a slough all work with triangles that have no right angle. SOH CAH TOA only works in right triangles. The **sine law** solves the others whenever you know a side and the angle across from it.

## Oblique triangles and matching pairs

:::definition Oblique triangle
An **oblique triangle** is any triangle with no $90^\circ$ angle. It can be **acute** (all three angles less than $90^\circ$) or **obtuse** (one angle greater than $90^\circ$).
:::

Name the vertices with capital letters. Name each side with the lower-case letter of the angle **opposite** it: side $a$ is across from $\angle A$, side $b$ is across from $\angle B$, and side $c$ is across from $\angle C$.

A side and the angle opposite it make a **matching pair**. The sine law is built from matching pairs.

:::tip Biggest across from biggest
The longest side is always opposite the largest angle, and the shortest side is opposite the smallest angle. Use this to check every answer you get.
:::

## The sine law

In any triangle, each side divided by the sine of its opposite angle gives the same number. (You can prove it by drawing the height from one vertex: the height equals $b \sin A$ and also $a \sin B$.)

:::definition The sine law
In any $\triangle ABC$,
$$\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B} = \dfrac{c}{\sin C} \qquad \text{or} \qquad \dfrac{\sin A}{a} = \dfrac{\sin B}{b} = \dfrac{\sin C}{c}$$
Use only two of the ratios at a time. Put the unknown on top: sides on top to find a side, sines on top to find an angle.
:::

:::mistake Calculator in the wrong mode
Every angle in this unit is in degrees. If your calculator is in radian mode, $\sin 74$ gives $-0.985$ instead of $0.961$. Check for "DEG" on the screen before you start.
:::

:::example Two angles and a side opposite one of them
In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A = 42^\circ$, $\angle B = 74^\circ$ and $a = 15\ \text{m}$. Determine $b$, to the nearest tenth of a metre.
@step Side $a$ and $\angle A$ are a matching pair. Side $b$ is opposite $\angle B$.
@step $\dfrac{b}{\sin 74^\circ} = \dfrac{15}{\sin 42^\circ}$
@step Multiply both sides by $\sin 74^\circ$: $b = \dfrac{15 \sin 74^\circ}{\sin 42^\circ} \approx \dfrac{15(0.9613)}{0.6691} \approx 21.549$
@answer $b \approx 21.5\ \text{m}$. Check: $b$ is opposite the larger angle ($74^\circ > 42^\circ$), so it should be longer than $a$. It is.
:::

In $\triangle PQR$, $\angle P = 108^\circ$, $\angle Q = 31^\circ$ and $r = 24.5\ \text{cm}$. Determine $p$, to the nearest tenth of a centimetre.
@step Side $r$ is opposite $\angle R$, which is not given. Angle sum: $\angle R = 180^\circ - 108^\circ - 31^\circ = 41^\circ$.
@step Now $r$ and $\angle R$ are a matching pair: $\dfrac{p}{\sin 108^\circ} = \dfrac{24.5}{\sin 41^\circ}$
@step $p = \dfrac{24.5 \sin 108^\circ}{\sin 41^\circ} \approx 35.516$
@answer $p \approx 35.5\ \text{cm}$. It is the longest side, opposite the obtuse angle.

:::

## Finding an angle

To find an angle, put the sines on top so the unknown is in a numerator. Solve for the sine of the angle, then press $\sin^{-1}$.

:::example An obtuse angle is given
In $\triangle DEF$, $d = 18.6\ \text{m}$, $e = 12.4\ \text{m}$ and $\angle D = 115^\circ$. Determine $\angle E$, to the nearest tenth of a degree.
@step $d$ and $\angle D$ are a matching pair, and $\angle E$ is opposite $e$.
@step $\dfrac{\sin E}{12.4} = \dfrac{\sin 115^\circ}{18.6}$
@step Multiply both sides by $12.4$: $\sin E = \dfrac{12.4 \sin 115^\circ}{18.6} \approx 0.6042$
@step $\angle E = \sin^{-1}(0.6042) \approx 37.17^\circ$
@answer $\angle E \approx 37.2^\circ$. Then $\angle F = 180^\circ - 115^\circ - 37.2^\circ \approx 27.8^\circ$.
:::

:::note Why there is only one answer
In every question in this course, the angle you find with $\sin^{-1}$ is opposite the **shorter** of the two known sides. That angle is smaller than the known angle, so it must be acute, and $\sin^{-1}$ gives it directly. (A triangle can have only one obtuse angle, and it is always opposite the longest side.)
:::

Once you know two angles, the angle sum gives the third, and the sine law can then give the last side. Keep full calculator values until the very end, and round only the final answer.

:::check m303-sine-law 3
:::

:::summary
- An oblique triangle has no right angle. It can be acute or obtuse.
- Label each side with the lower-case letter of its opposite angle. A side and its opposite angle are a matching pair.
- Sine law: $\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B} = \dfrac{c}{\sin C}$. Put the unknown on top.
- If the known side has no known opposite angle, use the angle sum first.
- To find an angle, solve for its sine, then use $\sin^{-1}$. Check that the largest angle is opposite the longest side.
:::
