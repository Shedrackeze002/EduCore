No measurement is perfect, and no part is made exactly to size. Machinists, carpenters, pipe fitters and bakers all work to a **tolerance**: a range of sizes that is close enough. In this lesson you will find that range and decide whether a part passes.

## Uncertainty

When you read a ruler to the nearest millimetre, the true length could be a little more or a little less than the mark you chose. The **uncertainty** says how far off the reading might be.

:::definition Uncertainty
The **uncertainty** of a measurement is $\pm$ half the smallest unit of the instrument. A length read as $4.7\ \text{cm}$ on a ruler marked in millimetres ($0.1\ \text{cm}$) is $4.7 \pm 0.05\ \text{cm}$.
:::

:::example The range of a reading
The bar above is read as $4.7\ \text{cm}$ on a ruler marked in millimetres. Between what least and greatest values is its true length?
@step The smallest unit is $0.1\ \text{cm}$, so the uncertainty is $\pm\tfrac{1}{2}(0.1) = \pm 0.05\ \text{cm}$.
@step Least: $4.7 - 0.05 = 4.65\ \text{cm}$. Greatest: $4.7 + 0.05 = 4.75\ \text{cm}$.
@answer The true length is between $4.65\ \text{cm}$ and $4.75\ \text{cm}$.
:::

The same idea works in imperial units. A tape measure marked in eighths has an uncertainty of $\pm\tfrac{1}{16}\ \text{in}$, so a board read as $20\tfrac{3}{8}\ \text{in}$ is really between $20\tfrac{5}{16}\ \text{in}$ and $20\tfrac{7}{16}\ \text{in}$. (Write $20\tfrac{3}{8}$ as $20\tfrac{6}{16}$, then subtract and add $\tfrac{1}{16}$.)

## Tolerance

:::definition Nominal value and tolerance
The **nominal value** is the target size on a drawing or recipe. The **tolerance** is how far above or below the nominal value a part may be and still be acceptable. A shaft drawn as $25.40 \pm 0.05\ \text{mm}$ has a nominal diameter of $25.40\ \text{mm}$ and a tolerance of $\pm 0.05\ \text{mm}$.
:::

$$\text{greatest} = \text{nominal} + \text{tolerance} \qquad \text{least} = \text{nominal} - \text{tolerance}$$

:::example Machining a shaft
A shaft must be $25.40 \pm 0.05\ \text{mm}$ in diameter. Three finished shafts measure $25.43\ \text{mm}$, $25.47\ \text{mm}$ and $25.34\ \text{mm}$. Which are acceptable?
@step Greatest: $25.40 + 0.05 = 25.45\ \text{mm}$. Least: $25.40 - 0.05 = 25.35\ \text{mm}$.
@step $25.43$ is between the limits. $25.47$ is above $25.45$. $25.34$ is below $25.35$.
@answer Only the $25.43\ \text{mm}$ shaft is within tolerance. The $25.47\ \text{mm}$ shaft is too large, and the $25.34\ \text{mm}$ shaft is too small.
:::

:::tip Check both limits
Write the least and greatest values down before you look at any measurements. A part can fail by being too big or too small.
:::

To go the other way, from the limits to the specification, the nominal value is halfway between the limits and the tolerance is half the gap. Limits of $74.6\ \text{mm}$ and $75.4\ \text{mm}$ give $\dfrac{74.6 + 75.4}{2} = 75.0\ \text{mm}$ and $\dfrac{75.4 - 74.6}{2} = 0.4\ \text{mm}$, so the part is $75.0 \pm 0.4\ \text{mm}$.

## Unequal and percent tolerances

Some drawings allow more room on one side than the other. The size $25^{+0.2}_{-0.1}\ \text{mm}$ (also written $25\ \text{mm}$ $+0.2/-0.1$) may be up to $0.2\ \text{mm}$ over but only $0.1\ \text{mm}$ under.

:::example An unequal tolerance
A bushing has an outside diameter of $25^{+0.2}_{-0.1}\ \text{mm}$. Find the greatest and least acceptable diameters and the tolerance range.
@step Greatest: $25 + 0.2 = 25.2\ \text{mm}$.
@step Least: $25 - 0.1 = 24.9\ \text{mm}$.
@answer Acceptable diameters are from $24.9\ \text{mm}$ to $25.2\ \text{mm}$, a range of $25.2 - 24.9 = 0.3\ \text{mm}$.
:::

Electricians and farmers often see tolerances as a percent. Change the percent into the unit first.

:::example A resistor
A resistor is rated at $470\ \Omega$ with a tolerance of $\pm 5\%$. What are its greatest and least acceptable values?
@step Tolerance: $0.05 \times 470 = 23.5\ \Omega$.
@step Greatest: $470 + 23.5 = 493.5\ \Omega$. Least: $470 - 23.5 = 446.5\ \Omega$.
@answer The resistance may be from $446.5\ \Omega$ to $493.5\ \Omega$.
:::

:::mistake Subtracting the wrong number
In $25^{+0.2}_{-0.1}$, the top number is **added** and the bottom number is **subtracted**. Do not use $\pm 0.2$ for both limits, and do not add a percent such as $5\%$ straight onto the nominal value: $470 + 5$ is not the greatest value.
:::

## Tolerance stacking

When parts are assembled end to end or stacked, their sizes add, and so do their tolerances. This is called **tolerance stacking**. The greatest total happens when every part is at its largest, and the least total when every part is at its smallest.

:::example Three spacers on a bolt
Three spacers are stacked on a bolt: $12.50 \pm 0.02\ \text{mm}$, $8.25 \pm 0.03\ \text{mm}$ and $20.00 \pm 0.05\ \text{mm}$. What are the greatest and least possible thicknesses of the stack?
@step Nominal total: $12.50 + 8.25 + 20.00 = 40.75\ \text{mm}$.
@step Total tolerance: $0.02 + 0.03 + 0.05 = 0.10\ \text{mm}$.
@step Greatest: $40.75 + 0.10 = 40.85\ \text{mm}$. Least: $40.75 - 0.10 = 40.65\ \text{mm}$.
@answer The stack is $40.75 \pm 0.10\ \text{mm}$: from $40.65\ \text{mm}$ to $40.85\ \text{mm}$.
:::

A shaft must also fit inside its hole. The **clearance** is the hole diameter minus the shaft diameter. It is smallest with the smallest hole and the largest shaft. For a hole of $30.10 \pm 0.03\ \text{mm}$ and a shaft of $30.00 \pm 0.02\ \text{mm}$, the least clearance is $30.07 - 30.02 = 0.05\ \text{mm}$ and the greatest is $30.13 - 29.98 = 0.15\ \text{mm}$.

:::check m303-tolerance 3
:::

:::summary
- The uncertainty of a measurement is $\pm$ half the smallest unit of the instrument.
- Greatest value $=$ nominal $+$ tolerance; least value $=$ nominal $-$ tolerance. A part passes if it is between them.
- For $25^{+0.2}_{-0.1}$, add the top number and subtract the bottom number. Change a percent tolerance into the unit first.
- The nominal value is halfway between the limits, and the tolerance is half the gap between them.
- For assembled parts, add the nominal sizes and add the tolerances.
:::
