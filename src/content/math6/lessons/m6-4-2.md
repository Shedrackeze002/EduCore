You can add $2$ apples and $3$ apples, but how do you add $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a pizza and $\dfrac{5}{12}$ of a pizza? The pieces are different sizes. In this lesson, you will make the pieces the same size first.

## Same-size pieces

Fractions with the same denominator have the same unit. The pieces are the same size, so you can add or subtract the numerators.

$$\dfrac{2}{8} + \dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{5}{8}$$

:::definition Common denominator
Two fractions have a **common denominator** when their denominators are the same number. For example, $\dfrac{3}{12}$ and $\dfrac{5}{12}$ have the common denominator $12$.
:::

When the denominators are different, change one or both fractions into **equivalent fractions** with a common denominator. Multiply the numerator and denominator by the same number.

## Related denominators

:::definition Related denominators
Two denominators are **related** when one is a multiple of the other. $4$ and $12$ are related, because $4 \times 3 = 12$. The number $3$ is the **factor** that relates them.
:::

:::example Quarters and twelfths
Find $\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{5}{12}$.
@step The denominators are related: $4 \times 3 = 12$. Use $12$ as the common denominator.
@step Multiply the top and bottom of $\dfrac{1}{4}$ by $3$: $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{3}{12}$.
@step Add the numerators: $\dfrac{3}{12} + \dfrac{5}{12} = \dfrac{8}{12}$.
@step Simplify. Divide the top and bottom by $4$: $\dfrac{8}{12} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
@answer $\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{5}{12} = \dfrac{2}{3}$
:::

## Denominators that are not related

$3$ and $4$ are not related. Neither one is a multiple of the other. The **product** of the denominators is always a common denominator: $3 \times 4 = 12$.

:::example Thirds and quarters
Find $\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{4}$.
@step Multiply the denominators: $3 \times 4 = 12$.
@step $\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{2 \times 4}{3 \times 4} = \dfrac{8}{12}$ and $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1 \times 3}{4 \times 3} = \dfrac{3}{12}$.
@step Add: $\dfrac{8}{12} + \dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac{11}{12}$.
@answer $\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{11}{12}$
:::

:::example Subtracting
Find $\dfrac{4}{5} - \dfrac{1}{2}$.
@step Multiply the denominators: $5 \times 2 = 10$.
@step $\dfrac{4}{5} = \dfrac{8}{10}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{5}{10}$.
@answer $\dfrac{8}{10} - \dfrac{5}{10} = \dfrac{3}{10}$
:::

:::example A sum bigger than 1
Find $\dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{2}{3}$. Write the answer as a mixed number.
@step Common denominator: $4 \times 3 = 12$. So $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{9}{12}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{8}{12}$.
@step Add: $\dfrac{9}{12} + \dfrac{8}{12} = \dfrac{17}{12}$.
@step $17 \div 12 = 1$ R $5$, so $\dfrac{17}{12} = 1\dfrac{5}{12}$.
@answer $\dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{2}{3} = 1\dfrac{5}{12}$
:::

:::tip Finish the job
Always check if your answer is in **lowest terms**. If the numerator and denominator share a factor, divide both by it.
:::

## Solving fraction problems

Read the problem. Decide if you add (put together, in all) or subtract (how much more, how much is left).

:::example Walking to the library
Kiona walked $\dfrac{3}{4}$ km to the park. Then she walked $\dfrac{2}{5}$ km to the library. How far did she walk in all?
@step "In all" means add: $\dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{2}{5}$.
@step Common denominator: $4 \times 5 = 20$. $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{15}{20}$ and $\dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{8}{20}$.
@step $\dfrac{15}{20} + \dfrac{8}{20} = \dfrac{23}{20} = 1\dfrac{3}{20}$.
@answer Kiona walked $1\dfrac{3}{20}$ km in all.
:::

:::example The rest of the garden
In a school garden, tomatoes fill $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the garden and peas fill $\dfrac{1}{4}$. The rest is for flowers. What fraction is for flowers?
@step Add the parts you know: $\dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{4}{12} + \dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac{7}{12}$.
@step The whole garden is $1 = \dfrac{12}{12}$.
@step Subtract: $\dfrac{12}{12} - \dfrac{7}{12} = \dfrac{5}{12}$.
@answer $\dfrac{5}{12}$ of the garden is for flowers.
:::

:::summary
- Fractions with a common denominator have the same-size pieces. Add or subtract the numerators.
- Related denominators: multiply the smaller denominator by the factor that relates them.
- Not related: the product of the denominators is a common denominator.
- Write answers in lowest terms, and as a mixed number when you are asked to.
:::
