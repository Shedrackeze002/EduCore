A prime factorization is like a picture of a number. It shows every number that divides it. In this lesson you will use prime factorizations to find common factors and to decide divisibility without dividing.

## Divisibility by a prime

:::definition Divisible
A number is **divisible** by another number if it divides evenly, with no remainder. $360$ is divisible by $5$ because $360 \div 5 = 72$.
:::

A number is divisible by a prime only if that prime is in its prime factorization.

:::example Divisible by 5? By 7?
A number is $2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5$. Is it divisible by $5$? Is it divisible by $7$?
@step $5$ is one of the prime factors. So the number is $5$ times the other factors, and it is divisible by $5$.
@step The only primes in the factorization are $2$, $3$ and $5$. $7$ is not there, so the number is not divisible by $7$.
@answer Divisible by $5$: yes. Divisible by $7$: no. (The number is $360$, and $360 \div 7$ has a remainder.)
:::

:::tip A power is divisible by its base
$7^4 = 7 \times 7 \times 7 \times 7$. The base $7$ is one of the factors, so $7^4$ is divisible by $7$.
:::

## Common factors

:::definition Common factor
A **common factor** of two numbers is a factor of both numbers. The **greatest common factor** is the largest one.
:::

To find the common factors, write both prime factorizations. Find the primes they share.

:::example Common factors of 24 and 36
List all the common factors of $24$ and $36$.
@step $24 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3$ and $36 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3$.
@step Both lists contain $2 \times 2 \times 3$. Their product is $12$, the greatest common factor.
@step Every factor of $12$ is a common factor: $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$.
@answer The common factors of $24$ and $36$ are $1, 2, 3, 4, 6$ and $12$.
:::

:::mistake Using a shared prime too many times
$24$ has three $2$s and $36$ has two $2$s. They share only **two** $2$s. Use each shared prime only as many times as it appears in **both** lists.
:::

## Divisibility by a composite number

To check a composite divisor, write it as a product of primes too. The number is divisible by it if all of those primes are in the number's prime factorization, repeats included.

:::example Divisible by 12? By 16?
A number is $2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5$. Is it divisible by $12$? Is it divisible by $16$?
@step $12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3$. The number has three $2$s and two $3$s, so it contains $2 \times 2 \times 3$. It is divisible by $12$.
@step $16 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2$. It needs four $2$s, but the number has only three. It is not divisible by $16$.
@answer Divisible by $12$: yes. Divisible by $16$: no. (Check: $360 \div 12 = 30$, and $360 \div 16 = 22.5$.)
:::

:::example A product of two numbers
Without multiplying, decide whether $14 \times 15$ is divisible by $21$.
@step Write each factor as primes: $14 \times 15 = 2 \times 7 \times 3 \times 5$.
@step $21 = 3 \times 7$. Both $3$ and $7$ are in the product.
@answer Yes, $14 \times 15$ is divisible by $21$. (Check: $210 \div 21 = 10$.)
:::

:::mistake One 2 is not enough for 4
$90 = 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 5$ has a $2$, but $4 = 2 \times 2$ needs two $2$s. So $90$ is not divisible by $4$.
:::

:::check m6-common-factors 3
:::

:::summary
- A number is divisible by a prime only if that prime is in its prime factorization. A power is always divisible by its base.
- Common factors come from the primes that two numbers share. Multiply the shared primes to get the greatest common factor. Every factor of it is a common factor.
- A number is divisible by a composite number if all the composite number's prime factors, with repeats, are in the number's prime factorization.
:::
