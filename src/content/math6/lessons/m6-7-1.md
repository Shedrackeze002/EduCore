Look at a butterfly, a snowflake or a patchwork quilt. You can find shapes that match each other exactly. In this lesson you will learn how two matching shapes can be related by a **flip** or a **turn**.

## Reflection symmetry

:::definition Reflection
A **reflection** flips a shape over a line, like a mirror. The line is called the **mirror line**. Each point of the image is the same distance from the mirror line as the matching point of the shape, but on the other side.
:::

Two shapes have **reflection symmetry** when one reflection maps one shape exactly onto the other.

:::example Checking a reflection
Shape 2 is shape 1 reflected in the dashed line $m$. How can you check?
@step Pick a corner of shape 1. The tip of the flag is $2$ squares to the left of line $m$.
@step The tip of shape 2 is $2$ squares to the right of line $m$, at the same height.
@step Every corner matches this way, and shape 2 faces the opposite way.
@answer The shapes are related by **reflection symmetry**.
:::

:::mistake A slide is not a flip
If a copy faces the **same** way as the shape, it was slid, not flipped. A mirror image always faces the opposite way.
:::

## Rotation symmetry

:::definition Rotation
A **rotation** turns a shape about a point, called the **centre of rotation**. A turn can be **clockwise** (the way the hands of a clock move) or **counter-clockwise** (the other way). A quarter turn is $90^\circ$ and a half turn is $180^\circ$.
:::

Two shapes have **rotation symmetry** when one rotation maps one shape exactly onto the other. The image is turned, but it is **not** flipped.

:::example Describing a rotation
Shape 2 is a rotation of shape 1 about point $P$. Describe the rotation.
@step Draw a line from $P$ to a corner of shape 1. It points to the left.
@step The line from $P$ to the matching corner of shape 2 points straight up.
@step Turning from "left" to "up" is a quarter turn, the same way a clock's hands move.
@answer Shape 2 is shape 1 after a quarter turn ($90^\circ$) **clockwise** about $P$.
:::

:::tip Use tracing paper
Trace shape 1 and point $P$. Hold your pencil on $P$ and turn the paper. If the tracing lands on shape 2 without flipping it over, the shapes have rotation symmetry.
:::

:::example A flip, then a turn
Shape 1 is reflected in line $m$. Then the mirror image (the outline) is rotated a half turn about point $P$ to make shape 2. Describe the two moves.
@step First move: a reflection in line $m$. The outline is the mirror image of shape 1.
@step Second move: a half turn ($180^\circ$) about $P$. The outline turns upside down onto shape 2.
@answer Shape 1 is reflected in line $m$, and then rotated a half turn about $P$.
:::

## Symmetry in tessellations

:::definition Tessellation
A **tessellation** covers a flat surface with shapes that fit together with **no gaps** and **no overlaps**. You can see tessellations in floor tiles, brick walls, quilts and beadwork.
:::

In this tessellation of right triangles, tiles 1 and 2 share a side. Fold along that side and tile 1 lands on tile 2. They are related by **reflection symmetry**.

@step The tiles share their longest side, but tile 2 is not a mirror image of tile 1.

Many First Nations and Métis quilters make **star blankets**. A star blanket often has a large eight-pointed star made of many diamond shapes that fit together with no gaps. The simple star below has $8$ congruent diamonds around its centre. Diamond 2 is diamond 1 turned $\dfrac{1}{8}$ of a full turn about the centre. Turning the whole star $\dfrac{1}{8}$ of a turn moves every diamond onto the next one, so the star has rotation symmetry.

:::summary
- A **reflection** flips a shape over a mirror line. The image faces the opposite way.
- A **rotation** turns a shape about a centre point, clockwise or counter-clockwise. The image is not flipped.
- Two shapes related by one reflection have **reflection symmetry**. Two shapes related by one rotation have **rotation symmetry**.
- Some shapes are related by two moves in a row. Do one move at a time.
- A **tessellation** covers a surface with no gaps or overlaps. Neighbouring tiles are often related by reflection or rotation symmetry.
:::
