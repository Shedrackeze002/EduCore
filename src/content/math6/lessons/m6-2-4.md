The class is ordering popcorn for a movie afternoon. A pack of juice boxes is on sale. Friends are splitting the cost of a gift. Each of these problems is solved by multiplying or dividing, often with money.

## Multiply or divide?

Read the problem and picture what is happening.

- **Many equal groups**, and you want the total: **multiply**. "$15$ bags at \$2.35 each" means $2.35 \times 15$.
- **Sharing equally** or finding **one** of something: **divide**. "\$5.28 for $16$ juice boxes" means $5.28 \div 16$.

:::example Buying popcorn
The class buys $15$ bags of popcorn. Each bag costs \$2.35. What is the total cost?
@step Equal groups, find the total: multiply $2.35 \times 15$.
@step Multiply as whole numbers: $235 \times 5 = 1175$ and $235 \times 10 = 2350$. Add: $1175 + 2350 = 3525$.
@step $2.35$ has $2$ decimal places, so the product is $35.25$.
@step Estimate: $2 \times 15 = 30$. The answer is close.
@answer The total cost is \$35.25.
:::

:::mistake Writing money with one decimal place
A product like $7.8 \times 14 = 109.2$ is \$109.20 in money. Always write cents with two digits.
:::

## Finding one of something

:::definition Unit price
The **unit price** is the price of one item. To find it, divide the total price by the number of items.
:::

:::example Price of one juice box
A pack of $16$ juice boxes costs \$5.28. What is the price of one juice box?
@step Divide: $5.28 \div 16$. Put the decimal point in the quotient above the one in $5.28$.
@step $16$ goes into $52$ three times ($48$), leaving $4$. Bring down the $8$: $16$ goes into $48$ three times.
@answer One juice box costs \$0.33.
:::

:::example Cutting a rope
Rosa cuts a rope that is $8.88$ m long into $12$ equal pieces. How long is each piece?
@step Divide: $8.88 \div 12$.
@step $12$ goes into $88$ seven times ($84$), leaving $4$. Bring down the $8$: $12$ goes into $48$ four times.
@answer Each piece is $0.74$ m long.
:::

## Two-step problems

Many problems need two steps. Make a plan before you calculate.

:::example Change after a big order
Simran buys $17$ packs of markers at \$3.15 each. She pays with a \$100 bill. How much change does she get?
@step Step 1: Multiply to find the total: $3.15 \times 17 = 53.55$.
@step Step 2: Subtract from \$100.00: $100.00 - 53.55 = 46.45$.
@answer Simran gets \$46.45 in change.
:::

:::example Sharing a cost
A group of $21$ friends shares the cost of a \$78.70 gift equally. How much does each friend pay, to the nearest cent?
@step Divide: $78.70 \div 21$. Keep dividing to the thousandths: $3.747\ldots$
@step Round to the nearest hundredth (the nearest cent). The thousandths digit is $7$, so round up.
@answer Each friend pays about \$3.75.
:::

:::example Which is the better buy?
Pack A has $12$ granola bars for \$5.40. Pack B has $15$ granola bars for \$6.45. Which pack is the better buy?
@step Pack A: $5.40 \div 12 = 0.45$, so one bar costs \$0.45.
@step Pack B: $6.45 \div 15 = 0.43$, so one bar costs \$0.43.
@answer Pack B is the better buy, even though its total price is higher.
:::

:::tip Think about what the remainder means
Each box holds $30$ cans, and there are $645$ cans. $645 \div 30 = 21$ R $15$. Only $21$ boxes are full, but you need $22$ boxes to pack all the cans.
:::

:::summary
- Multiply to find the total of many equal groups. Divide to share equally or to find the price of one item (the unit price).
- Write money with two decimal places, and round to the nearest cent when a quotient has more digits.
- Make a plan for two-step problems, and check your answer with an estimate.
- Think about what a remainder means in the problem: sometimes you need one more box.
:::
