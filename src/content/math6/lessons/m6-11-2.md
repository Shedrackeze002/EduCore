Kiona has \$20 and saves \$5 every week. When will she have enough for a \$95 pair of skates? An equation for her savings answers this quickly, without listing every week.

## Writing an equation from words

An **equation** for a function shows the rule with variables. The dependent variable goes on the left side of the equal sign.

| Words | Equation |
|---|---|
| $y$ is $4$ times $x$. | $y = 4x$ |
| $y$ is $7$ more than $x$. | $y = x + 7$ |
| Multiply $x$ by $3$, then add $2$. | $y = 3x + 2$ |

$3x$ means $3 \times x$. In $y = 3x + 2$, you multiply first, then add.

:::example From words to an equation
To find $y$, multiply $x$ by $6$, then add $1$. Write an equation.
@step Multiply $x$ by $6$: $6x$
@step Then add $1$: $6x + 1$
@answer $y = 6x + 1$
:::

## Writing an equation from a table

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["$x$","$y$"],"rows":[["0","5"],["1","9"],["2","13"],["3","17"],["4","21"]]}
:::

:::example Finding the equation
Write an equation for the function in the table.
@step Each time $x$ goes up by $1$, $y$ goes up by $4$. So the rule multiplies $x$ by $4$: $4x$.
@step When $x = 0$, $4x = 0$ but $y = 5$. So the rule also adds $5$.
@step Check another row: $x = 3$ gives $4 \times 3 + 5 = 12 + 5 = 17$ ✓
@answer $y = 4x + 5$
:::

:::mistake Using only the change
$y$ goes up by $4$ each row, but $y = x + 4$ is wrong: when $x = 1$ it gives $5$, not $9$. The change tells you what to **multiply** $x$ by. Then find what to add.
:::

:::check m6-function-equation 2
:::

## Finding the dependent value

To find $y$ for a given $x$, replace $x$ with its value and calculate.

:::example Substituting
For $y = 4x + 5$, find $y$ when $x = 12$.
@step Replace $x$ with $12$: $y = 4 \times 12 + 5$
@step Multiply first: $4 \times 12 = 48$. Then add: $48 + 5 = 53$
@answer When $x = 12$, $y = 53$.
:::

:::

## Finding the independent value

Sometimes you know $y$ and need $x$. Here are three strategies.

- **Work backwards:** undo each step in reverse order. Undo "add $5$" by subtracting $5$. Undo "multiply by $4$" by dividing by $4$.
- **Guess and check:** try a value of $x$, see if it gives the right $y$, and adjust.
- **Extend the table:** keep adding rows until you reach the value of $y$.

:::example Working backwards
For $y = 4x + 5$, which value of $x$ gives $y = 41$?
@step The rule multiplies by $4$, then adds $5$. Undo the last step first.
@step Undo "add $5$": $41 - 5 = 36$
@step Undo "multiply by $4$": $36 \div 4 = 9$
@step Check: $4 \times 9 + 5 = 36 + 5 = 41$ ✓
@answer $x = 9$
:::

:::tip Always check
Put your answer back into the equation. If you get the value you started with, your answer is right.
:::

## Solving problems

Kiona has \$20 and saves \$5 each week. Let $w$ be the number of weeks and $s$ be her savings in dollars. Her savings depend on the number of weeks, so $s$ goes on the left.

:::example Kiona's skates
(a) Write an equation for $s$. (b) After how many weeks will Kiona have \$95?
@step (a) Each week adds $5$, so multiply $w$ by $5$. She starts with $20$, so add $20$: $s = 5w + 20$.
@step (b) Put $s = 95$ into the equation: $95 = 5w + 20$.
@step Work backwards: $95 - 20 = 75$, then $75 \div 5 = 15$.
@step Check: $5 \times 15 + 20 = 75 + 20 = 95$ ✓
@answer (a) $s = 5w + 20$ (b) After $15$ weeks.
:::

:::

:::summary
- An equation for a function puts the dependent variable on the left: $y = 3x + 2$.
- From a table, the change in $y$ for each step of $1$ in $x$ is what you multiply $x$ by. Then find what to add.
- To find $y$, replace $x$ with its value. Multiply first, then add.
- To find $x$, work backwards, guess and check, or extend the table. Always check your answer.
:::
