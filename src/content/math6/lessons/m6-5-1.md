Kai's beadwork pattern uses $2$ red beads for every $3$ white beads. If he uses $12$ white beads, how many red beads does he need? Ratios that name the same relationship are called equivalent ratios. They help you scale a pattern or a recipe up or down.

## Ratios compare quantities

:::definition Ratio
A **ratio** compares two quantities. "$2$ red beads for every $3$ white beads" is the ratio $2 : 3$. You read it as "$2$ to $3$". You can also write it as a fraction, $\dfrac{2}{3}$.
:::

The order matters. Red to white is $2 : 3$. White to red is $3 : 2$.

## Equivalent ratios

:::definition Equivalent ratios
**Equivalent ratios** show the same relationship. You make them by multiplying (or dividing) **both** terms by the same number.
:::

A **ratio table** lists equivalent ratios. Each column is the pattern repeated one more time.

:::

$2 : 3$, $4 : 6$, $6 : 9$ and $8 : 12$ are all equivalent. Each one is $2 : 3$ with both terms multiplied by the same number. So for $12$ white beads, Kai needs $8$ red beads.

:::example Are they equivalent?
Are $4 : 6$ and $10 : 15$ equivalent?
@step Write each ratio in simplest form. Divide both terms by their greatest common factor.
@step $4 : 6$: divide both terms by $2$ to get $2 : 3$.
@step $10 : 15$: divide both terms by $5$ to get $2 : 3$.
@answer Both simplify to $2 : 3$, so they are equivalent.
:::

:::mistake Adding the same number
$2 : 3$ and $4 : 5$ are **not** equivalent. Adding $2$ to both terms does not keep the relationship. Check: $2 \times 2 = 4$, but $3 \times 2 = 6$, not $5$.
:::

## Proportions

:::definition Proportion
A **proportion** says that two ratios are equivalent. For example, $\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{8}{12}$, or $2 : 3 = 8 : 12$.
:::

You can use a proportion to find a missing term.

:::example Find the missing term
Solve the proportion $\dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{10}{?}$.
@step Simplify the first ratio: $\dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
@step The top changed from $2$ to $10$: $2 \times 5 = 10$.
@step Multiply the bottom by $5$ too: $3 \times 5 = 15$.
@answer $\dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{10}{15}$. The missing term is $15$.
:::

:::tip Check your answer
Simplify the new ratio. $10 : 15$ simplifies to $2 : 3$, and so does $4 : 6$. They match.
:::

## Solving ratio problems

:::example Pancakes for a crowd
A pancake recipe uses $3$ cups of flour for every $2$ cups of milk. How much flour goes with $8$ cups of milk?
@step Write a proportion: $\dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{?}{8}$ (flour over milk).
@step The milk changed from $2$ to $8$: $2 \times 4 = 8$.
@step Multiply the flour by $4$ too: $3 \times 4 = 12$.
@answer $12$ cups of flour go with $8$ cups of milk.
:::

:::example How many students?
In a Grade 6 class, the ratio of boys to girls is $3 : 4$. There are $12$ girls. How many students are in the class?
@step The girls' term changed from $4$ to $12$: $4 \times 3 = 12$.
@step Multiply the boys' term by $3$: $3 \times 3 = 9$. There are $9$ boys.
@step Add: $9 + 12 = 21$.
@answer There are $21$ students in the class.
:::

:::check m6-equivalent-ratios 3
:::

:::summary
- A ratio compares two quantities, such as $2 : 3$. The order matters.
- Equivalent ratios come from multiplying or dividing both terms by the same number.
- To check if two ratios are equivalent, write both in simplest form.
- A proportion says two ratios are equivalent. Use it to find a missing term.
:::
