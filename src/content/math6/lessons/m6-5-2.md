A train goes $180$ km in $2$ hours. A pack of $6$ juice boxes costs \$4.50. Each of these is a rate. Rates help you compare prices, speeds and much more.

## What is a rate?

:::definition Rate
A **rate** compares two quantities measured in different units, such as kilometres and hours, or dollars and juice boxes.
:::

"$180$ km in $2$ h" is a rate. If the train keeps the same speed, it makes a set of equivalent ratios.

:::figure

:::

Every column shows the same rate. Each one is $90 : 1$ with both terms multiplied by the same number.

## Unit rates

:::definition Unit rate
A **unit rate** is a rate with a second term of $1$. It tells how much for **one**: one hour, one day or one item.
:::

To find a unit rate, divide both terms by the second term.

:::example Speed of a train
A train travels $180$ km in $2$ h. What is its speed in kilometres per hour (km/h)?
@step Speed is a unit rate: the distance in one hour.
@step Divide both terms by $2$: $180 \div 2 = 90$ and $2 \div 2 = 1$.
@answer The speed is $90$ km/h.
:::

:::example Pay per hour
Mia earns \$36 for $4$ hours of babysitting. How much is that per hour?
@step Divide by the number of hours: $36 \div 4 = 9$.
@answer Mia earns \$9 per hour.
:::

## Unit price

:::definition Unit price
The **unit price** is the cost of one item. Unit price = total cost ÷ number of items.
:::

:::example Juice boxes
A pack of $6$ juice boxes costs \$4.50. What is the unit price?
@step Divide the cost by the number of juice boxes: $4.50 \div 6 = 0.75$.
@step Check: $0.75 \times 6 = 4.50$.
@answer The unit price is \$0.75 per juice box.
:::

:::mistake Dividing the wrong way
For a unit price, divide the **cost** by the **number of items**. $6 \div 4.50$ does not tell you the cost of one juice box.
:::

:::

## Using rates to solve problems

A unit rate lets you compare deals and extend a rate to new amounts.

:::example The better buy
Store A sells $4$ granola bars for \$3.00. Store B sells $6$ granola bars for \$4.20. Which is the better buy?
@step Store A: $3.00 \div 4 = 0.75$, so \$0.75 for one bar.
@step Store B: $4.20 \div 6 = 0.70$, so \$0.70 for one bar.
@step \$0.70 is less than \$0.75.
@answer Store B has the better buy.
:::

:::example How far in 5 hours?
A car travels $180$ km in $2$ h. At the same speed, how far does it go in $5$ h?
@step Unit rate: $180 \div 2 = 90$, so $90$ km/h.
@step In $5$ h: $90 \times 5 = 450$.
@answer The car travels $450$ km.
:::

:::tip Find the "one" first
When a problem gives a rate for several items or hours, find the rate for **one** first. Then multiply.
:::

:::check m6-unit-rates 3
:::

:::summary
- A rate compares two quantities with different units.
- A unit rate is the amount for one. Divide both terms by the second term.
- Speed (km/h) and unit price (cost of one item) are unit rates.
- Use unit rates to compare deals and to find amounts for any number of items or hours.
:::
