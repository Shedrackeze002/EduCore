A door swings open around its hinge. A windmill blade turns around its centre. In this lesson you will turn polygons in the Cartesian plane around one of their own vertices.

## Turning about a vertex

:::definition Rotation
A **rotation** turns a shape around a point called the **centre of rotation**. In this lesson the centre is always one of the shape's vertices, so that vertex does not move.
:::

:::definition Clockwise and counter-clockwise
**Clockwise** is the direction the hands of a clock move. **Counter-clockwise** is the opposite direction. A quarter turn is $90^\circ$, a half turn is $180^\circ$, and three quarter turns are $270^\circ$.
:::

To rotate a vertex, describe how to get to it from the centre. Then turn each direction.

:::example Turning one direction
From vertex $A$, vertex $B$ is $2$ units to the left. After a $90^\circ$ clockwise turn about $A$, where is $B'$?
@step Picture a clock hand pointing left (at the 9). Turn it clockwise a quarter turn. It now points up (at the 12).
@answer $B'$ is $2$ units **up** from $A$.
:::

:::example A quarter turn clockwise
Triangle $ABC$ has vertices $A(1,\ 1)$, $B(4,\ 1)$ and $C(1,\ 3)$. Rotate it $90^\circ$ clockwise about $A$. Find $B'$ and $C'$.
@step From $A$, vertex $B$ is $3$ units right. Clockwise, right becomes down: $B'$ is $3$ units down from $A$, so $B'(1,\ -2)$.
@step From $A$, vertex $C$ is $2$ units up. Clockwise, up becomes right: $C'$ is $2$ units right of $A$, so $C'(3,\ 1)$.
@answer $B'(1,\ -2)$ and $C'(3,\ 1)$. $A$ stays at $(1,\ 1)$.
:::

:::mistake Turn about the right point
The centre of rotation here is a **vertex** of the shape, not the origin. Always count from the centre vertex.
:::

## Half turns

A half turn ($180^\circ$) points every direction the opposite way. Clockwise and counter-clockwise give the same result.

:::example A half turn
Rotate triangle $ABC$ with $A(0,\ 1)$, $B(3,\ 2)$ and $C(1,\ 4)$ by $180^\circ$ about $A$. Find $B'$ and $C'$.
@step From $A$, $B$ is $3$ units right and $1$ unit up. After a half turn, $B'$ is $3$ units left and $1$ unit down from $A$: $B'(-3,\ 0)$.
@step From $A$, $C$ is $1$ unit right and $3$ units up. So $C'$ is $1$ unit left and $3$ units down from $A$: $C'(-1,\ -2)$.
@answer $B'(-3,\ 0)$ and $C'(-1,\ -2)$
:::

## Three quarter turns and describing a rotation

Three quarter turns one way end in the same place as one quarter turn the other way. So $270^\circ$ clockwise is the same as $90^\circ$ counter-clockwise.

:::example A 270° turn
Rotate triangle $ABC$ with $A(-1,\ 0)$, $B(2,\ 0)$ and $C(2,\ 2)$ by $270^\circ$ clockwise about $A$.
@step $270^\circ$ clockwise is the same as $90^\circ$ counter-clockwise: right becomes up, and up becomes left.
@step From $A$, $B$ is $3$ units right, so $B'$ is $3$ units up: $B'(-1,\ 3)$.
@step From $A$, $C$ is $3$ units right and $2$ units up, so $C'$ is $3$ units up and $2$ units left: $C'(-3,\ 3)$.
@answer $B'(-1,\ 3)$ and $C'(-3,\ 3)$
:::

To **describe** a rotation, look at one side from the centre and its image. In the last picture, side $AB$ points right and side $AB'$ points up. Right to up is a quarter turn counter-clockwise, or $270^\circ$ clockwise.

:::summary
- A rotation turns a shape about a centre of rotation. Here the centre is a vertex, which does not move.
- To rotate a vertex, describe how to get to it from the centre, then turn each direction.
- $90^\circ$ clockwise: right → down, down → left, left → up, up → right.
- $180^\circ$ reverses every direction. $270^\circ$ one way is the same as $90^\circ$ the other way.
:::
