A class orders $23$ T-shirts at \$4.56 each. A runner does $26$ laps of a $2.9$ km loop. To find the totals, we multiply a decimal number by a natural number. The standard algorithm works for both whole numbers and decimals.

## Multiplying natural numbers

:::definition Partial products
To multiply by a 2-digit number, multiply by the ones digit and by the tens separately. Each answer is a **partial product**. Add the partial products to get the product.
:::

:::example Multiply 347 by 26
Find $347 \times 26$.
@step Multiply by the ones: $347 \times 6 = 2082$.
@step Multiply by the tens: $347 \times 20 = 6940$.
@step Add the partial products:
$$\begin{array}{r} 347 \\ \times\ 26 \\ \hline 2082 \\ +\ 6940 \\ \hline 9022 \end{array}$$
@answer $347 \times 26 = 9022$
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the zero
When you multiply by the $2$ in $26$, you are really multiplying by $20$. The second partial product is $6940$, not $694$. Write a $0$ in the ones place first.
:::

:::

## Multiplying a decimal number

To multiply a decimal number by a natural number:

1. Multiply as if there were no decimal point.
2. Count the decimal places in the decimal number.
3. Give the product the same number of decimal places.

Why does this work? $4.56$ is $456$ hundredths. So $4.56 \times 23$ is $456 \times 23$ hundredths.

:::example Multiply 4.56 by 23
Find $4.56 \times 23$.
@step Multiply as whole numbers: $456 \times 3 = 1368$ and $456 \times 20 = 9120$.
$$\begin{array}{r} 456 \\ \times\ 23 \\ \hline 1368 \\ +\ 9120 \\ \hline 10\,488 \end{array}$$
@step $4.56$ has $2$ decimal places, so the product has $2$ decimal places.
@answer $4.56 \times 23 = 104.88$
:::

:::example Laps of a loop
A runner does $26$ laps of a $2.9$ km loop. How far does she run?
@step Multiply as whole numbers: $29 \times 26 = 174 + 580 = 754$.
@step $2.9$ has $1$ decimal place, so the product has $1$ decimal place.
@answer She runs $75.4$ km.
:::

:::mistake Putting the decimal point in the wrong place
$4.56 \times 23$ is not $1048.8$ or $10.488$. Count the decimal places in $4.56$: there are $2$. So the product is $104.88$.
:::

:::

## Is the answer reasonable?

Estimate to check your product. Round the decimal number to a whole number and the 2-digit number to the nearest ten. Then multiply.

:::example Estimate to check
Check that $4.56 \times 23 = 104.88$ is reasonable.
@step $4.56$ is about $5$ and $23$ is about $20$.
@step $5 \times 20 = 100$.
@answer $104.88$ is close to $100$, so the answer is reasonable.
:::

:::example Spot the mistake
Kai says $6.38 \times 42 = 26.796$. Is that reasonable?
@step Estimate: $6.38$ is about $6$ and $42$ is about $40$, so the product is about $6 \times 40 = 240$.
@step $26.796$ is about ten times smaller than $240$. The decimal point is in the wrong place.
@answer Not reasonable. The product should have $2$ decimal places: $6.38 \times 42 = 267.96$.
:::

:::tip Use a whole-number product
If you know $347 \times 26 = 9022$, then $3.47 \times 26 = 90.22$. Just count the decimal places, and check with an estimate: $3 \times 30 = 90$.
:::

:::check m6-decimal-multiply 3
:::

:::summary
- To multiply by a 2-digit number, find the partial products for the ones and the tens, then add them.
- To multiply a decimal number, multiply as if there were no decimal point. Then give the product the same number of decimal places as the decimal number.
- Estimate to check: round to friendly numbers and multiply. A misplaced decimal point makes the answer about ten times too big or too small.
:::
