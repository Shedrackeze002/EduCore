How much change do you get at the store? How far is it to the end of the bike trail? Problems with money and metric measurements are solved by adding and subtracting. The standard algorithms make this reliable every time.

## Adding with the standard algorithm

:::definition Standard algorithm
A **standard algorithm** is a set of steps that always works. For adding and subtracting, you write the numbers in columns and work one place value at a time, starting on the right.
:::

To add decimal numbers:

1. Line up the decimal points, so ones are under ones and tenths are under tenths.
2. Write zeros so the numbers have the same number of decimal places.
3. Add each column, starting on the right. Regroup when a column adds to $10$ or more.
4. Put the decimal point in the answer right below the others.

:::example Buying sports gear
Owen buys shin pads for \$12.65 and a skipping rope for \$8.70. What is the total cost?
@step Line up the decimal points:
$$\begin{array}{r} 12.65 \\ +\ 8.70 \\ \hline 21.35 \end{array}$$
@step Hundredths: $5 + 0 = 5$. Tenths: $6 + 7 = 13$, write $3$ and regroup $1$. Ones: $2 + 8 + 1 = 11$, write $1$ and regroup $1$. Tens: $1 + 1 = 2$.
@answer The total cost is \$21.35.
:::

:::mistake Lining up the last digits instead of the decimal points
To add $4.5 + 2.35$, do not line up the $5$s. Write $4.5$ as $4.50$ and line up the decimal points. The sum is $6.85$, not $2.80$.
:::

## Subtracting with the standard algorithm

Subtracting works the same way: line up the decimal points and start on the right. When a top digit is smaller than the digit below it, regroup from the next place to the left.

:::example Making change
Amara pays for a \$13.45 book with a \$20 bill. How much change does she get?
@step Write \$20 as $20.00$ so both numbers have two decimal places.
$$\begin{array}{r} 20.00 \\ -\ 13.45 \\ \hline 6.55 \end{array}$$
@step There are zeros on top, so regroup: $20.00$ is $19$ ones, $9$ tenths and $10$ hundredths.
@step Check by adding: $6.55 + 13.45 = 20.00$.
@answer Amara gets \$6.55 in change.
:::

:::example Cutting a board
A board is $5.4$ m long. Wei cuts off $4.18$ m. How long is the piece that is left?
@step Write $5.4$ as $5.40$ and line up the decimal points.
$$\begin{array}{r} 5.40 \\ -\ 4.18 \\ \hline 1.22 \end{array}$$
@answer The piece that is left is $1.22$ m long.
:::

:::tip Check subtraction with addition
Add your answer to the number you subtracted. You should get the number you started with.
:::

## Solving two-step problems

Some problems need two steps. Read the problem carefully and make a plan:

- "Total", "in all" and "altogether" usually mean add.
- "Change", "left", "how much more" and "difference" usually mean subtract.

:::example Getting change
Isabelle buys a binder for \$12.50 and scissors for \$16.25. She pays with a \$50 bill. How much change should she get?
@step Step 1: Add to find the total cost.
$$\begin{array}{r} 12.50 \\ +\ 16.25 \\ \hline 28.75 \end{array}$$
@step Step 2: Subtract the total from \$50.00.
$$\begin{array}{r} 50.00 \\ -\ 28.75 \\ \hline 21.25 \end{array}$$
@answer Isabelle should get \$21.25 in change.
:::

:::example Biking a trail
Darius’s family is biking a trail that is $25.4$ km long. They bike $10.2$ km on Saturday and $5.6$ km on Sunday. How much of the trail is left?
@step Step 1: Add the two days: $10.2 + 5.6 = 15.8$ km.
@step Step 2: Subtract from the length of the trail: $25.4 - 15.8 = 9.6$ km.
@step Estimate to check: about $25 - 16 = 9$. The answer $9.6$ is close.
@answer $9.6$ km of the trail is left.
:::

:::

:::summary
- The standard algorithm: line up the place values (and the decimal points), then add or subtract one column at a time from the right.
- Write zeros so both numbers have the same number of decimal places: $4.5 = 4.50$, and \$20 is \$20.00.
- Regroup when a column adds to $10$ or more, or when a top digit is too small to subtract from.
- For two-step problems, make a plan: what do you find first? Check with an estimate or by adding back.
:::
