Sails, roofs and pennants are often triangles. In this lesson you will see that every triangle is half of a parallelogram, and use that to find its area.

## Half of a parallelogram

Draw a diagonal across a parallelogram. It cuts the parallelogram into **two congruent triangles**. Each triangle has the same base and height as the parallelogram, and half of its area.

$$\text{Area of a triangle} = (\text{base} \times \text{height}) \div 2 \qquad A = (b \times h) \div 2$$

:::example Area of a triangle
The parallelogram above has a base of $8\ \text{cm}$ and a height of $5\ \text{cm}$. Find the area of one of the triangles.
@step The parallelogram has an area of $8 \times 5 = 40$, so $40\ \text{cm}^2$.
@step Each triangle is half of it: $40 \div 2 = 20$.
@answer Each triangle has an area of $20\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

## The height of a triangle

The **height** of a triangle is the distance from the base straight up to the opposite corner. It meets the base at a right angle.

- In a right triangle, the two sides that make the right angle are a base and a height.
- In some triangles the height is **outside** the triangle. Extend the base with a dashed line to meet it.

:::example A height outside the triangle
Find the area of the triangle above: base $6\ \text{m}$ and height $4\ \text{m}$.
@step $A = (b \times h) \div 2 = (6 \times 4) \div 2$
@step $24 \div 2 = 12$
@answer The area is $12\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Use the height, not a slanted side
In this triangle, the slanted side is $6.5\ \text{cm}$, but the height is $6\ \text{cm}$. The area is $(10 \times 6) \div 2 = 30$, so $30\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::example A missing height
A triangle has an area of $24\ \text{cm}^2$ and a base of $8\ \text{cm}$. Find its height.
@step Double the area to get the parallelogram with the same base and height: $24 \times 2 = 48$.
@step $h = 48 \div 8 = 6$
@step Check: $(8 \times 6) \div 2 = 24$ ✓
@answer The height is $6\ \text{cm}$.
:::

## Same base, same height, same area

These three triangles share the same base. Their top corners are all on a line parallel to the base, so they all have the same height.

:::example Which triangle is biggest?
Which triangle has the greatest area?
@step Each triangle has a base of $6\ \text{cm}$ and a height of $4\ \text{cm}$.
@step Each area is $(6 \times 4) \div 2 = 12$, so $12\ \text{cm}^2$.
@answer They all have the same area, even though they look different.
:::

:::tip Triangles with the same base and height
Two triangles with the same base and the same height **always** have the same area.
:::

:::summary
- A diagonal cuts a parallelogram into two congruent triangles.
- Area of a triangle $= (\text{base} \times \text{height}) \div 2$.
- The height meets the base at a right angle. It can be a side of a right triangle or fall outside the triangle.
- To find a missing base or height, double the area, then divide.
- Triangles with the same base and height have the same area.
:::
