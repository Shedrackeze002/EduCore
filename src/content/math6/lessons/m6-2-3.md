Twelve friends share a bill. A rope is cut into $17$ equal pieces. To share or split equally, we divide. Long division is the standard algorithm for dividing, and it works with decimal numbers too.

## Long division

:::definition Dividend, divisor and quotient
In $391 \div 17 = 23$, the number being divided ($391$) is the **dividend**. The number you divide by ($17$) is the **divisor**. The answer ($23$) is the **quotient**.
:::

Long division repeats four steps: **divide, multiply, subtract, bring down**.

:::example Divide 391 by 17
Find $391 \div 17$.
@step $17$ does not go into $3$, so use $39$. $17$ goes into $39$ two times: $2 \times 17 = 34$ and $39 - 34 = 5$.
@step Bring down the $1$ to make $51$. $17$ goes into $51$ three times: $3 \times 17 = 51$ and $51 - 51 = 0$.
@step Check by multiplying: $23 \times 17 = 391$.
@answer $391 \div 17 = 23$
:::

:::tip Use friendly multiples
Write a few multiples of the divisor first: $17,\ 34,\ 51,\ 68,\ 85, \ldots$ Then you can see how many times it goes in.
:::

## Dividing a decimal number

Divide a decimal number the same way. Put the decimal point in the quotient right above the decimal point in the dividend.

:::example Divide 7.82 by 17
A $7.82$ m rope is cut into $17$ equal pieces. How long is each piece?
@step Put the decimal point in the quotient above the one in $7.82$.
@step $17$ does not go into $7$, so write $0$ in the ones place and use $78$. $17$ goes into $78$ four times: $4 \times 17 = 68$ and $78 - 68 = 10$.
@step Bring down the $2$ to make $102$. $17$ goes into $102$ six times: $6 \times 17 = 102$ and $102 - 102 = 0$.
@answer $7.82 \div 17 = 0.46$, so each piece is $0.46$ m long.
:::

## Remainders as decimals

Sometimes there is a remainder. You can keep dividing: write a decimal point and zeros after the dividend. The zeros do not change its value.

:::example Divide 351 by 12
Find $351 \div 12$. Write the quotient as a decimal number.
@step $12$ goes into $35$ two times ($24$), leaving $11$. Bring down the $1$: $12$ goes into $111$ nine times ($108$), leaving $3$.
@step So far, $351 \div 12 = 29$ R $3$. Write $351$ as $351.00$ and keep going.
@step Bring down a $0$ to make $30$. $12$ goes into $30$ two times ($24$), leaving $6$.
@step Bring down another $0$ to make $60$. $12$ goes into $60$ five times ($60$), leaving $0$.
@answer $351 \div 12 = 29.25$
:::

:::mistake The remainder is not the decimal part
$351 \div 12$ is $29$ R $3$, but it is not $29.3$. The remainder $3$ means $3$ out of $12$ left over, and $\dfrac{3}{12} = 0.25$. So the quotient is $29.25$.
:::

:::example From a remainder to a decimal
Hamid found $437 \div 25 = 17$ R $12$. Write the quotient as a decimal number.
@step The remainder is $12$ out of $25$: $\dfrac{12}{25} = \dfrac{48}{100} = 0.48$.
@answer $437 \div 25 = 17.48$
:::

## Estimating and rounding quotients

Estimate to check a quotient. Use friendly numbers that divide easily.

:::example Is it reasonable?
Ava says $93.6 \div 24 = 39$. Is that reasonable?
@step $24$ is about $25$, and $93.6$ is about $100$. $100 \div 25 = 4$.
@step $39$ is about ten times too big. The decimal point is in the wrong place.
@answer Not reasonable. The quotient is $3.9$.
:::

When a question asks you to round, divide one place past the place you need. Then round.

:::example Round to the nearest tenth
Find $437 \div 25$ to the nearest tenth.
@step Divide to the hundredths: $437 \div 25 = 17.48$.
@step The hundredths digit is $8$. It is $5$ or more, so round the tenths up.
@answer $437 \div 25 \approx 17.5$
:::

:::

:::summary
- Long division: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down. Repeat.
- When the dividend is a decimal number, put the decimal point in the quotient right above it.
- A remainder can become a decimal: write a decimal point and zeros after the dividend and keep dividing. $351 \div 12 = 29.25$.
- Estimate with friendly numbers to check that the quotient is reasonable.
:::
