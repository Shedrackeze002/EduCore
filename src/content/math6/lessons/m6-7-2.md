Two puzzle pieces fit the same hole if they have exactly the same size and shape. Mathematicians have a special word for shapes like this: **congruent**. In this lesson you will decide when shapes are congruent, even when they face different ways.

## What congruent means

:::definition Congruent
Two shapes are **congruent** if they have the same size and the same shape. One shape can be moved (slid, turned or flipped) to cover the other exactly.
:::

Congruence does **not** depend on where a shape is or which way it faces.

:::example Which shapes are congruent?
Is shape A congruent to shape S? Is shape B?
@step Shape A is shape S turned a quarter turn. Every side has the same length: the long arm is $3$ squares and the short arm is $2$ squares.
@step Shape B has the same shape, but every side is twice as long. It is bigger.
@answer Shape A is congruent to shape S. Shape B is **not** congruent to shape S.
:::

:::mistake Same shape is not enough
A bigger or smaller copy has the same shape, but not the same size. Congruent shapes must match in **size** too.
:::

:::tip Trace it
Trace one shape. Turn or flip the tracing. If it covers the other shape exactly, the shapes are congruent.
:::

## Matching sides

When two shapes are congruent, their **matching sides** are equal. The shortest side matches the shortest side, and the longest side matches the longest side.

:::example Finding a matching side
Triangle 2 is congruent to triangle 1. How long is the side marked **?**
@step Triangle 1 has sides $5\ \text{cm}$, $7\ \text{cm}$ and $8\ \text{cm}$.
@step In triangle 2, the side marked ? is longer than one side and shorter than another. It is the middle-length side.
@step The middle-length side of triangle 1 is $7\ \text{cm}$.
@answer The side marked ? is $7\ \text{cm}$ long.
:::

## Symmetry and congruence

A reflection or a rotation moves a shape without changing its size or shape. So **shapes related by symmetry are always congruent**.

But **congruent shapes are not always related by symmetry**. Look at these two shapes. They are congruent, and they face exactly the same way. Shape 2 is only slid over. No single reflection and no single rotation maps shape 1 onto shape 2.

@step The side lengths and corners match, so the shapes are congruent.

:::summary
- **Congruent** shapes have the same size and the same shape, in any position or orientation.
- Matching sides of congruent shapes are equal.
- Shapes related by reflection or rotation symmetry are always congruent.
- Congruent shapes are not always related by symmetry: a shape that is only slid over is congruent, but not related by one reflection or one rotation.
:::
