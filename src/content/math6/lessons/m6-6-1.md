What is $2 + 3 \times 4$? If you add first, you get $20$. If you multiply first, you get $14$. Only one answer can be right! Mathematicians agree on an order, so everyone gets the same answer.

## The order of operations

:::definition Order of operations
The **order of operations** is the order everyone uses to evaluate an expression:
1. **Parentheses**: work inside them first.
2. **Powers**: evaluate them next.
3. **Multiply and divide**, from left to right.
4. **Add and subtract**, from left to right.
:::

So $2 + 3 \times 4 = 2 + 12 = 14$. Multiplying comes before adding.

| Step | What to do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parentheses | $(2 + 3) \times 4 = 5 \times 4$ |
| 2 | Powers | $2 + 3^{2} = 2 + 9$ |
| 3 | $\times$ and $\div$, left to right | $12 \div 4 \times 2 = 3 \times 2$ |
| 4 | $+$ and $-$, left to right | $10 - 4 + 1 = 6 + 1$ |

## Powers come early

Remember: a **power** such as $2^{3}$ means $2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8$. The **base** is $2$ and the **exponent** is $3$.

:::example A power and an addition
Evaluate $5 + 2^{3}$.
@step There are no parentheses. Do the power first: $2^{3} = 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8$.
@step Then add: $5 + 8 = 13$.
@answer $5 + 2^{3} = 13$
:::

:::example A power and a multiplication
Evaluate $3 \times 4^{2}$.
@step Do the power first: $4^{2} = 4 \times 4 = 16$.
@step Then multiply: $3 \times 16 = 48$.
@answer $3 \times 4^{2} = 48$
:::

:::mistake Two common power mistakes
- $4^{2}$ is $4 \times 4 = 16$, **not** $4 \times 2 = 8$.
- In $3 \times 4^{2}$, the exponent belongs only to the $4$. Do not multiply $3 \times 4$ first. $(3 \times 4)^{2} = 144$, but $3 \times 4^{2} = 48$.
:::

## Parentheses come first

Parentheses change the order. Work inside them before anything else.

:::example With and without parentheses
Evaluate $(2 + 3)^{2}$ and $2 + 3^{2}$.
@step $(2 + 3)^{2}$: parentheses first, $2 + 3 = 5$. Then $5^{2} = 25$.
@step $2 + 3^{2}$: no parentheses, so the power first, $3^{2} = 9$. Then $2 + 9 = 11$.
@answer $(2 + 3)^{2} = 25$, but $2 + 3^{2} = 11$.
:::

:::example Four steps
Evaluate $(6 - 2)^{2} \div 8 + 3 \times 5$.
@step Parentheses: $6 - 2 = 4$. Now it is $4^{2} \div 8 + 3 \times 5$.
@step Power: $4^{2} = 16$. Now it is $16 \div 8 + 3 \times 5$.
@step Divide and multiply: $16 \div 8 = 2$ and $3 \times 5 = 15$. Now it is $2 + 15$.
@answer $(6 - 2)^{2} \div 8 + 3 \times 5 = 17$
:::

:::check m6-order-of-operations 2
:::

## Longer expressions

Sometimes there is a power **inside** the parentheses. Follow the same order inside the parentheses first.

:::example A power inside parentheses
Evaluate $2 \times (5^{2} - 13) + 4$.
@step Inside the parentheses, the power comes first: $5^{2} = 25$. Then $25 - 13 = 12$.
@step Now it is $2 \times 12 + 4$. Multiply: $2 \times 12 = 24$.
@step Add: $24 + 4 = 28$.
@answer $2 \times (5^{2} - 13) + 4 = 28$
:::

:::tip Rewrite after each step
Write the whole expression again after each step. You can see what is left, and you will not skip a step.
:::

:::check m6-order-of-operations 3
:::

:::summary
- Order of operations: parentheses, then powers, then $\times$ and $\div$ from left to right, then $+$ and $-$ from left to right.
- A power such as $4^{2}$ means $4 \times 4$, not $4 \times 2$.
- An exponent belongs only to the number right before it, unless there are parentheses.
- Rewrite the expression after each step.
:::
