Every whole number greater than $1$ is built from special numbers called primes, the way words are built from letters. In this lesson you will find the prime "building blocks" of a number with a factor tree.

## Prime and composite numbers

:::definition Factor
A **factor** of a number divides it evenly, with no remainder. The factors of $12$ are $1, 2, 3, 4, 6$ and $12$.
:::

:::definition Prime and composite numbers
A **prime number** has exactly two factors: $1$ and itself. Examples: $2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13$.
A **composite number** has more than two factors. Examples: $4, 6, 9, 12, 15$.
:::

:::note The number 1
$1$ has only one factor, itself. So $1$ is neither prime nor composite.
:::

:::example Is 51 prime?
Is $51$ a prime number or a composite number?
@step Try dividing by small primes. $51$ is odd, so $2$ does not work.
@step The digits add to $5 + 1 = 6$, so try $3$: $51 \div 3 = 17$.
@step $51 = 3 \times 17$, so $51$ has the factors $1, 3, 17$ and $51$.
@answer $51$ has more than two factors, so it is composite.
:::

:::mistake Odd numbers are not always prime
$9$, $15$, $21$, $27$, $49$ and $51$ are all odd, but they are composite. For example, $49 = 7 \times 7$. Always check for factors.
:::

:::tip Quick checks
A number is divisible by $2$ if its last digit is even, by $5$ if its last digit is $0$ or $5$, and by $3$ if the sum of its digits is divisible by $3$.
:::

## Factor trees

:::definition Prime factorization
The **prime factorization** of a number writes it as a product of prime numbers. For example, $12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3$.
:::

:::definition Factor tree
A **factor tree** splits a number into two factors, then splits each composite factor again. The tree stops when every branch ends in a prime number.
:::

:::example A factor tree for 60
Write the prime factorization of $60$.
@step Split $60$ into two factors: $60 = 6 \times 10$.
@step Split each composite factor: $6 = 2 \times 3$ and $10 = 2 \times 5$.
@step Every branch now ends in a prime (the orange dots): $2, 3, 2, 5$.
@step Check: $2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5 = 4 \times 15 = 60$.
@answer $60 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5$
:::

You can start a tree in a different way. This tree starts with $60 = 4 \times 15$. It ends with the same primes!

## A product in many ways

A composite number can be written as a product in many ways, with two, three or more factors:

$$60 = 2 \times 30 = 3 \times 4 \times 5 = 2 \times 2 \times 15 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5$$

:::definition Associative property
When you multiply three or more numbers, you can group them in any way. The product stays the same: $(2 \times 5) \times 7 = 2 \times (5 \times 7) = 70$.
:::

:::example Group to make it easy
Find $25 \times 13 \times 4$.
@step The order and grouping of factors do not change the product.
@step Group the friendly pair first: $(25 \times 4) \times 13$.
@step $25 \times 4 = 100$ and $100 \times 13 = 1300$.
@answer $25 \times 13 \times 4 = 1300$
:::

:::check m6-prime-factorization 3
:::

:::summary
- A prime number has exactly two factors. A composite number has more than two. $1$ is neither.
- A factor tree splits a number until every branch ends in a prime.
- The prime factorization writes a number as a product of primes: $60 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5$. Every factor tree for a number gives the same primes.
- A product can be made in many ways, and factors can be grouped in any way (the associative property).
:::
