A muffin recipe uses $\dfrac{3}{4}$ cup of milk. How much milk do you need for $6$ batches? A class has $28$ students, and $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of them bring a lunch. How many is that? Both questions multiply a fraction and a natural number.

## Multiplying is repeated addition

$4 \times \dfrac{2}{3}$ means $4$ groups of $\dfrac{2}{3}$. Add $\dfrac{2}{3}$ four times.

:::example Four groups of two thirds
Find $4 \times \dfrac{2}{3}$.
@step $4 \times \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{2}{3}$.
@step The pieces are all thirds, so add the numerators: $2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8$. That is $\dfrac{8}{3}$.
@step $8 \div 3 = 2$ R $2$, so $\dfrac{8}{3} = 2\dfrac{2}{3}$.
@answer $4 \times \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{8}{3} = 2\dfrac{2}{3}$
:::

There is a shortcut. Multiply the natural number by the numerator. Keep the denominator.

$$4 \times \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{4 \times 2}{3} = \dfrac{8}{3}$$

:::mistake Multiplying the denominator too
$4 \times \dfrac{2}{3}$ is **not** $\dfrac{8}{12}$. You have $4$ groups of thirds, so the pieces are still thirds. Only the numerator is multiplied.
:::

## Multiplying by a unit fraction

:::definition Unit fraction
A **unit fraction** has a numerator of $1$, such as $\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\dfrac{1}{4}$ or $\dfrac{1}{10}$.
:::

Multiplying by a unit fraction is the same as dividing by its denominator.

:::example A quarter of twelve
Find $12 \times \dfrac{1}{4}$.
@step $12 \times \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{12}{4}$.
@step $\dfrac{12}{4}$ means $12 \div 4$.
@answer $12 \times \dfrac{1}{4} = 3$
:::

## A fraction of a quantity

$\dfrac{3}{4} \times 20$ means $\dfrac{3}{4}$ **of** $20$. Split $20$ into $4$ equal groups, then take $3$ of the groups.

:::example Three quarters of twenty
Find $\dfrac{3}{4} \times 20$.
@step One group: $20 \div 4 = 5$.
@step Three groups: $3 \times 5 = 15$.
@step Or use the rule: multiply the numerator by $20$ and keep the denominator. $\dfrac{3 \times 20}{4} = \dfrac{60}{4} = 15$.
@answer $\dfrac{3}{4} \times 20 = 15$
:::

:::example When it does not divide evenly
Find $5 \times \dfrac{3}{4}$. Write the answer as a mixed number.
@step Multiply the numerator by $5$: $\dfrac{5 \times 3}{4} = \dfrac{15}{4}$.
@step $15 \div 4 = 3$ R $3$.
@answer $5 \times \dfrac{3}{4} = 3\dfrac{3}{4}$
:::

## Bigger or smaller?

Multiplication does not always make a bigger number.

- $12 \times \dfrac{3}{4} = 9$. The fraction is less than $1$, so the answer is **less** than $12$.
- $12 \times \dfrac{5}{4} = 15$. The fraction is more than $1$, so the answer is **more** than $12$.
- $12 \times \dfrac{4}{4} = 12$. The fraction equals $1$, so the answer is $12$.

:::example Milk for muffins
A muffin recipe uses $\dfrac{3}{4}$ cup of milk for each batch. Leilani makes $6$ batches. How much milk does she need?
@step Find $6 \times \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{6 \times 3}{4} = \dfrac{18}{4}$.
@step Simplify: $\dfrac{18}{4} = \dfrac{9}{2}$.
@step $9 \div 2 = 4$ R $1$, so $\dfrac{9}{2} = 4\dfrac{1}{2}$.
@answer Leilani needs $4\dfrac{1}{2}$ cups of milk.
:::

:::example Who does not bring a lunch?
There are $28$ students in a class. $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of them bring a lunch from home. How many do **not** bring a lunch?
@step $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of $28$: $28 \div 4 = 7$, then $3 \times 7 = 21$. So $21$ students bring a lunch.
@step The rest: $28 - 21 = 7$.
@answer $7$ students do not bring a lunch from home.
:::

:::tip Check that it makes sense
$\dfrac{3}{4}$ is less than $1$, so $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of $28$ must be less than $28$. $21$ is less than $28$, so the answer makes sense.
:::

:::summary
- A natural number times a fraction is repeated addition: $4 \times \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{2}{3}$.
- Multiply the natural number by the numerator and keep the denominator.
- Multiplying by a unit fraction is dividing by its denominator.
- A fraction times a number is a fraction of that quantity. A fraction less than $1$ gives a smaller number.
:::
