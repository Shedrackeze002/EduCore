Counting cubes works for small models. But how do you find the volume of a moving truck or an aquarium? In this lesson you will find volume by multiplying.

## From cubes to a formula

:::definition Right rectangular prism
A **right rectangular prism** is a box shape. It has $6$ rectangular faces, and its edges meet at right angles.
:::

This prism is made of centimetre cubes. The bottom layer is $5$ cubes long and $3$ cubes wide, so it has $5 \times 3 = 15$ cubes. There are $4$ layers.

:::figure
{"type":"cubes","heights":[[4,4,4,4,4],[4,4,4,4,4],[4,4,4,4,4]],"showFront":true}
:::

$15 \times 4 = 60$, so the volume is $60\ \text{cm}^3$. We can get the same answer by multiplying the length, width and height:

$$V = l \times w \times h$$

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"rectPrism","labels":{"l":"5 cm","w":"3 cm","h":"4 cm"}}
:::

:::example Using the formula
Find the volume of a box that is $5\ \text{cm}$ long, $3\ \text{cm}$ wide and $4\ \text{cm}$ high.
@step Area of the base: $5 \times 3 = 15$, so $15\ \text{cm}^2$.
@step $V = 15 \times 4 = 60$
@answer The volume is $60\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

## Base area times height

A prism is like its base stacked up, layer after layer. So we can also write:

$$V = \text{area of the base} \times \text{height}$$

:::example Using the base area
A storage shed has a floor (base) with an area of $20\ \text{m}^2$. It is $3\ \text{m}$ high. Find its volume.
@step Each layer $1\ \text{m}$ thick holds $20\ \text{m}^3$, and there are $3$ layers.
@step $V = 20 \times 3 = 60$
@answer The volume of the shed is $60\ \text{m}^3$.
:::

## Finding a missing dimension

If you know the volume and two of the dimensions, multiply the two you know, then **divide**.

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"rectPrism","labels":{"l":"6 m","w":"4 m","h":"?"}}
:::

:::example A missing height
A storage unit has a volume of $72\ \text{m}^3$. It is $6\ \text{m}$ long and $4\ \text{m}$ wide. How high is it?
@step The base has an area of $6 \times 4 = 24$, so $24\ \text{m}^2$.
@step $h = 72 \div 24 = 3$
@step Check: $6 \times 4 \times 3 = 72$ ✓
@answer The storage unit is $3\ \text{m}$ high.
:::

:::mistake Divide by the base area
Divide the volume by the product of **both** known dimensions. Dividing $72$ by only $6$ or only $4$ gives the wrong answer.
:::

:::

## Solving volume problems

:::example An aquarium
A class aquarium is $50\ \text{cm}$ long, $20\ \text{cm}$ wide and $30\ \text{cm}$ high. What is its volume?
@step $50 \times 20 = 1000$, so the base is $1000\ \text{cm}^2$.
@step $1000 \times 30 = 30\,000$
@answer The volume is $30\,000\ \text{cm}^3$.
:::

:::example A sandbox
A sandbox is $3\ \text{m}$ long and $2\ \text{m}$ wide. Sand fills it to a depth of $0.5\ \text{m}$. How much sand is needed?
@step The sand is a rectangular prism. Its height is the depth: $0.5\ \text{m}$.
@step Base: $3 \times 2 = 6$. Volume: $6 \times 0.5 = 3$.
@answer The sandbox needs $3\ \text{m}^3$ of sand.
:::

:::tip Choose an easy order
You can multiply the three dimensions in any order. Start with a pair that is easy, like $50 \times 20 = 1000$.
:::

:::check m6-volume-prism 3
:::

:::summary
- A right rectangular prism is made of equal layers of cubes.
- $V = l \times w \times h$, or $V = \text{area of the base} \times \text{height}$.
- Volume is measured in cubic units, such as $\text{cm}^3$ or $\text{m}^3$.
- To find a missing dimension, divide the volume by the product of the two known dimensions.
:::
