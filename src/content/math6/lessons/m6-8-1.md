How does a map tell you exactly where the treasure is? It uses a grid with numbers. In this lesson you will use the **Cartesian plane** to describe the location of points and shapes, including points with negative coordinates.

## The Cartesian plane

The **Cartesian plane** is named after the French mathematician René Descartes. It is made of two number lines that cross.

:::definition Axes and origin
- The **x-axis** is a horizontal number line. Numbers to the right of zero are positive. Numbers to the left are negative.
- The **y-axis** is a vertical number line. Numbers above zero are positive. Numbers below are negative.
- The two axes cross at the **origin**, the point $(0,\ 0)$.
:::

:::example Negative numbers on the axes
Where is $-3$ on each axis?
@step On the $x$-axis, $-3$ is $3$ units to the **left** of the origin.
@step On the $y$-axis, $-3$ is $3$ units **below** the origin.
@answer Left on the $x$-axis, down on the $y$-axis.
:::

## Ordered pairs

:::definition Ordered pair
An **ordered pair** $(x,\ y)$ gives the location of a point.
- The $x$-coordinate tells how far the point is to the right (positive) or left (negative) of the $y$-axis.
- The $y$-coordinate tells how far the point is above (positive) or below (negative) the $x$-axis.
:::

:::example Reading a point
What are the coordinates of point $B$?
@step Start at the origin. $B$ is $4$ units to the left, so $x = -4$.
@step $B$ is $1$ unit up, so $y = 1$.
@answer $B(-4,\ 1)$
:::

:::mistake Order matters
The $x$-coordinate always comes first. $(-4,\ 1)$ and $(1,\ -4)$ are different points. Remember: go **across** first, then **up or down**.
:::

## Locating a point

To find a point, start at the origin. Move right or left for the $x$-coordinate. Then move up or down for the $y$-coordinate.

:::example Finding a point
Which point in the picture above is at $(-2,\ -3)$?
@step Start at the origin. $x = -2$, so move $2$ units to the left.
@step $y = -3$, so move $3$ units down.
@answer Point $C$ is at $(-2,\ -3)$.
:::

A point with $x = 0$ is on the $y$-axis, like $(0,\ 3)$. A point with $y = 0$ is on the $x$-axis, like $(-2,\ 0)$.

## Polygons in the Cartesian plane

A polygon is drawn by plotting its **vertices** (corners) and joining them in order. Rectangle $ABCD$ has vertices $A(-3,\ 2)$, $B(4,\ 2)$, $C(4,\ -1)$ and $D(-3,\ -1)$.

:::example Side lengths across an axis
Find the perimeter of rectangle $ABCD$. Each unit is $1\ \text{m}$.
@step $AB$ goes from $x = -3$ to $x = 4$. That is $3$ units to the $y$-axis and $4$ more units, so $AB = 3 + 4 = 7$.
@step $BC$ goes from $y = 2$ to $y = -1$. That is $2$ units down to the $x$-axis and $1$ more unit, so $BC = 2 + 1 = 3$.
@step Perimeter $= 7 + 3 + 7 + 3 = 20$
@answer The perimeter is $20\ \text{m}$.
:::

:::tip Count, don't subtract signs
When a side crosses an axis, count the units on each side of the axis and add them.
:::

:::summary
- The Cartesian plane has a horizontal $x$-axis and a vertical $y$-axis that cross at the origin $(0,\ 0)$.
- An ordered pair $(x,\ y)$ gives a location: $x$ is the distance right (+) or left (−), and $y$ is the distance up (+) or down (−).
- Always write the $x$-coordinate first.
- A polygon is drawn by plotting its vertices and joining them in order.
:::
