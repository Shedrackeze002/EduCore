How much paint do you need for a slanted shape on the playground? You need its **area**. In this lesson you will find the area of a parallelogram by turning it into a rectangle in your mind.

## Bases and heights

:::definition Parallelogram
A **parallelogram** is a quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel sides. Its opposite sides are equal.
:::

:::definition Base and height
Any side of a parallelogram can be the **base**. The **height** is the distance from the base straight across to the opposite side. The height makes a right angle with the base. We draw it as a dashed line with a small square.
:::

:::example Finding the height
Which length in the picture is the height for the $8\ \text{cm}$ base?
@step The dashed line goes straight from the base to the opposite side. It has a square corner.
@answer The height is $4\ \text{cm}$.
:::

:::mistake The slanted side is not the height
In the picture, the slanted side is $5\ \text{cm}$, but the height is $4\ \text{cm}$. Always use the height that meets the base at a right angle.
:::

## From a parallelogram to a rectangle

Cut the triangle off one end of a parallelogram along the height. Slide it to the other end. Now you have a rectangle with the **same base** and the **same height**, so it has the same area.

:::

$$\text{Area of a parallelogram} = \text{base} \times \text{height} \qquad A = b \times h$$

:::example Area of a parallelogram
Find the area of the parallelogram at the top of the lesson: base $8\ \text{cm}$, height $4\ \text{cm}$, slanted side $5\ \text{cm}$.
@step Use the base and the height. The slanted side is not used.
@step $A = b \times h = 8 \times 4 = 32$
@answer The area is $32\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::example A decimal base
A parallelogram-shaped garden bed has a base of $6.5\ \text{m}$ and a height of $4\ \text{m}$. Find its area.
@step $A = 6.5 \times 4$
@step $6 \times 4 = 24$ and $0.5 \times 4 = 2$, so $6.5 \times 4 = 26$.
@answer The area is $26\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

## Finding a base or a height

If you know the area and one of the two lengths, **divide** to find the other one.

:::example A missing height
A parallelogram has an area of $54\ \text{m}^2$ and a base of $9\ \text{m}$. Find its height.
@step $A = b \times h$, so $54 = 9 \times h$.
@step $h = 54 \div 9 = 6$
@step Check: $9 \times 6 = 54$ ✓
@answer The height is $6\ \text{m}$.
:::

## Any side can be the base

A parallelogram has the same area whichever side you use as the base. A different base has a different height.

:::

:::example Using the slanted side as the base
This parallelogram has a base of $10\ \text{cm}$, a height of $4\ \text{cm}$, and slanted sides of $5\ \text{cm}$. What is the height when a $5\ \text{cm}$ side is the base?
@step Area $= 10 \times 4 = 40$, so the area is $40\ \text{cm}^2$.
@step With the $5\ \text{cm}$ side as the base: $40 = 5 \times h$.
@step $h = 40 \div 5 = 8$
@answer The height to the slanted side is $8\ \text{cm}$.
:::

:::check m6-area-parallelogram 3
:::

:::summary
- A parallelogram can be cut and rearranged into a rectangle with the same base and height.
- Area of a parallelogram $= \text{base} \times \text{height}$. The height meets the base at a right angle.
- To find a missing base or height, divide the area by the length you know.
- Any side can be the base. The area stays the same.
:::
