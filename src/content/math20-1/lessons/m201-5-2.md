Formulas in physics and engineering often produce answers like $\dfrac{5}{2\sqrt{3}}$ or $\left(2 + \sqrt{3}\right)\left(4 - \sqrt{3}\right)$. In this lesson you will multiply radical expressions and rewrite quotients so that no radical is left in the denominator — the standard form for an exact answer.

## Multiplying radicals

Multiply the coefficients together and the radicands together, then simplify:

$$\left(a\sqrt{m}\right)\left(b\sqrt{n}\right) = ab\sqrt{mn} \qquad (m, n \ge 0)$$

:::example Monomials
Simplify $\left(2\sqrt{6}\right)\left(5\sqrt{15}\right)$.
@step $(2 \times 5)\sqrt{6 \times 15} = 10\sqrt{90}$
@step $\sqrt{90} = \sqrt{9}\,\sqrt{10} = 3\sqrt{10}$
@answer $10 \times 3\sqrt{10} = 30\sqrt{10}$
:::

:::example Variable radicands
Assume $x \ge 0$. Simplify $\left(3\sqrt{2x}\right)\left(4\sqrt{6x}\right)$.
@step $12\sqrt{12x^2}$
@step $\sqrt{12x^2} = \sqrt{4x^2}\,\sqrt{3} = 2x\sqrt{3}$
@answer $24x\sqrt{3}$
:::

:::tip Simplify before or after
You may simplify each radical first or multiply first — the result is the same. Multiplying first and then taking out the largest perfect square is usually less work.
:::

## Multiplying binomials

Use the distributive property (FOIL) exactly as with polynomials, and remember that $\left(\sqrt{m}\right)^2 = m$.

:::

:::example A product of binomials
Expand and simplify $\left(2 + \sqrt{3}\right)\left(4 - \sqrt{3}\right)$.
@step $8 - 2\sqrt{3} + 4\sqrt{3} - \left(\sqrt{3}\right)^2$
@step $= 8 - 2\sqrt{3} + 4\sqrt{3} - 3$
@answer $5 + 2\sqrt{3}$
:::

:::example Squaring a binomial
Expand $\left(3 - \sqrt{2}\right)^2$.
@step $\left(3 - \sqrt{2}\right)\left(3 - \sqrt{2}\right) = 9 - 3\sqrt{2} - 3\sqrt{2} + 2$
@answer $11 - 6\sqrt{2}$
:::

:::mistake The missing middle term
$\left(3 - \sqrt{2}\right)^2$ is **not** $9 + 2$ or $9 - 2$. Squaring a binomial always produces a middle term: $(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$.
:::

## Conjugates

:::definition Conjugate
The **conjugate** of $a + b\sqrt{m}$ is $a - b\sqrt{m}$ (change the sign between the terms). The conjugate of $\sqrt{p} + \sqrt{q}$ is $\sqrt{p} - \sqrt{q}$.
:::

A binomial times its conjugate is a difference of squares, so the radical disappears:

$$\left(a + b\sqrt{m}\right)\left(a - b\sqrt{m}\right) = a^2 - b^2m$$

The middle terms $-ab\sqrt{m}$ and $+ab\sqrt{m}$ cancel, and $\left(b\sqrt{m}\right)^2 = b^2m$ is rational.

:::example Products of conjugates
Simplify (a) $\left(5 + 2\sqrt{3}\right)\left(5 - 2\sqrt{3}\right)$ (b) $\left(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{2}\right)\left(\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{2}\right)$.
@step (a) $5^2 - \left(2\sqrt{3}\right)^2 = 25 - 12 = 13$
@step (b) $\left(\sqrt{7}\right)^2 - \left(\sqrt{2}\right)^2 = 7 - 2 = 5$
@answer (a) $13$ (b) $5$
:::

## Rationalizing a monomial denominator

:::definition Rationalize
To **rationalize** a denominator is to rewrite a fraction, without changing its value, so that the denominator has no radical.
:::

Multiply the numerator and the denominator by the radical in the denominator. You are multiplying by $\dfrac{\sqrt{m}}{\sqrt{m}} = 1$, so the value does not change.

:::example Monomial denominators
Rationalize (a) $\dfrac{5}{2\sqrt{3}}$ (b) $\dfrac{4\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{6}}$.
@step (a) $\dfrac{5}{2\sqrt{3}} \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{2 \times 3} = \dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{6}$
@step (b) $\dfrac{4\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{6}} \times \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{6}} = \dfrac{4\sqrt{30}}{6}$
@step Reduce: $\dfrac{4\sqrt{30}}{6} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{30}}{3}$
@answer (a) $\dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{6}$ (b) $\dfrac{2\sqrt{30}}{3}$
:::

## Rationalizing a binomial denominator

Multiplying by $\sqrt{3}$ will not clear $2 + \sqrt{3}$. Multiply by the **conjugate** instead.

:::example Using a conjugate
Rationalize $\dfrac{6}{2 + \sqrt{3}}$.
@step Multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate $2 - \sqrt{3}$.
@step Denominator: $\left(2 + \sqrt{3}\right)\left(2 - \sqrt{3}\right) = 4 - 3 = 1$
@step Numerator: $6\left(2 - \sqrt{3}\right) = 12 - 6\sqrt{3}$
@answer $12 - 6\sqrt{3}$
:::

:::example Radicals on top and bottom
Rationalize $\dfrac{\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{2}}$.
@step Multiply by $\dfrac{\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{2}}$. Denominator: $5 - 2 = 3$.
@step Numerator: $\left(\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{2}\right)^2 = 5 + 2\sqrt{10} + 2 = 7 + 2\sqrt{10}$
@answer $\dfrac{7 + 2\sqrt{10}}{3} = \dfrac{7}{3} + \dfrac{2}{3}\sqrt{10}$
:::

The same idea works with variables: for $x \ge 0$, $x \ne 1$,

$$\frac{4}{\sqrt{x} + 1} = \frac{4\left(\sqrt{x} - 1\right)}{\left(\sqrt{x}\right)^2 - 1^2} = \frac{4\left(\sqrt{x} - 1\right)}{x - 1}.$$

:::mistake Multiplying by the wrong thing
Multiplying $\dfrac{6}{2 + \sqrt{3}}$ by $\dfrac{2 + \sqrt{3}}{2 + \sqrt{3}}$ gives the denominator $7 + 4\sqrt{3}$ — still irrational. Use the conjugate, and multiply **both** the numerator and the denominator.
:::

:::summary
- $\left(a\sqrt{m}\right)\left(b\sqrt{n}\right) = ab\sqrt{mn}$; simplify the result.
- Expand binomials with the distributive property and use $\left(\sqrt{m}\right)^2 = m$.
- Conjugates such as $a + b\sqrt{m}$ and $a - b\sqrt{m}$ multiply to the rational number $a^2 - b^2m$.
- Rationalize a monomial denominator by multiplying by the radical over itself.
- Rationalize a binomial denominator by multiplying the numerator and denominator by its conjugate.
:::
