A quadratic equation has at most two solutions, but a quadratic inequality such as $x^2 - x - 6 \le 0$ usually has infinitely many — a whole interval of the number line, or two of them. The roots of the related equation are the key to finding them.

## From an equation to an inequality

:::definition Quadratic inequality in one variable
An inequality that can be written as $ax^2 + bx + c < 0$, $\le 0$, $> 0$ or $\ge 0$, where $a \ne 0$. Its **related equation** is $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$.
:::

The graph of $y = x^2 - x - 6$ crosses the $x$-axis only at its zeros, $x = -2$ and $x = 3$. Between two neighbouring zeros the graph stays on one side of the axis, so the sign of $x^2 - x - 6$ can change **only** at a root.

Reading the graph: $x^2 - x - 6$ is negative (below the axis) between $-2$ and $3$, and positive outside them. So the solution of $x^2 - x - 6 \le 0$ is $\{x \mid -2 \le x \le 3,\ x \in \mathbb{R}\}$.

## Test intervals

1. Solve the related equation to find the roots (the boundary values).
2. The roots split the number line into intervals.
3. Test one value in each interval.
4. Choose the intervals where the inequality is true. Include the roots for $\le$ or $\ge$; leave them out for $<$ or $>$.

:::definition Test interval
One of the intervals into which the roots divide the number line. Any value inside it gives the same sign, so one test value decides the whole interval.
:::

:::example Using test values
Solve $x^2 - x - 6 \le 0$.
@step Related equation: $x^2 - x - 6 = (x + 2)(x - 3) = 0$, so the roots are $x = -2$ and $x = 3$.
@step $x < -2$: test $x = -3$: $9 + 3 - 6 = 6$, positive.
@step $-2 < x < 3$: test $x = 0$: $-6$, negative.
@step $x > 3$: test $x = 4$: $16 - 4 - 6 = 6$, positive.
@answer We need negative or zero: $\{x \mid -2 \le x \le 3,\ x \in \mathbb{R}\}$
:::

## Sign diagrams

A **sign diagram** records the sign of each factor in each interval. The sign of the product follows from the signs of the factors: an even number of negative factors gives a positive product.

:::example A sign diagram
Solve $2x^2 + 5x - 3 > 0$.
@step Factor: $(2x - 1)(x + 3) > 0$. The roots are $x = \frac{1}{2}$ and $x = -3$.
@step Fill in the sign diagram below, one column for each interval.
@step The product is positive when $x < -3$ or $x > \frac{1}{2}$. The sign is $>$, so the roots are not included.
@answer $\{x \mid x < -3 \text{ or } x > \frac{1}{2},\ x \in \mathbb{R}\}$
:::

## Rearranging first, and problems

Move every term to one side so the other side is $0$. If you multiply or divide by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign.

:::example A negative leading coefficient
Solve $-x^2 + 2x + 8 > 0$.
@step Multiply by $-1$ and reverse the sign: $x^2 - 2x - 8 < 0$
@step Factor: $(x - 4)(x + 2) < 0$, with roots $-2$ and $4$.
@step Test $x = 0$: $(0 - 4)(0 + 2) = -8 < 0$ ✓, so the middle interval works.
@answer $\{x \mid -2 < x < 4,\ x \in \mathbb{R}\}$
:::

:::example A projectile
A model rocket's height is $h = -5t^2 + 30t$ metres, $t$ seconds after launch. When is it more than $25$ m high?
@step $-5t^2 + 30t > 25$, so $-5t^2 + 30t - 25 > 0$
@step Divide by $-5$ and reverse: $t^2 - 6t + 5 < 0$, so $(t - 1)(t - 5) < 0$
@step Test $t = 3$: $(2)(-2) = -4 < 0$ ✓ (the rocket is at $45$ m then).
@answer $\{t \mid 1 < t < 5,\ t \in \mathbb{R}\}$ — between 1 s and 5 s after launch.
:::

:::mistake Square roots of inequalities
From $x^2 < 9$, you cannot conclude $x < \pm 3$ or just $x < 3$ (try $x = -5$). Rewrite it as $x^2 - 9 < 0$, so $(x - 3)(x + 3) < 0$ and $-3 < x < 3$.
:::

:::

:::summary
- Solve the related equation; its roots are the boundary values.
- The sign of a quadratic can change only at a root, so test one value in each interval or use a sign diagram.
- Include the roots for $\le$ and $\ge$, not for $<$ and $>$.
- Get $0$ on one side first, and reverse the sign when you multiply or divide by a negative.
:::
