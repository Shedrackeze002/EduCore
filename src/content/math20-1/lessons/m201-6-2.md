To multiply $\tfrac{2}{3} \times \tfrac{9}{10}$ quickly, you cancel before you multiply: $\dfrac{2 \cdot 9}{3 \cdot 10} = \dfrac{\cancel{2} \cdot \cancel{3} \cdot 3}{\cancel{3} \cdot \cancel{2} \cdot 5} = \dfrac{3}{5}$. Rational expressions follow exactly the same rules — the "numbers" are now polynomial factors.

## Multiplying rational expressions

$$\frac{A}{B} \cdot \frac{C}{D} = \frac{AC}{BD}, \qquad B \ne 0,\ D \ne 0$$

1. Factor every numerator and denominator.
2. State the non-permissible values from every denominator.
3. Divide out factors common to any numerator and any denominator, then multiply what is left.

:::example Monomials
Simplify $\dfrac{6x^2}{5y} \times \dfrac{10y^2}{9x}$.
@step Restrictions: $x \ne 0$, $y \ne 0$.
@step Multiply: $\dfrac{60x^2y^2}{45xy}$.
@step Divide by the GCF $15xy$: $\dfrac{4xy}{3}$.
@answer $\dfrac{4xy}{3}$, $x \ne 0,\ y \ne 0$
:::

:::example Polynomials
Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 + 5x + 6} \times \dfrac{x + 2}{4x}$.
@step Factor: $\dfrac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{(x + 2)(x + 3)} \times \dfrac{x + 2}{4x}$.
@step Restrictions from the denominators: $x \ne -3,\ -2,\ 0$.
@step Divide out $(x + 3)$ and $(x + 2)$.
@answer $\dfrac{x - 3}{4x}$, $x \ne -3,\ -2,\ 0$
:::

:::tip Factor first, multiply last
Never expand the products before cancelling. $\dfrac{(x - 3)(x + 3)(x + 2)}{4x(x + 2)(x + 3)}$ shows its common factors; $\dfrac{x^3 + 2x^2 - 9x - 18}{4x^3 + 20x^2 + 24x}$ hides them.
:::

## Dividing rational expressions

Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal:

$$\frac{A}{B} \div \frac{C}{D} = \frac{A}{B} \cdot \frac{D}{C}, \qquad B \ne 0,\ C \ne 0,\ D \ne 0$$

The new condition $C \ne 0$ matters. If the numerator of the divisor is $0$, the divisor itself is $0$, and you cannot divide by zero. So a division has **three** sources of restrictions: both denominators and the divisor's numerator.

:::example Dividing
Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - x - 6}{x^2 - 16} \div \dfrac{x - 3}{x + 4}$.
@step Factor: $\dfrac{(x - 3)(x + 2)}{(x - 4)(x + 4)} \div \dfrac{x - 3}{x + 4}$.
@step Restrictions: denominators give $x \ne 4,\ -4$; the divisor's numerator gives $x \ne 3$.
@step Multiply by the reciprocal: $\dfrac{(x - 3)(x + 2)}{(x - 4)(x + 4)} \times \dfrac{x + 4}{x - 3}$.
@step Divide out $(x - 3)$ and $(x + 4)$.
@answer $\dfrac{x + 2}{x - 4}$, $x \ne -4,\ 3,\ 4$
:::

:::mistake Missing the hidden restriction
In the example above, the answer $\dfrac{x + 2}{x - 4}$ looks fine at $x = 3$, but the original division at $x = 3$ is $\dfrac{0}{-7} \div \dfrac{0}{7}$, a division by $0$. Always write the restrictions **before** flipping and cancelling.
:::

:::

## Mixed operations

Work from left to right, turning each division into multiplication by the reciprocal. Collect all the restrictions first.

:::example Three expressions
Simplify $\dfrac{2x + 6}{x^2 - 4} \div \dfrac{x^2 + 3x}{x - 2} \times \dfrac{x + 2}{8}$.
@step Factor: $\dfrac{2(x + 3)}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} \div \dfrac{x(x + 3)}{x - 2} \times \dfrac{x + 2}{8}$.
@step Restrictions: denominators give $x \ne \pm 2$; the divisor's numerator $x(x + 3)$ gives $x \ne 0,\ -3$.
@step Flip the divisor: $\dfrac{2(x + 3)}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} \times \dfrac{x - 2}{x(x + 3)} \times \dfrac{x + 2}{8}$.
@step Divide out $(x + 3)$, $(x - 2)$, $(x + 2)$ and the factor $2$: $\dfrac{1}{4x}$.
@answer $\dfrac{1}{4x}$, $x \ne -3,\ -2,\ 0,\ 2$
:::

:::check m201-rational-multiply-divide 3
:::

:::summary
- Multiply: factor, state restrictions, divide out common factors, multiply what remains.
- Divide: multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor (flip only the second expression).
- Restrictions come from every denominator **and** from the numerator of any divisor.
- Write restrictions before you cancel anything; they stay with the final answer.
:::
