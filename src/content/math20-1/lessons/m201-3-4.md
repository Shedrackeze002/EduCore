How high does a punted football go? What ticket price brings in the most money? What is the largest pen you can build with a fixed length of fencing? Each answer is the vertex of a parabola.

## Optimal values

:::definition Maximum and minimum value
The **maximum value** of a quadratic function that opens down, or the **minimum value** of one that opens up, is the $y$-coordinate of its vertex. Finding it is called **optimization**.
:::

Three ways to locate the vertex:

1. **Vertex form** $y = a(x - p)^2 + q$: the vertex is $(p, q)$.
2. **Standard form** $y = ax^2 + bx + c$: use $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$.
3. **Factored form** $y = a(x - r)(x - s)$: the vertex is halfway between the zeros, at $x = \dfrac{r + s}{2}$.

Always answer the question asked: the maximum value is the $y$-coordinate, and *where* it happens is the $x$-coordinate.

:::example A number problem
Two numbers differ by $8$. What is the smallest possible product?
@step Let the numbers be $x$ and $x + 8$. Product: $P(x) = x(x + 8)$.
@step The zeros are $x = 0$ and $x = -8$, so the vertex is at $x = \dfrac{0 + (-8)}{2} = -4$.
@step The numbers are $-4$ and $4$, and $P = (-4)(4) = -16$.
@answer The minimum product is $-16$.
:::

@step $t = -\dfrac{b}{2a} = -\dfrac{20}{2(-5)} = 2$.
@step $h(2) = -5(2)^2 + 20(2) + 1.5 = -20 + 40 + 1.5 = 21.5$.

## Area problems

When the total amount of fencing is fixed, write one dimension in terms of the other, then write the area as a quadratic function.

:::example Fencing against a wall
Sam has $60$ m of fencing for a rectangular pen against a barn wall, so only three sides need fencing. Find the maximum area.
@step Let $w$ be the length of each side perpendicular to the wall. The side parallel to the wall is $l = 60 - 2w$.
@step Area: $A(w) = w(60 - 2w)$. Its zeros are $w = 0$ and $w = 30$.
@step The vertex is halfway between the zeros: $w = 15$. Then $l = 60 - 2(15) = 30$.
@answer The maximum area is $15 \times 30 = 450$ m², with $w = 15$ m and $l = 30$ m.
:::

## Revenue problems

$$\text{revenue} = \text{price} \times \text{number sold}$$

When raising the price means selling fewer items, both factors depend on the number of price increases, so revenue is a quadratic function.

:::example Ticket prices
A theatre sells $300$ tickets per show at \$20 each. For every \$1 increase in price, $10$ fewer tickets are sold. What price maximizes revenue?
@step Let $x$ be the number of \$1 increases. Price: $20 + x$; tickets sold: $300 - 10x$.
@step $R(x) = (20 + x)(300 - 10x)$. The zeros are $x = -20$ and $x = 30$.
@step The vertex is halfway between: $x = \dfrac{-20 + 30}{2} = 5$.
@step Price $20 + 5 = 25$ dollars; tickets $300 - 10(5) = 250$; revenue $25 \times 250 = 6250$ dollars.
@answer A price of \$25 gives the maximum revenue, \$6250.
:::

:::tip x is not the price
In revenue problems, $x$ usually counts the number of price changes. After finding $x$, calculate the actual price.
:::

:::check m201-quad-optimize 3
:::

:::summary
- The maximum or minimum value of a quadratic function is the $y$-coordinate of its vertex; the $x$-coordinate tells where it happens.
- Find the vertex from vertex form, from $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$, or halfway between the zeros of factored form.
- For area and revenue problems, define a variable, write the quantity as a product of two expressions, and find the vertex.
- State the answer in context, with units, and check that it makes sense in the domain.
:::
