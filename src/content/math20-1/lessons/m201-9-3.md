A parabola splits the coordinate plane into two parts: the points above it and the points below it. A quadratic inequality in two variables picks one of those parts, just as a linear inequality picks one side of a line.

## Testing points

:::definition Quadratic inequality in two variables
An inequality such as $y < ax^2 + bx + c$, $y \le ax^2 + bx + c$, $y > ax^2 + bx + c$ or $y \ge ax^2 + bx + c$, where $a \ne 0$. Its boundary is the parabola $y = ax^2 + bx + c$.
:::

To test a point, evaluate the quadratic at the point's $x$-coordinate and compare the result with its $y$-coordinate.

:::example Testing points
Are $(1, 2)$ and $(3, 1)$ solutions of $y > x^2 - 3x + 1$?
@step $(1, 2)$: $1^2 - 3(1) + 1 = -1$. Is $2 > -1$? Yes, so $(1, 2)$ is a solution.
@step $(3, 1)$: $3^2 - 3(3) + 1 = 1$. Is $1 > 1$? No. The point is on the boundary, and the boundary is not included for $>$.
@answer $(1, 2)$ is a solution; $(3, 1)$ is not.
:::

## Graphing the solution region

1. Graph the boundary parabola: **solid** for $\le$ or $\ge$, **dashed** for $<$ or $>$.
2. Choose a test point that is not on the parabola, such as $(0, 0)$.
3. Shade the region that contains the test point if it makes the inequality true; otherwise shade the other region.

When $y$ is alone on the left, $y >$ or $y \ge$ means the points **above** the parabola and $y <$ or $y \le$ means the points **below** it. Depending on which way the parabola opens, that region may be inside or outside the curve.

:::example Graphing a region
Graph $y \le -x^2 + 2x + 3$.
@step The boundary $y = -x^2 + 2x + 3 = -(x - 1)^2 + 4$ has vertex $(1, 4)$, opens down and crosses the $x$-axis at $x = -1$ and $x = 3$.
@step The sign is $\le$, so draw the parabola solid.
@step Test $(0, 0)$: $-(0)^2 + 2(0) + 3 = 3$, and $0 \le 3$ is true.
@answer Shade the region containing $(0, 0)$: the points on or below the parabola (inside the arch).
:::

In the graph, the solution region is shown with orange hatching.

## Reading an inequality from a graph

Work backwards: find the equation of the boundary, decide on $<$, $>$, $\le$ or $\ge$ from the line style, and decide the direction from the shading.

:::example From graph to inequality
The graph shows a dashed parabola with vertex $(-1, -2)$ that passes through $(0, -1)$ and $(1, 2)$, with the region above it shaded. Write the inequality.
@step Vertex form: $y = a(x + 1)^2 - 2$. At $(0, -1)$: $-1 = a(1)^2 - 2$, so $a = 1$.
@step Expand: $y = (x + 1)^2 - 2 = x^2 + 2x - 1$
@step Dashed boundary: $<$ or $>$. Shaded above: $y >$.
@answer $y > x^2 + 2x - 1$
:::

:::example Will the truck fit?
The opening under a bridge arch is the region $y \le -0.1x^2 + 6$, with $x$ and $y$ in metres and the road along the $x$-axis. A truck $4$ m wide and $3$ m tall drives along the centre of the road. Does it fit under the arch?
@step The truck's top corners are at $(-2, 3)$ and $(2, 3)$.
@step At $x = 2$: $-0.1(2)^2 + 6 = 5.6$. Is $3 \le 5.6$? Yes (and $x = -2$ gives the same value).
@answer Yes. Both top corners are inside the solution region, so the truck fits with $2.6$ m to spare at its corners.
:::

:::summary
- The boundary of $y < ax^2 + bx + c$ (or $>$, $\le$, $\ge$) is the parabola $y = ax^2 + bx + c$: solid for $\le$, $\ge$ and dashed for $<$, $>$.
- To test a point, evaluate the quadratic at its $x$-coordinate and compare with its $y$-coordinate.
- $y >$ and $y \ge$ shade above the parabola; $y <$ and $y \le$ shade below it. Confirm with a test point off the boundary.
- To read an inequality from a graph, find the boundary's equation, then use the line style and the shading.
:::
