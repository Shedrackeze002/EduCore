Before solving a quadratic equation, you can predict how many real roots it has from a single number under the radical sign of the quadratic formula. Then you will put quadratic equations to work on rectangles, triangles and flying objects.

## The discriminant

In $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$, everything depends on the value under the radical.

:::definition Discriminant
For $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, the **discriminant** is $b^2 - 4ac$.
:::

- If $b^2 - 4ac > 0$, the $\pm$ gives two different values: **two distinct real roots**. The parabola crosses the $x$-axis twice.
- If $b^2 - 4ac = 0$, both values are $\dfrac{-b}{2a}$: **one real root** (two equal roots). The vertex touches the $x$-axis.
- If $b^2 - 4ac < 0$, the square root of a negative number is not real: **no real roots**. The parabola misses the $x$-axis.

When the discriminant is a perfect square, the roots are rational and the equation factors over the integers.

:::example Predicting the roots
Describe the roots of (a) $2x^2 - 3x + 5 = 0$ (b) $4x^2 + 12x + 9 = 0$ (c) $x^2 - 5x + 2 = 0$.
@step (a) $(-3)^2 - 4(2)(5) = 9 - 40 = -31 < 0$: no real roots.
@step (b) $12^2 - 4(4)(9) = 144 - 144 = 0$: one real root, $x = \dfrac{-12}{8} = -\dfrac{3}{2}$.
@step (c) $(-5)^2 - 4(1)(2) = 25 - 8 = 17 > 0$: two distinct real roots (irrational, since 17 is not a perfect square).
:::

:::mistake Standard form first
For $4x^2 + 9 = 12x$, the values of $a$, $b$ and $c$ come from $4x^2 - 12x + 9 = 0$, so $b = -12$, not $12$ or $0$. Rearrange before you read the coefficients.
:::

## Finding an unknown coefficient

Set the discriminant equal to zero (for one root), greater than zero (for two roots) or less than zero (for no roots), then solve for the unknown.

:::example One real root
For which values of $k$ does $x^2 + kx + 9 = 0$ have exactly one real root?
@step $b^2 - 4ac = k^2 - 4(1)(9) = k^2 - 36$
@step $k^2 - 36 = 0$, so $k^2 = 36$
@answer $k = 6$ or $k = -6$
:::

:::example Two distinct real roots
For which values of $k$ does $x^2 - 6x + k = 0$ have two distinct real roots?
@step $b^2 - 4ac = 36 - 4k$
@step $36 - 4k > 0$, so $36 > 4k$
@answer $k < 9$
:::

## Solving problems with quadratic equations

1. Define a variable for the unknown, with units.
2. Write an equation from the situation.
3. Solve it by factoring, completing the square or the quadratic formula.
4. **Reject** any root that does not make sense, such as a negative length or time.
5. Answer the question in a sentence, with units.

:::example A rectangle
The length of a rectangular garden is $3$ m more than twice its width. Its area is $44\ \text{m}^2$. Find its dimensions.
@step Let the width be $w$ metres. The length is $2w + 3$, so $w(2w + 3) = 44$.
@step $2w^2 + 3w - 44 = 0$; $ac = -88$, sum $3$: $11$ and $-8$, so $(w - 4)(2w + 11) = 0$
@step $w = 4$ or $w = -\frac{11}{2}$. A width cannot be negative, so $w = 4$.
@answer The garden is $4$ m wide and $11$ m long ($4 \times 11 = 44$ ✓).
:::

:::example A right triangle
One leg of a right triangle is $7$ cm longer than the other, and the hypotenuse is $13$ cm. Find the legs.
@step Let the shorter leg be $x$. Pythagorean theorem: $x^2 + (x + 7)^2 = 13^2$
@step $2x^2 + 14x - 120 = 0$, so $x^2 + 7x - 60 = 0$ and $(x - 5)(x + 12) = 0$
@answer $x = 5$ (reject $-12$). The legs are $5$ cm and $12$ cm.
:::

:::mistake Keeping the impossible root
Both roots solve the equation, but only one may fit the situation. Always ask: can a length, a time or a number of people be negative?
:::

## Projectiles and borders

The height of an object thrown upward is often modelled by $h = -4.9t^2 + v_0t + h_0$, where $t$ is in seconds, $v_0$ is the starting upward speed in metres per second, and $h_0$ is the starting height in metres. The $-4.9$ comes from gravity pulling the object down.

:::example A projectile
A ball is thrown upward from a height of $2$ m. Its height is $h = -4.9t^2 + 15t + 2$. When does it hit the ground, to the nearest hundredth of a second?
@step Set $h = 0$: $-4.9t^2 + 15t + 2 = 0$
@step $t = \dfrac{-15 \pm \sqrt{15^2 - 4(-4.9)(2)}}{2(-4.9)} = \dfrac{-15 \pm \sqrt{264.2}}{-9.8}$
@step $t \approx 3.19$ or $t \approx -0.13$. Time after the throw cannot be negative.
@answer The ball hits the ground after about $3.19$ s.
:::

:::example A frame of uniform width
A $20$ cm by $30$ cm photograph has a frame of uniform width. The photograph and frame together cover $936\ \text{cm}^2$. How wide is the frame?
@step Let the frame be $x$ cm wide: $(20 + 2x)(30 + 2x) = 936$
@step $4x^2 + 100x + 600 = 936$, so $4x^2 + 100x - 336 = 0$ and $x^2 + 25x - 84 = 0$
@step $(x - 3)(x + 28) = 0$, so $x = 3$ (reject $-28$).
@answer The frame is $3$ cm wide.
:::

:::

:::summary
- The discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$ tells you the number of real roots: positive, two; zero, one; negative, none.
- Set the discriminant $= 0$, $> 0$ or $< 0$ to find an unknown coefficient.
- For problems: define a variable, write an equation, solve, reject roots that make no sense, and answer with units.
- Projectile models such as $h = -4.9t^2 + v_0t + h_0$ give the height at time $t$; set $h$ equal to the height you need.
:::
