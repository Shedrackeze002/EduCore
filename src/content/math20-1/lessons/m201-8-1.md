A model rocket rises and falls along a parabola while a camera drone climbs in a straight line. When are they at the same height? Questions like this lead to systems in which at least one equation is quadratic.

## Linear-quadratic and quadratic-quadratic systems

:::definition System of equations
Two or more equations in the same variables, considered together. A **linear-quadratic system** has one linear and one quadratic equation; a **quadratic-quadratic system** has two quadratic equations.
:::

:::definition Point of intersection
A point that lies on both graphs. Each point of intersection is a solution of the system: its coordinates make **both** equations true.
:::

A line can meet a parabola in **two** points, touch it at exactly **one** point (a tangent line), or miss it completely (**no** solution). Two parabolas can meet in $0$, $1$ or $2$ points, or they can be the same parabola, giving **infinitely many** solutions.

## Solving a linear-quadratic system by graphing

1. Graph both equations on the same grid.
2. Find every point of intersection.
3. Verify each point in **both** equations.

Solve $y = x^2 - 4$ and $y = -x^2 + 2x + 8$ by graphing.
@step The parabolas intersect at $(-2, 0)$ and $(3, 5)$.
@step Check $(-2, 0)$: $(-2)^2 - 4 = 0$ ✓ and $-(-2)^2 + 2(-2) + 8 = 0$ ✓

:::mistake Trusting an estimate
On a hand-drawn graph, an intersection near $(1.4, 2.9)$ might look like $(1, 3)$. A point you read from a graph is only an estimate until it checks in both equations. Graphing technology (the intersect feature) or algebra gives accurate values.
:::

## Predicting the number of solutions

A quick sketch from each graph's key features often tells you how many solutions to expect.

:::example No solution
How many solutions does the system $y = (x - 1)^2 + 3$ and $y = -(x + 2)^2 + 1$ have?
@step The first parabola opens up from $(1, 3)$, so every point on it has $y \ge 3$.
@step The second opens down from $(-2, 1)$, so every point on it has $y \le 1$.
@answer The graphs never meet: there is no solution.
:::

Some other patterns:

- Two parabolas with the **same vertex** but different values of $a$ meet only at the vertex: one solution.
- Two parabolas with the **same $a$** and the same axis are vertical translations of each other: no solution.
- A non-horizontal line through the vertex of a parabola crosses it twice: two solutions.
- Equations that expand to the same standard form describe the **same parabola**: infinitely many solutions.

:::tip Use the vertex and the direction of opening
Compare the vertex heights with the directions of opening. An upward parabola whose minimum is above a downward parabola's maximum can never meet it.
:::

:::check m201-system-graph 3
:::

:::summary
- The solutions of a system are the points of intersection of the graphs.
- A line and a parabola can have $0$, $1$ or $2$ solutions; two parabolas can have $0$, $1$, $2$ or infinitely many.
- Verify every point read from a graph in both equations.
- Vertices and directions of opening help you predict the number of solutions before you solve.
:::
