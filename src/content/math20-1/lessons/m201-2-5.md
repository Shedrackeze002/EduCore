The sine law needs a complete side–angle pair. When you know two sides and the angle **between** them, or all three sides, there is no pair to start with. The **cosine law** handles both cases — and it turns out to be the Pythagorean theorem with a correction term.

## Proving the cosine law

Draw the height $h$ from $C$ to side $c$, meeting it at $D$. Let $AD = x$, so $DB = c - x$.

- In right $\triangle ADC$: $x = b\cos A$ and $h^2 = b^2 - x^2$.
- In right $\triangle BDC$: $a^2 = h^2 + (c - x)^2$.

Substitute for $h^2$ and expand:
$$a^2 = b^2 - x^2 + c^2 - 2cx + x^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2cx$$
Finally, replace $x$ with $b\cos A$.

:::definition The cosine law
In any triangle $ABC$,
$$a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$$
and in the same way $b^2 = a^2 + c^2 - 2ac\cos B$ and $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$.
:::

If $\angle A = 90^\circ$, then $\cos A = 0$ and the law becomes $a^2 = b^2 + c^2$: the Pythagorean theorem. If $\angle A$ is obtuse, $\cos A < 0$, so the last term **adds** and side $a$ is longer than a right triangle would give.

:::example Two sides and the contained angle
In $\triangle ABC$, $b = 8$ m, $c = 11$ m and $\angle A = 52^\circ$. Determine $a$ to the nearest tenth of a metre.
@step $a^2 = 8^2 + 11^2 - 2(8)(11)\cos 52^\circ$
@step $a^2 \approx 185 - 108.36 = 76.64$
@answer $a \approx \sqrt{76.64} \approx 8.8$ m
:::

:::example An obtuse angle
In $\triangle ABC$, $b = 6.5$ cm, $c = 9.2$ cm and $\angle A = 118^\circ$. Determine $a$.
@step $a^2 = 6.5^2 + 9.2^2 - 2(6.5)(9.2)\cos 118^\circ$
@step $\cos 118^\circ \approx -0.4695$ is negative, so the last term adds: $a^2 \approx 183.04$
@answer $a \approx 13.5$ cm
:::

:::mistake Order of operations
Find $2bc\cos A$ as one product and subtract it last. Working left to right as $(b^2 + c^2 - 2bc)\cos A$ is a common calculator error that gives a very wrong answer.
:::

## Finding an angle (SSS)

Rearrange the cosine law to isolate the cosine:
$$\cos A = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}$$
The side opposite the angle you want is the one that is subtracted. Unlike the sine law, this never gives an ambiguous answer: a negative cosine means an obtuse angle, and $\cos^{-1}$ returns it directly.

:::example The largest angle
In $\triangle PQR$, $p = 13$ cm, $q = 7$ cm and $r = 9$ cm. Determine the largest angle.
@step The largest angle is opposite the longest side, so find $\angle P$.
@step $\cos P = \dfrac{7^2 + 9^2 - 13^2}{2(7)(9)} = \dfrac{-39}{126} \approx -0.3095$
@answer $\angle P = \cos^{-1}(-0.3095) \approx 108.0^\circ$
:::

:::tip Find the largest angle first
When solving a whole triangle from three sides, use the cosine law for the largest angle. The other two angles must then be acute, so you can safely finish with the sine law.
:::

## Choosing a law

| You know | Use |
|---|---|
| a right angle | the primary trigonometric ratios |
| two angles and any side (AAS or ASA) | the sine law (find the third angle first if needed) |
| two sides and a non-contained angle (SSA) | the sine law — check the ambiguous case |
| two sides and the contained angle (SAS) | the cosine law |
| three sides (SSS) | the cosine law |

Many problems take two steps: one law finds a missing piece, and the other law (or a primary ratio) finishes the job.

:::example Height of a tree
From point $A$, the angle of elevation to the top of a tree is $28^\circ$. After walking 40 m toward the tree to point $B$, the angle of elevation is $52^\circ$. How tall is the tree?
@step In $\triangle ABT$: $\angle ABT = 180^\circ - 52^\circ = 128^\circ$, so $\angle ATB = 180^\circ - 28^\circ - 128^\circ = 24^\circ$.
@step Sine law: $\dfrac{BT}{\sin 28^\circ} = \dfrac{40}{\sin 24^\circ}$, so $BT \approx 46.17$ m.
@step In the right triangle under the tree: $h = BT\sin 52^\circ \approx 46.17\sin 52^\circ$
@answer The tree is about 36.4 m tall.
:::

:::example Paddling across a lake
Ji-woo paddles 12 km from $S$ to $T$, turns $40^\circ$ to the left, and paddles 9 km to $E$. How far is $E$ from $S$?
@step The turn is measured from the original direction, so the angle inside the triangle at $T$ is $180^\circ - 40^\circ = 140^\circ$.
@step SAS, so use the cosine law: $SE^2 = 12^2 + 9^2 - 2(12)(9)\cos 140^\circ \approx 390.47$
@answer $SE \approx 19.8$ km
:::

:::summary
- Cosine law: $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$; for an angle, $\cos A = \dfrac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}$.
- Use it for SAS (find the third side) and SSS (find an angle).
- A negative cosine means an obtuse angle, so the cosine law is never ambiguous.
- Use the sine law when you have a complete side–angle pair; use the cosine law when you do not.
- In two-step problems, keep unrounded values between steps.
:::
