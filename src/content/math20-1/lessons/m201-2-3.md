Forest-fire lookouts, surveyors and ship navigators rarely get a right angle to work with. The **sine law** lets you solve any triangle where you know a side and the angle opposite it.

## Oblique triangles

:::definition Oblique triangle
A triangle with no right angle. It can have three acute angles, or one obtuse angle.
:::

We label an oblique triangle so that each side is named by the lower-case letter of the angle **opposite** it: side $a$ is opposite $\angle A$, side $b$ is opposite $\angle B$, and side $c$ is opposite $\angle C$. A side and its opposite angle form a **pair**.

The height splits $\triangle ABC$ into two right triangles.

- In the left triangle, $\sin A = \dfrac{h}{b}$, so $h = b\sin A$.
- In the right triangle, $\sin B = \dfrac{h}{a}$, so $h = a\sin B$.

Both expressions equal $h$, so $a\sin B = b\sin A$. Dividing both sides by $\sin A\sin B$ gives $\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B}$. Using the height from $A$ instead brings in $c$ and $\sin C$ the same way. (When $\angle A$ is obtuse, the height falls outside the triangle, and the proof still works because $\sin(180^\circ - A) = \sin A$.)

:::definition The sine law
In any triangle $ABC$,
$$\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C} \qquad\text{or}\qquad \frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin C}{c}$$
Use it when you know one complete pair (a side and its opposite angle) plus one more side or angle.
:::

:::example Two angles and a side (AAS)
In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A = 48^\circ$, $\angle B = 65^\circ$ and $a = 12$ cm. Determine $b$ to the nearest tenth of a centimetre.
@step The pair $a$, $\angle A$ is complete. $b$ is opposite $\angle B$.
@step $\dfrac{b}{\sin 65^\circ} = \dfrac{12}{\sin 48^\circ}$
@step $b = \dfrac{12\sin 65^\circ}{\sin 48^\circ}$
@answer $b \approx 14.6$ cm
:::

:::example The side between two angles (ASA)
In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A = 38^\circ$, $\angle B = 64^\circ$ and $c = 15.2$ m. Determine $a$.
@step No pair is complete yet, so find the third angle: $\angle C = 180^\circ - 38^\circ - 64^\circ = 78^\circ$.
@step $\dfrac{a}{\sin 38^\circ} = \dfrac{15.2}{\sin 78^\circ}$, so $a = \dfrac{15.2\sin 38^\circ}{\sin 78^\circ}$
@answer $a \approx 9.6$ m
:::

## Finding an angle

Put the angles in the numerators, solve for the sine, then use $\sin^{-1}$.

:::example An acute angle
In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A = 72^\circ$, $a = 15$ cm and $b = 11$ cm. Determine $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ to the nearest tenth of a degree.
@step $\dfrac{\sin B}{11} = \dfrac{\sin 72^\circ}{15}$, so $\sin B = \dfrac{11\sin 72^\circ}{15} \approx 0.6974$
@step $\angle B = \sin^{-1}(0.6974) \approx 44.2^\circ$. It must be acute: $b < a$, so $\angle B < \angle A$.
@step $\angle C \approx 180^\circ - 72^\circ - 44.22^\circ$
@answer $\angle B \approx 44.2^\circ$ and $\angle C \approx 63.8^\circ$
:::

Every sine value between 0 and 1 belongs to **two** angles: an acute angle and its supplement, since $\sin(180^\circ - x) = \sin x$. The calculator always gives the acute one.

:::example An obtuse angle
In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A = 31^\circ$, $a = 9$ m and $b = 14.5$ m, and $\angle B$ is obtuse. Determine $\angle B$.
@step $\sin B = \dfrac{14.5\sin 31^\circ}{9} \approx 0.8298$
@step $\sin^{-1}(0.8298) \approx 56.1^\circ$, but $\angle B$ is obtuse.
@answer $\angle B \approx 180^\circ - 56.08^\circ \approx 123.9^\circ$
:::

:::mistake Rounding too early
Keep the full calculator value of $\sin B$ (or store it) until the last step. Rounding $\sin B$ to $0.7$ in the first example gives $\angle B \approx 44.4^\circ$ instead of $44.2^\circ$.
:::

:::example Fire lookouts
Two fire lookout towers, $A$ and $B$, are 18.4 km apart. The ranger in tower $A$ sees smoke at $F$ with $\angle FAB = 52^\circ$, and the ranger in tower $B$ measures $\angle FBA = 61^\circ$. How far is the fire from tower $B$?
@step $\angle F = 180^\circ - 52^\circ - 61^\circ = 67^\circ$, which pairs with the known side $AB$.
@step $BF$ is opposite $\angle A$: $\dfrac{BF}{\sin 52^\circ} = \dfrac{18.4}{\sin 67^\circ}$
@answer $BF = \dfrac{18.4\sin 52^\circ}{\sin 67^\circ} \approx 15.8$ km
:::

:::

:::summary
- Side $a$ is opposite $\angle A$; a side and its opposite angle form a pair.
- Sine law: $\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B} = \dfrac{c}{\sin C}$. It needs one complete pair.
- For a side, put sides in the numerators; for an angle, put sines in the numerators.
- $\sin^{-1}$ gives the acute angle. If the angle is obtuse, use $180^\circ$ minus that value.
- Find a missing third angle first when no pair is complete.
:::
