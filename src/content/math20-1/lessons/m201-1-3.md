A bacteria culture that doubles every hour, a truck that loses 15% of its value each year, a ball that bounces back to three-quarters of its height — in each case the next value comes from **multiplying** by the same number. These are geometric sequences, and they grow (or shrink) far faster than arithmetic ones.

## The common ratio

:::definition Geometric sequence
A sequence in which each term after the first is found by multiplying the previous term by the same non-zero number, the **common ratio** $r$:
$$r = \frac{t_n}{t_{n-1}}$$
:::

| Sequence | Common ratio |
|---|---|
| $3,\ 6,\ 12,\ 24,\ \ldots$ | $r = \frac{6}{3} = 2$ |
| $80,\ 20,\ 5,\ 1.25,\ \ldots$ | $r = \frac{20}{80} = \frac{1}{4}$ |
| $2,\ -6,\ 18,\ -54,\ \ldots$ | $r = \frac{-6}{2} = -3$ |

To decide which kind of sequence you have, test both: an **arithmetic** sequence has a constant *difference*, a **geometric** sequence has a constant *ratio*. For example, $2,\ 6,\ 18,\ 54$ is geometric ($r = 3$), while $2,\ 6,\ 10,\ 14$ is arithmetic ($d = 4$).

A negative ratio makes the signs alternate. A ratio between $-1$ and $1$ makes the terms shrink toward 0.

## The general term

Start at $t_1$ and keep multiplying by $r$:

$$t_1,\quad t_2 = t_1 r,\quad t_3 = t_1 r^2,\quad t_4 = t_1 r^3,\quad \ldots$$

To reach term $n$ you multiply by $r$ exactly $n - 1$ times.

:::definition General term of a geometric sequence
$$t_n = t_1 r^{n-1}$$
:::

:::example A positive ratio
Find $t_{10}$ for $3,\ 6,\ 12,\ \ldots$
@step $t_1 = 3$ and $r = 2$
@step $t_{10} = 3(2)^{9} = 3(512)$
@answer $t_{10} = 1536$
:::

:::example A negative fractional ratio
Find $t_8$ for $64,\ -32,\ 16,\ \ldots$
@step $r = \dfrac{-32}{64} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$
@step $t_8 = 64\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{7} = 64\left(-\dfrac{1}{128}\right)$
@answer $t_8 = -\dfrac{1}{2}$
:::

:::mistake Brackets around a negative ratio
$(-2)^4 = 16$ but $-2^4 = -16$. Always put a negative or fractional $r$ in brackets before raising it to a power. Also, $t_1 r^{n-1}$ means "raise $r$ to the power, then multiply by $t_1$" — not $(t_1 r)^{n-1}$.
:::

## Two terms that are not next to each other

Between $t_p$ and $t_q$ there are $q - p$ factors of $r$, so $t_q = t_p\, r^{\,q - p}$.

:::example A cube root
In a geometric sequence, $t_2 = 12$ and $t_5 = 324$. Find $t_7$.
@step $t_5 = t_2 r^3$: $324 = 12r^3$, so $r^3 = 27$ and $r = 3$.
@step $t_1 = \dfrac{t_2}{r} = \dfrac{12}{3} = 4$
@step $t_7 = 4(3)^6 = 4(729)$
@answer $t_7 = 2916$
:::

:::example Two possible ratios
In a geometric sequence, $t_1 = 5$ and $t_3 = 45$. Find all possible values of $r$.
@step $t_3 = t_1 r^2$: $45 = 5r^2$, so $r^2 = 9$.
@step $r = \pm 3$. Both work: $5,\ 15,\ 45$ and $5,\ -15,\ 45$.
@answer $r = 3$ or $r = -3$
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the negative square root
When the gap between the two terms is even ($r^2$, $r^4$, …), there are two real ratios. Keep both unless the question says the ratio (or every term) is positive. An odd gap ($r^3$) has only one real root.
:::

## Finding the position of a term

Divide by $t_1$, then write the result as a power of $r$.

:::example Which term is it?
Which term of $3,\ 6,\ 12,\ \ldots$ is $1536$?
@step $1536 = 3(2)^{n-1}$, so $2^{n-1} = 512$.
@step $512 = 2^9$, so $n - 1 = 9$.
@answer $n = 10$: $1536$ is the 10th term.
:::

## Growth and decay

Growth by $p\%$ each period multiplies by $r = 1 + \dfrac{p}{100}$. Decay by $p\%$ multiplies by $r = 1 - \dfrac{p}{100}$.

:::example Depreciation
A truck bought for \$40,000 loses 15% of its value each year. What is it worth after 3 years?
@step Each year the value is multiplied by $r = 1 - 0.15 = 0.85$.
@step $40\,000(0.85)^3 = 40\,000(0.614125) = 24\,565$
@answer The truck is worth \$24,565 after 3 years.
:::

:::example A bouncing ball
A ball is dropped from 256 cm, and after each bounce it rises to $\frac{3}{4}$ of the height it fell from. After which bounce does it rise to 81 cm?
@step After bounce $n$ the height is $256\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^n$.
@step $81 = 256\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^n$, so $\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^n = \dfrac{81}{256} = \left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)^4$
@answer It rises to 81 cm after the 4th bounce.
:::

:::tip Check the starting point
Decide whether the starting value is $t_1$ or the value "at time 0". The truck's price when new is at time 0, so after 3 years we multiply by $r^3$, not $r^2$.
:::

:::check m201-geo-unknown 3
:::

:::summary
- A geometric sequence multiplies by the same common ratio $r = \dfrac{t_n}{t_{n-1}}$ at every step.
- General term: $t_n = t_1 r^{n-1}$. Bracket a negative or fractional $r$.
- From two terms: $t_q = t_p\, r^{\,q-p}$. An even power gives two possible ratios, $\pm$.
- Growth by $p\%$: $r = 1 + \frac{p}{100}$; decay by $p\%$: $r = 1 - \frac{p}{100}$.
:::
