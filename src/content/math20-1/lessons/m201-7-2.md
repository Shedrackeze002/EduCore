What happens to a graph if negative outputs are not allowed? Taking the absolute value of a function keeps every point on or above the $x$-axis and flips every point below it — the result is a V shape for a line and a "W" or bounce for a parabola.

## Graphing y = |f(x)|

:::definition Absolute value function
A function of the form $y = |f(x)|$. Its graph keeps the points of $y = f(x)$ where $f(x) \ge 0$ and reflects the points where $f(x) < 0$ in the $x$-axis: $(x, y)$ becomes $(x, -y)$.
:::

:::definition Invariant point
A point that stays where it is after a transformation. When $y = f(x)$ becomes $y = |f(x)|$, every point on or above the $x$-axis is unchanged; in particular the $x$-intercepts are invariant, and they are where the graph "bounces".
:::

## Domain, range and intercepts

An absolute value is never negative, so the range of $y = |f(x)|$ never includes negative numbers. For a linear $f$ (not horizontal), the domain is $\{x \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$ and the range is $\{y \mid y \ge 0,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$.

For a quadratic, find the $x$-intercepts and the vertex of $f$ first.

:::example A quadratic
Graph $y = |x^2 - 2x - 8|$ and state its intercepts, domain and range.
@step $x^2 - 2x - 8 = (x - 4)(x + 2)$, so the $x$-intercepts are $-2$ and $4$ (invariant points).
@step $y$-intercept: $|{-}8| = 8$.
@step The vertex of $f$ is $(1, -9)$, below the axis, so it is reflected to $(1, 9)$, a local maximum of $y = |f(x)|$.
@answer Domain $\{x \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$, range $\{y \mid y \ge 0,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$
:::

## Writing y = |f(x)| as a piecewise function

:::definition Piecewise function
A function made of different expressions on different intervals of its domain.
:::

Because $|A| = A$ when $A \ge 0$ and $|A| = -A$ when $A < 0$, every absolute value function can be written piecewise. Solve $f(x) \ge 0$ to find where each piece applies.

:::example Linear
Write $y = |2x - 4|$ in piecewise form.
@step $2x - 4 \ge 0$ when $x \ge 2$; there $y = 2x - 4$.
@step When $x < 2$, $y = -(2x - 4) = -2x + 4$.
@answer $y = \begin{cases} 2x - 4, & x \ge 2 \\ -2x + 4, & x < 2 \end{cases}$
:::

:::example Quadratic
Write $y = |x^2 - 2x - 8|$ in piecewise form.
@step The parabola opens up with zeros $-2$ and $4$, so $f(x) \ge 0$ when $x \le -2$ or $x \ge 4$, and $f(x) < 0$ when $-2 < x < 4$.
@answer $y = \begin{cases} x^2 - 2x - 8, & x \le -2 \text{ or } x \ge 4 \\ -(x^2 - 2x - 8), & -2 < x < 4 \end{cases}$
:::

:::mistake A negative coefficient
For $y = |{-}3x + 6|$, solving $-3x + 6 \ge 0$ gives $-3x \ge -6$, so $x \le 2$ — dividing by $-3$ reverses the inequality. The pieces are $-3x + 6$ for $x \le 2$ and $3x - 6$ for $x > 2$.
:::

:::check m201-abs-function 3
:::

## When the parabola does not cross the axis

If the graph of $f$ never goes below the $x$-axis, nothing changes: $y = |(x - 1)^2 + 3|$ is the same graph as $y = (x - 1)^2 + 3$, with range $\{y \mid y \ge 3\}$.

If the graph of $f$ is entirely below the axis, all of it is reflected: $y = |{-}(x + 2)^2 - 1|$ is the same graph as $y = (x + 2)^2 + 1$, with range $\{y \mid y \ge 1\}$.

:::tip Finding the range quickly
Ask two questions: which way does $f$ open, and is its vertex above or below the $x$-axis? If the graph crosses the axis, the range of $|f(x)|$ is $y \ge 0$; if it does not, the range is $y \ge |q|$, where $q$ is the $y$-coordinate of the vertex.
:::

:::summary
- $y = |f(x)|$ keeps points with $f(x) \ge 0$ and reflects points with $f(x) < 0$ in the $x$-axis.
- The $x$-intercepts are invariant; the range never includes negative values.
- Piecewise form: $f(x)$ where $f(x) \ge 0$, and $-f(x)$ where $f(x) < 0$.
- Find where $f(x) \ge 0$ from its zeros, reversing the inequality when you divide by a negative.
:::
