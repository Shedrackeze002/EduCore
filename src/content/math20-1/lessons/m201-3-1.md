The arc of a basketball shot, the curve of a satellite dish and the path of water from a fountain are all **parabolas**. Written in vertex form, a quadratic function tells you its most important features at a glance.

## Parabolas and their features

:::definition Quadratic function
A **quadratic function** can be written as $y = ax^2 + bx + c$, where $a \ne 0$. Its graph is a U-shaped curve called a **parabola**.
:::

:::definition Vertex and axis of symmetry
The **vertex** is the turning point of a parabola: its lowest point (a **minimum**) when it opens up, or its highest point (a **maximum**) when it opens down. The **axis of symmetry** is the vertical line through the vertex. The parabola is its own mirror image in this line.
:::

## How a, p and q transform y = x²

The **vertex form** of a quadratic function is
$$y = a(x - p)^2 + q$$
Each parameter transforms the graph of $y = x^2$:

- $a$ gives a **vertical stretch** by a factor of $\lvert a \rvert$. When $a < 0$, the graph is also **reflected in the $x$-axis**, so it opens down.
- $p$ gives a **horizontal translation**: right when $p > 0$, left when $p < 0$.
- $q$ gives a **vertical translation**: up when $q > 0$, down when $q < 0$.

The graph of $y = (x - 3)^2 + 2$ is the graph of $y = x^2$ moved $3$ units right and $2$ units up, so the vertex moves from $(0, 0)$ to $(3, 2)$.

:::mistake The sign of p
In $y = (x + 3)^2$ the graph moves $3$ units **left**, not right. Write $x + 3$ as $x - (-3)$: then $p = -3$.
:::

:::example Describing transformations
Describe how the graph of $y = x^2$ is transformed to give $y = -2(x + 4)^2 + 1$.
@step $a = -2$: a reflection in the $x$-axis and a vertical stretch by a factor of $2$.
@step $p = -4$, because $x + 4 = x - (-4)$: a translation $4$ units left.
@step $q = 1$: a translation $1$ unit up.
@answer Reflect in the $x$-axis, stretch vertically by a factor of $2$, then translate $4$ units left and $1$ unit up. The vertex moves to $(-4, 1)$.
:::

## Reading features from vertex form

Why is $(p, q)$ the vertex? Suppose $a > 0$. A square is never negative, so $a(x - p)^2 \ge 0$ for every $x$. Therefore
$$y = a(x - p)^2 + q \ge q,$$
with $y = q$ only when $x = p$. So $q$ is the minimum value, reached at $x = p$. When $a < 0$, $a(x - p)^2 \le 0$, so $y \le q$ and $q$ is the maximum value. The symmetry follows too: $x = p + h$ and $x = p - h$ give the same $y$-value, because $h^2 = (-h)^2$.

For $y = a(x - p)^2 + q$:

- the vertex is $(p, q)$ and the axis of symmetry is $x = p$;
- if $a > 0$ the parabola opens up and $q$ is the minimum value;
- if $a < 0$ the parabola opens down and $q$ is the maximum value.

:::example Features from the equation
State the vertex, the axis of symmetry, the direction of opening and the maximum or minimum value of $y = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 6)^2 + 8$.
@step Compare with $y = a(x - p)^2 + q$: $a = -\frac{1}{2}$, $p = 6$, $q = 8$.
@step The vertex is $(6, 8)$ and the axis of symmetry is $x = 6$.
@step $a < 0$, so the parabola opens down and the vertex is a maximum point.
@answer Vertex $(6, 8)$; axis $x = 6$; opens down; maximum value $8$.
:::

## Domain and range

:::definition Domain and range
The **domain** is the set of all possible $x$-values of a function, and the **range** is the set of all possible $y$-values. In set notation, $\{y \mid y \ge 3,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$ is read "the set of all real numbers $y$ such that $y$ is greater than or equal to $3$".
:::

Any real number can be substituted for $x$, so the domain of every quadratic function is $\{x \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$. The range starts at the $y$-coordinate of the vertex:

- if $a > 0$, the range is $\{y \mid y \ge q,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$;
- if $a < 0$, the range is $\{y \mid y \le q,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$.

:::example Domain and range
State the domain and range of $y = 3(x + 2)^2 - 5$.
@step The vertex is $(-2, -5)$ and $a = 3 > 0$, so the parabola opens up and $-5$ is the minimum value.
@answer Domain $\{x \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$; range $\{y \mid y \ge -5,\ y \in \mathbb{R}\}$.
:::

:::mistake Using p in the range
The range depends on $q$, the $y$-coordinate of the vertex. For $y = 3(x + 2)^2 - 5$ the range is $y \ge -5$, not $y \ge -2$.
:::

:::tip Interval notation
The range $y \ge -5$ can also be written $[-5, \infty)$, and $y \le 8$ as $(-\infty, 8]$. A square bracket means the endpoint is included.
:::

## Mapping points and modelling

Each point $(x, y)$ on $y = x^2$ maps to the point $(x + p,\ ay + q)$ on $y = a(x - p)^2 + q$: stretch and reflect the $y$-coordinate first, then translate.

:::example Image of a point
The point $(2, 4)$ is on $y = x^2$. Find its image on $y = -2(x + 4)^2 + 1$.
@step $x$: $2 + (-4) = -2$.
@step $y$: $-2(4) + 1 = -7$.
@step Check: $-2(-2 + 4)^2 + 1 = -2(4) + 1 = -7$ ✓
@answer $(-2, -7)$
:::

In a context, the domain and range may be limited by the situation.

:::example A thrown ball
A ball thrown from a height of $1$ m follows $h = -0.2(d - 5)^2 + 6$, where $h$ is its height and $d$ the horizontal distance, both in metres. Find its maximum height and the range from release until it lands.
@step The vertex is $(5, 6)$ and $a < 0$, so the maximum height is $6$ m, at $d = 5$ m.
@step The ball starts at $1$ m, rises to $6$ m, then falls to the ground, $0$ m.
@answer Maximum height $6$ m; range $\{h \mid 0 \le h \le 6,\ h \in \mathbb{R}\}$.
:::

:::summary
- In $y = a(x - p)^2 + q$, the vertex is $(p, q)$ and the axis of symmetry is $x = p$.
- $a > 0$: opens up, minimum value $q$. $a < 0$: opens down, maximum value $q$.
- $\lvert a \rvert$ is the vertical stretch factor; $p$ translates horizontally (watch the sign) and $q$ translates vertically.
- Domain: $\{x \mid x \in \mathbb{R}\}$. Range: $y \ge q$ or $y \le q$, unless a context limits it.
:::
