A robot arm swings to a point 3 m to the left of its base and 4 m up. What angle did it turn through, and what are the sine, cosine and tangent of that angle? Any point on a terminal arm answers both questions.

## Ratios from a point on the terminal arm

Let $P(x, y)$ be any point other than the origin on the terminal arm of $\theta$, and let $r$ be its distance from the origin.

:::definition Primary trigonometric ratios of any angle
$$r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \qquad \sin\theta = \frac{y}{r} \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{x}{r} \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{y}{x}\ (x \ne 0)$$
:::

Why do these make sense? If $\theta$ is acute, $P$ is in quadrant I and the legs of the right triangle under $P$ are $x$ (adjacent) and $y$ (opposite), with hypotenuse $r$: these are exactly SOH CAH TOA. Any other point on the same arm gives a similar triangle, so the ratios depend only on the angle. The definition simply keeps working past $90^\circ$, where $x$ or $y$ becomes negative.

:::example A point with whole-number distance
$P(-3, 4)$ is on the terminal arm of $\theta$. Determine $r$ and the three ratios.
@step $r = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$
@step $\sin\theta = \dfrac{4}{5}$, $\cos\theta = \dfrac{-3}{5} = -\dfrac{3}{5}$, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{4}{-3} = -\dfrac{4}{3}$
@answer $r = 5$; $\sin\theta = \dfrac{4}{5}$, $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{3}{5}$, $\tan\theta = -\dfrac{4}{3}$
:::

:::example Finding the angle
$P(2, -5)$ is on the terminal arm of $\theta$, $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$. Determine the exact value of $\cos\theta$ and the measure of $\theta$.
@step $r = \sqrt{2^2 + (-5)^2} = \sqrt{29}$, so $\cos\theta = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{29}} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{29}}{29}$.
@step Reference angle: $\theta_R = \tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{5}{2}\right) \approx 68.2^\circ$
@step $P$ is in quadrant IV, so $\theta = 360^\circ - 68.2^\circ$.
@answer $\cos\theta = \dfrac{2\sqrt{29}}{29}$ and $\theta \approx 291.8^\circ$
:::

## The CAST rule

Because $r$ is always positive, the sign of each ratio depends only on the signs of $x$ and $y$:

| Quadrant | $x$ | $y$ | Positive ratios |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | $+$ | $+$ | all three |
| II | $-$ | $+$ | sine |
| III | $-$ | $-$ | tangent ($\frac{-}{-} = +$) |
| IV | $+$ | $-$ | cosine |

Reading counterclockwise from quadrant IV spells **CAST**: **C**osine, **A**ll, **S**ine, **T**angent.

:::example Locating an angle
$\sin\theta < 0$ and $\tan\theta > 0$. In which quadrant is the terminal arm?
@step $\sin\theta < 0$ in quadrants III and IV.
@step $\tan\theta > 0$ in quadrants I and III.
@answer Both are true only in quadrant III.
:::

:::mistake Tangent in quadrant III
In quadrant III both $x$ and $y$ are negative. Their quotient is **positive**, so $\tan\theta > 0$ there even though sine and cosine are both negative.

## From one ratio to the others

If you know one ratio and the quadrant, build a point on the terminal arm and use $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$.

:::example Given cosine
$\cos\theta = -\dfrac{5}{13}$ and $\theta$ is in quadrant III. Determine the exact values of $\sin\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.
@step Take $x = -5$ and $r = 13$ (in quadrant III, $x < 0$).
@step $y^2 = 13^2 - (-5)^2 = 144$, and $y < 0$ in quadrant III, so $y = -12$.
@step $\sin\theta = \dfrac{-12}{13}$ and $\tan\theta = \dfrac{-12}{-5}$
@answer $\sin\theta = -\dfrac{12}{13}$, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{12}{5}$
:::

## Solving for angles from 0° to 360°

An equation such as $\sin\theta = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ usually has **two** solutions between $0^\circ$ and $360^\circ$. Use three steps:

1. Find the reference angle from the **positive** value of the ratio.
2. Use CAST to find the quadrants where the ratio has the given sign.
3. Place the reference angle in each of those quadrants.

:::example Special value
Solve $\sin\theta = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ for $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$.
@step $\sin\theta_R = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so $\theta_R = 60^\circ$.
@step Sine is negative in quadrants III and IV.
@step $\theta = 180^\circ + 60^\circ$ or $\theta = 360^\circ - 60^\circ$
@answer $\theta = 240^\circ$ or $\theta = 300^\circ$
:::

:::example With a calculator
Solve $5\cos\theta + 2 = 0$ for $0^\circ \le \theta < 360^\circ$, to the nearest tenth of a degree.
@step $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{2}{5} = -0.4$
@step $\theta_R = \cos^{-1}(0.4) \approx 66.42^\circ$
@step Cosine is negative in quadrants II and III: $\theta = 180^\circ - 66.42^\circ$ or $\theta = 180^\circ + 66.42^\circ$.
@answer $\theta \approx 113.6^\circ$ or $\theta \approx 246.4^\circ$
:::

:::mistake Trusting the calculator's single answer
A calculator gives only one angle. $\sin^{-1}(-0.42) \approx -24.8^\circ$ is not even between $0^\circ$ and $360^\circ$. Use the positive value to get $\theta_R \approx 24.8^\circ$, then place it in quadrants III and IV: $\theta \approx 204.8^\circ$ or $335.2^\circ$.
:::

:::summary
- For a point $P(x, y)$ on the terminal arm, $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$, $\sin\theta = \dfrac{y}{r}$, $\cos\theta = \dfrac{x}{r}$, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{y}{x}$.
- CAST: all ratios are positive in quadrant I, sine in II, tangent in III, cosine in IV.
- Given one ratio and the quadrant, build $x$, $y$ and $r$ with the correct signs.
- To solve for $\theta$: reference angle from the positive value, quadrants from CAST, then place $\theta_R$ in each quadrant.
:::
