You can already solve equations. Solving an inequality uses almost exactly the same steps, with one important exception. In this lesson you will find that exception, learn why it happens, and use inequalities to answer questions like "What is the greatest number of tickets I can buy?"

## Solving inequalities like equations

Adding or subtracting the same number on both sides keeps an inequality true. So does multiplying or dividing both sides by the same **positive** number. That means you can solve many inequalities just like equations.

:::example Two steps
Solve $3x - 4 \ge 11$ and show the solution on a number line.
@step Add $4$ to both sides: $3x \ge 15$
@step Divide both sides by $3$ (a positive number, so the sign stays the same): $x \ge 5$
@step Check with a value in the solution, $x = 6$: $3(6) - 4 = 14$, and $14 \ge 11$ is true.
@answer $x \ge 5$: a closed dot at $5$ with an arrow to the right.
:::

## Multiplying or dividing by a negative number

Start with a true statement: $-2 < 3$. Multiply both sides by $-1$ and you get $2$ and $-3$. But $2 < -3$ is false! The true statement is $2 > -3$. Multiplying by a negative number flips the order of numbers on the number line, so the inequality sign must reverse.

:::definition Reversing the inequality sign
When you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a **negative** number, reverse the inequality sign: $<$ becomes $>$, $\le$ becomes $\ge$, and so on.
:::

:::example Dividing by a negative
Solve $-2x + 7 < 15$.
@step Subtract $7$ from both sides: $-2x < 8$
@step Divide both sides by $-2$. It is negative, so reverse the sign: $x > -4$
@step Check with $x = 0$, which is in the solution: $-2(0) + 7 = 7$, and $7 < 15$ is true.
@step Check with $x = -5$, which is not in the solution: $-2(-5) + 7 = 17$, and $17 < 15$ is false, as expected.
@answer $x > -4$
:::

:::mistake When to reverse
Reverse the sign **only** when you multiply or divide by a negative number. Subtracting $7$ or adding $-3$ never reverses it. Forgetting to reverse in the example gives $x < -4$, which fails the check: $x = -5$ gives $17 < 15$, which is false.
:::

:::example Variables on both sides
Solve $5 - 2x \le 3x + 20$.
@step Subtract $3x$ from both sides: $5 - 5x \le 20$
@step Subtract $5$ from both sides: $-5x \le 15$
@step Divide both sides by $-5$ and reverse the sign: $x \ge -3$
@step Check with $x = 0$: $5 - 0 = 5$ and $0 + 20 = 20$, and $5 \le 20$ is true.
@answer $x \ge -3$
:::

:::tip Avoid the negative
You can also collect the $x$-terms on the side where the coefficient stays positive. From $5 - 2x \le 3x + 20$, add $2x$ and subtract $20$: $-15 \le 5x$, so $-3 \le x$. That is the same as $x \ge -3$.
:::

## Solving problems with inequalities

In many problems the answer must be a whole number: you cannot buy part of a ticket or win part of a game. Solve the inequality, then choose the whole number that answers the question.

:::example The greatest number
Priya has \$50 to spend on movie tickets that cost \$8 each. What is the greatest number of tickets she can buy?
@step Let $t$ be the number of tickets. The cost must be at most 50 dollars: $8t \le 50$
@step Divide both sides by $8$: $t \le 6.25$
@step $t$ must be a whole number, so the greatest value is $6$.
@step Check: 6 tickets cost \$48, which is within \$50. 7 tickets would cost \$56, which is too much.
@answer Priya can buy at most 6 tickets.
:::

:::example The least number
A soccer team earns 2 points for each win. It has 41 points and needs at least 60 points to make the playoffs. What is the least number of additional wins it needs?
@step Let $w$ be the number of wins. The points must be at least 60: $41 + 2w \ge 60$
@step Subtract $41$ from both sides: $2w \ge 19$
@step Divide both sides by $2$: $w \ge 9.5$
@step $w$ must be a whole number, so the least value is $10$.
@step Check: 10 wins give $41 + 20 = 61$ points. 9 wins give only $59$ points.
@answer The team needs at least 10 more wins.
:::

:::mistake Rounding the wrong way
For "greatest" with $\le$, round **down**: $t \le 6.25$ gives $6$, because $7$ breaks the rule. For "least" with $\ge$, round **up**: $w \ge 9.5$ gives $10$, because $9$ is not enough. Always check the whole number you choose and the next one.
:::

:::summary
- Solve an inequality like an equation: undo addition and subtraction first, then multiplication and division.
- When you multiply or divide both sides by a **negative** number, reverse the inequality sign.
- Check with a value from the solution set; it must make the original inequality true.
- In problems, the answer is often a whole number: round down for "greatest", round up for "least", and check.
:::
