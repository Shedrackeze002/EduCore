A trophy is a cylinder on a block. A building may have a smaller block on its roof. Objects like these are made of simpler solids joined together. To find how much paint, wrapping or icing they need, add the surface areas of the parts and take away the hidden parts.

## Composite objects and overlap

:::definition Composite object
A **composite object** is made of two or more simple solids joined together, such as prisms, cubes and cylinders.
:::

:::definition Overlap
The **overlap** is the area where two parts touch. It is hidden inside the object, so it is not part of the surface area.
:::

When two parts touch, **two** faces are hidden: the face of the bottom part that is covered, and the face of the top part that rests on it. So the overlap area is subtracted **twice**:

$$SA = SA_1 + SA_2 - 2 \times (\text{overlap area})$$

## Objects made of cubes

Each cube has $6$ faces. Every place where two cubes touch hides $2$ faces, one on each cube.

:::figure

:::

:::example Four cubes
Mei glues $4$ cubes together as shown. Each cube has edges of $2\ \text{cm}$. Find the surface area, including the bottom.
@step Faces of $4$ separate cubes: $6 \times 4 = 24$.
@step The cubes touch in $3$ places: $2$ side by side and $1$ stacked. That hides $2 \times 3 = 6$ faces.
@step Faces on the outside: $24 - 6 = 18$.
@step Each face has an area of $2 \times 2 = 4\ \text{cm}^2$.
@answer $SA = 18 \times 4 = 72\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Forgetting the bottom
The faces on the table are still on the outside of the object. Count them unless the problem says the bottom is not covered.
:::

:::

:::example A two-layer cake
A cake has a bottom layer $40\ \text{cm}$ by $30\ \text{cm}$ by $10\ \text{cm}$. A top layer $20\ \text{cm}$ by $15\ \text{cm}$ by $10\ \text{cm}$ sits on the middle of it. Find the surface area of the whole cake.
@step Bottom layer: $2(40 \times 30 + 40 \times 10 + 30 \times 10) = 2(1200 + 400 + 300) = 3800\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Top layer: $2(20 \times 15 + 20 \times 10 + 15 \times 10) = 2(300 + 200 + 150) = 1300\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Overlap: the bottom of the top layer, $20 \times 15 = 300\ \text{cm}^2$. It hides $300\ \text{cm}^2$ on each layer.
@step $SA = 3800 + 1300 - 2 \times 300 = 4500$
@answer The surface area is $4500\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Subtracting the overlap once
Subtracting $300$ only once leaves the hidden top of the bottom layer in the total. Both touching faces are inside the cake, so subtract the overlap twice.
:::

If the bottom of the object is not covered (a cake is not iced underneath), subtract the area of the bottom too: here $4500 - 40 \times 30 = 3300\ \text{cm}^2$.

:::example A trophy
A trophy is a cylinder with a radius of $3\ \text{cm}$ and a height of $15\ \text{cm}$, standing on a block $12\ \text{cm}$ by $10\ \text{cm}$ by $4\ \text{cm}$. Find its surface area to the nearest tenth of a square centimetre.
@step Block: $2(12 \times 10 + 12 \times 4 + 10 \times 4) = 416\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Cylinder: $2\pi(3)^2 + 2\pi(3)(15) \approx 56.55 + 282.74 = 339.29\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Overlap: one circle, $\pi(3)^2 \approx 28.27\ \text{cm}^2$, subtracted twice: $56.55\ \text{cm}^2$.
@step $SA \approx 416 + 339.29 - 56.55 = 698.74$
@answer The surface area is about $698.7\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::tip A shortcut
In the trophy, the top of the cylinder has the same area as the circle it covers on the block. So the answer is also the block plus only the curved side of the cylinder: $416 + 2\pi(3)(15) \approx 698.7\ \text{cm}^2$. Use it to check your work.
:::

:::summary
- A composite object is made of simpler solids joined together.
- Find the surface area of each part, then subtract the overlap area twice, because two faces are hidden where the parts touch.
- For cubes: outside faces $= 6 \times (\text{number of cubes}) - 2 \times (\text{places where cubes touch})$.
- For a cylinder on a prism, the overlap is a circle with area $\pi r^2$.
- Read the problem: leave out any surface, like the bottom, that is not covered.
:::
