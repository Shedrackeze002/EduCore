Multiplying $2^{10} \times 2^{6}$ by writing out all sixteen factors would take a while. Exponent laws are shortcuts that come straight from what a power means. Learn why each one works and you will never need to memorize them blindly.

## The product law

:::definition Exponent law
An **exponent law** is a rule for simplifying products, quotients and powers of powers. The laws work when the base is not $0$ and the exponents are whole numbers.
:::

Look at what happens when you multiply two powers with the same base.

:::example Why the product law works
Write $2^{3} \times 2^{4}$ as a single power.
@step $2^{3} = 2 \times 2 \times 2$ and $2^{4} = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2$.
@step Together there are $3 + 4 = 7$ factors of $2$.
@answer $2^{3} \times 2^{4} = 2^{3 + 4} = 2^{7}$
:::

:::note Product law
$a^{m} \times a^{n} = a^{m + n}$: keep the base and **add** the exponents.
:::

:::mistake Do not multiply the bases
$3^{4} \times 3^{2}$ is **not** $9^{6}$. The base stays the same: $3^{4} \times 3^{2} = 3^{6}$.
:::

## The quotient law and the zero exponent

When you divide, common factors divide out.

:::example Why the quotient law works
Write $5^{6} \div 5^{2}$ as a single power.
@step $\dfrac{5^{6}}{5^{2}} = \dfrac{5 \times 5 \times 5 \times 5 \times 5 \times 5}{5 \times 5}$
@step Two factors of $5$ divide out, leaving $6 - 2 = 4$ factors.
@answer $5^{6} \div 5^{2} = 5^{6 - 2} = 5^{4}$
:::

:::note Quotient law
$a^{m} \div a^{n} = a^{m - n}$: keep the base and **subtract** the exponents.
:::

What does an exponent of $0$ mean? Follow the pattern. Each time the exponent goes down by $1$, the value is divided by $3$.

The quotient law agrees: $\dfrac{3^{2}}{3^{2}} = 3^{2 - 2} = 3^{0}$, and any number divided by itself is $1$.

:::definition Zero exponent
A power with a base that is not $0$ and an exponent of $0$ equals $1$: $a^{0} = 1$. So $8^{0} = 1$ and $(-5)^{0} = 1$, but $-5^{0} = -(5^{0}) = -1$.
:::

## Powers of powers, products and quotients

:::example Power of a power
Write $\left(4^{2}\right)^{3}$ as a single power.
@step $\left(4^{2}\right)^{3} = 4^{2} \times 4^{2} \times 4^{2}$
@step That is $3$ groups of $2$ factors, or $2 \times 3 = 6$ factors of $4$.
@answer $\left(4^{2}\right)^{3} = 4^{2 \times 3} = 4^{6}$
:::

:::example Power of a product
Evaluate $(2 \times 5)^{3}$ two ways.
@step Brackets first: $(2 \times 5)^{3} = 10^{3} = 1000$.
@step Power of a product: $(2 \times 5)^{3} = 2^{3} \times 5^{3} = 8 \times 125 = 1000$.
@answer Both ways give $1000$.
:::

:::example Power of a quotient
Evaluate $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{3}$.
@step Raise the top and the bottom to the exponent: $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{3} = \dfrac{2^{3}}{3^{3}}$
@answer $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{3} = \dfrac{8}{27}$
:::

:::note The exponent laws
- Product: $a^{m} \times a^{n} = a^{m + n}$
- Quotient: $a^{m} \div a^{n} = a^{m - n}$
- Power of a power: $\left(a^{m}\right)^{n} = a^{m \times n}$
- Power of a product: $(ab)^{n} = a^{n}b^{n}$
- Power of a quotient: $\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^{n} = \dfrac{a^{n}}{b^{n}}$, where $b \ne 0$
- Zero exponent: $a^{0} = 1$, where $a \ne 0$
:::

## Simplify, then evaluate

With several laws in one question, simplify to a single power first. Then the number you have to evaluate is small.

:::example Simplify, then evaluate
Evaluate $\left(2^{3} \times 2^{4}\right) \div 2^{5}$.
@step Brackets first, product law: $2^{3} \times 2^{4} = 2^{7}$
@step Quotient law: $2^{7} \div 2^{5} = 2^{2}$
@answer $2^{2} = 4$
:::

:::mistake The laws are for multiplying and dividing
$2^{3} + 2^{4}$ is **not** $2^{7}$. There is no law for adding powers. Evaluate each one: $2^{3} + 2^{4} = 8 + 16 = 24$, while $2^{7} = 128$.
:::

:::check m9-exponent-laws 3
:::

:::summary
- Same base, multiplying: add the exponents. Dividing: subtract the exponents.
- Power of a power: multiply the exponents.
- An exponent outside brackets applies to every factor of a product or quotient inside.
- Any non-zero base with exponent $0$ equals $1$.
- Simplify to one power first, then evaluate.
:::
