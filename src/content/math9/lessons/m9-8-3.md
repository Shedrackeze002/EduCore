When a bike wheel rolls along a flat road, the road touches the wheel at just one point. A line that touches a circle like that is called a tangent, and it meets the radius in a very special way.

## Tangents meet the radius at a right angle

:::definition Tangent and point of tangency
A **tangent** is a line that touches a circle at exactly one point. That point is the **point of tangency**.
:::

**Tangent property:** a tangent is **perpendicular** to the radius at the point of tangency. If $PT$ is tangent to the circle at $P$, then $\angle OPT = 90^\circ$.

## Tangents and the Pythagorean theorem

Triangle $OPT$ has a right angle at $P$. The side across from the right angle, $OT$, is the hypotenuse:
$$OT^2 = OP^2 + PT^2$$

:::example Length of a tangent segment
A circle has a radius of $5\ \text{cm}$. Point $T$ is $13\ \text{cm}$ from the centre. Find the length of the tangent segment $x = PT$.
@step $\angle OPT = 90^\circ$, so $OT$ is the hypotenuse.
@step $x^2 = 13^2 - 5^2 = 169 - 25 = 144$
@answer $x = \sqrt{144} = 12$, so $PT = 12\ \text{cm}$.
:::

:::example Distance to the centre
A circle has a radius of $6\ \text{m}$, and the tangent segment $PT$ is $8\ \text{m}$ long. How far is $T$ from the centre?
@step $OT^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100$
@answer $OT = \sqrt{100} = 10\ \text{m}$
:::

:::mistake Picking the wrong hypotenuse
The hypotenuse is always $OT$, the side across from the right angle at $P$. With a radius of $4\ \text{cm}$ and $OT = 9\ \text{cm}$, subtract: $PT = \sqrt{81 - 16} = \sqrt{65} \approx 8.1\ \text{cm}$. Adding would give $\sqrt{97}$, which is longer than $OT$ and impossible.
:::

## Two tangents from one point

From a point $T$ outside a circle, you can draw two tangents, touching the circle at $P$ and $Q$. Both angles at $P$ and $Q$ are $90^\circ$, and the angles of quadrilateral $OPTQ$ add to $360^\circ$.

:::example Perimeter of the kite
The circle has a radius of $8\ \text{cm}$ and $OT = 17\ \text{cm}$. Find the perimeter of $OPTQ$.
@step $TP = \sqrt{17^2 - 8^2} = \sqrt{289 - 64} = \sqrt{225} = 15$, and $TQ = 15$ too.
@step Perimeter $= 8 + 15 + 15 + 8 = 46$
@answer The perimeter of $OPTQ$ is $46\ \text{cm}$.

:::summary
- A tangent touches a circle at exactly one point, the point of tangency.
- A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency: $\angle OPT = 90^\circ$.
- In right $\triangle OPT$, $OT$ is the hypotenuse: $OT^2 = OP^2 + PT^2$.
- Two tangents from the same point are equal in length, and $\angle PTQ + \angle POQ = 180^\circ$.
:::
