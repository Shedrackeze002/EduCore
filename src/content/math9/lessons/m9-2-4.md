A video shared online can reach thousands of people in a few hours. Bacteria in a warm kitchen can double every 20 minutes. Powers are the natural way to describe growth like this, and also areas and volumes.

## Area and volume

The area of a square and the volume of a cube are powers of the side length.

:::note Squares and cubes
- Area of a square with side $s$: $A = s^{2}$
- Volume of a cube with edge $s$: $V = s^{3}$
- Surface area of a cube: $SA = 6s^{2}$, because a cube has $6$ square faces.
:::

:::figure

:::

:::example A cube-shaped box
A cube-shaped gift box has edges of $4\ \text{cm}$. Find its volume and its surface area.
@step Volume: $V = 4^{3} = 4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64$
@step One face: $4^{2} = 16\ \text{cm}^{2}$. Six faces: $6 \times 16 = 96$
@answer The volume is $64\ \text{cm}^{3}$ and the surface area is $96\ \text{cm}^{2}$.
:::

:::mistake Squared units
Area is measured in square units ($\text{cm}^{2}$) and volume in cubic units ($\text{cm}^{3}$). The exponent on the unit matches the exponent in the formula.
:::

## Doubling

When an amount doubles again and again, each doubling multiplies it by $2$. After $n$ doublings, the amount has been multiplied by $2^{n}$.

:::figure
{"type":"table","headers":["Time (min)","Doublings","Cells"],"rows":[["0","0","50"],["20","1","100"],["40","2","200"],["60","3","400"]]}
:::

:::example Bacteria
A sample starts with $50$ bacteria cells. The number doubles every $20$ minutes. How many cells are there after $2$ hours?
@step $2$ hours is $120$ minutes. Number of doublings: $120 \div 20 = 6$.
@step Number of cells $= 50 \times 2^{6}$
@step $2^{6} = 64$, so $50 \times 64 = 3200$.
@answer There are $3200$ cells after $2$ hours.
:::

:::tip Count the doublings first
The exponent is the number of doublings, not the number of minutes or hours. Divide the total time by the doubling time.
:::

## Sharing and folding

:::example A story shared online
Priya posts a story about a school fundraiser and shares it with $3$ friends. In each round after that, every person who just got it shares it with $3$ new people. How many new people get it in round $4$? How many people have it after $4$ rounds, not counting Priya?
@step New people: round $1$: $3$, round $2$: $3^{2} = 9$, round $3$: $3^{3} = 27$, round $4$: $3^{4} = 81$.
@step Total: $3 + 9 + 27 + 81 = 120$
@answer $81$ new people get it in round $4$, and $120$ people have it after $4$ rounds.
:::

Folding paper in half doubles the number of layers each time, so the thickness grows as a power of $2$.

:::example Folding paper
A sheet of paper is $0.1\ \text{mm}$ thick. What is the least number of folds that makes the stack thicker than $1\ \text{cm}$?
@step $1\ \text{cm} = 10\ \text{mm}$. After $n$ folds, the thickness is $0.1 \times 2^{n}$ mm, so we need $2^{n} > 100$.
@step $2^{6} = 64$ is too small. $2^{7} = 128$ is enough: $0.1 \times 128 = 12.8\ \text{mm}$.
@answer The least number of folds is $7$.
:::

:::summary
- Area of a square: $s^{2}$. Volume of a cube: $s^{3}$. Surface area of a cube: $6s^{2}$.
- After $n$ doublings, an amount is multiplied by $2^{n}$. Find $n$ by dividing the total time by the doubling time.
- When each person shares with $k$ new people, round $n$ reaches $k^{n}$ new people.
- To find when an amount first passes a target, try powers until one is big enough.
:::
