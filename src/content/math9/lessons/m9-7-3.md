A $1 : 24$ model truck, an enlarged photo and the shadow of a flagpole all involve shapes that are the same shape but a different size. In math, these shapes are called **similar**, and similar shapes let you measure things you cannot reach.

## What makes polygons similar?

:::definition Similar polygons
Two polygons are **similar** when **both** of these are true:
- every pair of **corresponding angles** is equal, and
- every pair of **corresponding sides** is in the same ratio (the sides are **proportional**).
:::

:::definition Corresponding angles and sides
**Corresponding** angles and sides are the ones in matching positions. If $\triangle ABC$ is similar to $\triangle DEF$ (written $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$), then $A$ matches $D$, $B$ matches $E$ and $C$ matches $F$, so side $AB$ matches side $DE$.
:::

:::example Two rectangles
Rectangle A is $6\ \text{cm}$ by $4\ \text{cm}$. Rectangle B is $9\ \text{cm}$ by $6\ \text{cm}$. Are they similar?
@step Angles: every angle of a rectangle is $90^\circ$, so the corresponding angles are equal.
@step Long sides: $\dfrac{9}{6} = 1.5$. Short sides: $\dfrac{6}{4} = 1.5$.
@answer The ratios are equal, so the sides are proportional. The rectangles **are** similar.
:::

## One condition is not enough

**Equal angles, but not proportional.** A $4\ \text{m}$ square and an $8\ \text{m}$ by $4\ \text{m}$ rectangle both have four $90^\circ$ angles. But $\dfrac{8}{4} = 2$ while $\dfrac{4}{4} = 1$, so they are **not** similar.

:::mistake "Similar" does not mean "same size"
Similar shapes can be different sizes. Shapes with the same shape **and** the same size are called *congruent*. Adding the same amount to every side does not make a similar shape either: a $10\ \text{cm} \times 15\ \text{cm}$ photo and a $20\ \text{cm} \times 25\ \text{cm}$ print are not similar, because $\dfrac{20}{10} = 2$ but $\dfrac{25}{15} \approx 1.67$.
:::

:::tip Triangles are special
For triangles, one condition is enough. If the three pairs of angles are equal, the sides are proportional. If the three pairs of sides are proportional, the angles are equal.
:::

## Finding a missing side

In similar polygons, every pair of corresponding sides has the same ratio. Use a pair where you know both lengths to find the scale factor, then use it on the missing side.

:::example Using the vertex order
$\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$. $AB = 6\ \text{cm}$, $BC = 8\ \text{cm}$ and $DE = 9\ \text{cm}$. Find $x = EF$.
@step The letters match in order, so $AB \leftrightarrow DE$ and $BC \leftrightarrow EF$.
@step $\dfrac{EF}{BC} = \dfrac{DE}{AB}$, so $\dfrac{x}{8} = \dfrac{9}{6} = 1.5$
@step $x = 8 \times 1.5 = 12$
@answer $EF = 12\ \text{cm}$
:::

:::mistake Matching the wrong sides
Use the order of the letters, not the position on the page. The second shape may be turned or flipped.
:::

## Measuring heights with similar triangles

At the same moment, the sun's rays hit every object at the same angle. A person and their shadow make a triangle that is similar to the triangle made by a tree and its shadow.

:::example A tree's shadow
Mateo is $1.6\ \text{m}$ tall and casts a shadow $2.4\ \text{m}$ long. At the same time, a tree casts a shadow $15\ \text{m}$ long. How tall is the tree?
@step Height matches height and shadow matches shadow: $\dfrac{h}{15} = \dfrac{1.6}{2.4}$
@step $h = 15 \times \dfrac{1.6}{2.4} = 10$
@answer The tree is $10\ \text{m}$ tall.
:::

A mirror on the ground works too. Light leaves a mirror at the same angle it arrives, so the two right triangles beside the mirror are similar.

:::example The mirror method
Talia's eyes are $1.5\ \text{m}$ above the ground. She stands $2\ \text{m}$ from a mirror that is $12\ \text{m}$ from the base of a flagpole, and sees the top of the pole in the mirror. How tall is the flagpole?
@step $\dfrac{h}{12} = \dfrac{1.5}{2}$
@step $h = 12 \times \dfrac{1.5}{2} = 9$
@answer The flagpole is $9\ \text{m}$ tall.
:::

:::summary
- Similar polygons have equal corresponding angles **and** proportional corresponding sides.
- For triangles, equal angles or proportional sides is enough.
- Match sides by the order of the letters: in $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$, $AB$ matches $DE$.
- Find the scale factor from one known pair, then use it on the missing side.
- Shadows and mirrors make similar triangles, so you can find heights you cannot reach.
:::
