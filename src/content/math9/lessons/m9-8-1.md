Bicycle wheels, round tables, culverts under farm roads and hockey face-off circles are all circles. A few simple facts about lines in circles let you find lengths you cannot measure directly.

## Parts of a circle

:::definition Centre, radius and diameter
The **centre** of a circle is the point the same distance from every point on the circle; we usually call it $O$. A **radius** is a segment from the centre to the circle. A **diameter** passes through the centre and is two radii long.
:::

:::definition Chord
A **chord** is a segment that joins two points on a circle. A diameter is the longest chord.
:::

## The perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord

:::definition Perpendicular bisector
Two lines are **perpendicular** when they meet at $90^\circ$. To **bisect** a segment is to cut it into two equal parts.
:::

**Chord property:** the perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord **bisects** the chord. If $OM \perp AB$, then $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $AM = MB$.

:::example Finding a chord
$OM$ is perpendicular to the chord $AB$, and $AM = 7\ \text{cm}$. How long is $AB$?
@step The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so $MB = AM = 7\ \text{cm}$.
@step $AB = 7 + 7 = 14$
@answer The chord is $14\ \text{cm}$ long.
:::

:::tip It works both ways
The segment from the centre to the **midpoint** of a chord is perpendicular to the chord. So the perpendicular bisector of any chord passes through the centre. Two chords are enough to find the centre of a round table.
:::

:::example Length of a chord
A circle has a radius of $13\ \text{cm}$. The chord $AB$ is $5\ \text{cm}$ from the centre. How long is $AB$?
@step In right $\triangle OMA$: $AM^2 = 13^2 - 5^2 = 169 - 25 = 144$
@step $AM = \sqrt{144} = 12$
@step $OM$ bisects the chord, so $AB = 2 \times 12 = 24$
@answer The chord is $24\ \text{cm}$ long.
:::

:::example Distance from the centre
A circle has a radius of $10\ \text{m}$ and a chord $12\ \text{m}$ long. How far is the chord from the centre?
@step Half the chord: $AM = 12 \div 2 = 6$
@step $OM^2 = 10^2 - 6^2 = 100 - 36 = 64$
@answer $OM = \sqrt{64} = 8$, so the chord is $8\ \text{m}$ from the centre.
:::

:::mistake Using the whole chord
The right triangle uses **half** the chord. With a radius of $9\ \text{cm}$ and $OM = 4\ \text{cm}$, $AM = \sqrt{81 - 16} = \sqrt{65} \approx 8.06$, so $AB \approx 16.1\ \text{cm}$. Do not forget to double at the end.
:::

:::example Water in a pipe
The end of a drainage pipe is a circle with a radius of $13\ \text{cm}$. The top of the water is a chord $24\ \text{cm}$ wide, and the pipe is less than half full. How deep is the water?
@step Half the chord is $12\ \text{cm}$. The distance $d$ from the centre to the water surface is $\sqrt{13^2 - 12^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.
@step The surface is below the centre, so the depth is the radius minus $d$: $13 - 5 = 8$.
@answer The water is $8\ \text{cm}$ deep: $h = 8\ \text{cm}$.
:::

:::summary
- A chord joins two points on a circle; a diameter is a chord through the centre.
- The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.
- A radius, half a chord and the distance from the centre make a right triangle: $r^2 = (\text{half chord})^2 + d^2$.
- Use half the chord in the triangle, and double it at the end if you need the whole chord.
:::
