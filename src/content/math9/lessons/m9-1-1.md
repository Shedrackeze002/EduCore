A square photo, a square floor tile and a square garden all share one fact: the side length is the square root of the area. In Grade 8 you found square roots of whole numbers. Now you will find square roots of fractions and decimals.

## Perfect squares and square roots

:::definition Perfect square
A **perfect square** is a number that is the square of another number. In Grade 9, that number can be a fraction or a decimal: $0.49 = 0.7^2$ and $\frac{9}{16} = \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^2$ are perfect squares.
:::

:::definition Square root
The **square root** of a number is a number that, multiplied by itself, gives the original number. $\sqrt{0.49} = 0.7$ because $0.7 \times 0.7 = 0.49$.
:::

Think of a square with an area of $1$ split into $100$ small squares. Shade $49$ of them as a square: it is $7$ small squares wide. So a square with an area of $0.49$ has sides of $0.7$.

## Square roots of fractions

For a fraction, take the square root of the numerator and the square root of the denominator:
$$\sqrt{\frac{9}{16}} = \frac{\sqrt{9}}{\sqrt{16}} = \frac{3}{4}$$

:::example A fraction not in lowest terms
Find $\sqrt{\dfrac{8}{18}}$.
@step Neither $8$ nor $18$ is a perfect square, so write the fraction in lowest terms first: $\frac{8}{18} = \frac{4}{9}$.
@step $\sqrt{4} = 2$ and $\sqrt{9} = 3$.
@step Check: $\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{4}{9} = \frac{8}{18}$.
@answer $\sqrt{\dfrac{8}{18}} = \dfrac{2}{3}$
:::

## Square roots of decimals

Write the decimal as a fraction with a denominator of $100$ or $10\,000$, which are perfect squares.

:::example Two decimals
Find $\sqrt{1.44}$ and $\sqrt{0.0009}$.
@step $1.44 = \frac{144}{100}$, so $\sqrt{1.44} = \frac{12}{10} = 1.2$.
@step $0.0009 = \frac{9}{10\,000}$, so $\sqrt{0.0009} = \frac{3}{100} = 0.03$.
@answer $\sqrt{1.44} = 1.2$ and $\sqrt{0.0009} = 0.03$. Check: $1.2^2 = 1.44$ and $0.03^2 = 0.0009$.
:::

:::tip Count the decimal places
A decimal perfect square has an **even** number of decimal places, and its square root has **half** as many: $1.44$ has $2$, and $1.2$ has $1$.
:::

:::mistake Taking the root of the digits only
$\sqrt{0.4}$ is **not** $0.2$, because $0.2^2 = 0.04$. In fact $0.4 = \frac{2}{5}$, and $0.4$ is not a perfect square at all.
:::

:::check m9-sqrt-perfect 1
:::

## Is it a perfect square?

A fraction in **lowest terms** is a perfect square when its numerator and its denominator are both perfect squares. To test a decimal, write it as a fraction first.

:::example Testing four numbers
Which of $0.36$, $3.6$, $\frac{25}{45}$ and $0.0036$ are perfect squares?
@step $0.36 = \frac{36}{100}$: yes, $\sqrt{0.36} = 0.6$.
@step $3.6 = \frac{36}{10} = \frac{18}{5}$: neither $18$ nor $5$ is a perfect square, so no.
@step $\frac{25}{45} = \frac{5}{9}$ in lowest terms: $5$ is not a perfect square, so no.
@step $0.0036 = \frac{36}{10\,000}$: yes, $\sqrt{0.0036} = 0.06$.
@answer $0.36$ and $0.0036$ are perfect squares. $3.6$ and $\frac{25}{45}$ are not.
:::

:::example A square garden
A square vegetable garden has an area of $6.25\ \text{m}^2$. How much fencing goes around it?
@step Side length: $\sqrt{6.25} = 2.5\ \text{m}$, because $2.5^2 = 6.25$.
@step Perimeter: $4 \times 2.5 = 10$
@answer The garden needs $10\ \text{m}$ of fencing.
:::

:::

:::summary
- A perfect square is the square of a number; that number can be a fraction or a decimal.
- $\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}$ when $a$ and $b$ are perfect squares. Write the fraction in lowest terms first.
- For a decimal, write it over $100$ or $10\,000$: $\sqrt{1.44} = \frac{12}{10} = 1.2$.
- Always check by squaring your answer.
- The side of a square is the square root of its area.
:::
