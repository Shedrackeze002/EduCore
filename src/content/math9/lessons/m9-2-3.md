Two friends work out $4 + 5^{2} \times 2$. One gets $54$ and the other gets $162$. Only one of them is right. An agreed order of operations makes sure everyone gets the same answer.

## The order of operations

:::definition Order of operations
The **order of operations** is the agreed order for evaluating an expression:
1. **B**rackets
2. **E**xponents (powers)
3. **D**ivide and **M**ultiply, from left to right
4. **A**dd and **S**ubtract, from left to right

Many people remember it as BEDMAS.
:::

:::example Powers before multiplying
Evaluate $4 + 5^{2} \times 2$.
@step Exponent first: $4 + 25 \times 2$
@step Multiply: $4 + 50$
@step Add: $54$
@answer $4 + 5^{2} \times 2 = 54$
:::

The friend who got $162$ added first: $(4 + 5)^{2} \times 2 = 81 \times 2 = 162$. That is a different expression.

:::example Brackets first
Evaluate $(3 + 4)^{2} - 2^{3}$.
@step Brackets: $7^{2} - 2^{3}$
@step Exponents: $49 - 8$
@answer $(3 + 4)^{2} - 2^{3} = 41$
:::

## Negative numbers and fraction bars

With integers, take extra care with the sign of each power.

:::example Integers and powers
Evaluate $(-3)^{2} - 4 \times (2 - 5)^{3}$.
@step Brackets: $(-3)^{2} - 4 \times (-3)^{3}$
@step Exponents: $(-3)^{2} = 9$ and $(-3)^{3} = -27$, so we have $9 - 4 \times (-27)$.
@step Multiply: $9 - (-108)$
@step Subtracting a negative is adding: $9 + 108 = 117$
@answer $117$
:::

A fraction bar groups the top and the bottom, as if each had brackets around it.

:::example A fraction bar
Evaluate $\dfrac{6^{2} - 4}{2^{3} - 4}$.
@step Top: $36 - 4 = 32$. Bottom: $8 - 4 = 4$.
@step Divide: $32 \div 4 = 8$
@answer $8$
:::

:::mistake The base of $-3^{2}$
In $-3^{2} + 10$, the base is $3$, so $-3^{2} = -9$ and the value is $-9 + 10 = 1$. Writing $-3^{2} = 9$ gives the wrong answer, $19$.
:::

:::

## Using a calculator

Most scientific calculators follow the order of operations. Still, you must enter the expression the way it is written.

:::tip Brackets on a calculator
To find $(-3)^{4}$, type the brackets: `(` `(−)` `3` `)` `^` `4` gives $81$. Typing `(−)` `3` `^` `4` without brackets gives $-81$, because the calculator reads it as $-3^{4}$. Estimate the sign first, so you can catch a slip.
:::

## Finding errors

Checking a solution one step at a time is a good way to find mistakes, including your own.

:::example Find the first error
Owen evaluated $20 - 2 \times (1 - 4)^{2}$ like this:

**Step 1:** $= 20 - 2 \times (-3)^{2}$

**Step 2:** $= 20 - 2 \times (-9)$

**Step 3:** $= 20 + 18 = 38$

@step Step 1 is correct: $1 - 4 = -3$.
@step Step 2 is wrong. $(-3)^{2} = (-3) \times (-3) = 9$, not $-9$.
@step Correct work: $20 - 2 \times 9 = 20 - 18 = 2$
@answer The first error is in Step 2. The correct value is $2$.
:::

:::mistake Left to right
$24 \div 2 \times 3$ is $12 \times 3 = 36$, not $24 \div 6 = 4$. Multiply and divide in order from left to right. The same is true for adding and subtracting: $10 - 3 + 2 = 9$, not $5$.
:::

:::check m9-order-ops-powers 3
:::

:::summary
- Use BEDMAS: brackets, exponents, divide and multiply from left to right, add and subtract from left to right.
- Watch the base: $(-3)^{2} = 9$ but $-3^{2} = -9$.
- A fraction bar groups the top and the bottom.
- Check a solution one step at a time to find the first error.
:::
