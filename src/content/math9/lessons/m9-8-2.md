Stand anywhere on the edge of a round skating pond and look at two hockey nets on the far side. The angle you see is linked to the angle at the centre of the pond in a surprisingly simple way.

## Central and inscribed angles

:::definition Arc
An **arc** is a part of a circle between two points on the circle.
:::

:::definition Central angle and inscribed angle
A **central angle** has its vertex at the centre $O$, like $\angle AOB$. An **inscribed angle** has its vertex on the circle, like $\angle ACB$. Both angles below have arms that end at $A$ and $B$.
:::

:::definition Subtend
An angle is **subtended** by an arc when its arms end at the two ends of the arc. Below, $\angle AOB$ and $\angle ACB$ are both subtended by arc $AB$.
:::

## A central angle is twice an inscribed angle

**Central angle property:** the central angle is **twice** the inscribed angle subtended by the same arc.
$$\angle AOB = 2 \times \angle ACB$$

:::example From central to inscribed
In the diagram, $\angle AOB = 110^\circ$. Find $x = \angle ACB$.
@step Both angles are subtended by arc $AB$.
@step The inscribed angle is half the central angle: $x = 110^\circ \div 2$
@answer $x = 55^\circ$
:::

Going the other way, an inscribed angle of $28^\circ$ goes with a central angle of $2 \times 28^\circ = 56^\circ$ on the same arc.

:::mistake Halving or doubling the wrong way
The angle at the **centre** is the bigger one. If you double the central angle instead of halving it, your inscribed angle will be too big. Check your answer against the diagram.
:::

## Angles in a semicircle

When $AB$ is a diameter, the central angle $\angle AOB$ is a straight angle, $180^\circ$. So any inscribed angle on a diameter is $180^\circ \div 2 = 90^\circ$.

**Semicircle property:** an angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle.

:::example A right triangle in a circle
$AB$ is a diameter and $\angle CAB = 32^\circ$. Find $x = \angle ABC$.
@step $\angle ACB$ is inscribed in a semicircle, so $\angle ACB = 90^\circ$.
@step The angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$: $x = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 32^\circ$
@answer $x = 58^\circ$
:::

:::tip Look for radii
Two radii make an isosceles triangle with the centre. If $\angle AOB = 100^\circ$, then $\angle OAB = \angle OBA = (180^\circ - 100^\circ) \div 2 = 40^\circ$.
:::

:::summary
- A central angle has its vertex at the centre; an inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle.
- The central angle is twice the inscribed angle subtended by the same arc.
- Inscribed angles subtended by the same arc are equal.
- An angle inscribed in a semicircle is $90^\circ$.
- Radii are equal, so a triangle with two radii as sides is isosceles.
:::
