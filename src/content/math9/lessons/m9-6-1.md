Two phone plans charge different monthly fees and different prices per gigabyte. For how much data do they cost the same? Questions like this lead to equations with the variable on **both** sides. In this lesson you will solve them, even when the numbers are decimals or fractions.

## Keeping the balance

An equation is like a balanced scale: the left side and the right side have the same value. If you do the same thing to both sides, the scale stays balanced. Your goal is to get the $x$-terms on one side and the numbers on the other.

:::definition Solution of an equation
The **solution** of an equation is the value of the variable that makes the left side equal to the right side.
:::

:::example Variables on both sides
Solve $5x = 12 + 2x$.
@step Subtract $2x$ from both sides to collect the $x$-terms on the left: $3x = 12$
@step Divide both sides by $3$: $x = 4$
@step Check: the left side is $5(4) = 20$ and the right side is $12 + 2(4) = 20$. Both sides are equal.
@answer $x = 4$
:::

## Solving ax + b = cx + d

When both sides have an $x$-term and a constant, first move the $x$-terms to one side, then move the constants to the other side, and finally divide.

:::example Negative coefficients
Solve $4x - 9 = -2x + 6$.
@step Add $2x$ to both sides: $6x - 9 = 6$
@step Add $9$ to both sides: $6x = 15$
@step Divide both sides by $6$: $x = \dfrac{15}{6} = \dfrac{5}{2}$, which is $2.5$.
@step Check: left side $4(2.5) - 9 = 1$; right side $-2(2.5) + 6 = 1$. Both sides are equal.
@answer $x = \dfrac{5}{2}$, or $2.5$
:::

A solution does not have to be an integer. Write it as a fraction in lowest terms or as a decimal.

:::mistake Moving a term without changing the operation
To remove $-2x$ from the right side, you must **add** $2x$ to both sides. Some students subtract $2x$ instead and get $2x - 9 = 6$, which leads to the wrong answer. Ask yourself: what operation makes this term disappear from its side? Then do it to both sides.
:::

:::tip Choose the side
You can collect the $x$-terms on either side. Collecting them on the side with the larger coefficient keeps the coefficient positive. For $4x - 9 = -2x + 6$, the left side has the larger coefficient ($4 > -2$).
:::

:::

## Decimals and fractions as coefficients

The steps are the same when the coefficients are decimals.

:::example Decimal coefficients
Solve $1.5x + 2.4 = 0.3x - 1.2$.
@step Subtract $0.3x$ from both sides: $1.2x + 2.4 = -1.2$
@step Subtract $2.4$ from both sides: $1.2x = -3.6$
@step Divide both sides by $1.2$: $x = -3$
@step Check: left side $1.5(-3) + 2.4 = -4.5 + 2.4 = -2.1$; right side $0.3(-3) - 1.2 = -0.9 - 1.2 = -2.1$.
@answer $x = -3$
:::

With fractions, it is often easiest to clear the fractions first. Multiply **every** term on both sides by the lowest common denominator (LCD).

:::example Fraction coefficients
Solve $\dfrac{2}{3}x - 4 = \dfrac{1}{6}x + 1$.
@step The LCD of $3$ and $6$ is $6$. Multiply every term by $6$: $4x - 24 = x + 6$
@step Subtract $x$ from both sides: $3x - 24 = 6$
@step Add $24$ to both sides: $3x = 30$, so $x = 10$.
@step Check: left side $\dfrac{2}{3}(10) - 4 = \dfrac{20}{3} - \dfrac{12}{3} = \dfrac{8}{3}$; right side $\dfrac{1}{6}(10) + 1 = \dfrac{5}{3} + \dfrac{3}{3} = \dfrac{8}{3}$.
@answer $x = 10$
:::

:::mistake Forgetting to multiply every term
When you multiply by the LCD, multiply the constants too. In the example, $-4$ becomes $-24$ and $1$ becomes $6$. Writing $4x - 4 = x + 1$ unbalances the equation.
:::

:::check m9-equation-both-sides 3
:::

:::summary
- Do the same operation to both sides to keep an equation balanced.
- Collect the $x$-terms on one side and the constants on the other, then divide by the coefficient of $x$.
- To remove a term from a side, use the opposite operation on both sides.
- With fraction coefficients, multiply every term by the LCD first. With decimals, work with the decimals or multiply every term by $10$.
- Always check by substituting the solution into both sides of the original equation.
:::
