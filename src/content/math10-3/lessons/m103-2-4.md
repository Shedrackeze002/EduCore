Your first pay cheque can be a surprise. You worked 40 hours at \$18 an hour, but the deposit is not \$720. The difference is **deductions**: money your employer must hold back and send to the government or your union.

## Payroll deductions

Every pay stub lists your gross pay and the amounts taken off it.

:::definition Deductions
A **deduction** is an amount subtracted from gross pay. The most common ones are:
- **CPP** (Canada Pension Plan): savings for your retirement pension.
- **EI** (Employment Insurance): pays you for a while if you lose your job.
- **Income tax**: pays for government services such as roads, schools and health care.
- **Union dues**: pay for the union, if you belong to one.
:::

:::note Simplified rates
Real CPP, EI and income tax amounts change every year and are found with tables or calculators from the Canada Revenue Agency. In this course, each question gives you simplified rates to use.
:::

:::example Two deductions
Priya’s gross pay for two weeks is \$1,240.00. Use these simplified rates: CPP is 5.95% of gross pay and EI is 1.66% of gross pay. Find each deduction to the nearest cent.
@step CPP: $0.0595 \times 1{,}240 = 73.78$
@step EI: $0.0166 \times 1{,}240 = 20.584$, which rounds to $20.58$
@answer CPP is \$73.78 and EI is \$20.58.
:::

## Deductions above an exemption

Some deductions only apply to the part of your pay **above** a set amount. For example, a simplified rule might say CPP is 5.95% of gross pay above \$135, and income tax is 15% of gross pay above \$600.

:::example Using an exemption
Priya’s gross pay is \$1,240.00. Find CPP (5.95% of gross pay above \$135) and income tax (15% of gross pay above \$600).
@step CPP: $1{,}240 - 135 = 1{,}105$, and $0.0595 \times 1{,}105 = 65.7475$, which rounds to $65.75$
@step Income tax: $1{,}240 - 600 = 640$, and $0.15 \times 640 = 96.00$
@answer CPP is \$65.75 and income tax is \$96.00.
:::

:::mistake Subtract the exemption first
The rate applies only to the pay **above** the exemption. Multiplying the rate by the whole gross pay takes off too much.
:::

:::

## Net pay

The money that actually lands in your bank account is your gross pay minus every deduction on the stub.

:::definition Net pay
**Net pay** (take-home pay) is what is left after all deductions:
$$\text{net pay} = \text{gross pay} - \text{total deductions}$$
:::

:::example Reading a pay stub
Priya’s pay stub shows gross pay \$1,240.00, CPP \$65.75, EI \$20.58, income tax \$96.00 and union dues \$32.50. Find the net pay.
@step Total deductions: $65.75 + 20.58 + 96.00 + 32.50 = 214.83$
@step Net pay: $1{,}240.00 - 214.83 = 1{,}025.17$
@answer Priya’s net pay is \$1,025.17.
:::

:::tip Check with an estimate
Net pay is usually about 70% to 85% of gross pay. Here, $1{,}025.17 \div 1{,}240 \approx 0.83$, so the answer is reasonable.
:::

## From timecard to take-home pay

Putting it all together: find gross pay, then each deduction, then net pay.

:::example A week with overtime
Wyatt earns \$21.40 per hour, with time-and-a-half for hours over 40 in a week. Wyatt worked 44 hours. Use these simplified rates: CPP 5.95%, EI 1.66% and income tax 15%, all of gross pay. Find the net pay.
@step Gross pay: $40 \times 21.40 + 4 \times (1.5 \times 21.40) = 856.00 + 128.40 = 984.40$
@step CPP: $0.0595 \times 984.40 \approx 58.57$. EI: $0.0166 \times 984.40 \approx 16.34$. Tax: $0.15 \times 984.40 = 147.66$
@step Total deductions: $58.57 + 16.34 + 147.66 = 222.57$
@step Net pay: $984.40 - 222.57 = 761.83$
@answer Wyatt’s net pay is \$761.83.
:::

## Working backwards with formulas

Income formulas can be rearranged to answer “how many hours?” or “how much in sales?” questions. Undo each operation by doing the opposite to both sides.

| Formula | Meaning | Rearranged |
|---|---|---|
| $G = rh$ | gross pay = rate × hours | $h = \dfrac{G}{r}$ or $r = \dfrac{G}{h}$ |
| $C = rS$ | commission = rate × sales | $S = \dfrac{C}{r}$ |
| $E = B + rS$ | earnings = base + commission | $S = \dfrac{E - B}{r}$ |

:::example How many hours?
Kiona earns \$22.50 per hour and wants to earn \$900. How many hours must Kiona work?
@step $h = \dfrac{G}{r} = \dfrac{900}{22.50}$
@answer $h = 40$, so Kiona must work 40 hours.
:::

:::example Hours with overtime
Lucas earns \$20 per hour with time-and-a-half for hours over 40. Last week Lucas earned \$1,010. How many hours did Lucas work?
@step Regular pay: $40 \times 20 = 800$, so overtime pay is $1{,}010 - 800 = 210$
@step Overtime rate: $1.5 \times 20 = 30$, so overtime hours $= 210 \div 30 = 7$
@answer Lucas worked $40 + 7 = 47$ hours.
:::

:::mistake Dividing the wrong way
From $G = rh$, the hours are $\dfrac{G}{r}$, not $\dfrac{r}{G}$. Check: 40 hours makes sense; $22.50 \div 900 = 0.025$ hours does not.
:::

:::summary
- Deductions such as CPP, EI, income tax and union dues come off gross pay.
- Change each percent to a decimal, multiply, and round each deduction to the nearest cent.
- When there is an exemption, subtract it before multiplying by the rate.
- Net pay $=$ gross pay $-$ total deductions.
- Rearrange income formulas by undoing operations: $h = \dfrac{G}{r}$, $S = \dfrac{C}{r}$, $S = \dfrac{E - B}{r}$.
:::
