If you buy supplies for a restaurant kitchen, a work crew or your own home, the same product often comes in several sizes. The price on the shelf does not tell you which size is the best deal. The **unit price** does.

## Unit price

A unit price is a unit rate for money: the cost of one unit of the product. Stores often print it in small type on the shelf tag.

:::definition Unit price
The **unit price** is the cost for one unit, such as one item, one kilogram, one litre, or a group like $100\ \text{g}$.
$$\text{unit price} = \text{price} \div \text{number of units}$$
:::

:::example Price per item
A pack of 8 spark plugs costs \$38. What is the price per spark plug?
@step Unit price $=$ price $\div$ number of units
@step $38 \div 8 = 4.75$
@answer Each spark plug costs \$4.75.
:::

Small items like cereal or coffee are usually priced **per 100 g** or **per 100 mL**. Count how many groups of 100 are in the package, then divide.

:::example Price per 100 g
A $750\ \text{g}$ box of cereal costs \$5.49. Find the unit price per $100\ \text{g}$.
@step Groups of 100 g: $750 \div 100 = 7.5$
@step $5.49 \div 7.5 = 0.732$
@answer The unit price is about \$0.73 per $100\ \text{g}$.
:::

## When the units do not match

Sometimes the package and the unit price use different units. Convert first, using $1\ \text{kg} = 1000\ \text{g}$ and $1\ \text{L} = 1000\ \text{mL}$.

:::example Kilograms to groups of 100 g
A $2.5\ \text{kg}$ bag of rice costs \$8.99. Find the unit price per $100\ \text{g}$.
@step Convert: $2.5\ \text{kg} = 2500\ \text{g}$, which is $2500 \div 100 = 25$ groups of $100\ \text{g}$.
@step $8.99 \div 25 = 0.3596$
@answer The unit price is about \$0.36 per $100\ \text{g}$.
:::

:::example Grams to kilograms
A $680\ \text{g}$ package of ground beef costs \$8.79. What is the price per kilogram?
@step Convert: $680\ \text{g} = 680 \div 1000 = 0.68\ \text{kg}$
@step $8.79 \div 0.68 \approx 12.93$
@answer The beef costs about \$12.93 per kilogram.
:::

:::mistake Dividing the wrong way
$680 \div 8.79$ gives about 77 grams **per dollar**, not dollars per gram. For a unit price, the money goes first: price $\div$ amount.
:::

## Finding the best buy

To compare sizes fairly, find every unit price using the **same** unit. The lowest unit price is the **best buy**.

:::definition Best buy
The **best buy** is the choice with the lowest unit price, as long as you need that amount and will use it.
:::

:::figure

:::

:::example Three sizes of yogurt
A grocery store sells yogurt in the three sizes shown in the table. Which is the best buy?
@step $175\ \text{g}$: $1.29 \div 1.75 \approx 0.737$, about \$0.737 per $100\ \text{g}$
@step $650\ \text{g}$: $3.99 \div 6.5 \approx 0.614$, about \$0.614 per $100\ \text{g}$
@step $1500\ \text{g}$: $9.79 \div 15 \approx 0.653$, about \$0.653 per $100\ \text{g}$
@answer The $650\ \text{g}$ tub is the best buy. The biggest size is **not** always the cheapest per unit.
:::

:::tip A quick check without a calculator
If one size is exactly double another, compare the big price with **two** of the small ones. Two 4-packs of batteries at \$4.99 cost $2 \times 4.99 = 9.98$ dollars, so an 8-pack for \$8.49 is the better deal.
:::

## Deals, coupons and common sense

Sales like "buy 2, get 1 free" or a coupon change the real price. Work out what you actually pay and what you actually get, then find the unit price.

:::example Buy 2, get 1 free
A $1\ \text{L}$ jug of windshield washer fluid costs \$2.49 with a "buy 2, get 1 free" deal. A $4\ \text{L}$ jug costs \$6.99. Which is the better buy?
@step Deal: pay for 2 and get 3, so \$4.98 for $3\ \text{L}$. Unit price: $4.98 \div 3 = 1.66$, or \$1.66 per litre.
@step Big jug: $6.99 \div 4 \approx 1.748$, about \$1.75 per litre.
@answer The deal on the $1\ \text{L}$ jugs is the better buy.
:::

:::note The lowest price is not always the best choice
A big package is only a good deal if you use it all. Think about:
- **Need:** will you use 10 kg of flour before it goes stale?
- **Waste:** fresh food can spoil before you finish it.
- **Quality and storage:** a cheaper brand may not work as well, and you need room to store a bulk pack.
:::

:::mistake Bigger is always cheaper
Stores know shoppers assume this. Medium sizes and sale items are sometimes cheaper per unit, so always check.
:::

:::check m103-best-buy 3
:::

:::summary
- Unit price $=$ price $\div$ number of units. Put the money first.
- For small items, divide by the number of groups of $100\ \text{g}$ or $100\ \text{mL}$.
- Convert units first when the size and the unit price do not match.
- The best buy has the lowest unit price, but also think about need, waste and quality.
- For deals and coupons, find what you really pay and what you really get.
:::
