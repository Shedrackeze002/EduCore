Flooring is priced per square foot, carpet per square yard and sod per square metre, and none of it is sold in partial boxes or rolls. To order materials and quote a job you need to switch between square units, work out how much to buy, and find the cost.

## Square units are squared

A square that is $1\ \text{ft}$ on each side is $12\ \text{in}$ on each side. It holds $12 \times 12 = 144$ one-inch squares, so $1\ \text{ft}^2 = 144\ \text{in}^2$. **For area, square the length conversion.**

| Length fact | Area fact |
|---|---|
| $1\ \text{ft} = 12\ \text{in}$ | $1\ \text{ft}^2 = 12^2 = 144\ \text{in}^2$ |
| $1\ \text{yd} = 3\ \text{ft}$ | $1\ \text{yd}^2 = 3^2 = 9\ \text{ft}^2$ |
| $1\ \text{m} = 100\ \text{cm}$ | $1\ \text{m}^2 = 100^2 = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$ |
| $1\ \text{cm} = 10\ \text{mm}$ | $1\ \text{cm}^2 = 10^2 = 100\ \text{mm}^2$ |

:::definition Square foot and square yard
A **square foot** ($\text{ft}^2$) is the area of a square $1\ \text{ft}$ on each side. A **square yard** ($\text{yd}^2$) is the area of a square $1\ \text{yd}$ (3 ft) on each side, so it holds 9 square feet.
:::

:::example Square feet to square inches
A piece of plywood has an area of $6\ \text{ft}^2$. What is its area in square inches?
@step $1\ \text{ft}^2 = 144\ \text{in}^2$
@step Converting to a smaller unit gives a bigger number, so multiply: $6 \times 144 = 864$
@answer $6\ \text{ft}^2 = 864\ \text{in}^2$
:::

:::example Square centimetres to square metres
A sheet of glass has an area of $4500\ \text{cm}^2$. What is its area in square metres?
@step $1\ \text{m}^2 = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Converting to a larger unit gives a smaller number, so divide: $4500 \div 10\,000 = 0.45$
@answer $4500\ \text{cm}^2 = 0.45\ \text{m}^2$
:::

:::mistake Using the length factor for area
$1\ \text{yd}^2$ is **not** $3\ \text{ft}^2$, and $1\ \text{m}^2$ is **not** $100\ \text{cm}^2$. Picture the square: it is 3 ft by 3 ft, so it holds $9\ \text{ft}^2$. Using the length factor gives an answer that is far too small or far too big.
:::

## Between SI and imperial

Canadian homes are often listed in square feet, while building plans may use square metres. A metre is a little longer than a yard, so a square metre is a bit more than 10 square feet. In this course we use

$$1\ \text{m}^2 \approx 10.76\ \text{ft}^2.$$

:::example A condo listing
A condo has a floor area of $75\ \text{m}^2$. What is its area in square feet?
@step Square feet are smaller, so the number should get bigger: multiply.
@step $75 \times 10.76 = 807$
@answer The condo is about $807\ \text{ft}^2$.
:::

:::example Carpet by the square yard
A living room is $12\ \text{ft}$ by $15\ \text{ft}$. Carpet is sold by the square yard. How many square yards is the room?
@step Area: $12 \times 15 = 180\ \text{ft}^2$
@step $1\ \text{yd}^2 = 9\ \text{ft}^2$, so divide by 9: $180 \div 9 = 20$
@answer The room is $20\ \text{yd}^2$. (Check: $12\ \text{ft} = 4\ \text{yd}$ and $15\ \text{ft} = 5\ \text{yd}$, and $4 \times 5 = 20$.)
:::

## How much to buy

:::definition Coverage
The **coverage** of a product is the area that one box, can, bundle or roll will cover. It is printed on the package, for example "covers $22.5\ \text{ft}^2$".
:::

Divide the area by the coverage to find how many you need. Stores do not sell part of a box, so **always round up**.

:::example Laminate for a bedroom
A bedroom is $14\ \text{ft}$ by $12\ \text{ft}$. Each box of laminate covers $22.5\ \text{ft}^2$ and costs \$54.99. How many boxes are needed, and what do they cost before tax?
@step Area: $14 \times 12 = 168\ \text{ft}^2$
@step Boxes: $168 \div 22.5 \approx 7.47$, so round up to 8 boxes.
@step Cost: $8 \times 54.99 = 439.92$
@answer Buy 8 boxes for \$439.92 before tax.
:::

:::mistake Rounding down
Rounding $7.47$ to 7 boxes leaves part of the floor bare. Even $7.1$ boxes means you must buy 8. Round up whenever you buy whole items.
:::

When a price is given **per square unit**, multiply the area by the price. Sod at \$4.29 per square metre for a $12\ \text{m}$ by $9\ \text{m}$ yard costs $108 \times 4.29 = 463.32$, or \$463.32.

## Waste allowance and GST

Installers cut pieces to fit walls and corners, and the offcuts are wasted. So they order extra, often 10% to 15% more than the area. After that, GST is added to the price. In this course, use a GST rate of 5%.

:::example Laminate with waste and tax
For the same $168\ \text{ft}^2$ bedroom, the installer adds 10% for waste. Boxes cover $22.5\ \text{ft}^2$ and cost \$54.99. GST is 5%. Find the number of boxes and the total cost.
@step Add 10%: $168 \times 1.10 = 184.8\ \text{ft}^2$
@step Boxes: $184.8 \div 22.5 \approx 8.21$, so round up to 9 boxes.
@step Before tax: $9 \times 54.99 = 494.91$
@step Add 5% GST: $494.91 \times 1.05 \approx 519.66$
@answer Buy 9 boxes. The total cost is \$519.66.
:::

:::tip Multipliers save steps
Adding 10% is the same as multiplying by $1.10$, and adding 5% GST is the same as multiplying by $1.05$. Round money to the nearest cent only at the very end.
:::

:::

:::summary
- For area units, square the length factor: $1\ \text{ft}^2 = 144\ \text{in}^2$, $1\ \text{yd}^2 = 9\ \text{ft}^2$, $1\ \text{m}^2 = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.
- Bigger unit means a smaller number (divide); smaller unit means a bigger number (multiply).
- Between systems, use the factor you are given, such as $1\ \text{m}^2 \approx 10.76\ \text{ft}^2$.
- Number to buy = area ÷ coverage, always rounded up.
- Add waste first, round up the boxes, find the cost, then add GST.
:::
