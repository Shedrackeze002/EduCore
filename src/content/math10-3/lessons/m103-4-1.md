A cabinet door cut $3\ \text{mm}$ too wide will not close. A copper pipe cut $\tfrac14$ in too short will leak at the fitting. On a job site, reading a ruler or tape measure quickly and correctly saves time, money and material.

## Precision and the tape measure

Every measuring tool has a smallest mark. That smallest mark sets how exact your reading can be.

:::definition Precision
The **precision** of a measurement is the size of the smallest unit you can read on the tool. A ruler marked in millimetres is more precise than one marked only in centimetres.
:::

:::definition Tape measure
A **tape measure** is a long, flexible ruler that rolls up into a case. Most tapes sold in Canada show centimetres and millimetres on one edge and inches and feet on the other.
:::

The metal hook on the end of a tape slides a little. It moves in to account for its own thickness when you push against a wall, and out when you hook it over the end of a board. That is not a fault, so do not bend it.

:::tip "Burn an inch"
If the end of a tape is worn, start your measurement at the $1$ in mark (or the $10$ cm mark) instead of the end. Then subtract that amount from your reading. Carpenters call this "burning an inch."
:::

## Reading a metric ruler

On a metric ruler, the numbered marks are centimetres. There are $10$ small spaces between numbers, so each small mark is $1\ \text{mm} = 0.1\ \text{cm}$. The half-way mark (5 mm) is a little longer, to help you count.

:::figure

:::

The bar above starts at $0$. Its right end is $3$ small marks past the $6$, so it is $6.3\ \text{cm}$ long. In millimetres that is $6.3 \times 10 = 63\ \text{mm}$.

:::check m103-ruler-metric 1
:::

When a part does not start at $0$, read **both** ends and subtract.

:::example A part that starts at 2.4 cm
A bolt lies along a ruler from the $2.4\ \text{cm}$ mark to the $9.1\ \text{cm}$ mark. How long is it in millimetres?
@step Length = end − start
@step $9.1 - 2.4 = 6.7\ \text{cm}$
@step Change to millimetres: $6.7 \times 10 = 67\ \text{mm}$
@answer The bolt is $6.7\ \text{cm}$, or $67\ \text{mm}$, long.
:::

:::mistake Reading only the end mark
If a part starts at $2.4\ \text{cm}$ and ends at $9.1\ \text{cm}$, its length is **not** $9.1\ \text{cm}$. Always subtract the starting mark.
:::

:::

## Reading an imperial ruler

An imperial ruler or tape splits each inch into $16$ equal spaces. The marks get shorter as the pieces get smaller:

- the longest marks are whole inches,
- the next longest is the $\tfrac12$ in mark,
- then the $\tfrac14$ in marks, then the $\tfrac18$ in marks,
- and the shortest are the $\tfrac{1}{16}$ in marks.

:::figure
{"type":"ruler","system":"imperial","length":5,"start":0,"end":3.4375,"division":16}
:::

To read a length, count the whole inches. Then count the sixteenths past the last whole inch. Write the fraction in lowest terms.

:::example Reading to the nearest sixteenth
The bar above ends $7$ small spaces past the $3$ in mark. How long is it?
@step Whole inches: $3$
@step Extra: $\tfrac{7}{16}$ in. Seven is odd, so this fraction cannot be simplified.
@answer The bar is $3\tfrac{7}{16}$ in long.
:::

:::tip Simplify the fraction
If a bar ends $12$ sixteenths past the $2$ in mark, it is $2\tfrac{12}{16} = 2\tfrac34$ in. On the job, people say "two and three-quarter inches," not "two and twelve-sixteenths."
:::

## Measuring from a mark other than zero

Sometimes a part sits in the middle of a ruler, or you burned an inch. The rule is the same as in metric: **length = end − start**. With fractions, it is easiest to change both readings to sixteenths first.

:::figure
{"type":"ruler","system":"imperial","length":5,"start":1.375,"end":4.0625,"division":16}
:::

:::example A part that starts at 1 3/8 in
The part above starts at $1\tfrac38$ in and ends at $4\tfrac{1}{16}$ in. How long is it?
@step Change both readings to sixteenths: $1\tfrac38 = \tfrac{22}{16}$ and $4\tfrac{1}{16} = \tfrac{65}{16}$.
@step Subtract: $\tfrac{65}{16} - \tfrac{22}{16} = \tfrac{43}{16}$
@step Change back to a mixed number: $43 \div 16 = 2$ remainder $11$.
@answer The part is $2\tfrac{11}{16}$ in long.
:::

:::mistake Counting marks instead of spaces
Count the **spaces** between marks, not the marks themselves. From the $1$ in mark to the $1\tfrac{3}{16}$ in mark there are $3$ spaces but $4$ marks (including the one you start on).
:::

:::

:::check m103-ruler-imperial 3
:::

:::summary
- Precision is set by the smallest mark on the tool.
- Metric: each small mark is $1\ \text{mm} = 0.1\ \text{cm}$; multiply centimetres by $10$ to get millimetres.
- Imperial: each inch has $16$ spaces. Count whole inches, then sixteenths, then simplify.
- If the part does not start at $0$, length = end − start. Change fractions to sixteenths before subtracting.
:::
