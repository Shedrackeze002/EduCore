Railway tracks, the studs in a wall and the lines in a parking lot are all parallel. When a road, a brace or a stair stringer crosses them, the angles it makes follow a pattern. Once you know one angle, you can find all the others.

## Parallel lines and transversals

:::definition Parallel, perpendicular and transversal
- **Parallel** lines are always the same distance apart and never meet. In diagrams they are marked with matching arrowheads.
- **Perpendicular** lines meet at a right angle ($90^\circ$).
- A **transversal** is a line that crosses two or more other lines.
:::

In the diagram, line $t$ is a transversal that crosses the parallel lines $m$ and $n$. It makes eight angles, four at each crossing point. Angles **between** the parallel lines (3, 4, 5 and 6) are **interior** angles. Angles **outside** them (1, 2, 7 and 8) are **exterior** angles.

## Naming angle pairs

:::definition Angle pairs
- **Corresponding angles** are in the same position at each crossing, such as $\angle 2$ and $\angle 6$.
- **Alternate interior angles** are between the lines, on opposite sides of the transversal, such as $\angle 3$ and $\angle 6$.
- **Alternate exterior angles** are outside the lines, on opposite sides of the transversal, such as $\angle 1$ and $\angle 8$.
- **Co-interior angles** (same-side interior angles) are between the lines, on the same side of the transversal, such as $\angle 4$ and $\angle 6$.
:::

For the diagram above:

| Pair | Examples | When $m \parallel n$ |
|---|---|---|
| Corresponding | 1 and 5, 2 and 6, 3 and 7, 4 and 8 | equal |
| Alternate interior | 3 and 6, 4 and 5 | equal |
| Alternate exterior | 1 and 8, 2 and 7 | equal |
| Co-interior | 3 and 5, 4 and 6 | add to $180^\circ$ |
| Vertically opposite | 1 and 4, 2 and 3, 5 and 8, 6 and 7 | always equal |

:::tip Look for letter shapes
Corresponding angles sit inside an **F** shape. Alternate interior angles sit inside a **Z** shape. Co-interior angles sit inside a **C** (or U) shape. The F, Z or C can be backwards or upside down.
:::

## Finding angles when lines are parallel

When the lines are parallel, the eight angles come in only two sizes. All the acute angles are equal, all the obtuse angles are equal, and an acute angle plus an obtuse angle makes $180^\circ$.

:::example Using the angle pairs
In the diagram above, $m \parallel n$ and $\angle 2 = 65^\circ$. Find $\angle 3$, $\angle 6$ and $\angle 5$.
@step $\angle 3$ is vertically opposite $\angle 2$, so $\angle 3 = 65^\circ$.
@step $\angle 6$ corresponds to $\angle 2$, so $\angle 6 = 65^\circ$.
@step $\angle 5$ and $\angle 3$ are co-interior, so $\angle 5 = 180^\circ - 65^\circ = 115^\circ$.
@answer $\angle 3 = 65^\circ$, $\angle 6 = 65^\circ$ and $\angle 5 = 115^\circ$.
:::

:::mistake Co-interior angles are not equal
Corresponding and alternate angles are equal, but co-interior angles **add to** $180^\circ$. If one co-interior angle is acute, the other must be obtuse.
:::

:::example Angles with algebra
A straight road crosses two parallel railway tracks. A pair of alternate interior angles measure $(3x + 10)^\circ$ and $(5x - 30)^\circ$. Find $x$ and the angle.
@step Alternate interior angles are equal: $3x + 10 = 5x - 30$.
@step Subtract $3x$ from both sides: $10 = 2x - 30$.
@step Add $30$ to both sides: $40 = 2x$, so $x = 20$.
@step $3(20) + 10 = 70$ and $5(20) - 30 = 70$.
@answer $x = 20$ and each angle measures $70^\circ$.
:::

## Are the lines parallel?

The rules also work backwards. If a transversal crosses two lines and **any one** of these is true, the lines are parallel:

- a pair of corresponding angles are equal;
- a pair of alternate angles (interior or exterior) are equal;
- a pair of co-interior angles add to $180^\circ$.

If the test fails, the lines are not parallel, even if they look parallel.

:::example Checking studs
A framer checks two studs nailed to a bottom plate. Measured on the left side of each stud, the angles with the plate are $89^\circ$ and $91^\circ$. Are the studs parallel?
@step The plate is the transversal. Both angles are on the left of a stud and above the plate, so they are corresponding angles.
@step Corresponding angles must be equal for the studs to be parallel. $89^\circ \ne 91^\circ$.
@answer No, the studs are not parallel. One of them needs to be adjusted.
:::

:::example Checking shelves
A sloped support runs between two shelves. Between the shelves, on the same side of the support, it makes angles of $72^\circ$ and $108^\circ$. Are the shelves parallel?
@step The angles are co-interior angles.
@step $72^\circ + 108^\circ = 180^\circ$
@answer Yes, the shelves are parallel.
:::

:::mistake Close enough is not parallel
Angles of $89^\circ$ and $91^\circ$ add to $180^\circ$, but they are corresponding angles, so they needed to be **equal**. Always use the rule that matches the kind of angle pair.
:::

:::summary
- A transversal crossing two lines makes eight angles: four interior and four exterior.
- When the lines are parallel, corresponding, alternate interior and alternate exterior angles are equal.
- When the lines are parallel, co-interior angles add to $180^\circ$.
- Vertically opposite angles are always equal, and angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$.
- To test for parallel lines, check one angle pair with the matching rule. Do not trust your eyes.
:::
