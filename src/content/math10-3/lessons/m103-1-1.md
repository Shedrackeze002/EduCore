A trucker plans how much diesel a run will take. A camp cook turns a recipe for 6 into a recipe for 60. A painter decides how many cans to buy. Each of them compares two quantities, and the tools they use are **rates** and **proportions**.

## Ratios and rates

A **ratio** compares two quantities of the same kind. A concrete mix might use 2 shovels of cement for every 3 shovels of sand. We write this as $2:3$ or $\dfrac{2}{3}$.

A **rate** compares two quantities measured in **different** units, like kilometres and hours, or dollars and kilograms.

:::definition Ratio and rate
A **ratio** compares quantities with the same units, such as $2:3$ (cement to sand).
A **rate** compares quantities with different units, such as $450\ \text{km}$ in $5\ \text{h}$, or \$12.50 for $5\ \text{kg}$.
:::

Rates are everywhere on the job: a pump moves litres per minute, a bricklayer lays bricks per hour, a truck burns litres per 100 km.

## Unit rates

A rate is easiest to use when it is written for **one** unit. Then you can compare it, or multiply it to answer "how much" questions.

:::definition Unit rate
A **unit rate** is a rate where the second quantity is $1$, such as $84\ \text{km/h}$ (kilometres in one hour) or \$2.50/kg (dollars for one kilogram). To find a unit rate, divide by the number of units.
:::

:::example Average speed
Rosa drives a gravel truck $378\ \text{km}$ in $4$ h $30$ min. What is the average speed?
@step Write the time in hours: $30\ \text{min} = \dfrac{30}{60} = 0.5\ \text{h}$, so the time is $4.5\ \text{h}$.
@step Divide the distance by the time: $378 \div 4.5 = 84$
@answer The average speed is $84\ \text{km/h}$.
:::

:::mistake Minutes are not decimals
$4$ h $30$ min is **not** $4.30$ h. There are 60 minutes in an hour, so 30 minutes is half an hour: $4.5$ h. Likewise, 15 min $= 0.25$ h and 45 min $= 0.75$ h.
:::

Fuel use is usually written as a rate per **100 km**, not per kilometre, because the numbers are easier to read.

:::example Litres per 100 km
A delivery van used $46.8\ \text{L}$ of diesel to travel $520\ \text{km}$. Find the fuel consumption in L/100 km.
@step Set up equal rates: $\dfrac{46.8\ \text{L}}{520\ \text{km}} = \dfrac{x}{100\ \text{km}}$
@step Multiply both sides by 100: $x = \dfrac{46.8 \times 100}{520} = 9$
@answer The van uses $9\ \text{L}$ per $100\ \text{km}$, written $9\ \text{L/100 km}$.
:::

## Comparing rates

Unit rates let you compare things fairly, even when the amounts are different.

:::example Who types faster?
Two people apply for a dispatcher job. Amara types 312 words in 6 minutes. Jordan types 275 words in 5 minutes. Who types faster?
@step Amara: $312 \div 6 = 52$ words per minute.
@step Jordan: $275 \div 5 = 55$ words per minute.
@answer Jordan types faster, by $55 - 52 = 3$ words per minute, even though Amara typed more words in total.
:::

:::tip Is bigger better?
For speed or pay, a bigger unit rate is better. For fuel consumption (L/100 km) or price per kilogram, a **smaller** unit rate is better. Always ask what the number means.
:::

## Proportions

A **proportion** says two ratios or rates are equal. If you know three of the four numbers, you can find the fourth.

:::definition Proportion
A **proportion** is an equation with two equal ratios, such as $\dfrac{450\ \text{g}}{24\ \text{muffins}} = \dfrac{x}{60\ \text{muffins}}$. Keep the same units in the same positions (top with top, bottom with bottom).
:::

:::example Scaling a recipe
A bakery recipe uses $450\ \text{g}$ of flour for 24 muffins. How much flour is needed for 60 muffins?
@step Set up the proportion: $\dfrac{450\ \text{g}}{24} = \dfrac{x}{60}$
@step Multiply both sides by 60: $x = \dfrac{450 \times 60}{24} = 1125$
@step Check by estimating: 60 is $2.5$ times 24, and $450 \times 2.5 = 1125$.
@answer $1125\ \text{g}$ of flour is needed.
:::

:::example Fuel for a trip
A car uses $8.5\ \text{L}$ of gas per $100\ \text{km}$. How much gas does a $340\ \text{km}$ trip use?
@step $\dfrac{8.5\ \text{L}}{100\ \text{km}} = \dfrac{x}{340\ \text{km}}$
@step $x = \dfrac{8.5 \times 340}{100} = 28.9$
@answer The trip uses $28.9\ \text{L}$ of gas.
:::

:::mistake Flipping one side
$\dfrac{450}{24} = \dfrac{60}{x}$ is set up wrong: grams are on top on the left but muffins are on top on the right. Write the units beside each number. If they do not match across the equals sign, fix the set-up before you calculate.
:::

## Problems with more than one step

On the job, a proportion is often one step in a bigger problem. Read carefully and decide what to find first.

:::example How many cans?
Isabelle is painting walls with a total area of $96\ \text{m}^2$. They need two coats, and one can of paint covers about $37\ \text{m}^2$. How many cans must she buy?
@step Two coats: $2 \times 96 = 192\ \text{m}^2$.
@step Cans: $192 \div 37 \approx 5.19$
@step A store will not sell part of a can, so round **up**.
@answer Isabelle must buy 6 cans.
:::

:::example The cost of a road trip
A pickup uses $11.2\ \text{L}$ per $100\ \text{km}$. Suppose gas costs \$1.52 per litre. What does gas cost for a $420\ \text{km}$ trip?
@step Litres needed: $x = \dfrac{11.2 \times 420}{100} = 47.04\ \text{L}$
@step Cost: $47.04 \times 1.52 = 71.5008$
@answer The gas costs about \$71.50.
:::

:::summary
- A ratio compares quantities with the same units; a rate compares quantities with different units.
- A unit rate is a rate for one unit. Divide to find it.
- Change minutes to a decimal part of an hour before you divide.
- Compare unit rates to decide which is faster, cheaper or more efficient.
- A proportion sets two rates equal. Keep matching units in matching positions, then solve.
- Estimate to check, and round up when you cannot buy part of something.
:::
