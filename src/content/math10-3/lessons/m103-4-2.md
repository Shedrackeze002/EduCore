Lumber, plumbing fittings, bolts and most tape measures in Canadian trades still use inches and feet. A carpenter who can add $3\tfrac58$ in and $2\tfrac{3}{16}$ in without a calculator works faster and wastes less wood.

## Fractions of an inch

Measurements in inches are usually written as **mixed numbers**.

:::definition Mixed number
A **mixed number** has a whole-number part and a fraction part, such as $5\tfrac{13}{16}$. It means $5 + \tfrac{13}{16}$.
:::

:::definition Common denominator
Two fractions have a **common denominator** when their bottom numbers are the same. You need one before you can add or subtract fractions.
:::

Ruler fractions have denominators of $2$, $4$, $8$ or $16$. Each of these divides evenly into the next, so the common denominator is simply the **largest** denominator in the question.

| Halves | Quarters | Eighths | Sixteenths |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\tfrac12$ | $\tfrac24$ | $\tfrac48$ | $\tfrac{8}{16}$ |
| | $\tfrac14$ | $\tfrac28$ | $\tfrac{4}{16}$ |
| | $\tfrac34$ | $\tfrac68$ | $\tfrac{12}{16}$ |

## Adding fractional measurements

1. Give the fractions a common denominator.
2. Add the whole numbers, then add the fractions.
3. If the fraction is $1$ or more, regroup it as $1$ whole inch. Simplify.

:::example Two pieces of trim
A carpenter joins a $3\tfrac58$ in piece of trim to a $2\tfrac{3}{16}$ in piece. What is the total length?
@step Common denominator $16$: $3\tfrac58 = 3\tfrac{10}{16}$
@step Add: $(3 + 2) + \tfrac{10 + 3}{16} = 5\tfrac{13}{16}$
@answer The total length is $5\tfrac{13}{16}$ in.
:::

:::example Regrouping
A plumber joins a $4\tfrac34$ in pipe nipple to a $2\tfrac{7}{16}$ in fitting. How long is the joined piece?
@step Common denominator $16$: $4\tfrac34 = 4\tfrac{12}{16}$
@step Add: $(4 + 2) + \tfrac{12 + 7}{16} = 6\tfrac{19}{16}$
@step $\tfrac{19}{16}$ is more than $1$: $\tfrac{19}{16} = 1\tfrac{3}{16}$, so $6\tfrac{19}{16} = 7\tfrac{3}{16}$
@answer The joined piece is $7\tfrac{3}{16}$ in long.
:::

## Subtracting and borrowing

Subtracting works the same way. If the top fraction is too small, **borrow** $1$ whole inch and write it as $\tfrac{16}{16}$ (or $\tfrac88$, $\tfrac44$, whatever matches your denominator).

:::example Cutting flat bar
A welder has a piece of steel flat bar $6\tfrac14$ in long and cuts off $2\tfrac{7}{16}$ in. Ignoring the cut, how long is the piece that is left?
@step Common denominator $16$: $6\tfrac14 = 6\tfrac{4}{16}$
@step You cannot take $\tfrac{7}{16}$ from $\tfrac{4}{16}$, so borrow $1$: $6\tfrac{4}{16} = 5\tfrac{20}{16}$
@step Subtract: $(5 - 2) + \tfrac{20 - 7}{16} = 3\tfrac{13}{16}$
@answer The piece left is $3\tfrac{13}{16}$ in long.
:::

:::mistake Borrowing 10 instead of 16
When you borrow $1$ inch, it becomes $\tfrac{16}{16}$, not $10$ of anything. $6\tfrac{4}{16}$ becomes $5\tfrac{20}{16}$, not $5\tfrac{14}{16}$.
:::

:::

Every saw blade turns a thin strip of wood into sawdust. That strip is the **saw kerf**, and on a long cut list it adds up.

:::example Allowing for the saw kerf
Mateo cuts $3$ pieces, each $14\tfrac34$ in long, from a $48$ in board. Each of his $3$ cuts wastes $\tfrac18$ in. How long is the leftover piece?
@step Pieces: $3 \times 14\tfrac34 = 3 \times \tfrac{59}{4} = \tfrac{177}{4} = 44\tfrac14$ in
@step Kerf: $3 \times \tfrac18 = \tfrac38$ in
@step Used: $44\tfrac14 + \tfrac38 = 44\tfrac28 + \tfrac38 = 44\tfrac58$ in
@step Leftover: $48 - 44\tfrac58 = 47\tfrac88 - 44\tfrac58 = 3\tfrac38$ in
@answer The leftover piece is $3\tfrac38$ in long.
:::

## Feet and inches

Longer measurements are often written in feet and inches, such as $5\ \text{ft}\ 9\ \text{in}$. Remember that $12\ \text{in} = 1\ \text{ft}$. Add or subtract the feet and the inches **separately**, then regroup.

:::example Adding feet and inches
Two fence rails are $5\ \text{ft}\ 9\ \text{in}$ and $3\ \text{ft}\ 7\ \text{in}$ long. What is their total length?
@step Feet: $5 + 3 = 8\ \text{ft}$. Inches: $9 + 7 = 16\ \text{in}$.
@step $16\ \text{in} = 1\ \text{ft}\ 4\ \text{in}$, so $8\ \text{ft}\ 16\ \text{in} = 9\ \text{ft}\ 4\ \text{in}$
@answer The total length is $9\ \text{ft}\ 4\ \text{in}$.
:::

:::example Subtracting feet and inches
A garage door opening is $8\ \text{ft}\ 2\ \text{in}$ high. A camper is $3\ \text{ft}\ 7\ \text{in}$ tall. What is the difference in height?
@step You cannot take $7\ \text{in}$ from $2\ \text{in}$, so borrow $1\ \text{ft} = 12\ \text{in}$: $8\ \text{ft}\ 2\ \text{in} = 7\ \text{ft}\ 14\ \text{in}$
@step Subtract: $(7 - 3)\ \text{ft}\ (14 - 7)\ \text{in} = 4\ \text{ft}\ 7\ \text{in}$
@answer The difference is $4\ \text{ft}\ 7\ \text{in}$.
:::

:::mistake Feet and inches are not decimals
$5\ \text{ft}\ 9\ \text{in}$ is **not** $5.9\ \text{ft}$. Nine inches is $\tfrac{9}{12} = 0.75$ of a foot, so $5\ \text{ft}\ 9\ \text{in} = 5.75\ \text{ft}$. Keep feet and inches as two separate numbers.
:::

:::summary
- Use the largest denominator ($2$, $4$, $8$ or $16$) as the common denominator.
- Add or subtract whole numbers and fractions separately, then regroup or borrow $1$ inch $= \tfrac{16}{16}$.
- Each saw cut wastes a little length (the kerf); include it in cutting problems.
- $12\ \text{in} = 1\ \text{ft}$. Work with feet and inches separately, regroup $12$ inches as $1$ foot, and borrow $1$ foot as $12$ inches.
:::
