Right triangles are hiding all over a job site: a ladder against a wall, a rafter on a roof, a brace across a gate, the diagonal of a concrete slab. Once you can spot the right triangle, the Pythagorean theorem tells you lengths you cannot easily measure.

## Find the right triangle first

Every word problem in this lesson follows the same plan.

1. **Sketch** the situation and mark the right angle (usually where a wall meets the ground, or where length meets width).
2. **Label** the lengths you know and the one you want.
3. **Decide** whether the unknown is the hypotenuse (across from the right angle) or a leg.
4. **Use** $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ for the hypotenuse, or $a^2 = c^2 - b^2$ for a leg.
5. **Check** that the answer makes sense and has units.

:::example How high does the ladder reach?
A $20\ \text{ft}$ extension ladder leans against a house. Its foot is $5\ \text{ft}$ from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach, to the nearest tenth of a foot?
@step The wall and the ground meet at a right angle. The ladder is across from it, so the ladder is the hypotenuse. The height $h$ is a leg.
@step $h^2 = 20^2 - 5^2 = 400 - 25 = 375$
@step $h = \sqrt{375} \approx 19.36$
@answer The ladder reaches about $19.4\ \text{ft}$ up the wall.
:::

## Rafters and roofs

:::definition Rafter, run and rise
A **rafter** is a sloping board that holds up a roof. The **run** is the horizontal distance it covers, and the **rise** is how much it climbs. The run and the rise are the legs, and the rafter is the hypotenuse.
:::

:::example Cutting a shed rafter
A shed roof has a run of $7\ \text{ft}$ and a rise of $3.5\ \text{ft}$. How long is each rafter, from the top of the wall to the peak, to the nearest tenth of a foot?
@step $L^2 = 7^2 + 3.5^2 = 49 + 12.25 = 61.25$
@step $L = \sqrt{61.25} \approx 7.83$
@answer Each rafter is about $7.8\ \text{ft}$ long.
:::

:::tip Order extra
A real rafter also needs extra length for the overhang past the wall and for cutting the ends. Find the slope length first, then add the extras.
:::

:::check m103-pyth-apply 2
:::

## Diagonals: screens, slabs and braces

:::definition Diagonal
A **diagonal** joins two opposite corners of a rectangle. It splits the rectangle into two right triangles, so the diagonal is a hypotenuse.
:::

TVs and monitors are sold by the length of the screen's diagonal. Concrete crews measure both diagonals of a form: if the two diagonals are equal, the form is a true rectangle.

:::example What size is the TV?
A TV screen is $48\ \text{in}$ wide and $27\ \text{in}$ tall. What size is it, to the nearest inch?
@step $d^2 = 48^2 + 27^2 = 2304 + 729 = 3033$
@step $d = \sqrt{3033} \approx 55.07$
@answer It is a $55\ \text{in}$ TV.
:::

:::example Checking a slab form
A form for a concrete slab is $6\ \text{m}$ long and $4.5\ \text{m}$ wide. What should each diagonal measure?
@step $d^2 = 6^2 + 4.5^2 = 36 + 20.25 = 56.25$
@step $d = \sqrt{56.25} = 7.5$
@answer Each diagonal should be $7.5\ \text{m}$.
:::

:::mistake Mixing units
All three sides must be in the **same unit**. A wheelchair ramp that rises $24\ \text{in}$ over a run of $24\ \text{ft}$ does **not** have a sloped length of $\sqrt{24^2 + 24^2}$. Change $24\ \text{in}$ to $2\ \text{ft}$ first: $\sqrt{24^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{580} \approx 24.08\ \text{ft}$.
:::

:::check m103-pyth-apply 2
:::

## Two-step problems

Some jobs need the Pythagorean theorem plus one more step: adding an overhang, comparing with a board length, or finding how much a shortcut saves.

:::example The shortcut across a field
A farmer can drive across a rectangular field diagonally instead of along two fence lines. The field is $120\ \text{m}$ long and $50\ \text{m}$ wide. How much shorter is the diagonal route?
@step Along the two fence lines: $120 + 50 = 170\ \text{m}$.
@step Diagonal: $d = \sqrt{120^2 + 50^2} = \sqrt{14\,400 + 2500} = \sqrt{16\,900} = 130\ \text{m}$.
@step Difference: $170 - 130 = 40$
@answer The diagonal route is $40\ \text{m}$ shorter.
:::

:::example A rafter with an overhang
A garage is $24\ \text{ft}$ wide. The peak is centred, $6\ \text{ft}$ above the walls, and each rafter extends $1.5\ \text{ft}$ past the wall for the overhang. How long is each rafter, to the nearest tenth of a foot?
@step The peak is centred, so the run is $24 \div 2 = 12\ \text{ft}$.
@step Wall to peak: $\sqrt{12^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{180} \approx 13.42\ \text{ft}$.
@step Add the overhang: $13.42 + 1.5 = 14.92$
@answer Each rafter is about $14.9\ \text{ft}$ long.
:::

:::check m103-pyth-apply 3
:::

:::summary
- Sketch, label, find the right angle, then decide: is the unknown the hypotenuse or a leg?
- Ladders, rafters, ramps, guy wires, braces and diagonals are all hypotenuses.
- Put every length in the same unit before you use the formula.
- For two-step problems, find the missing side first, then add, subtract or compare.
- Check that your answer is sensible: a leg is shorter than the hypotenuse.
:::
