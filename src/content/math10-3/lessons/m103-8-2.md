A painter knows how long the ladder is. A carpenter knows the length of a rafter. In many jobs the side you know, or the side you need, is the **hypotenuse**, and the tangent ratio does not use it. Two more ratios, sine and cosine, do.

## Sine and cosine

Tangent compares the two legs. Sine and cosine compare a leg with the hypotenuse. Like tangent, they come from similar triangles: they are the same in every right triangle with the same angle, so they depend only on the angle.

:::definition Sine and cosine
For an acute angle $\theta$ in a right triangle,
$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$$
:::

:::example Writing sine and cosine
In $\triangle ABC$ above, $\angle C = 90^\circ$, $AB = 13$ cm, $BC = 5$ cm and $AC = 12$ cm. Find $\sin A$ and $\cos A$.
@step The hypotenuse is $AB = 13$. For $\angle A$, the opposite side is $BC = 5$ and the adjacent side is $AC = 12$.
@step $\sin A = \dfrac{5}{13} \approx 0.3846$
@step $\cos A = \dfrac{12}{13} \approx 0.9231$
@answer $\sin A \approx 0.3846$ and $\cos A \approx 0.9231$
:::

:::tip Always less than 1
The hypotenuse is the longest side, so for an acute angle $\sin\theta$ and $\cos\theta$ are always less than $1$. If you get a sine or cosine bigger than $1$, your fraction is upside down.
:::

## Choosing the right ratio

You now have three ratios. To choose one, follow three steps:

1. Label the sides from the angle: opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse.
2. Find the side you **know** and the side you **want**.
3. Pick the ratio that uses exactly those two sides.

:::definition SOH CAH TOA
A memory aid for the three **primary trigonometric ratios**:
- **SOH**: **S**ine $=$ **O**pposite $\div$ **H**ypotenuse
- **CAH**: **C**osine $=$ **A**djacent $\div$ **H**ypotenuse
- **TOA**: **T**angent $=$ **O**pposite $\div$ **A**djacent
:::

:::example Which ratio?
A $6.5$ m ladder leans against a wall and makes an angle of $75^\circ$ with the ground. You want the height $h$ the ladder reaches up the wall. Which ratio should you use?
@step The ladder ($6.5$ m) is across from the right angle, so it is the hypotenuse.
@step The height $h$ is across from the $75^\circ$ angle, so it is the opposite side.
@step Opposite and hypotenuse: that is **SOH**.
@answer Use sine: $\sin 75^\circ = \dfrac{h}{6.5}$
:::

:::mistake Using tangent for everything
Tangent never uses the hypotenuse. If the hypotenuse is the side you know or the side you want, you need sine or cosine.
:::

## Finding a side with sine or cosine

The method is the same as with tangent: write the ratio, put in what you know, then solve. If the unknown is on top, multiply. If it is on the bottom, divide.

:::example The unknown is on top
How high up the wall does the $6.5$ m ladder at $75^\circ$ reach? Round to the nearest tenth of a metre.
@step $\sin 75^\circ = \dfrac{h}{6.5}$
@step Multiply both sides by $6.5$: $h = 6.5\sin 75^\circ \approx 6.5 \times 0.9659$
@answer $h \approx 6.3\ \text{m}$
:::

:::example The unknown is on the bottom
A roof rafter covers a horizontal run of $4.5$ m and makes an angle of $28^\circ$ with the horizontal. How long is the rafter? Ignore the overhang and round to the nearest tenth of a metre.
@step The run is adjacent to the $28^\circ$ angle and the rafter $L$ is the hypotenuse. Use CAH: $\cos 28^\circ = \dfrac{4.5}{L}$
@step Multiply both sides by $L$, then divide both sides by $\cos 28^\circ$: $L = \dfrac{4.5}{\cos 28^\circ} \approx \dfrac{4.5}{0.8829}$
@answer $L \approx 5.1\ \text{m}$
:::

:::mistake The hypotenuse must be the longest side
If you are finding the hypotenuse and your answer is **shorter** than a leg, you multiplied when you should have divided. For the rafter, $4.5 \times \cos 28^\circ \approx 4.0$ m. That is shorter than the $4.5$ m run, so it cannot be the rafter.
:::

## Finding an angle with sine or cosine

To find an angle from a leg and the hypotenuse, use the inverse keys $\sin^{-1}$ or $\cos^{-1}$, just as you used $\tan^{-1}$.

:::example Checking a ladder
The foot of a $5$ m ladder is $1.3$ m from the wall. What angle does the ladder make with the ground? Round to the nearest tenth of a degree.
@step The $1.3$ m distance touches $\theta$, so it is adjacent. The $5$ m ladder is the hypotenuse. Use CAH.
@step $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1.3}{5}$, so $\theta = \cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1.3}{5}\right)$
@answer $\theta \approx 74.9^\circ$
:::

:::example A plumbing offset
A plumber moves a drain line sideways with a diagonal piece of pipe. The diagonal piece (the travel) is $50$ cm long and moves the line over $35$ cm (the offset). The offset is opposite the angle $\theta$ between the diagonal and the original pipe. Find $\theta$ to the nearest tenth of a degree.
@step The offset is opposite $\theta$ and the travel is the hypotenuse. Use SOH: $\sin\theta = \dfrac{35}{50}$
@step $\theta = \sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{35}{50}\right)$
@answer $\theta \approx 44.4^\circ$
:::

:::summary
- $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$ and $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$.
- SOH CAH TOA: pick the ratio that uses the side you know and the side you want.
- Unknown on top: multiply. Unknown on the bottom: divide.
- A hypotenuse must be longer than either leg, and a sine or cosine is always less than 1.
- Use $\sin^{-1}$ or $\cos^{-1}$ in degree mode to find an angle.
:::
