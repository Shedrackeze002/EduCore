Puzzles train the same skill a tradesperson uses to picture how parts fit together before building. The key strategy in this lesson is **systematic counting**: sort things into groups so that you count every one exactly once.

## Counting systematically

:::definition Systematic counting
**Systematic counting** means following a plan, such as sorting by size or by position, so that nothing is missed and nothing is counted twice.
:::

Good strategies for spatial puzzles:

- Start with a smaller version of the puzzle and look for a pattern.
- Sort what you are counting into groups (by size, by position, by number of painted faces).
- Record your counts in a table, then check that the groups add up to the total.

## Counting squares in a grid

How many squares are in this $3 \times 3$ grid? There are more than 9, because bigger squares are made of several small ones.

:::example Squares of all sizes
Count all the squares in a $3 \times 3$ grid.
@step $1 \times 1$ squares: $3$ across and $3$ down, so $3 \times 3 = 9$.
@step $2 \times 2$ squares: they fit in $2$ positions across and $2$ down, so $2 \times 2 = 4$.
@step $3 \times 3$ squares: just $1$.
@answer $9 + 4 + 1 = 14$ squares.
:::

The pattern: in an $n \times n$ grid, a $k \times k$ square fits in $(n - k + 1)$ positions across and the same number down. Add the square numbers:

| Grid | $1 \times 1$ | $2 \times 2$ | $3 \times 3$ | $4 \times 4$ | $5 \times 5$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total squares | $1$ | $1 + 4 = 5$ | $5 + 9 = 14$ | $14 + 16 = 30$ | $30 + 25 = 55$ |

:::mistake Forgetting the bigger squares
Counting only the small squares gives $9$ for a $3 \times 3$ grid. Always work through every size, from $1 \times 1$ up to the whole grid.
:::

To count **rectangles**, notice that every rectangle is fixed by choosing 2 of the vertical grid lines and 2 of the horizontal grid lines. A $2 \times 3$ grid has 4 vertical lines (6 ways to choose a pair) and 3 horizontal lines (3 ways), so it has $6 \times 3 = 18$ rectangles.

## The painted cube

A cube is painted on all six faces, then cut into small cubes. How many small cubes have 3, 2, 1 or 0 painted faces?

Sort the small cubes by **where** they sit:

| Position | Painted faces | Number in an $n \times n \times n$ cube |
|---|---|---|
| Corner | 3 | $8$ |
| Edge, not a corner | 2 | $12(n - 2)$ |
| Middle of a face | 1 | $6(n - 2)^2$ |
| Inside | 0 | $(n - 2)^3$ |

:::example A 4 cm cube
A wooden cube $4\ \text{cm}$ on each edge is painted, then cut into $1\ \text{cm}$ cubes. Sort the small cubes.
@step Corners: always $8$ cubes with 3 painted faces.
@step Edges: each of the 12 edges has $4 - 2 = 2$ middle cubes, so $12 \times 2 = 24$ cubes with 2 painted faces.
@step Faces: each of the 6 faces has a $2 \times 2$ middle, so $6 \times 4 = 24$ cubes with 1 painted face.
@step Inside: a $2 \times 2 \times 2$ cube, so $8$ cubes with no paint.
@answer $8 + 24 + 24 + 8 = 64 = 4^3$, so every small cube is counted once.
:::

:::tip Check the total
The four groups must add to $n^3$. If they do not, a group has been missed or counted twice.
:::

## Dice puzzles

On a **standard die**, opposite faces always add to 7: 1 is opposite 6, 2 is opposite 5, and 3 is opposite 4. So all six faces add to $3 \times 7 = 21$.

:::example A tower of dice
Three dice are stacked in a tower on a table. The top face shows 4. What is the total of the hidden faces (faces touching another die or the table)?
@step Each of the two lower dice has its top and bottom hidden. These are opposite faces, so each die hides $7$: $7 + 7 = 14$.
@step The top die hides only its bottom face, which is opposite the 4: $7 - 4 = 3$.
@answer The hidden faces add to $14 + 3 = 17$.
:::

A **net** is a flat pattern that folds into a cube. In a straight row of three squares, the first and third squares end up opposite each other.

The net above folds into a standard die. What number goes on the face marked ?
@step In the row 2, ?, 5, 4, the 2 and the 5 are one square apart, so they are opposite. Check: $2 + 5 = 7$.
@step The ? and the 4 are also one square apart, so they are opposite.
@step The 6 and the 1 are above and below the same square, so they are opposite. Check: $6 + 1 = 7$.
@answer The ? face is $7 - 4 = 3$.

:::summary
- Count systematically: sort into groups, record them in a table and check the total.
- An $n \times n$ grid has $1^2 + 2^2 + \dots + n^2$ squares. Rectangles: choose 2 vertical lines and 2 horizontal lines.
- Painted $n \times n \times n$ cube: 8 corners, $12(n - 2)$ edge cubes, $6(n - 2)^2$ face cubes, $(n - 2)^3$ inside cubes.
- Opposite faces of a standard die add to 7, and all six faces add to 21.
- In a cube net, squares that are one apart in a straight row become opposite faces.
:::
