A plumber cuts copper pipe in millimetres, a cook measures stock in litres, and a grain truck is weighed in tonnes. All of these units belong to one system, the **SI** (metric) system, and they are linked by powers of 10. Learn the prefixes once and you can convert any of them.

## SI prefixes

The SI system has one **base unit** for each kind of measurement: the **metre** (m) for length, the **gram** (g) for mass and the **litre** (L) for capacity. A **prefix** in front of the base unit tells you how many times bigger or smaller the unit is.

:::definition SI and prefixes
The **SI** (Système International) is the metric system used in Canada and most of the world. A **prefix** is the part at the start of a unit name, like *kilo-* in kilometre, that multiplies or divides the base unit by a power of 10.
:::

| Prefix | Symbol | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| kilo- | k | $1000$ times the base unit | $1\ \text{km} = 1000\ \text{m}$ |
| hecto- | h | $100$ times | $1$ hectolitre $= 100\ \text{L}$ |
| deca- | da | $10$ times | $1$ decametre $= 10\ \text{m}$ |
| (base unit) | | | metre, gram, litre |
| deci- | d | $\tfrac{1}{10}$ of the base unit | $10\ \text{dm} = 1\ \text{m}$ |
| centi- | c | $\tfrac{1}{100}$ | $100\ \text{cm} = 1\ \text{m}$ |
| milli- | m | $\tfrac{1}{1000}$ | $1000\ \text{mL} = 1\ \text{L}$ |

The three you will use every day on the job are **kilo**, **centi** and **milli**. A **tonne** (t) is also used for heavy loads: $1\ \text{t} = 1000\ \text{kg}$.

:::example How many in one?
How many millimetres are in $1$ metre?
@step *Milli-* means $\tfrac{1}{1000}$, so a millimetre is one thousandth of a metre.
@step That means it takes $1000$ millimetres to make one metre.
@answer $1\ \text{m} = 1000\ \text{mm}$
:::

:::tip Referents help you choose a unit
A millimetre is about the thickness of a dime. A centimetre is about the width of your little fingernail. A kilogram is the mass of one litre of water. A millilitre is just a few drops. If a measurement does not match your picture, the prefix is probably wrong.
:::

:::mistake Centi- is not one hundred
*Centi-* means one **hundredth**, not one hundred. A centimetre is tiny; it takes 100 of them to make a metre. The same goes for milli-: a millimetre is one thousandth of a metre, not a thousand metres.
:::

## Converting within SI

To convert, ask one question first: **is the new unit bigger or smaller?**

- Changing to a **smaller** unit gives a **bigger** number, so **multiply**.
- Changing to a **larger** unit gives a **smaller** number, so **divide**.

Because every step is a power of 10, multiplying or dividing just moves the decimal point.

:::example Kilometres to metres
A pipeline crew lays $3.75\ \text{km}$ of pipe. How many metres is that?
@step $1\ \text{km} = 1000\ \text{m}$. Metres are smaller, so multiply.
@step $3.75 \times 1000 = 3750$
@answer $3.75\ \text{km} = 3750\ \text{m}$
:::

:::example Millilitres to litres
A recipe uses $2350\ \text{mL}$ of stock. How many litres is that?
@step $1000\ \text{mL} = 1\ \text{L}$. Litres are bigger, so divide.
@step $2350 \div 1000 = 2.35$
@answer $2350\ \text{mL} = 2.35\ \text{L}$
:::

:::mistake Multiplying when you should divide
Changing $450\ \text{mm}$ to centimetres by multiplying gives $4500\ \text{cm}$, which is 45 metres! Centimetres are **bigger** than millimetres, so divide: $450 \div 10 = 45\ \text{cm}$. Always check that your answer makes sense.
:::

## Mixed units on the job

Measurements on a job site often come in different units. Before you add or subtract, **change everything to the same unit**.

:::example Cutting a board
A carpenter has a $3\ \text{m}$ board and cuts pieces that are $1.2\ \text{m}$, $85\ \text{cm}$ and $450\ \text{mm}$ long. Ignoring saw cuts, how much is left, in centimetres?
@step Change everything to centimetres: $3\ \text{m} = 300\ \text{cm}$, $1.2\ \text{m} = 120\ \text{cm}$, $450\ \text{mm} = 45\ \text{cm}$.
@step Total cut: $120 + 85 + 45 = 250\ \text{cm}$
@step Left over: $300 - 250 = 50\ \text{cm}$
@answer $50\ \text{cm}$ of the board is left.
:::

The same idea lets you compare measurements. Is $0.85\ \text{kg}$ more or less than $900\ \text{g}$? Change to grams: $0.85\ \text{kg} = 850\ \text{g}$, which is less than $900\ \text{g}$.

## Volume, capacity and area

**Volume** is the space an object takes up, measured in cubic units such as $\text{cm}^3$ or $\text{m}^3$. **Capacity** is how much a container can hold, measured in millilitres and litres. The two are linked by one key fact.

:::definition Volume and capacity
A cube $1\ \text{cm}$ on each side holds exactly $1\ \text{mL}$. So
$$1\ \text{cm}^3 = 1\ \text{mL}, \qquad 1000\ \text{cm}^3 = 1\ \text{L}, \qquad 1\ \text{m}^3 = 1000\ \text{L}.$$
:::

:::example Litres in a cooler
The inside of a cooler is $40\ \text{cm}$ long, $25\ \text{cm}$ wide and $30\ \text{cm}$ deep. How many litres does it hold?
@step Volume: $V = 40 \times 25 \times 30 = 30\,000\ \text{cm}^3$
@step $30\,000\ \text{cm}^3 = 30\,000\ \text{mL}$
@step $30\,000 \div 1000 = 30$
@answer The cooler holds $30\ \text{L}$.
:::

Area units work the same way, but you **square** the conversion factor. A square metre is $100\ \text{cm}$ by $100\ \text{cm}$, so $1\ \text{m}^2 = 100 \times 100 = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.

:::mistake Using 100 for square units
$2.5\ \text{m}^2$ is **not** $250\ \text{cm}^2$. Picture the square metre: it holds $10\,000$ one-centimetre squares. So $2.5\ \text{m}^2 = 2.5 \times 10\,000 = 25\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::

:::example A water trough
A cattle trough is $2.4\ \text{m}$ long, $60\ \text{cm}$ wide and $50\ \text{cm}$ deep. How many litres does it hold?
@step Use one unit: $2.4\ \text{m} = 240\ \text{cm}$.
@step $V = 240 \times 60 \times 50 = 720\,000\ \text{cm}^3$
@answer $720\,000 \div 1000 = 720\ \text{L}$
:::

:::summary
- Base units: metre (length), gram (mass), litre (capacity). Kilo- $= 1000$, centi- $= \tfrac{1}{100}$, milli- $= \tfrac{1}{1000}$.
- To a smaller unit: multiply. To a bigger unit: divide. Check that the answer makes sense.
- Change mixed measurements to one unit before adding, subtracting or comparing.
- $1\ \text{cm}^3 = 1\ \text{mL}$, $1000\ \text{cm}^3 = 1\ \text{L}$, $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1000\ \text{L}$.
- For area, square the factor: $1\ \text{m}^2 = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$.
:::
