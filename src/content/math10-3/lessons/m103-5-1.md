A flooring installer, a landscaper and a roofer all start a job the same way: they measure the surface and work out its **area**. Every material order, quote and bill depends on getting that number right.

## What area measures

Area tells you how much flat surface a shape covers. You measure it by counting how many **unit squares** fit inside the shape. A square that is $1\ \text{m}$ on each side has an area of $1\ \text{m}^2$ (one square metre).

:::definition Area
The **area** of a flat shape is the number of square units needed to cover it. It is written in square units such as $\text{cm}^2$, $\text{m}^2$, $\text{in}^2$ or $\text{ft}^2$.
:::

A rectangle that is 5 squares long and 3 squares wide holds $5 \times 3 = 15$ squares. That gives the rectangle formula:

$$A = l \times w$$

:::example A concrete pad
A rectangular concrete pad for a shed is $3.5\ \text{m}$ long and $2.4\ \text{m}$ wide. What is its area?
@step Use $A = lw$.
@step $A = 3.5 \times 2.4 = 8.4$
@answer The pad has an area of $8.4\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::tip Same units first
Both measurements must be in the same unit before you multiply. A countertop that is $2.4\ \text{m}$ by $65\ \text{cm}$ is $2.4\ \text{m}$ by $0.65\ \text{m}$.
:::

## Triangles, parallelograms and trapezoids

Every one of these formulas uses a **height** that is measured at a right angle to the base.

| Shape | Area formula |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | $A = lw$ |
| Parallelogram | $A = bh$ |
| Triangle | $A = \tfrac{1}{2}bh$ |
| Trapezoid | $A = \dfrac{a + b}{2} \times h$ |

A triangle is exactly half of a parallelogram with the same base and height. That is where the $\tfrac{1}{2}$ comes from.

:::definition Base, height and trapezoid
The **base** is the side you measure along. The **height** is the perpendicular (straight up, at $90^\circ$) distance from the base to the opposite side or corner. A **trapezoid** is a four-sided shape with exactly one pair of parallel sides, called $a$ and $b$.
:::

:::example A trapezoid garden bed
A garden bed is shaped like a trapezoid. Its parallel sides are $6\ \text{ft}$ and $10\ \text{ft}$, and they are $4\ \text{ft}$ apart. Find the area.
@step Average the parallel sides: $\dfrac{6 + 10}{2} = 8\ \text{ft}$.
@step Multiply by the height: $8 \times 4 = 32$
@answer The garden bed has an area of $32\ \text{ft}^2$.
:::

:::example A gable end with a fraction
The triangular gable end of a shed has a base of $9\ \text{ft}$ and a height of $3\tfrac{1}{2}\ \text{ft}$. Find its area.
@step Write the mixed number as a decimal: $3\tfrac{1}{2} = 3.5$.
@step $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 9 \times 3.5 = 15.75$
@answer The gable end has an area of $15.75\ \text{ft}^2$ (or $15\tfrac{3}{4}\ \text{ft}^2$).
:::

:::mistake The slanted side is not the height
In a parallelogram or triangle, the slanted side is longer than the height. Using it makes your area too big. Always use the distance measured at a right angle to the base.
:::

## Circles

The area of a circle depends on its **radius**:

$$A = \pi r^2$$

Square the radius first, then multiply by $\pi$. Use the $\pi$ key on your calculator and round only at the end.

:::definition Radius and diameter
The **radius** is the distance from the centre of a circle to its edge. The **diameter** goes all the way across through the centre, so the diameter is twice the radius: $r = d \div 2$.
:::

:::example A hot tub cover
A round hot tub cover has a diameter of $7\ \text{ft}$. What is its area, to the nearest tenth of a square foot?
@step The radius is half the diameter: $r = 7 \div 2 = 3.5\ \text{ft}$.
@step $A = \pi (3.5)^2 \approx 38.48$
@answer The cover has an area of about $38.5\ \text{ft}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Using the diameter in the formula
Putting the diameter into $A = \pi r^2$ gives $\pi (7)^2 \approx 153.9\ \text{ft}^2$, which is **four times** too big. Always halve the diameter first.
:::

:::tip Half and quarter circles
A semicircle is half a circle, so find $\pi r^2$ and divide by 2. A quarter circle (like a shower base in a corner) is $\pi r^2 \div 4$. A semicircular window that is $36\ \text{in}$ wide has $r = 18\ \text{in}$ and an area of $\pi(18)^2 \div 2 \approx 508.9\ \text{in}^2$.
:::

## Working backwards to a missing length

Sometimes you know the area you need and must find a length. Substitute what you know into the formula, then undo the operations one at a time.

:::example Sizing a dog run
A rectangular dog run must have an area of $60\ \text{m}^2$. It will be $5\ \text{m}$ wide. How long must it be?
@step $A = lw$, so $60 = l \times 5$.
@step Divide both sides by 5: $l = \dfrac{60}{5} = 12$
@answer The dog run must be $12\ \text{m}$ long. Check: $12 \times 5 = 60$ ✓
:::

:::example Radius of a skating rink
A farmer floods a circular rink with an area of $300\ \text{m}^2$. What is its radius, to the nearest tenth of a metre?
@step $300 = \pi r^2$
@step Divide both sides by $\pi$: $r^2 = \dfrac{300}{\pi} \approx 95.49$
@step Take the square root: $r = \sqrt{95.49} \approx 9.77$
@answer The radius is about $9.8\ \text{m}$.
:::

:::tip Triangles: deal with the one-half first
For $A = \tfrac{1}{2}bh$, multiply both sides by 2 first. A gable with an area of $30\ \text{ft}^2$ and a base of $10\ \text{ft}$ gives $60 = 10h$, so $h = 6\ \text{ft}$.
:::

:::summary
- Area is measured in square units: $\text{cm}^2$, $\text{m}^2$, $\text{in}^2$, $\text{ft}^2$.
- Rectangle $A = lw$; parallelogram $A = bh$; triangle $A = \tfrac{1}{2}bh$; trapezoid $A = \dfrac{a + b}{2} \times h$.
- The height is always measured at a right angle to the base.
- Circle $A = \pi r^2$. Halve the diameter to get the radius.
- To find a missing length, substitute into the formula and undo each operation.
:::
