A carpenter cutting trim, a welder fitting pipe and a truck driver backing a trailer up to a loading dock all work with angles. If you can name an angle and estimate its size, you can catch a mistake before you cut, bend or turn.

## Naming angles

An **angle** is made by two rays that start at the same point. That point is the **vertex**, and the rays are the **arms** of the angle. We measure the amount of turn between the arms in **degrees** ($^\circ$). One full turn is $360^\circ$.

:::definition Angle
An angle is formed by two rays with a common endpoint, called the **vertex**. The angle below is named $\angle ABC$ or $\angle CBA$. The vertex letter always goes in the middle.
:::

## Five kinds of angles

We sort angles by comparing them with a square corner ($90^\circ$) and a straight line ($180^\circ$).

:::definition Kinds of angles
- An **acute** angle is less than $90^\circ$.
- A **right** angle is exactly $90^\circ$.
- An **obtuse** angle is between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$.
- A **straight** angle is exactly $180^\circ$.
- A **reflex** angle is between $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$.
:::

| Kind | Size | On the job |
|---|---|---|
| Acute | less than $90^\circ$ | the thin end of a wood shim |
| Right | exactly $90^\circ$ | the corner of a sheet of plywood |
| Obtuse | between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$ | a $135^\circ$ bend in a run of trim |
| Straight | exactly $180^\circ$ | two boards butted end to end in a straight line |
| Reflex | between $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$ | a sprinkler that waters most of a circle |

:::example Classifying angles
Classify each angle: $35^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $172^\circ$, $180^\circ$ and $250^\circ$.
@step $35^\circ$ is less than $90^\circ$, so it is acute.
@step $90^\circ$ is a square corner, so it is a right angle.
@step $172^\circ$ is between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, so it is obtuse.
@step $180^\circ$ is a straight angle.
@step $250^\circ$ is more than $180^\circ$, so it is reflex.
@answer Acute, right, obtuse, straight and reflex.
:::

:::mistake Close is not the same as equal
An angle of $89^\circ$ is **acute** and an angle of $181^\circ$ is **reflex**. An angle is right or straight only when it is **exactly** $90^\circ$ or $180^\circ$.
:::

## Estimating angles

Always estimate before you measure. Use **benchmark angles** that are easy to picture: $45^\circ$ (half a square corner), $90^\circ$, $135^\circ$ (halfway from a square corner to a straight line), $180^\circ$ and $270^\circ$ (three square corners).

## Measuring with a protractor

:::definition Protractor
A **protractor** is a half-circle tool marked from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$. Most protractors have an inner scale and an outer scale that run in opposite directions.
:::

To measure an angle:

1. Put the centre mark of the protractor on the vertex.
2. Line up the zero line with one arm.
3. Start at the $0$ on that arm and follow **the same scale** until you reach the other arm.

:::mistake Reading the wrong scale
Suppose the second arm crosses the scales at $65$ and $115$. The angle is one of these, not both. Reading the wrong scale gives $180^\circ$ minus the correct answer. Check against your estimate: if the angle is obtuse, it is $115^\circ$.
:::

A protractor only goes to $180^\circ$. To find a **reflex** angle, measure the angle on the other side of the vertex and subtract it from $360^\circ$.

## Angles around a point and on a clock

Angles all the way around a point add to $360^\circ$. A clock face is a handy example. Its 12 numbers share $360^\circ$, so they are $30^\circ$ apart. The minute hand turns $360^\circ \div 60 = 6^\circ$ each minute, and the hour hand turns $30^\circ \div 60 = 0.5^\circ$ each minute.

:::example The angle between clock hands
A shift at a bakery starts at 2:30. What kind of angle is the smaller angle between the hands of the clock?
@step Minute hand: $30 \times 6^\circ = 180^\circ$ past 12.
@step Hour hand: $2 \times 30^\circ + 30 \times 0.5^\circ = 75^\circ$ past 12.
@step Difference: $180^\circ - 75^\circ = 105^\circ$.
@answer The smaller angle is $105^\circ$, an obtuse angle.
:::

:::example Angles around a point
Three pipes meet at a joint. Two of the angles between them are $110^\circ$ and $135^\circ$. Find the third angle, $x$.
@step $110^\circ + 135^\circ + x = 360^\circ$
@step $x = 360^\circ - 245^\circ$
@answer $x = 115^\circ$
:::

:::tip The hour hand moves too
At 2:30 the hour hand is halfway between the 2 and the 3, not pointing at the 2. Forgetting this is the most common clock error.
:::

:::summary
- An angle is two rays from a common vertex, measured in degrees. A full turn is $360^\circ$.
- Acute: less than $90^\circ$. Right: $90^\circ$. Obtuse: between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$. Straight: $180^\circ$. Reflex: between $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$.
- Estimate with benchmarks ($45^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $135^\circ$, $180^\circ$) before you measure.
- On a protractor, start at $0$ on one arm and read that same scale.
- Reflex angle $= 360^\circ -$ the non-reflex angle. Angles around a point add to $360^\circ$.
:::
