When a framer nails a diagonal brace across a square frame, or a roofer sets a rafter on a level joist, one angle tells you several others for free. In this lesson you will find missing angles from the relationships between them, without measuring.

## Complementary and supplementary angles

:::definition Complementary and supplementary angles
- Two angles are **complementary** when they add to $90^\circ$. Each angle is the **complement** of the other.
- Two angles are **supplementary** when they add to $180^\circ$. Each angle is the **supplement** of the other.
:::

Angles that sit side by side on a straight line always add to $180^\circ$, so they are supplementary. Angles that fill a square corner are complementary.

:::tip Corner and straight
**C**omplementary goes with a **C**orner ($90^\circ$). **S**upplementary goes with a **S**traight line ($180^\circ$).
:::

:::example A ramp and a post
A wheelchair ramp makes a $5^\circ$ angle with the level ground. A vertical post stands on the ground beside it. What angle does the ramp make with the post?
@step The ground and the post meet at $90^\circ$, so the two angles are complementary.
@step $90^\circ - 5^\circ = 85^\circ$
@answer The ramp makes an $85^\circ$ angle with the post.
:::

## Vertically opposite angles

When two straight lines cross, they make four angles. The angles across from each other are **vertically opposite angles**.

:::definition Vertically opposite angles
**Vertically opposite angles** are the pairs of angles across from each other where two lines cross. They are always equal. Angles side by side at the crossing are supplementary.
:::

Only the angles **across** from each other are equal. Angles next to each other add to $180^\circ$. They are equal only when both are $90^\circ$.

:::example Vertically opposite angles with algebra
Two vertically opposite angles measure $(3x - 10)^\circ$ and $(2x + 25)^\circ$. Find $x$ and the size of each angle.
@step Vertically opposite angles are equal: $3x - 10 = 2x + 25$.
@step Subtract $2x$ from both sides: $x - 10 = 25$, so $x = 35$.
@step $3(35) - 10 = 95$ and $2(35) + 25 = 95$.
@answer $x = 35$ and each angle measures $95^\circ$.
:::

## Bisecting an angle

:::definition Angle bisector
An **angle bisector** is a ray that divides an angle into two equal angles. Each part is half of the whole angle.
:::

Carpenters bisect angles all the time. To join two pieces of trim at a corner, the joint line must bisect the corner angle so both pieces meet cleanly. This is called a **mitre joint**. At a square corner, each piece is cut at $45^\circ$.

:::example Finding each half
Ray $BD$ bisects $\angle ABC = 70^\circ$. Find $\angle ABD$ and $\angle DBC$.
@step A bisector makes two equal angles.
@step $70^\circ \div 2 = 35^\circ$
@answer $\angle ABD = \angle DBC = 35^\circ$
:::

:::example A mitre joint
Two walls meet at a $135^\circ$ corner. What angle does the joint line of the baseboard make with each wall?
@step The joint line bisects the corner angle.
@step $135^\circ \div 2 = 67.5^\circ$
@answer The joint line makes $67.5^\circ$ with each wall.
:::

To **construct** a bisector with a compass and a straightedge:

1. Put the compass point on the vertex. Draw an arc that crosses both arms.
2. From each crossing point, draw an arc inside the angle. Keep the compass width the same, so the two arcs cross.
3. Draw a ray from the vertex through the point where the arcs cross.

:::tip Check by folding
Draw the angle on paper and fold it so one arm lies exactly on the other. The fold line is the bisector.
:::

:::note Two bisectors on a straight line
If two angles sit side by side on a straight line, their bisectors always meet at $90^\circ$. For example, $64^\circ \div 2 + 116^\circ \div 2 = 32^\circ + 58^\circ = 90^\circ$, because half of $180^\circ$ is $90^\circ$.
:::

:::summary
- Complementary angles add to $90^\circ$. Supplementary angles add to $180^\circ$.
- Angles side by side on a straight line are supplementary.
- Vertically opposite angles are equal.
- A bisector cuts an angle into two equal halves: half $=$ whole $\div 2$, whole $= 2 \times$ half.
- With algebra: write an equation from the relationship, solve for $x$, then substitute to find the angle.
:::
