A roofer needs to know how steep a roof is. A surveyor needs the height of a grain elevator without climbing it. Both jobs use **trigonometry**, the maths of right triangles, and it starts with giving each side a name.

## Naming the sides

Every right triangle has one $90^\circ$ angle and two smaller (acute) angles. The longest side is always across from the right angle. The other two sides get their names from the acute angle you are working with. We often call that angle $\theta$ (the Greek letter *theta*).

:::definition Hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent
- The **hypotenuse** is across from the right angle. It is always the longest side.
- The **opposite** side is across from $\theta$. It does not touch $\theta$.
- The **adjacent** side touches $\theta$ and is not the hypotenuse.
:::

:::example Labelling a ladder
A ladder leans against a wall. $\theta$ is the angle between the ladder and the ground. Name the three sides of the triangle.
@step The right angle is where the wall meets the ground. The ladder is across from it, so the ladder is the **hypotenuse**.
@step The height up the wall does not touch $\theta$, so it is the **opposite** side.
@step The distance along the ground touches $\theta$ and is not the ladder, so it is the **adjacent** side.
@answer Ladder: hypotenuse. Height up the wall: opposite. Distance along the ground: adjacent.
:::

:::mistake Labels change when the angle changes
"Opposite" and "adjacent" depend on which angle you use. If $\theta$ is the angle at the **top** of the ladder (between the ladder and the wall), the distance along the ground becomes the opposite side and the height up the wall becomes the adjacent side. Only the hypotenuse never changes.

## The tangent ratio

A roof with a 6/12 pitch rises $6$ in for every $12$ in of horizontal run. Cut right triangles of different sizes from the same roof:

| Part of the roof | Rise (opposite) | Run (adjacent) | $\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| small | 6 in | 12 in | 0.5 |
| medium | 1 ft | 2 ft | 0.5 |
| whole rafter | 2.4 m | 4.8 m | 0.5 |

All three triangles have the same angle, so they are **similar**: same shape, different size. That is why $\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$ is the same in every one of them. The ratio depends only on the angle, not on the size of the triangle. For this roof the angle is about $26.6^\circ$.

:::definition Tangent ratio
For an acute angle $\theta$ in a right triangle,
$$\tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$$
:::

:::tip Degree mode
Your calculator must be in **degree mode**. Test it: $\tan 45^\circ$ should give exactly $1$, and $\tan 26.6^\circ$ should give about $0.5$.
:::

:::example Writing a tangent ratio
In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle C = 90^\circ$, $BC = 6$ m and $AC = 8$ m. Find $\tan A$ and $\tan B$.
@step For $\angle A$: the opposite side is $BC = 6$ and the adjacent side is $AC = 8$. So $\tan A = \dfrac{6}{8} = 0.75$.
@step For $\angle B$: the opposite side is $AC = 8$ and the adjacent side is $BC = 6$. So $\tan B = \dfrac{8}{6} \approx 1.33$.
@answer $\tan A = 0.75$ and $\tan B \approx 1.33$
:::

## Finding a side with tangent

If you know an acute angle and one leg, tangent gives you the other leg. Write the ratio, put in what you know, then solve for the unknown.

:::example The unknown is on top
A guy wire is anchored $12$ m from the base of a radio tower. The wire makes an angle of $58^\circ$ with the ground. How high up the tower is the wire attached? Round to the nearest tenth of a metre.
@step The height $h$ is opposite the $58^\circ$ angle. The $12$ m is adjacent. Use tangent.
@step $\tan 58^\circ = \dfrac{h}{12}$
@step Multiply both sides by $12$: $h = 12\tan 58^\circ \approx 12 \times 1.6003$
@answer $h \approx 19.2\ \text{m}$
:::

:::example The unknown is on the bottom
A loading dock is $1.2$ m high. A ramp up to the dock makes an angle of $15^\circ$ with the ground. What horizontal distance does the ramp cover? Round to the nearest tenth of a metre.
@step The rise ($1.2$ m) is opposite the $15^\circ$ angle. The run $x$ is adjacent. Use tangent.
@step $\tan 15^\circ = \dfrac{1.2}{x}$
@step Multiply both sides by $x$, then divide both sides by $\tan 15^\circ$: $x = \dfrac{1.2}{\tan 15^\circ} \approx \dfrac{1.2}{0.2679}$
@answer $x \approx 4.5\ \text{m}$
:::

:::mistake Multiplying when you should divide
When $x$ is on the bottom of the fraction, the answer is $\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\tan\theta}$, **not** opposite $\times \tan\theta$. For the ramp, $1.2 \times \tan 15^\circ \approx 0.3$ m, which is far too short for a gentle ramp that climbs $1.2$ m. Always ask: does my answer make sense?
:::

## Finding an angle with tangent

If you know both legs, use the **inverse tangent**, $\tan^{-1}$, to find the angle. On most calculators it is the **2nd** or **SHIFT** key, then **TAN**.

:::definition Inverse tangent
$\tan^{-1}$ undoes tangent. It turns a ratio back into an angle. If $\tan\theta = 0.5$, then $\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.5) \approx 26.6^\circ$.
:::

:::example The angle of a staircase
Each step of a staircase has a rise of $18$ cm and a run of $28$ cm. What angle does the staircase make with the floor? Round to the nearest tenth of a degree.
@step The rise is opposite $\theta$ and the run is adjacent. Use tangent: $\tan\theta = \dfrac{18}{28}$
@step $\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{18}{28}\right)$
@answer $\theta \approx 32.7^\circ$
:::

:::tip Is the angle reasonable?
If the opposite side is shorter than the adjacent side, the angle is less than $45^\circ$. If it is longer, the angle is more than $45^\circ$. For the stairs, $18 < 28$, so an angle under $45^\circ$ makes sense.
:::

:::summary
- Hypotenuse: across from the right angle. Opposite: across from $\theta$. Adjacent: touches $\theta$ and is not the hypotenuse.
- Similar right triangles have the same side ratios, so a ratio depends only on the angle.
- $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$
- Unknown on top: multiply. Unknown on the bottom: divide.
- To find an angle, use $\tan^{-1}$ with your calculator in degree mode.
:::
