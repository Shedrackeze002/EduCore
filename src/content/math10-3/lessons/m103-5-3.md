How much sheet metal does it take to build a furnace duct? How much paint covers a grain bin? Tradespeople answer these questions by finding the **surface area** of a 3-D object, then deciding which faces the job really needs.

## Surface area of a box

:::definition Surface area and net
The **surface area** of a 3-D object is the total area of all its outside faces. A **net** is the flat pattern you get when you unfold the object. The area of the net equals the surface area.
:::

Unfold a closed cardboard box and you get **six rectangles**: a top and a bottom, a front and a back, and two ends. Each face has a matching partner of the same size.

:::definition Prism and cylinder
A **rectangular prism** is a box shape with six rectangular faces. A **cylinder** is a can shape with two equal circles joined by a curved side.
:::

To find the surface area of a rectangular prism, add the three pairs of faces:

$$SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh$$

:::

:::example A shipping crate
A closed wooden crate is $60\ \text{cm}$ long, $40\ \text{cm}$ wide and $30\ \text{cm}$ high. How much plywood covers all six faces?
@step Top and bottom: $2 \times 60 \times 40 = 4800\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Front and back: $2 \times 60 \times 30 = 3600\ \text{cm}^2$
@step Two ends: $2 \times 40 \times 30 = 2400\ \text{cm}^2$
@answer $SA = 4800 + 3600 + 2400 = 10\,800\ \text{cm}^2$
:::

:::tip Organize your work
Write one line for each pair of faces. It is easy to lose track of faces if you try to do everything in one long calculation.
:::

## Only the faces the job needs

On the job, you rarely cover every face. A planter has no lid. A raised garden bed has no top and no bottom. When you paint a room you paint the walls, but not the door, the window or the floor. **Decide which faces to include before you calculate.**

:::example An open-top planter
A cedar planter box with no top is $36\ \text{in}$ long, $12\ \text{in}$ wide and $10\ \text{in}$ high. How much cedar covers the outside?
@step Bottom only: $36 \times 12 = 432\ \text{in}^2$
@step Front and back: $2 \times 36 \times 10 = 720\ \text{in}^2$
@step Two ends: $2 \times 12 \times 10 = 240\ \text{in}^2$
@answer $432 + 720 + 240 = 1392\ \text{in}^2$
:::

:::example Painting a bedroom
A bedroom is $12\ \text{ft}$ long, $10\ \text{ft}$ wide and $8\ \text{ft}$ high. It has a door that is $3\ \text{ft}$ by $7\ \text{ft}$ and a window that is $4\ \text{ft}$ by $3\ \text{ft}$. Only the walls are painted. What area is painted?
@step Long walls: $2 \times 12 \times 8 = 192\ \text{ft}^2$. Short walls: $2 \times 10 \times 8 = 160\ \text{ft}^2$.
@step All four walls: $192 + 160 = 352\ \text{ft}^2$
@step Subtract the door ($3 \times 7 = 21$) and the window ($4 \times 3 = 12$): $352 - 21 - 12 = 319$
@answer The painted area is $319\ \text{ft}^2$.
:::

:::mistake Including faces that are not covered
Using the full formula $2lw + 2lh + 2wh$ for an open box counts a lid that does not exist. Read the question carefully and list the faces you need: "no lid", "walls only" and "both ends open" all change the answer.
:::

## Cylinders

Peel the label off a can and lay it flat: it is a rectangle. Its length is the distance around the can, the circumference $2\pi r$, and its width is the height $h$. So a closed cylinder unfolds into **two circles and one rectangle**:

$$SA = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h$$

:::figure
{"type":"solid","shape":"cylinder","labels":{"r":"0.5 m","h":"1.2 m"}}
:::

:::example A closed water tank
A closed cylindrical water tank has a radius of $0.5\ \text{m}$ and a height of $1.2\ \text{m}$. Find its surface area, to the nearest tenth of a square metre.
@step Two circles: $2\pi(0.5)^2 = 0.5\pi\ \text{m}^2$
@step Curved side: $2\pi(0.5)(1.2) = 1.2\pi\ \text{m}^2$
@step $SA = 0.5\pi + 1.2\pi = 1.7\pi \approx 5.34$
@answer The tank has a surface area of about $5.3\ \text{m}^2$.
:::

:::example An open stock tank
A round stock tank for cattle has no lid. It has a diameter of $8\ \text{ft}$ and a height of $2\ \text{ft}$. What area of steel is on the outside, to the nearest tenth of a square foot?
@step $r = 8 \div 2 = 4\ \text{ft}$. With no lid, there is only one circle.
@step Bottom: $\pi(4)^2 = 16\pi$. Curved side: $2\pi(4)(2) = 16\pi$.
@step $SA = 16\pi + 16\pi = 32\pi \approx 100.53$
@answer About $100.5\ \text{ft}^2$ of steel.
:::

:::mistake Diameter or radius?
Plans and labels usually give the **diameter**. Both parts of the cylinder formula use the **radius**, so halve the diameter first.
:::

:::summary
- Surface area is the total area of the outside faces, the same as the area of the net.
- Rectangular prism: $SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh$.
- Closed cylinder: $SA = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h$. The curved side unrolls into a rectangle $2\pi r$ long.
- On the job, include only the faces that get covered: no lid, walls only, ends open.
- Subtract doors and windows, and halve the diameter to get the radius.
:::
